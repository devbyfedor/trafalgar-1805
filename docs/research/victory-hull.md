# HMS Victory in October 1805: hull appearance research (starboard elevation)

Prepared for: Trafalgar 1805, scene 1 "Dawn" (`ShipOfTheLine`, `VICTORY_1805` spec, history-notes.md).
Scope: hull only. Rig, sails and flags are researched separately.
Research date: 24 October 2026.

Confidence key:
- **high**: official/museum or primary source, or several independent sources agree.
- **medium**: one good source, or my own measurement with the assumptions stated.
- **low**: inference, model-maker or forum source, or conflicting evidence.

Note on sources: the official site hms-victory.com no longer resolves (DNS failure on 24 Oct 2026). I used the Wayback Machine copies of its 2012 pages, which were written under the Royal Navy / Victory curator. The single most valuable new source is the **contemporary 1:48 model of Victory made during the 1800–03 repair** (RMG object SLR0513, bought by the NMRN in 2022), photographed broadside-on by Royal Museums Greenwich. Several numbers below come from my own measurements of those photographs and of RMG ship plans; each says so.

## 0. Key points for the drawing

1. **The brief's port counts are gun counts.** Ports per side in 1805: lower deck **16**, middle deck **15** broadside ports plus a small **double-door bow port**, upper deck **15**, quarterdeck about **7–8** openings, forecastle about **2–3**. Guns per side: 15 / 14 / 15 / 6 / 1 long 12-pdr + 1 carronade. So some ports carried no gun.
2. **The ochre is pale and slightly pinkish, not golden.** The NMRN's 2015 paint archaeology gave "Victory Hull Ochre", NCS S 3020-Y40R, about `#C19979`. The "black" is a very dark neutral grey, NCS S 8500-N, about `#2D2C2C`. Port linings and the inner faces of the lids are a strong red, NCS S 2075-Y70R, about `#C04426`. The brief's `#C99A3E` is too yellow and saturated, and `#1B1714` is too brown.
3. **Nelson chequer: yes.** At Trafalgar the lids were black inside ochre bands. When the ship left the 1803 repair the lids were yellow.
4. **Lidless ports stay open.** The 8 central upper-deck ports (3rd–10th from forward) and the forward quarterdeck openings had no lids. Even with "ports closed at dawn" they show as dark openings.
5. **No canopied entry port in 1805 (probably).** The contemporary 1800–03 model has a plain row of side steps at the after end of the waist and an ordinary gun port where today's ornate entry port is. Draw the side steps and leave out the canopy.
6. **Channels are high.** All three channels sit above the upper-deck ports; they were moved up in 1800–03. Chains run down across the upper ochre band.
7. **The bow has an open beakhead, not a round bow.** It has a beakhead bulkhead at upper-deck level, three curved headrails, and a figurehead of the crowned royal arms held by two cupids. Photos from before 1922 show the 1814 round bow, which is wrong for 1805.
8. **Stern.** The stern was closed in 1803 with three tiers of windows. The quarter gallery on the side also has three tiers, from middle deck to quarterdeck. There were no stern davits; they were removed in 1800–03.
9. **Copper shows only as a thin strip at the waterline.** The hull is black from the copper line up to the main wale and the lowest band.
10. **Sheer is gentle.** The wales and bands rise about 1.5 m (5 ft) at each end compared with the lowest point, which is a little abaft amidships. The top of the side steps up in drifts: waist about 29 ft, quarterdeck and forecastle 33–36 ft, poop/taffrail about 41 ft above water.

### 1.1 Heights above the waterline (measured)

**Method.** I measured the RMG broadside photograph of the contemporary 1:48 model SLR0513 (starboard side). I set the scale at about 4.9 px/ft from the 186 ft gun deck. The 226 ft overall length cross-checks within 2%. I assumed the load waterline sits at the lowest point of the main wale's lower edge amidships. That assumption is consistent with the documented 4 ft 6 in sill height: I measured about 5 ft. Expect ±1 ft. **Confidence: medium-low.**

### 2.2 Ports per side in 1805: verified count

The evidence:

1. **Midshipman William Rivers**, who served in Victory at Trafalgar (quoted by Martyn Downer, Sotheby's 2022): *"she had an additional port cut on the lower deck, which made 16 on either side, and the two transome-ports filled in, which made only two."* The official 2012 site also says two extra ports were cut on the lower gun deck during 1800–03.
2. **My count on the present ship**, from a 2013 starboard broadside photo cropped deck by deck, cross-checked against a port-side photo. Lower 16; middle 15 positions (one of them is today's entry port) plus a double-door bow port; upper 15.
3. **My count on RMG plan ZAZ0120**, the ship before 1800. Lower 15, each directly under one of 15 upper-deck ports.
4. **My count on the contemporary model SLR0513**, 1800–03, both broadsides. Lower 15, upper 15, middle 15 plus a bow port. Compared with the pre-1800 plan, the model's lower deck has the **new bow port** (one port-space forward of the foremost upper-deck port) but lacks the aftmost port, under the aftmost upper-deck port.

## 3. Paint scheme in 1805

### 3.1 The 2015 NMRN repaint: what the research found (paraphrased; links in §8)

- NMRN press release, June 2015 (Culture24): research showed that at Trafalgar she was painted outside in **pale yellow and dark grey**. The paint survey was led by conservator **Michael Crick-Smith (University of Lincoln)**, who took several hundred complete paint samples and found up to 72 layers in places. Andrew Baines, Head of Historic Ships, said the colours were fixed by combining that evidence with the original accounts for Victory's stores. Hardy chose pigments the Navy supplied free, among them lead white and ochre. **high**
- Baines, interviewed by All About History (2015), in our summary: the repaint aims at the ship Nelson joined on 14 September 1805. Her paints were chosen for cost (black, white lead and a yellow ochre whose tone can vary a good deal). In his account Nelson wanted the ochre on her sides lightened with white; the request was still on its way to the Admiralty at his death and was refused there. The chequer dates from her becoming Nelson's flagship in 1803. **high** (as the curator's statement)
- **Official paint specification**, NMRN "HMS Victory ITT Repainting", October 2016:
  - Sikkens Ochre **NCS S 3020-Y40R**, for everything previously painted buff
  - Sikkens Black **NCS S 8500-N**, for everything previously painted black
  - Iron work black RAL 9005
  - Gun port maroon **NCS 2075-Y70R**, for the gun ports and tompions
  - A blue for the beakhead bulkhead (a Brewers local formula)
  - Lead White NCS S 1005-Y20R, used inboard
  
  **high**
- The colour's name, "**Victory Hull Ochre**". Baines said the colour changes with the light and is close to a pale terracotta (Telegraph, October 2015, via forum reposts). Press and public called it "faded light peach", "pastel pink" and "smoked trout". **medium** (the newspaper text itself could not be fetched)
- The NCS colour pages give approximate screen hex values: S 3020-Y40R = **#C19979**; S 8500-N = **#2D2C2C**; S 2075-Y70R = **#C04426**. **high** (as approximations; NCS is defined physically)
- Artelia, the project manager, October 2015, called the gun port colour particularly fiery. **medium**

## 4. Details for the drawing

### 4.1 Bow and head

- **Beakhead bulkhead and open head.** In 1805 the forecastle ended in a flat beakhead bulkhead at upper-deck level. Forward of it was the open head platform, with gratings, seats of ease and "round houses", and three curved headrails on each side sweeping up to the figurehead. The 1814 repair replaced this with a Seppings round bow, and the 1920s restoration rebuilt the beakhead (McGowan pp. 27, 34 via Wikipedia). **high**
- The head structure on today's ship is mostly modern. The NMRN log says the beakhead was much altered both before and after Trafalgar, and its external timbers are now mostly modern. **high**
- **Figurehead (1801–03):** two cupids holding the royal arms under the royal crown, with the Garter and its Norman French motto, and the escutcheon of Hanover (white horse and Hanoverian crown) at the centre. The starboard cupid wears a blue sash, the port cupid a red one. It was carved in 1801 by George Williams for £50 (official site, Figurehead page). Rivers, the eyewitness: "two naked boys supported a shield with the Standard engraved thereupon, with a Royal Crown on the top." A scroll below reads "DIEU ET MON DROIT" (present replica). **high**. At Trafalgar the starboard cupid lost a leg and the port cupid an arm (Rivers, via the official site); not needed for dawn. The replacement figurehead of 1815 was about 10 ft tall, a guide to scale (Yahoo/Independent on the 1815 figurehead). **medium**
- **Catheads** project at forecastle level just abaft the beakhead bulkhead, about 0.875 L from the taffrail. **medium**
- **Anchors at sea:** the bowers were catted and fished. The ring hangs at the cathead and the shank and flukes lie against the side on the bill-board/anchor lining between the 1st and 2nd upper-deck ports, resting on the middle wale; stocks stand roughly vertical. The sheet anchor is stowed against the side abaft the fore channel, palm on a block at the waist capping. Bower and sheet anchors were all the same size. Sources: JoTiKa 2003 manual; present-ship photos (2007, 2012, 2013). **medium**
- **Hawse holes:** 2 per side, low on the bow under the head's cheeks. **medium-high**

### 4.2 Channels, chains, deadeyes

- **Level:** the fore, main and mizzen channels were above the upper-deck ports in 1805. The 1800–03 repair moved the chainwales above the gun ports so that working the guns would not foul the rigging (McGowan pp. 20–21 via Wikipedia). The contemporary model and the present ship confirm it: the channels sit on the black strake directly above the upper ochre band, about 23 ft above the waterline. Before 1800 they sat lower, between the middle- and upper-deck ports (ZAZ0120, ZAZ0121). **high**
- **Chains and preventer plates** run from each deadeye down and slightly aft (raked to the shroud line) across the upper ochre band and end on the black strake (channel wale) above the middle band (model; photos). **high**
- About 216 deadeyes are fitted on the standing rigging in total (official site). Channel extents along the hull are in §1.3. Shroud counts belong to the rig research. **medium**

### 4.3 Entry port or side steps

- **Present ship:** an ornate carved and gilded canopied entry port on the middle deck, at the 7th middle-deck position from aft, just abaft the waist break. Battened side steps run up and down from it, and there is one on each side. The starboard canopy dates from 1992, in mahogany; the original carved oak canopy was decayed by 1923 (NMRN log). **high** (as present-day fact)
- **1805:** the 1800–03 contemporary model has **no entry port on either side**. It shows a vertical row of side steps from the top of the side down to the lower band at the after end of the waist (≈0.45 L from the taffrail), with an ordinary lidded port where the entry port is now. A researcher on Ships of Scale ("dafi") reads the evidence as: an entry port until 1788, **none from 1788 to 1828**, one port further aft from 1828, and the "traditional" position recreated in 1922. He cites the 1788 plans, Dodd and Swaine paintings, Constable's 1803 and Turner's 1805 drawings (neither shows a port), Todd 1807 and Christian 1824/27. Steel describes such steps as "about three feet in length, six inches asunder, and five inches deep, from the top of the side down to the load-water line". **medium**: the contemporary model is strong evidence; I have not seen the Turner and Constable drawings myself.
- **Recommendation:** draw side steps, a narrow ladder of short horizontal battens, perhaps with man-ropes, and leave out the canopied entry port.

### 4.4 Stern and quarter galleries

- **Closed stern since 1803.** Rivers: "the stern galleries were taken away and her stern made a flat one." Downer's sale note on the model gives the closed stern three arches across the transom, glazed, with balustrades carved in shallow relief and moulded work at the taffrail and the quarter pieces. **high**
- **Stern windows:** three tiers, from the bottom at middle-deck level (wardroom), then upper deck (admiral's great cabin), then quarterdeck (captain's cabin). The tiers are separated by two carved balustrade bands, which are false galleries. On the model the tiers have about 10, 9 and 9 lights from bottom to top. The name VICTORY is on the counter below the lowest tier; the 1800–03 model shows it. Three stern lanterns on today's ship; their 1805 number is not verified. From a pure side view only the stern's outline, the taffrail and the lanterns show. **medium-high**
- **Quarter gallery (side view):** three tiers of windows, each with 3 lights in the side elevation, at middle-deck, upper-deck and quarterdeck levels. Carved balustrade bands separate them. There is a black upper finishing at the top and a carved lower finishing (a "drop") hanging below the lowest tier to about the top of the lower ochre band. The gallery rakes aft with the stern. Sources: model, ZAZ0120/0121, present ship. **high**
- No stern davits in 1805: the stern jolly-boat davits were removed during 1800–03 (Downer). Today's ship has port and starboard boat davits on the poop (ITT 2016). **medium-high**
- The rudder hangs on the sternpost with copper-alloy gudgeons and pintles. Rudder chains run from the rudder to eyebolts under the quarter gallery drops (JoTiKa; model). **medium**

### 4.5 Wales, rails, bulwarks, gangways, poop

- **Wales:** the main wale (black) runs below the lower ports at the waterline, then the middle wale (black strake between the lower and middle bands), then the channel wale (black strake between the middle and upper bands). These are thick strakes standing slightly proud of the side; a thin shadow line under each is enough in a flat style. Steel's terminology uses main wales, sheer- or middle-wales and channel wales; JoTiKa calls them lower, middle and top wale. **medium-high**
- **Topside:** black from the upper band to the rail, with narrow moulded sheer rails, also black. The waist is lower than the quarterdeck and forecastle (§1.1). **medium**
- **Gangways and booms:** gangways along both sides link the quarterdeck and forecastle over the open waist. Skid beams (booms) span the waist and carry the boats; they show in the model's three-quarter view. **high**
- **Quarterdeck and forecastle bulwarks:** solid black bulwarks pierced with ports, topped by hammock cranes and nettings. That is the model, today's ship and JoTiKa. Downer's note that the model shows some details that were never completed on the ship, such as the poop railing and solid bulwarks, means the real 1805 arrangement may have been more open. See §7. **low-medium**
- **Poop:** long, running from the taffrail to about 0.23 L. It may have had a solid barricade fitted in 1780 for poop carronades, with 3 disused ports a side (JoTiKa), or the open rail shown on the model. Either way it carried hammock nettings. No guns in 1805. **low-medium**
- **Hammock nettings:** rope nets on iron hammock cranes along the poop, quarterdeck, gangways and forecastle. By dawn on 21 Oct the hammocks would be stowed in them and the ship cleared for action. Draw them as a light grey-white irregular band 2–4 ft high above the rails. **low-medium** (the hammock cloths' colour and use are unverified)
- **Fenders:** two vertical fender timbers in the waist, abreast the main hatch, on today's ship. Steel describes such fenders "from the top of the side down to the upper edge of the main-wales". They are not shown on the 1800–03 model, so they are optional. **low**

### 4.6 Boats

- Victory's boats, from the official site: a 34 ft launch, 32 ft barge, 28 ft pinnace, 2 × 25 ft cutters and an 18 ft cutter (jolly boat). JoTiKa (2003) says only **4 were aboard at Trafalgar**: launch, barge, pinnace and the 18 ft cutter. **medium / low**
- **Stowage:** nested on the skid beams over the waist, between the fore and main masts. A 2019 port-side photo of today's ship shows the covered boats just above the waist hammock nettings, so the top of the launch stack would **just show above the nettings in the waist**. **low-medium**
- Paint: white sides, black and ochre top strakes; the barge olive green with gold (§3.2). **medium**

## 7. Doubts and conflicts

1. **Lower-deck ports, 16 or 15.** Rivers (an eyewitness) and the official site say an extra port each side was cut in 1800–03, making 16, and today's ship has 16 by my count. The only contemporary model shows 15: it has the new bow port but no port under the aftmost upper-deck port. The pre-1800 plan shows 15 aligned under the upper ports. **I recommend 16.** My photo counts are subject to perspective error and one starboard lower port is hidden by visitor stairs; I inferred it from the spacing and the upper-deck port above it.
2. **Entry port in 1805.** Today's ship has canopied entry ports on both sides. The 1800–03 model has none, and a forum researcher dates the gap from 1788 to 1828 using period drawings. The McKay *Anatomy* drawings (1987) reportedly follow the 1920s reconstruction. **I recommend side steps only.**
3. **Quarterdeck, forecastle and poop bulwarks.** The model shows solid quarterdeck and forecastle bulwarks with ports and an open poop rail. Downer says the model shows details, such as the poop railing and solid bulwarks, that were never completed on the ship, so in reality they may have been more open. JoTiKa's "Trafalgar condition" has a solid poop barricade with 3 disused ports a side. The numbers of quarterdeck openings (7 or 8) and forecastle openings (2–3) are uncertain.
4. **Ochre shade.** The NMRN says "pale yellow"; the press says pinkish, peach or terracotta; 1922–2015 used a bright yellow later found to be wrong. Baines says ochre can range from straw to a pale brown, and that Nelson wanted it paler. The NCS code is official; how it reads on screen depends on lighting. A forum reports that Goodwin (2013, *Mariner's Mirror*) quotes a six-to-one white-to-yellow mix requested by Nelson; I could not verify this.
5. **Black or dark grey.** The NMRN says "dark grey" while the specification says "Black NCS S 8500-N". Either way it is a neutral charcoal, not a warm brown-black.
6. **Red inside the ports.** The official 2012 scheme and the 2016 specification both use red ("red ochre" in 2012, a vermilion "gun port maroon" in 2016). A forum reports that Goodwin (2013) found no red paint in the 1805 carpenters' accounts (black, yellow, white, verdigris, a little Prussian blue); another forum post says Baines' analysis found traces of vermilion. Not directly visible with closed lids, except in the lidless upper ports.
7. **Lid colour, timing.** The 1800–03 model shows yellow lids. Baines says the chequer was applied in 1803; the official 2012 text and Downer say the lids were painted black "later" or "before Trafalgar". Black at Trafalgar is the consensus.
8. **Head, beakhead and cathead colours.** The model (black/gilt rails, blue-grey knee, black beakhead bulkhead), the official 2012 scheme (ochre and blue rails, blue bulkhead) and today's ship (ochre and blue) disagree. The model's paint may have been restored at some point.
9. **Figurehead supporters.** The official site and Rivers say two cupids. A 1931 John Player cigarette card claims the Trafalgar figurehead had a marine (port) and a sailor (starboard) as supporters. I treat the card as an error.
10. **Draught.** 24 ft 3 in / 25 ft 6 in (Goodwin 2015) against 28 ft 9 in (Wikipedia infobox, unsourced). Also 2,162 vs 2,142 tons burthen; 51 ft 10 in vs 51 ft 7½ in vs 52 ft breadth; 151 ft 3⅝ in vs 152 ft 6⅝ in keel; threedecks even gives 184 ft for the gun deck, against 186 ft everywhere else.
11. **"Bowsprit to taffrail 226 ft 6 in"** on the official site conflicts with its own 110 ft bowsprit projection. Wharton's "figure head to taffrail" reading is the plausible one.
12. **New figurehead date:** 1990 per McGowan via Wikipedia, 1992 per the NMRN.
13. **Boats:** 6 boats (official) or 4 at Trafalgar (JoTiKa); whether the boats were hoisted out or towed astern before action is not established for dawn.
14. **Upper-deck lids:** the present ship and JoTiKa have lids on the 2 forward and 5 aft ports and none on the central 8. The 1800–03 model shows all upper ports open and does not model lids.
15. **Heights, sheer and positions** come from measuring photographs: one of a contemporary model, which is near-orthographic but still a model, and low-resolution plan scans. There is about ±1 ft vertical error, ±3 ft longitudinal error and an assumed waterline. McKay's *The 100-Gun Ship Victory* profiles would refine them; I had no access.
16. **Copper strip height and colour** are inferences; I found no documented figure.
17. **Not inspected first-hand:** Turner's December 1805 pencil sketches (Tate, "Nelson" sketchbook, Turner Bequest LXXXIX; e.g. D05447 starboard view towards stern, D05485–86 bow and fore part of starboard side) and Constable's April 1803 sketches. The Tate image server was rate-limiting. The Turner oil *The Victory Returning from Trafalgar* (Yale) is too loose for detail.
18. **Things I could not reach:** McKay (1987/2000), McGowan (2003), Goodwin's *Pocket Manual* (2015) and his 2013 *Mariner's Mirror* paint article; the Telegraph article of October 2015. Their content appears here only second-hand (Wikipedia, forums), as marked.

# Cádiz harbour, morning of 19 October 1805 (about 7 to 8.15 a.m.)

Which vessels lay in the bay, and where, as far as the sources say. Written for the scene 2 chart (`src/scenes/02-road/fleets.ts`, `movements.ts`) and the history notes.

## Method

- Main source: Desbrière, *La campagne maritime de 1805: Trafalgar* (1907), narrative and annexes, from the archive.org plain text. Every figure that matters below was checked on the page image: Fougueux (annex pp. 213, 216), Argus (p. 268), Thémis (p. 273), Hermione (p. 242), Escaño's relation (p. 8).
- British side: the logs in *Logs of the Great Sea Fights* II (NRS 1900); James, *Naval History* III; Nicolas VII.
- Spanish side: the Spanish annexes in Desbrière (the *Montañés* and *Príncipe de Asturias* diaries, Escaño's letter); Fernández Duro, *Armada española* VIII (1902); Ferrer de Couto (1851), which adds nothing on the anchorage.
- Also: Fraser, *The Enemy at Trafalgar* (1906); H. Robinson, *Sea Drift* (1858), a memoir written long after.
- Page numbers are as printed. "Annex" means Desbrière's second part (*pièces annexes*). Leaf links are given where I checked the leaf.
- Times are ships' times (apparent time) as written.

## Short answers

1. **All 33 of the line were in the bay.** Escaño's own list of the 15 Spanish ships is headed as the squadron "al ancla en la bahía de Cádiz el 19 de Octubre de 1805" (Fernández Duro VIII, p. 318, appendix 1 to ch. XIV). The 18 French are in Villeneuve's state of 16 September (annex pp. 85–86) and all sailed on the 19th or 20th. **High.**
2. **Only one ship of the line is placed by name in the narrows: the Fougueux.** No source read puts the San Justo, the Rayo, the Santa Ana or any other ship there on the 19th. **High** for the Fougueux. **No evidence** for the San Justo.
3. **The Fougueux warped out in the evening, not the morning.** She was ordered to warp at 5.30 p.m. on the 19th. She moved, with her own boats and hawsers, to a berth "en travers de Cadix" (abreast of the city), on one anchor (annex pp. 213, 216). On the morning of the 19th she still lay between the forts, hove short on one anchor, boats in. **High.**
4. **The outermost ships were the seven that got out**: Algésiras, Achille, Argonaute, Neptune, Héros, Duguay-Trouin and Bahama. Two eyewitnesses on board other ships say so: the Fougueux and the Montañés. **High.**
5. **The frigate Thémis lay at the head of the anchorage**, with "quelques vaisseaux et frégates qui étaient à la tête" (annex p. 273). The Hermione and Rhin weighed early with the outer group (annex pp. 207–208, 242). No source puts any frigate inside the lines. **High** for the Thémis, **medium** for the Hermione and Rhin.
6. **The brigs Argus and Furet and the brig (or corvette) Observateur were at anchor in the bay.** No source gives their berths. **High** for presence, **none** for places.
7. **Not of the sailing fleet:** the Terrible 74 (disarmed) and the San Fulgencio 64 (docked in September; James says she did not move down with the fleet on 9–10 October). Both were most likely in the arsenal of La Carraca, about 6 nm south-east of the anchorage. They were not with the fleet and were very probably outside a close view of the outer bay and the city. **Medium** for the state, **low** for the place.
8. **The Spanish frigate Flora and the corvettes Mercurio and Indagadora** came from Ferrol with the fleet in August (Desbrière p. 99). They did not sail on 19–20 October. No source read says where they were on the 19th. **Low.**
9. **No gunboat stations that morning.** The fleet's launches, armed as gunboats, were taken off their stations, unarmed and hoisted in on the evening of the 18th (*Montañés*; *Príncipe de Asturias*; Escaño). **High.** The port's own gunboats existed (James III 380), but no source places them on the 19th.
10. **The morning was busy.** By 8 a.m. the Euryalus counted 19 of the enemy under way. All the others but two had their topsails hoisted to the mastheads. The Argus counted 17 under sail at 9. In the calm many then anchored again where they were. **High.** This is far more movement than the scene's licence gives (see section 6).

## 1. The combined fleet in the bay

### 1.1 How many

- **33 of the line, 5 frigates and 2 brigs** sailed (Thémis, annex p. 273; Quiot of the Achille, annex p. 266; James III 385).
- **The Observateur** stayed behind by order. On the 18th Villeneuve left her because she sailed badly. He sent her seamen to the Indomptable and her gunners to the Algésiras (Villeneuve to Decrès, 18 October, annex p. 102; Desbrière p. 174; Croizé's letter of 20 October, annex p. 158). On the morning of the 19th she was a brig at anchor with few men aboard. **High.**
- **Name.** Villeneuve's state of 16 September lists her among the "bricks" (annex p. 86). Croizé calls her a "brick" in his letter. Desbrière's heading calls her a corvette (annex p. 158). **High** for "brig".
- **French part:** 18 of the line; the frigates Hortense, Cornélie, Thémis, Rhin and Hermione; the brigs Furet, Observateur and Argus (Villeneuve's state of 16 September, annex pp. 85–86). **High.**

### 1.2 Where the fleet lay: what the sources give in general

- **Position.** From 9–10 October the fleet lay near the harbour mouth, out of range of the city's guns, moored with springs (already in the history notes; Escaño, annex p. 98).
- **Villeneuve chose it.** On 2 October he wrote that placing the ships further in would have been safer. He refused, because weighing would have been even harder (annex p. 95). **High.**
- **A defensive arrangement.** Gravina and Villeneuve had a defensive "ligne d'embossage" studied (Desbrière p. 137). Churruca wrote on 11 October that, three days before, the ships at the anchorage were being gone over to arrange them so that they could not be attacked (Fernández Duro VIII p. 319, letter printed from Salas). No source read gives the shape of the lines or the order of the ships. The chart's three WNW–ESE lines are a choice, not a finding. Faden's plan of 1797 draws that year's Spanish defensive line running north–south. **Medium.**
- **The fast ships went first.** Within ten days of arrival Villeneuve moved a number of his fastest ships down from the inner port to just within the harbour mouth. They were a squadron for particular service under Magon (Fraser p. 29). On 17–18 October Magon was given a night sortie: four French and three Spanish of the line and the Hermione (Villeneuve, annex p. 101; Escaño, annex p. 100). Philibert says the Algésiras made sail at once on the 19th, together with several ships of the division that was to sail under Magon (annex p. 229). **Medium.** This fits the outermost berths of Magon's ships, but the seven that got out came from several squadrons (Desbrière p. 161 n. 3).

### 1.3 By group, as far as any source goes

**(a) Outermost, toward the passes: the seven that got out.**
- The Fougueux saw "7 ou 8 des vaisseaux les plus en dehors" weigh (annex p. 213).
- The Montañés: by 8.30 a.m. most of the ships anchored further out were already under sail (annex p. 373).
- Desbrière's narrative: only the most advanced ships and frigates seem to have carried out the order (p. 160).
- Philibert names the seven (annex pp. 229–230).
- **High.**
- A weak hint on their place within the bay: at 7 the Euryalus "saw the northernmost ships under way" (NRS II 145). If those were the outer group, the outer group lay at the north side of the anchorage. **Low.**

**(b) The main body: the other 25 of the line.**
- No source gives individual berths.
- The Montañés lay further in than the outer group, since she made sail only at 8.30, after most of the outer ships (annex p. 373). **Medium.**
- **Two ships of the line without topsails.** At 8 a.m. the Euryalus saw all the ships not under way with "their topsails to the mast head", "except the Spanish Rear-Admiral and another line-of-battle ship" (NRS II 146). The flag of Cisneros, a *jefe de escuadra* (rear-admiral), flew in the Santísima Trinidad, so she is the likeliest. But British logs used the term loosely. On the 21st two logs have a Spanish "Rear-Admiral" striking at about 4 p.m.: the Victory's at 4.15, to windward (NRS II 185), and another at 4 (NRS II 287). British observers may have taken a Spanish brigadier's broad pennant for a rear-admiral's flag. The second ship is not named. **Low** for the identification.
- Between about 7 and 8 the Spanish Neptuno asked for a pilot to weigh (Montañés diary, annex p. 373). **Medium** that the Spanish ship is meant; the French Neptune was among the seven.

**(c) The narrows between the Puntal and Matagorda forts: the Fougueux.**
- She lay "entre les forts du Pontal et du Port-Réal", "trop enfoncé dans la baie" for an easy weighing (annex pp. 213, 216).
- "Port-Réal" is read as the Matagorda fort, which stands on the Puerto Real shore opposite the Puntal. Escaño names the same pair for the Argonauta in August, "entre los castillejos de Puntal y Matagorda" (annex p. 8). **Medium-high.**
- Details in section 2.

**(d) The inner bay beyond the narrows.**
- No ship of the sailing fleet is placed there on the 19th.
- The Thémis says "Les vaisseaux les plus en dedans" stayed at anchor, ready, waiting for a breeze (annex p. 273). This means the inner ships of the fleet in general and names none.
- Fraser says that on the evening of the 19th every effort was made to tow or warp as many as possible of the ships in the harbour down towards the sea (pp. 84–85). He gives no names or source.
- **High** that the sources name no other inner ship.

## 2. The narrows: the Fougueux, and who else

### 2.1 The Fougueux

**18 October**
- At 9 p.m. she was unmoored, hove short on one anchor, with her boats hoisted in.
- With the westerly wind she could not weigh from between the forts.
- (Bazin's reports, annex pp. 213 and 216.) **High.**

**19 October, morning**
- She stayed where she was. She made no attempt to weigh. **High.**

**19 October, evening: the warp**
- She saw "7 ou 8" of the outermost ships weigh, and several frigates.
- Her captain, Baudoin, ordered her warped into a position from which she could weigh. "Il était alors 5 h. 1/2 du soir" (annex p. 213).
- She had only her own boats, hawsers and grelins. She got into a berth from which she could weigh with any wind, on one anchor, and hoisted her boats in again.
- Bazin's second report: she asked to be placed better for weighing, warped, and "vint se placer en travers de Cadix" (annex p. 216).
- **High** for the hour and the method. **Medium** for the berth: "en travers de" means abreast of, so she lay off the city in the outer bay, between the narrows and the main group. The exact spot is not given.

**20 October**
- She weighed at first light with the general signal.
- **Leaf links.** Annex p. 213: https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n492/mode/1up
- Annex p. 216: https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n495/mode/1up

### 2.2 The San Justo

- **She came out of the arsenal in September.** Desbrière's narrative counts 12 Spanish in the roads on 16 September: 9 from Ferrol and 3 armed at Cádiz. It adds the Rayo and San Leandro on the 28th and the Santa Ana in early October (p. 134). By elimination the San Justo was one of the three in the roads on 16 September, with the Santísima Trinidad and the Bahama. **Medium.**
- **She was new and badly manned.** On 8 October the council of war called the Santa Ana, the Rayo and the San Justo "à peine sortis de l'arsenal" (procès-verbal, annex p. 97, quoted by Desbrière p. 128). Villeneuve's letter of the same day has "à peine sortis du port" (annex p. 96). This says they were fresh from fitting out, not where they lay. **High.**
- **No source places her in the narrows on 19 October.** James says the whole fleet but one ship moved to the entrance on 9–10 October (III 385, see 2.4). **Verdict: no evidence.**

### 2.3 The Rayo, the Santa Ana, other ships fitted out at Cádiz

**Mid-September: the Pontal as a staging berth**
- On 16 September Villeneuve wrote that 13 Spanish ships were in the roads and "trois sont encore au Pontal" for repairs, fitting out or crews (annex p. 87). So the Puntal was the staging berth for ships coming out of La Carraca. **High.**

**By 24 September they had moved down**
- Villeneuve on 24 September: all 15 were in the roads except the Santa Ana. She had already come out of La Carraca. The Rayo was in the roads but not ready (annex p. 88). **High.**
- The Santa Ana was ready in the first days of October (Desbrière p. 134). Gravina expected her with the fleet about nine days after 28 September (Desbrière p. 136 n.). **Medium.**

**The Rayo on the 20th**
- She was the last out. She told the Argus "qu'il avait eu beaucoup d'ancres à lever" (annex p. 269).
- The Observateur says she had much trouble getting under sail (annex p. 158).
- This fits a heavy mooring. It does not place her. The Argus was able to tack about near her while waiting and to send a boat. That suggests the outer bay rather than the narrows. **Low.**

**Verdict**
- From October no source places any Spanish ship at the Puntal or further in. **Medium** (absence of evidence in the sources read).

### 2.4 James's "one ship"

- **What he says.** On 9–10 October the fleet moved to the entrance of the harbour, "with the exception of one ship, the San-Fulgencio 64 (for some unknown reason detained)" (James III 385). James wrote from British reports. In mid-September Gravina had put the San Fulgencio into dock and used her crew elsewhere (section 3), so she would not have moved.
- **The other reading.** The one ship the British saw stay behind may have been the Fougueux, which did stay in the narrows. The British could not have told the two apart at that range.
- **Low** for either reading. James's sentence is no evidence that the Fougueux moved.

### 2.5 Recommendation for the chart

- Keep the Fougueux alone in the narrows.
- Move the San Justo into the main body.
- If the chart wants to hint at "the innermost ships" (Thémis), it can only do so without names.

## 3. Men-of-war in the harbour that were not part of the sailing fleet

### 3.1 Terrible 74 and San Fulgencio 64

**Where they came from**
- Both came to Cádiz with the fleet. The Terrible had come from the West Indies with Gravina and was damaged at Finisterre. The San Fulgencio sailed from Ferrol (Desbrière p. 99; James III 373–374). So the bay held 35 of the line: 18 French, 11 Spanish from Ferrol and 6 Spanish fitted out at Cádiz. **High.**

**What happened to them: the sources**
- **Gravina, 16 September (annex p. 84):** "j'ai désarmé le Terrible". He was putting the San Fulgencio "dans le bassin" (into dock) to survey her bottom. The crews of both went to the newly armed ships. **High.**
- **Lauriston, 2 September (annex p. 112):** Gravina would send the leaking Terrible back in. **High.**
- **Desbrière's narrative (p. 134):** has the Terrible go into the dock. This is the only source that puts the Terrible rather than the San Fulgencio in the dock. **Low** for that detail.
- **Fernández Duro (VIII p. 308):** Gravina exchanged the Terrible, damaged at Finisterre, for "otro de los desarmados en el arsenal". This implies the Terrible went to the arsenal as a laid-up ship. **Medium.**
- **James (III 385):** the Terrible was disarmed and her crew shared out. The San Fulgencio did not move down on 9–10 October (2.4). **High** for the Terrible.

**Where they were on 19 October**
- No source gives either ship's berth.
- The dry docks of the bay were at the arsenal of La Carraca, at the inner end of the bay near San Fernando, about 36°29.8′ N 6°10.6′ W. Its three docks were completed in 1788 (Wikipedia, as a pointer, citing secondary works).
- The 1796 phrase "the Ships going up to the Caraccas" shows that laid-up Spanish ships went there (Nicolas VII, addenda, pp. cv, cix).
- **Most likely both lay at La Carraca**, in or near the dock and unmanned. **Low** for the place, **medium** for "not with the fleet".

**Distances, for the view**
- La Carraca from the anchorage centre (36°33.8′ N 6°16.4′ W): about 6.1 nm, south-east.
- From the city (Torre Tavira): about 6.2 nm.
- From the narrows glyph (36°31.0′ N 6°15.2′ W): about 3.9 nm.
- La Carraca lies behind the narrows, among the low marshes of the Isla de León. A disarmed ship (topmasts down, at least) or a ship in dock would show little. **Medium.**

**What to record in the history notes**
- Neither was in the anchorage on 19 October. Neither belongs in a close view of the outer bay and the city.

**British counts (a side note)**
- British counts in late September and early October ran from 34 to 36 of the line: 34 on 1 and 8 October (James III 380); 35–36 in Nelson's letters (Nicolas VII).
- On 1 October the Euryalus saw "18 French and 16 Spanish" in the outer harbour. One more than the 15 that sailed would fit the San Fulgencio or the Terrible being seen before 9 October. It may also be a miscount. **Low.**

### 3.2 Spanish frigate and corvettes

- **What came from Ferrol.** The Spanish contingent that sailed from Ferrol on 10–13 August included the frigate Flora (34) and the corvettes Mercurio and Indagadora (Desbrière p. 99, from the Spanish state). Desbrière sums the Spanish share as 11 of the line, 1 frigate and 1 corvette, so one of the two corvettes may not have come. Escaño gives the same Spanish share (annex p. 7). **High** for the frigate, **medium** for the corvettes.
- **Not seen at sea.** None sailed with the fleet on 19–20 October. The fleet at sea had only the five French frigates and the two French brigs (Thémis, annex p. 273; James III 385; Collingwood). **High.**
- **Not seen in the outer harbour.** On 1 October the Euryalus saw only "four frigates and two brigs" with the fleet in the outer harbour (James III 380). That fits the French ones. **Medium.**
- **Where they were on 19 October: not found.** A modern summary lists the Flora at Cádiz in 1808 (Poza de Santa Isabel), which suggests she stayed there. **Low.**
- **For the history notes:** "Their place on 19 October is not recorded."

### 3.3 Gunboats and armed boats (*fuerzas sutiles*)

**What they were**
- From late August the Spaniards prepared gunboats, howitzer and mortar boats, faluchos, boats and barges to defend the city and the fleet (Escaño, annex p. 98).
- They were organised in divisions as in 1797 (Fernández Duro VIII p. 308). In 1797 most of that flotilla had been ships' launches with a 24-pounder each (Fernández Duro VIII p. 143).

**Before the 18th**
- About 6 October Gravina dissolved the flotilla and sent its officers and men back to their ships.
- After the council of 8 October the divisions of small craft were posted again at their former stations, manned from the fleet (Escaño, annex p. 99).
- On 4 October Spanish gunboats pulled out and fired on the Euryalus and Hydra (James III 380).
- **No source gives where the stations were.**

**On the 18th**
- In Villeneuve's presence Gravina ordered the gunboat stations withdrawn and the men back to their ships. The orders were carried out that morning (Escaño, annex p. 100; Fernández Duro VIII p. 314).
- At 5–5.30 p.m. the signals were made to take the guns out of the launches, call the crews aboard and hoist in the boats. The *Príncipe de Asturias* records that the launches came off the stations, their guns were removed and all small craft were hoisted in (*Montañés*, annex p. 372; *Príncipe de Asturias*, annex p. 383).
- **High:** the fleet's armed launches were not on station on the morning of the 19th.
- On the 19th the only boats in the water in numbers were those towing ships out (section 4.2).

**The port's own gunboats**
- No source read says whether the port's own gunboats (not the fleet's launches) lay anywhere on the 19th. **Not found.**

### 3.4 Hulks

- **Not found.**
- On the evening of the 20th Croizé planned to keep spare men "dans un ponton mouillé à cet effet près du brick" (annex p. 158). That is a future arrangement, not evidence for the 19th.
- The Sirius saw "7 sail and a hulk at anchor in the mouth of Cadiz Harbour" on 24 October (NRS II 315). That is after the battle; the hulk is probably a dismasted ship.

### 3.5 Merchant shipping

- Not researched. The bay certainly held merchant and coasting vessels kept in by the blockade. None is a man-of-war.

## 4. Frigates and brigs in the anchorage, and that morning

### 4.1 Frigates

**Thémis.** Jugan's report:
- At the 6 a.m. signal she weighed "avec quelques vaisseaux et frégates qui étaient à la tête", making for the bay entrance.
- She had to drop anchor because of the calm and of the current, which would have carried her back in (annex p. 273).
- Desbrière: she anchored at the bay entrance to hold against the flood (p. 160).
- At 4.30 p.m. she weighed again on the signal to all frigates and beat out.
- **High.** Before 6 a.m. she lay at the head (outer end) of the anchorage. Between about 7 and 8.15 she was either under way toward the entrance or already anchored at the entrance. The hour of anchoring is not given.
- Leaf: https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n554/mode/1up

**Hermione.** Mahé's report:
- She weighed in the morning (hour left blank) in a very light ESE air.
- When it fell calm, "the ships dropped an anchor where they were" so as not to drift back in on the flood.
- Ordered by Magon to reconnoitre, she went out with the help of her boats (annex p. 242). Desbrière: towed out by her boats (p. 160).
- By about 9.30–10 she was reconnoitring outside (Philibert, annex p. 229; Desbrière p. 161).
- She was named for Magon's night sortie of the 18th (annex p. 101).
- **Medium** that she lay with the outer group. **High** that she was under way or being towed in the bay during 7–8.15.
- Leaf: https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n521/mode/1up

**Rhin.** Her journal:
- She weighed at 6.15 on the order, in a very faint easterly, and went out with the help of her boats (annex pp. 207–208).
- Desbrière has her leave the bay with Magon's division from about 10, the last of them through the passes about 1 p.m. (p. 161).
- **High** that she was under way (towed) in the bay during 7–8.15. **Medium** that she lay with the outer group.

**Cornélie, Hortense.**
- Nothing on their berths.
- They were still at anchor in the morning: only one to three frigates got out (Rhin: three; Philibert: two; *Príncipe*: one).
- **Medium.** See section 6 on the evening.

**Inside the lines?**
- No source puts any frigate inside the lines, toward the city.
- The only frigate whose berth is described, the Thémis, lay at the head.
- On 1 October frigates and brigs lay with the fleet in the outer harbour (James III 380). **Medium.**

### 4.2 Brigs

**Argus.** Taillard's report (annex p. 268):
- On the evening of the 18th she unmoored. At 10.30 p.m. she was hove short, held by a small kedge.
- At 6 a.m. on the 19th she was ready to sail, topsails hoisted, when the signal came.
- At 8.30 Croizé of the Observateur brought her the order to sail only with the last ship of the fleet. She stayed at anchor all the 19th and sailed at 8.30 a.m. on the 20th.
- **High.** Berth not given.
- Leaf: https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n549/mode/1up

**Furet.** Demay's report (annex pp. 173–174):
- Signals and counts only. No berth. She sailed on the 20th.
- **High** for presence.

**Observateur.**
- At anchor, staying behind, her crew drafted away (section 1.1).
- Her captain was moving about by boat that morning with orders (Argus, 8.30).
- **High** for presence. Berth not given.

## 5. The morning hour by hour

**Weather**
- Dawn clear, with a very light air from the north-east or east (*Montañés*; Philibert; Argus; Furet). The Héros has a light southerly to SSE, almost calm (annex p. 165). The Hermione has "très faible" from the ESE.
- Calm by about 9.
- From about 10 a light SSW breeze (Desbrière p. 161).
- Euryalus: moderate and clear; "At 10, light airs, inclinable to calm. At noon, calm."
- **High.**

**Tide**
- The flood was running into the bay through the morning (Thémis; Hermione). Philibert says the seven got out "dans cette marée". **High.**

**Sunrise**
- My computation for 36°32′ N on 19 October: about 6.26 a.m. apparent time, at about 102° true (ESE). **Medium.**
- Robinson of the Euryalus remembered the sun rising "over the Trocadero" as the fleet inside "let fall and hoist their topsails" (*Sea Drift*, p. 209, written about 50 years later). **Low** as a record of positions; good for the look.

**Hour by hour**
- **Night of 18–19.** Ships unmoored and hove short. The Montañés crossed her topgallant yards at nightfall and rode to her north-west anchor (annex p. 372).
- **Daylight.** The Euryalus saw the ships in Cádiz with topgallant yards across, and eight with topsails hoisted to the mastheads (NRS II 145). **High.**
- **5.45 to 6.15.** The signal to weigh "sans autre signal", by the reports' various hours: 5.45 with a gun (Héros); 6 (Argus, Furet, Thémis, Philibert, Observateur); 6.15 (Rhin); "between 5 and 6" (Desbrière p. 160). **High.**
- **6 onward.** The Algésiras made sail at once, together with several ships of the division that was to sail under Magon (annex p. 229). The Rhin weighed at 6.15 and went out with her boats. **High.**
- **6.30.** The Héros could not cast in the light air. With two boats towing ahead she was under way by 6.30, anchor catted. She went on working toward the passes with her boats through the calm forenoon (annex pp. 164–165). **High.**
- **7.**
  - Gravina repeated the signal.
  - The Montañés hoisted her boat in and, hove short, hoisted her topsails (annex p. 373).
  - The Euryalus saw "the northernmost ships under way" (NRS II 145).
  - The Sirius saw part of the fleet under way (NRS II 312).
  - **High.**
- **7 to 8.** The Spanish Neptuno asked for a pilot to weigh (annex p. 373). **Medium.**
- **8.**
  - The Euryalus "saw 19 of the enemy under way".
  - All the rest had their topsails to the masthead, except two ships of the line (1.3 b; NRS II 146).
  - **High** for the count as seen from 4–6 nm off. It includes frigates.
- **8.30.**
  - The Montañés made sail under three topsails in a very light easterly. By then most of the outer ships were under sail.
  - The Observateur's captain brought the Argus her order.
  - **High.**
- **9.**
  - The Argus counted 17 ships under sail, calm. Several anchored and clewed up their sails at the mastheads (annex p. 268, page image checked).
  - The Hermione: when it fell calm the ships dropped an anchor where they were.
  - **High.**
- **9.30.**
  - The Montañés, making no headway, clewed up and anchored again (annex p. 373).
  - Magon sent the Hermione to reconnoitre (annex p. 229).
  - **High.**
- **Noon.**
  - The Sirius: "Four sail of the enemy's line at anchor at the mouth of the harbour, some others under way and two frigates without the lighthouse" (NRS II 312).
  - The Naiad: eleven of the line outside San Sebastián (NRS II 302), an overcount from 11–12 miles.
  - The *Montañés*: only the first ships to sail, six French and one Spanish, remained under sail.

**How many got out: the versions**
- **Seven of the line**, named (Philibert, annex pp. 229–230).
- **Six French and one Spanish under sail at noon** (*Montañés*, annex p. 373).
- **Six of the line and a frigate "en franquía"** outside the port (*Príncipe de Asturias*, annex p. 383).
- **Eight of the line, one of them Spanish** (Villeneuve's own postscript, annex pp. 102–103; Observateur, annex p. 158).
- **14 sail, ships and frigates** (Furet, annex p. 174).
- **Six to seven of the line and three frigates** (Rhin).
- **Seven or eight of the line and several frigates** (Fougueux).
- **Nine of the line** (Prigny, from memory, annex p. 138).
- **Six "en franquía"** (Fernández Duro VIII 329).
- **Many weighed in the morning and anchored again.** The Euryalus, Argus, Montañés, *Príncipe de Asturias* and Hermione all say so.
- **Desbrière's narrative differs:** only the most advanced seem to have tried, and the others stayed at anchor (p. 160). The eyewitnesses outweigh him on this. **High.**

## 6. Beyond the window: points that bear on the scene's licences

**1. The morning movement**
- The licence (history notes, "Assumptions and licences", 9): the seven weigh from 7 a.m. about an hour apart, and the Hermione and Rhin weigh in the early afternoon.
- The sources:
  - The signal came at 6.
  - The Rhin weighed at 6.15 and the Héros was under way at 6.30, both towed by boats.
  - The Hermione was towed out and was reconnoitring by about 9.30–10.
  - The Euryalus counted 19 under way at 8.
  - Ships then anchored again in the calm.
  - The seven and the Rhin got through the passes between about 10 and 1 p.m. (Desbrière p. 161; Sirius at noon).
- **Medium-high** that the licence understates the movement in the 7–8.15 window and puts the Hermione and Rhin far too late.

**2. The evening of the 19th**
- At 4 p.m. the Swiftsure and all the French frigates were signalled to weigh (Furet, annex p. 174). The Thémis weighed at 4.30.
- At 5–5.15 the Argus counted 9 of the line and 5 frigates under sail (annex p. 268).
- The Hortense says that from the 19th several French and Spanish ships and all the frigates were beating outside the roads (annex pp. 188–189).
- So the Cornélie and Hortense probably went out on the evening of the 19th, not at 7 a.m. on the 20th as the licence has it.
- The Swiftsure and one more ship of the line may have weighed that evening too. That would explain Prigny's "nine", against Philibert's seven "dans cette marée".
- **Medium.**

**3. The Fougueux's evening warp**
- From 5.30 p.m., to a berth abreast of the city (section 2.1). **High.**

## 7. Suggested statements for the history notes

1. **The anchorage.** On the morning of 19 October all 33 of the line lay in the bay of Cádiz with the five frigates, the brigs Argus and Furet and the brig Observateur, which stayed behind by order. **High.**
2. **The narrows.** Only the Fougueux is recorded in the narrows, between the Puntal and Matagorda forts. She warped out from 5.30 p.m. to a berth abreast of the city (Desbrière 1907, annex pp. 213, 216). No source places another ship there. **High.**
3. **The outer ships.** The ships that got out on the 19th were the outermost (Fougueux; *Montañés*). The frigate Thémis lay at the head of the anchorage with them (annex p. 273). **High.**
4. **The two ships left in port.** The Terrible 74, disarmed, and the San Fulgencio 64, docked in September to survey her bottom, were not with the fleet. Their crews manned the new Spanish ships. They lay most likely in the arsenal of La Carraca, about 6 nm south-east of the anchorage, and are not drawn. **Medium.** **Low** for the place.
5. **The Spanish frigate and corvettes.** The frigate Flora and the corvettes Mercurio and Indagadora came from Ferrol with the fleet. They did not sail with it, and their place on 19 October is not recorded. **Low.**
6. **No gunboats.** The fleet's armed launches had been taken off their stations and hoisted in on the evening of the 18th. **High.**

# The dip in the first seconds after load (session 21, 12 November 2026)

> **Status (session 22, 12 November 2026): closed by the owner.** He watched the production preview several times and sees no dip, so this work stops here. This note was brought to main on its own. The tools and prototypes it names (`scripts/dev/perf/`, `look-diff.mjs`'s `INIT=`) are on branch `s21-perf`, not on main; the first changes from its plan are on branch `s21-load-wip` (not measured, not merged). Both branches are kept. What is parked and what was proposed: `docs/backlog.md`, "The start-up dip".

The owner's measurement (his `?debug` on the production preview, his laptop, Chrome): a steady 60 fps through the whole site, the only dip in the first seconds after the site loads. He wants that start-up work spread so that 60 holds from the first second, and no general frame optimisation. This note finds what is fetched, parsed, compiled, uploaded and computed in those seconds, on a first visit (empty caches) and a repeat visit, which of it reaches frames, and plans how to spread or pre-empt each piece. Nothing in `src/` was changed; the tools are new or fixed in `scripts/dev/perf/` (`INDEX.txt`, "Session 21"), and three of the plan's steps were tried as injected prototypes.

## In short

1. **The overlay's first reading is mostly the page appearing.** The `?debug` overlay starts counting right after hydration (its effect runs after the commit that also makes the chart's context). On a repeat visit the first contentful paint comes 90 to 140 ms after that, so the overlay's first 500 ms hold scene 1's first raster: **with no chart at all its first reading is 37 to 41 fps** (under other sessions' GPU load; 52 to 59 in two traced runs), then 59 to 61 throughout. On a first visit the page is painted during hydration and scene 1 alone reads 51 to 58 first. The chart deepens that first reading (30 to 35 on a repeat visit) with a main-thread task of 85 to 163 ms: **three's renderer starts with synchronous GPU queries** (`SAMPLES`, `VERSION`, `SCISSOR_BOX`) that wait 13 to 33 ms each on a GPU process busy with that first raster. **Served from values read during hydration (a prototype), the task is gone and the first reading equals scene 1's own** (39 to 41), with frames identical at all 51 points of `look-diff.mjs`.
2. **After the first reading, the dips are the chart's load.** With scene 1 alone, none of 33 later readings fell under 58; with the chart, 3 to 6 of 33 did (51 to 56). The steadiest: **at about two seconds one microtask chain of 39 to 73 ms** (209 ms once): the rivers' geometry, the tracks, the heads' labels and troika's start, with troika's glyph atlas taking a **second WebGL context of its own** (a test render, program links waited for, a pixel read). Then, mostly on first visits and under load, the warm-up's start (one idle continuation of up to about 100 ms at about 4.3 s) and the readback's stalls.
3. **The GPU process's main thread is blocked by the load in ways headless frames hide.** Program links the first draws wait for (one GPU task of 20 ms), **a geometry shader ANGLE compiles synchronously at the tracks' first draw** (3.5 to 7.3 ms, every visit), the readback's polls and copies, big texture uploads. Four headed runs (the display's own swap chain; disturbed, and not gated on a quiet GPU) showed 30 fps stretches of up to 0.9 s during the load with no main-thread cause: a lead to confirm. **With the first-vertex convention (a prototype) the geometry shader is gone and the frames identical.**
4. **Every repeat visit compiles the chart's programs again.** Programs linked through `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` are mostly not kept in Chrome's GPU program cache: 16 to 20 of them (2.0 to 2.3 s of CPU on ANGLE's threads) on every visit, and the warm-up waits about 0.7 s for them. Linked synchronously once, they come from the cache on later visits, even with parallel compile on.
5. Before the overlay starts, hydration holds the page 103 to 153 ms on a repeat visit and 468 to 512 ms on a first one (the chart's WebGL context alone 230 to 283 ms cold, 16 to 26 warm; scene 1's GSAP setup about 100 to 150 ms). The overlay never counts it.

The plan, ranked (details below): (1) the renderer's start without round trips; (2) troika without its own WebGL context; (3) every main-thread piece of the load in slices of 4 ms or in workers, the readback replaced by a decode in a worker; (4) nothing that blocks the GPU process's main thread (the first-vertex convention, one first draw and one big upload per frame, GPU slices sized by measured GPU time); (5) the chart's chunk in pieces; (6) scene 1's own first frames, the owner's call; then the traced oddities, the compile cache and hydration. Holding the chart's start until scene 1's first frames were out was tried and is not recommended: the first reading did not improve and the chart was ready a second later.

## How it was measured

- A production export of `9f41b56` built in this worktree, served by `npx serve out` on 127.0.0.1:4175; Playwright's Chromium 156 (`channel: "chromium"`, headless), 1280×720 at 1.5, ANGLE on Direct3D 11 on the Intel Arc of the owner's laptop, on mains.
- **Other work on the machine.** Other sessions' Chromiums drew on the GPU much of the time (the main session's `glyph-clash.mjs` and `look-diff.mjs`, the scrim helper's `chapter-scrim.mjs`, `fleet-gates.mjs`: 20 to 90 % of the 3D engine when sampled), and someone used the machine (real input two seconds before one check; Telegram and Gemini drawing). The first batch (`a-*`, twelve runs) and the first traces (`t-*`) were taken when a sample showed the GPU quiet, but nothing logged it then; the prototype comparisons (`abw-*`, `abc-*`) ran under 20 to 70 % of foreign load, logged, with their arms interleaved run by run so that the load weighs on each alike. All timings here are indicative: they identify, they do not certify. `s21-measure.sh` now waits for a quiet GPU and logs it (protocol below).
- **First visit:** a fresh profile each run (empty HTTP cache, empty GPU shader and program caches). **Repeat visit:** one persistent profile, primed by one run (`PROFILE=`).
- **LIGHT runs** (no WebGL wrappers): rAF frames, long tasks, long animation frames (LoAF) with script attribution, the loading steps seen from outside (`instrument.js`). **Instrumented runs** (`LIGHT=0`): every WebGL call's main-thread time, now with the queried parameter's name. **Traces** through CDP (now also for a persistent profile, through the page's DevTools session) with `gpu.angle` (ANGLE's `D3DCompile`, the GPU main thread's waits on links) and V8's CPU samples.
- **Headed runs** (`HEADED=1`, the display's real swap chain): four, then abandoned. In two of them the window was occluded (rAF throttled to 1 Hz) or scrolled by someone else, and windows popping up disturb whoever uses the machine. What they showed before the disturbance is used below as a lead, not as numbers: nothing then logged whether other sessions were drawing on the GPU.
- **What the overlay sees.** `DebugOverlay` counts gsap.ticker frames over 500 ms windows. Its ticker is added in a passive effect after the hydration commit, so it never counts hydration. With `?debug`, `instrument.js` now records the overlay's own readings; without it, `startup.mjs` emulates them from the end of that commit (the long task holding the chart's `getContext`).
- **Prototypes:** `INIT=` scripts injected before the page's own (`proto-glcache.js`, `proto-provoking.js`, `proto-settle.js`), timed with `s21-ab.sh` against the site as built and against scene 1 alone (`NOCHART=1`), and checked for the look with `look-diff.mjs` (which now takes `INIT=` too) against reference frames of the same build: scene 2 at p 0 to 1 every 0.02, 1280×720 at 1.5.
- **Not causes:** the `?debug` overlay itself (a tick and two text writes a second); garbage collection (minor GCs of 1.5 to 1.7 ms); a task of 24 to 42 ms at about 1.0 s posted by `WidgetBase::Shutdown` and destroying a `LayerTreeHost` (the harness's initial blank page being torn down, it seems; not in a normal browser).

## The first ten seconds, first visit

Times from the navigation's start; LIGHT runs, traced figures (about 1.5 times slower) in brackets. "Frames" says whether the work overlaps frames the visitor sees.

- **0.13 to 0.26 s.** React's hydration render, in slices of 5 to 46 ms. Main thread.
- **0.22 to 0.75 s: the hydration commit, one task of 468 to 512 ms** (57 ms context in one run, a commit of 140 ms). Scene 1's and scene 2's `useGSAP` setup: CSSPlugin reading start values through `getComputedStyle` for each tween (44 ms of self time in the trace), ScrollTrigger's pins and refresh, SplitText at `fonts.ready`; and the chart's **WebGL 2 context, 230 to 283 ms** (the GPU process sets up WebGL; `GpuChannelHost::CreateViewCommandBuffer`). The first paint (0.42 to 0.57 s) falls inside it, so the painted page is frozen until about 0.7 s. The overlay starts after it. The GPU process meanwhile rasters scene 1 for the first time: **Skia compiles about a hundred programs** (96 and 108 in two traces, 342 and 441 ms of CPU on ANGLE's threads) and the GPU process's main thread waits 208 to 280 ms in all on their links in the first second.
- **0.62 to 0.82 s: the chart's chunk** (`32nwrtgtg17rk.js`, 808 KB, 229 KB over the wire: three, troika, the engine) evaluated: **24 to 35 ms** of script, a long frame of 52 to 145 ms with the frame it holds back. Then, in the next idle callback, **three's `WebGLRenderer` starts** [34 ms]: the `getParameter` calls Chrome sends to the GPU process (`SAMPLES`, `VERSION`, `SCISSOR_BOX`) wait for it (`WaitForCmd`: 12.8 and 13.4 ms in the trace; 13.7 to 16.9 ms each in an instrumented run). Then [15 ms] `getMaxAnisotropy()` waits 4.5 to 12 ms and the overview's packed tile goes up from its ImageBitmap (1840×1295, 9.5 MB copied on the main thread: `texSubImage2D` 5.7 to 9.4 ms). Frames: yes; on a first visit at the end of hydration or in the overlay's first window (its first reading 45 to 58 with the chart, 51 to 58 without).
- **0.8 to 1.9 s: the field.** Decode, land masks and horizon bakes in bands (about 2 ms of GPU each), one band per idle callback; the surround's and the detail tile's packed bitmaps uploaded (1.2 to 2.2 ms main thread each). The main thread is nearly idle; the GPU process's main thread does 4 to 8 ms per 100 ms besides the swap. Frames: none lost headless.
- **About 2.0 to 2.2 s: one microtask chain of 39 to 58 ms** [47.8 ms] (73 ms once under load, 209 ms once headed). In the trace's samples: the rivers (their widths per point and `ribbonGeometry` over 20,002 points) about 15 ms; `new Tracks` (each track's points, the pattern material's style texture) about 6 ms; `new HeadLabels` and troika's start about 8 ms: the first `Text.sync` builds **troika's worker modules** on the main thread, from the stringified source of their functions, a Blob and `new Worker` each (the typesetter's worker and one more now, four SDF workers a little later); and about 15 ms for **troika's glyph atlas getting a WebGL 1 context of its own** (`warmUpSDFCanvas` → `isSupported`): its creation (3 to 13 ms; 114 ms once headed), its test programs' links waited for (`GetProgramiv` 6.1 and 3.3 ms) and a test render read back (`readPixels` 1.9 ms; 47.5 ms instrumented). Frames: one, every run; the overlay's third reading 56.
- **2.1 to 2.7 s.** The glyph atlas on 2D canvases, in slices meant for 4 ms that overrun to 8 to 28 ms [traced] when one item is long (the lit channel's `drawImage` composite up to 9.8 ms, a large level's `drawShape`, a `getImageData`). Troika's SDFs come back from its workers and are copied glyph by glyph into its WebGL canvas (107 glyphs, a `texImage2D` and a draw each, 3 to 5 ms per 100 ms); the first copy waits 21.5 ms on its program's link inside a worker-message task [23.2 ms]. Frames: none lost headless; risky while scrolling.
- **3.3 to 3.4 s.** The digits worker's reply, one task [20.6 ms]: the soundings' layout begins, then **the director's constructor, not sliced** (`easeLegs`, a Gaussian over every leg's 2,000 steps: about 10 to 12 ms of it), then `director.prepare` in slices.
- **3.5 to 4.1 s: `field.readBack`.** For each of 11 bands: the ENCODE pass, `readPixels` into a buffer, then three's `probeAsync` **polls `clientWaitSync` every 4 ms, and each poll is a synchronous round trip** (`WaitForCmd` 1 to 11.5 ms in timer tasks of 3.6 to 14 ms), then `getBufferSubData` (1 to 13 ms). The GPU process's main thread does 17 to 29 ms per 100 ms then. Frames: none lost headless on a quiet GPU; a stream of 4 to 14 ms stalls.
- **3.5 to 4.4 s: `Chart.warm`.** Its start runs in the same idle continuation as the end of the stage's set-up (`new GlyphLayout`, the director's first `apply`): `renderer.compile` builds every program's source and issues every compile and link in one task [27.6 ms on a quiet GPU; a scriptless long frame of 94 to 98 ms in two first visits under load]. ANGLE compiles the chart's 20 programs on its worker threads (1.26 s of CPU cold, about 0.5 s of wall time; the terrain's fragment shader alone about 0.45 s); three polls their completion every 10 ms. Then one texture per idle slice (the glyph atlas 2048×975 RGBA, 8 MB and its mipmaps; troika's atlas canvas, copied across contexts; the digits), then each object drawn once into a 4×4 target: **the first draws wait on their links on the GPU process's main thread** (one GPU task of 20.2 ms with 15.8 ms of `Program::MainLinkLoadEvent::wait`), and **the tracks' first draw makes ANGLE compile a geometry shader on the GPU process's main thread** (`D3DCompile` 3.5 to 6.2 ms; it emulates GLES's last-vertex rule for the pattern shader's six `flat` varyings). Then the canvas is cleared once.
- **4.1 to 4.6 s: ready** (5.3 to 5.9 s under load); the glyph layout's bake runs two passes over 1,001 steps in idle slices, done by about 5.2 to 5.5 s; no long frame from it.
- **About 7.3 s** (scrolling runs): the handoff, the chart's first visible frames: one late frame.

## Findings in detail

### 1. The overlay's first window: scene 1 appearing, and three's renderer starting on top of it

`Scene02Road` waits for the first idle callback (`idle(2500)`), which comes as soon as hydration has committed: the main thread is free while the GPU process is still rastering scene 1. The chart's chunk is then evaluated (6 to 35 ms) and, one idle callback later, `Chart.create` makes three's `WebGLRenderer`. Three asks the context for about fifteen parameters at its start; Chrome answers most from the client's own copy, but `SAMPLES`, `VERSION` and `SCISSOR_BOX` (and `MAX_TEXTURE_MAX_ANISOTROPY_EXT` later, for the field's tiles) go to the GPU process and wait for it. Busy with scene 1's first frames, it answers each in 13 to 33 ms. The context was made during hydration precisely to keep such waits out of frames (session 16); these four came after it.

The prototype `proto-glcache.js` reads those four values when the context is made (inside the hydration commit, which waits on the GPU anyway) and serves them to three from that copy. Interleaved with the site as built, three repeat visits each, under the same foreign load (`abw-*`):
- as built: the overlay's first reading 30, 33 and 35; a long task of 85 to 101 ms after hydration in every run;
- with the prototype: 39, 39 and 41; no long task after hydration but the chain at two seconds;
- scene 1 alone (`NOCHART=1`): 37, 37 and 41.

On first visits (`abc-*`): as built 45, 51 and 55; with the prototype 49, 54 and 57; scene 1 alone 51 to 58. So the prototype takes the chart's part out of the first window. What is left there is scene 1's own first raster: on a repeat visit the overlay starts 88 to 143 ms before the first contentful paint (the commit ends at about 0.3 s, the paint comes at 0.36 to 0.47 s), and the GPU process then rasters every layer of scene 1 for the first time (frames of 33 ms for about a quarter of a second, one held 50 ms). Those frames are the page appearing; scene 1's idle loops (the swell, the haze, the glitter) start with hydration, so they move at about 30 fps for that quarter second.

### 2. Troika's start and its second WebGL context

`gpuAccelerateSDF = false` keeps troika's distance fields in its workers, but troika (0.52.5) still keeps its glyph atlas in a WebGL canvas: on the first text it calls `warmUpSDFCanvas`, which makes a WebGL 1 context on that canvas and runs `webgl-sdf-generator`'s support test (three programs compiled and linked, a 4×4 render read back with `readPixels`), and each glyph's field from the workers is then drawn into that canvas with a texture upload and a draw (`renderImageData`). The chart's context later uploads the canvas as a texture, a copy across contexts. Every visit, in the chain at about two seconds and in the worker-message tasks after it. Troika's worker modules are also built on the main thread, from the stringified source of their functions.

### 3. The GPU process's main thread

Headless frames hide most of it: the GPU process's main thread spends 8 to 24 ms of every frame in `SkiaOutputSurfaceImplOnGpu::SwapBuffers`, pacing a hidden swap chain, and the work in between rarely costs a frame. With the display's own swap chain the same load cost frames in the headed runs (30 fps for 0.9 s at 3.0 to 3.9 s and 4.9 to 5.2 s on a first visit; at 0.9 to 1.25 s and 2.9 to 3.1 s on a repeat visit while scrolling), with no long task on the main thread (not gated on a quiet GPU: a lead to confirm). What blocks that thread during the load, from the traces:
- the first draws of programs whose link is not finished: one 20.2 ms task with 15.8 ms of `Program::MainLinkLoadEvent::wait` (first visit);
- the tracks' geometry shader, compiled synchronously at their first draw: 3.5 to 6.2 ms (first visits), 7.2 to 7.3 ms (repeat visits; it is never cached). Its source in the trace is ANGLE's `GS_INPUT` with six `nointerpolation float4`, the pattern shader's `flat out vec4 vPiece, vStyle, vInk, vWash, vState, vHead` (`lines/ribbon.ts`). With `proto-provoking.js` (the first-vertex convention set on the chart's context) a traced first visit compiles nothing on the GPU process's main thread (`pv-proto`: 0 `D3DCompile`s there, against one of 6.2 ms in `pv-control`), and all 51 frames of `look-diff.mjs` are identical to the reference (`ld-prov`: 0 pixels differ);
- the readback: 11 ENCODE passes, 11 copies into buffers, a synchronous poll every 4 ms and 11 synchronous reads;
- texture uploads: the three packed tiles (9.5, 2.0 and 4.4 MB from ImageBitmaps), the glyph atlas (8 MB and mipmaps), troika's canvas across contexts;
- troika's own context: its creation and its test's links and read;
- the field's bands themselves, about 2 ms of GPU each, sometimes two in one frame (idle callbacks can run back to back in one idle period).

### 4. The compile cache

On a repeat visit the chart's programs are compiled again (16 to 20 `D3DCompile`s, 2.0 to 2.3 s of CPU), Skia's are not. Experiments with a persistent profile (`d3d.mjs` over traces with `gpu.angle`):
- primed and revisited with parallel compile (the site as it is): 16 to 20 of the chart's programs recompiled;
- primed with parallel compile, revisited without it (`INIT=no-parallel.js`): 12 recompiled, and synchronously, **706 ms of link waits on the GPU process's main thread**;
- primed without parallel compile, revisited without it: none recompiled (only 10 to 12 of Skia's);
- primed without parallel compile, revisited with it: none recompiled;
- primed with parallel compile and each program's link status read as soon as it completed (`INIT=resolve-link.js`): 12, then 6 recompiled on the next two visits.

So with this Chromium, programs linked in parallel are mostly not stored in the GPU program cache, while programs stored once are loaded on later visits either way. The owner's Chrome may differ; it should be checked there (protocol below). The cost is off the main threads (ANGLE's worker threads), but it delays the warm-up by about 0.7 s, competes for the CPU and leaves the link waits above. Synchronous links are no way out: an uncached program then compiles on the GPU process's main thread (the terrain's alone about 0.45 s).

### 5. Smaller pieces, each a frame at risk while the visitor scrolls

Each fits the headless frames' slack when nothing scrolls on a quiet GPU, and each is longer than a frame's budget with scene 1's own scroll work beside it (GSAP, ScrollTrigger and Lenis take 2 to 5 ms of every frame) or a busy GPU:
- the glyph atlas's slices overrunning to 8 to 28 ms (traced) on one long item;
- the director's constructor (about 20 ms with the soundings' start, traced), not sliced;
- the end of the stage's set-up and the warm-up's `renderer.compile` in one idle continuation (27.6 ms traced on a quiet GPU; 94 to 98 ms in two first visits under load);
- the readback's polls and reads (4 to 14 ms each, every 30 ms or so for 0.3 to 0.6 s);
- troika's first glyph copy waiting on its program (21.5 ms, traced);
- the packed tiles' uploads from ImageBitmaps (5.7 to 9.4 ms of main thread for the overview);
- in two traced runs of eight, one mid-load frame with a whole-document `Paint` of 19 ms (`t-warm-ns-1`, 4.40 s) or a `Layerize` of 32 ms after a 50 ms rAF callback (`t-warm-ns-2`, 4.22 s), and in a third an 86 ms gsap.ticker callback (`m-warm-1`, 4.27 s) with nothing traced inside it; none of them in the untraced runs' long frames, none attributed yet.

### 6. Hydration

The commit runs scene 1's and scene 2's setup and makes the chart's context: 103 to 153 ms on a repeat visit, 468 to 512 ms on a first. It cannot be sliced, and the overlay never counts it. On a first visit the page is painted at about 0.45 s and held until about 0.7 s by the context's 230 to 283 ms. Moving the context anywhere later would put that wait into frames the overlay counts; moving it before the first paint (an inline script during parsing) would delay the first paint by as much. Left where it is (see the plan's last step for the GSAP part).

## The plan, ranked

Ranked by the frames they return in the first seconds as the owner reads them. "Look" risks: scene 1's look must stay within its regression check's tolerance; the chart's must not change at all (`look-diff.mjs` against the reference frames, identical up to the build's own noise). Each step is a commit of its own, measured by the protocol below; a prototype injected by `INIT=` lets a step be measured before it is written into `src/`.

1. **The renderer's start without round trips.** *(Prototyped: `proto-glcache.js`.)* In `engine/context.ts`, right after making the context (inside the hydration commit, which waits on the GPU anyway), read `SAMPLES`, `VERSION`, `SCISSOR_BOX` and, with `EXT_texture_filter_anisotropic`, `MAX_TEXTURE_MAX_ANISOTROPY_EXT`, and give the context an own `getParameter` that serves them: the state values once (three reads them only to seed its state), the constants always; delete it once `Chart.create` has built the renderer, or keep only the constants.
   - Effect, measured: the long task of 85 to 163 ms after hydration is gone; the overlay's first reading becomes scene 1's own (39 to 41 against 30 to 35 on repeat visits; 49 to 57 against 45 to 55 on first visits).
   - Look, measured: identical (`ld-glcache`: 51 frames, 0 pixels differ). Verify again on the change: an instrumented run shows no `getParameter` of 1 ms or more after hydration; `loafs-after.mjs` shows no scriptless long frame at 0.3 to 0.5 s.

2. **Troika without its own WebGL context.** Keep troika's atlas in a `DataTexture` written on the CPU: each glyph's field from the JS generator copied into its channel of an RGBA array, uploaded with `texSubImage2D` of the rows that changed (one upload per slice): no second context, no support test, no copy across contexts. Troika has no option for it: alias `webgl-sdf-generator` in `next.config.ts` (`turbopack.resolveAlias`) to a small module whose `webgl.isSupported` says no and whose `webglUtils` write into the array, and give `TextBuilder` the array (a vendored, patched copy of troika's `TextBuilder.js`, MIT, with its licence; troika's own `safariPre15Workaround` already shows the data-texture path). Build troika's workers early, in an idle slice of their own, by setting one character of text before the gazetteer.
   - Effect: removes the second context (3 to 114 ms), its test (link waits 6 to 21 ms, `readPixels` 2 to 47 ms), 107 glyph copies on the GPU and the copies across contexts: about 20 ms of the chain at two seconds, and the worker-message tasks' GPU waits.
   - Look: none if the bytes are the same: the WebGL path copies the generator's bytes exactly (a full-quad copy with a channel mask). Prove it on the development server: troika's canvas read back against the new array, byte for byte, then `look-diff.mjs`. Risk: a patched dependency to keep in step with troika's updates (pin it, note it in `decisions.md`).

3. **Every main-thread piece of the load in slices of 4 ms, or in a worker.**
   - The chain at two seconds: an idle slice between `loadRivers`, `new Rivers`, `new Tracks` and `new HeadLabels`; `ribbonGeometry` for the rivers in a worker (pure arithmetic over 20,002 points into typed arrays, transferred) or written by `npm run data:build`.
   - The director's constructor: `easeLegs` and the per-squadron tables into `prepare`'s slices.
   - The glyph atlas in a worker with an `OffscreenCanvas` (2D contexts there have `Path2D`, fills, strokes, `drawImage` and `getImageData`; `buildShip` is pure), as the digits atlas is; at least, items cut finer (each sail and channel an item, `getImageData` in bands of rows).
   - The stage's last steps (`new GlyphLayout`, the first `apply`) and the warm-up's start in slices of their own; `renderer.compile` by groups of objects, a group per slice; the engine's own programs (terrain, sky) compiled right after `Chart.create` and each layer's as it is made, so the compile overlaps the load instead of opening the warm-up.
   - **The readback replaced by a decode in a worker:** the packed WebP tiles (`createImageBitmap` and an `OffscreenCanvas` in the worker, or the bytes the field already decoded for the GPU) through the DECODE arithmetic into the same 16-bit codes the ENCODE pass writes. Removes 11 GPU passes, 11 copies, about 100 synchronous polls and 11 synchronous reads. Look: the coast field must be identical byte for byte, or the glyph layout's decisions could move a ship: the GPU decodes in 32-bit floats (`sinh`), so compare every byte of every tile against the GPU path in a development check before switching, then run the clash, motion and look-diff checks. If they differ, keep the GPU path but poll once per frame, after rAF, and read each band only when its fence has signalled.
   - Effect: no task over about 5 ms from the chart's load after hydration; the readings after the first stay at 60 (scene 1 alone shows that they can: 0 of 33 under 58).

4. **Nothing that blocks the GPU process's main thread in a frame.**
   - **The first-vertex convention for the tracks.** *(Prototyped: `proto-provoking.js`.)* Turn on `WEBGL_provoking_vertex` (Chromium 156 has it) and call `provokingVertexWEBGL(FIRST_VERTEX_CONVENTION_WEBGL)` when the context is made (`chartContext()`). ANGLE then needs no geometry shader for the pattern shader's `flat` varyings: no synchronous compile at the first draw (3.5 to 7.3 ms, every visit), and no geometry stage on every draw of the tracks after. Look, measured: identical (`ld-prov`: 51 frames, 0 pixels differ), as it must be: every vertex of a ribbon triangle carries the same flat values (one piece's `piece`, one line's `tag`; `ribbonGeometry` indexes only within a piece). Measured: no `D3DCompile` on `CrGpuMain` (`pv-proto`).
   - **First draws one per frame:** the warm-up's draws on a frame-aligned schedule (one object right after a rAF, never two in a frame), each only once `COMPLETION_STATUS_KHR` says its program is linked, so no draw waits on a link (the 20 ms GPU task falls to under 5 ms per frame).
   - **Big uploads in bands:** the packed tiles and the glyph atlas in rows of about 1 to 2 MB per frame (`texSubImage2D` with `UNPACK_SKIP_ROWS` from the bitmap, or from bytes a worker decoded), each texture's storage made first and its mipmaps once at the end, as the field's passes already do.
   - **GPU slices sized by measured GPU time:** the field's bands timed with `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2` (available) and sized to about 1 ms, issued right after a rAF, at most one per frame; every other frame while the visitor scrolls scene 1's heavy band (p 0.08 to 0.64).
   - Effect: the 30 fps stretches seen with the real display during the load; only the owner's `?debug` or headed runs on a free machine can confirm it. Look: none (the same passes and uploads, in other slices).

5. **The chart's chunk in pieces.** The chunk's evaluation (24 to 35 ms on a first visit, 6 to 18 ms from the code cache) is one task. Load it in parts that each evaluate in a task of their own: `await import("three")`, then troika, then `./stage`, with an idle slice between (Turbopack puts separately imported modules in chunks of their own; check the export's chunk list). Effect: each part about 10 ms or less. Look: none.
   - **Tried and not recommended:** holding the chart's start until 30 frames in a row came on time (`proto-settle.js`, three repeat visits interleaved with the others): the first reading did not improve (35 to 40, against 39 to 41 with step 1's prototype alone, since scene 1's first raster dominates it), 7 of 33 later readings fell under 58 (3 without the gate), and the chart was ready about a second later (6.0 to 6.7 s against 4.8 to 5.5 s under that load), closer to a fast scroller's handoff.

6. **Scene 1's own first frames: the owner's call.** On a repeat visit the overlay's first reading holds the page's first raster: with no chart at all 37 to 41 under load (52 to 59 in two traced runs), one held frame and about a quarter of a second at 30 fps, while scene 1's idle loops already move. Two ways, neither touching what scene 1 shows: (a) start scene 1's idle loops (the swell, the haze, the glitter, the heave) once its first frames have come on time, so the first raster happens on a still picture and the loops start smooth; the overlay would still count the raster's frames, but nothing on screen would stutter; (b) make the first raster lighter: find which layers it rasters first and how large they are (`layers.mjs` over a `NOCHART=1` trace with the `cc` category; the strips' loops are wider than the screen, since they slide by a period), and whether any can be rastered before the commit ends. Scene 1 is approved: either needs its regression check and the owner's eye.

7. **Pin down the traced oddities** (finding 5's last item) before acting on them: each came once, only under tracing, in the middle of the load, none in the untraced runs' long frames. Trace repeat visits with `disabled-by-default-devtools.timeline.invalidationTracking` and `gpu` until one recurs, then act on what it is; if it is tracing's own cost, drop it.

8. **The compile cache.** Leave parallel compile on (synchronous links are worse when the cache misses), spread the link waits (step 4), and check on the owner's Chrome whether his repeat visits recompile (a trace with `gpu.angle`, or `chrome://tracing`). If they do, report it upstream with the experiment above; a workaround that links synchronously only when a mark in `localStorage` says this build compiled before is not recommended: one evicted program would cost up to 0.45 s on the GPU process's main thread.

9. **Hydration, optional.** Scene 1's setup in the commit (about 100 to 150 ms; CSSPlugin's `getComputedStyle` reads are the largest part) could be shortened by giving GSAP its start values instead of letting it read them, or by deferring the parts no first frame needs (the idle loops, the scenes' markers). Scene 1 is approved: only how it is set up may change, proven by its regression check. Not counted by the overlay; it matters only to a visitor who scrolls in the first 0.3 to 0.7 s.

## Measurement protocol for the before and after

- **The machine:** on mains; nothing else drawing on the GPU (no test, sweep or other browser; `scripts/dev/perf/gpu-others.ps1` near 0). `quiet.sh`, sourced by `s21-measure.sh`, waits for three quiet samples before every run and logs what drew before and after it into `runs/<label>.gpulog`; a run with more than 3 % before or after is discarded and taken again. If the machine cannot be had quiet, interleave before and after run by run instead (`s21-ab.sh` with the two builds on two ports) and say so.
- **The build:** `npm run build`, then `npm run preview` (127.0.0.1:4173). "Before" is the commit before the first change; "after" each step's commit; both on the same day.
- **The runs:** `BASE=http://127.0.0.1:4173 bash scripts/dev/perf/s21-measure.sh <label> 5`: five runs each of a first visit without scrolling, a first visit scrolling from 1 s after load (100 px wheel steps every 150 ms to 4,320 px, through scene 1 and the handoff), a repeat visit without scrolling and a repeat visit scrolling, with `?debug` (the overlay's readings are recorded), the repeat visits on one persistent profile primed by a first run. 20 runs, about 8 to 10 minutes on a quiet machine. Once per session, the same with `KINDS="warm-ns cold-ns"` and `NOCHART=1` exported, for scene 1's own baseline.
- **The metrics** (printed per run by `startup.mjs`; `node scripts/dev/perf/loafs-after.mjs runs/<label>-*.json` for the long frames):
  1. the overlay's readings: the first, against scene 1's own baseline (the target is the baseline, until step 6 is decided); the next ten: **target 60 in every one of every run** (58 tolerated once per run: one frame), against 3 to 6 of 33 under 58 now;
  2. missed vsyncs (frames later than one 16.7 ms interval) in the 3 s and in the 6 s after hydration: target scene 1's baseline plus at most one;
  3. long tasks over 50 ms and long animation frames after hydration: target none, against the renderer's start, the chunk, the chain at two seconds and the warm-up's start now;
  4. time to `road ready`: no more than 0.5 s later than before;
  5. the GPU process, two traced runs of each visit (`bash s21-trace.sh <label> 2` and `NOSCROLL=1 bash s21-trace-warm.sh <label> 2`, `BASE` as above): `gpumain.mjs` shows no task over 8 ms on the GPU process's main thread after hydration but the swap, and ANGLE waits of 5 ms at most per task; `d3d.mjs` shows no `D3DCompile` on `CrGpuMain`, and how many of the chart's programs a repeat visit compiles.
- **Report** the medians and the worst run of each metric, before and after, per visit and scroll mode, and the runs' GPU log.
- **The look, after each step:** scene 1's `regression` project and `look-diff.mjs` against the session's reference frames (`.claude/s21/look-ref/`), plus, for steps 2 and 3, the byte checks named there.
- **The verdict** stays the owner's `?debug` on his own Chrome and display: headless frames hide the GPU process's blocking work (finding 3). If the machine is free, two or three `HEADED=1` runs per visit (they open a window for about 12 s each) are the closest check here.

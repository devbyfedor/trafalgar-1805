# Cádiz harbour, morning of 19 October 1805 (about 7 to 8.15 a.m.): checked version

Which vessels lay in the bay, and where, as far as the sources say. For the scene 2 chart (`src/scenes/02-road/fleets.ts`, `movements.ts`) and the history notes.

This is the checked version of `anchorage-19oct.md`. An independent reader went back to each cited passage. What survives is kept. What was wrong is corrected. Contested points give each version and who holds it. The changes are listed at the end ("Checker's changes"), with the open doubts.

## Method

- **Main source.** Desbrière, *La campagne maritime de 1805: Trafalgar* (1907), narrative and annexes, read in the archive.org OCR text, leaf by leaf.
  - Page images read by the first writer: annex pp. 8, 213, 216, 241, 242, 268, 273.
  - Page images re-read by the checker: annex pp. 213, 268, 273.
- **British side.** *Logs of the Great Sea Fights* II (NRS 1900): Euryalus, Naiad, Sirius, Victory. James, *Naval History* III (1902 printing of the 1886 edition, same text as 1837). Nicolas VII. Corbett, *The Campaign of Trafalgar* (1910), for one reading only.
- **Spanish side.** The Spanish annexes in Desbrière: Escaño's relation and his letter to MacDonnell, Gravina's report, the *Montañés* and *Príncipe de Asturias* diaries. Fernández Duro, *Armada española* VIII (1902); the checker read the page images of appendix 1 to ch. XIV (p. 318) and of its index entry (p. 487).
- **Also.** Fraser, *The Enemy at Trafalgar* (1906). H. Robinson, *Sea Drift* (1858), a memoir written about 50 years later.
- Page numbers are as printed. "Annex" means Desbrière's second part (*pièces annexes*). Times are ships' times as written.

## Short answers

1. **All 33 of the line were in the bay of Cádiz that morning.** **High.**
   - All 33 sailed from the bay on the 19th or the 20th.
   - The 18 French are in Villeneuve's state of 16 September (annex pp. 85–86).
   - Escaño's list of the 15 Spanish ships is headed as the squadron at anchor in the bay "el 19 de Octubre de 1805" (Fernández Duro VIII, p. 318, appendix 1 to ch. XIV; page image read). The volume's own index gives the same list as of **16** October (p. 487). The list includes the Bahama, which was outside by noon on the 19th. So it dates the squadron, not the morning. **Medium** for the list's date.
2. **Only one ship of the line is placed by name in the narrows: the Fougueux.** **High.**
   - No source read places the San Justo, the Rayo, the Santa Ana or any other named ship there on the 19th.
   - Two sources hint that more than one ship lay further in than the main group, without names. The Thémis writes that "les vaisseaux les plus en dedans" stayed at anchor (annex p. 273). Fraser says that on the evening of the 19th as many as possible of the ships in Cádiz harbour were towed or warped towards the sea (pp. 84–85; no source given). Neither puts a ship in the narrows. **Low** for any second ship there.
   - **No evidence** for the San Justo.
3. **The Fougueux spent the whole morning in the narrows and warped out only in the evening.** **High** for the morning and for the hour.
   - At 9 p.m. on the 18th she was unmoored, hove short on one anchor, boats in. She stayed so (annex p. 213).
   - Her captain ordered the warp after seeing the outermost ships get under sail. "Il était alors 5 h. 1/2 du soir" (annex p. 213; page image read).
   - With her own boats, hawsers and grelins she reached a berth from which she could weigh with any wind, on one anchor (annex p. 213). The second report says she came to lie "en travers de Cadix" (annex p. 216). **Medium** for "off the city"; **Low** for any exact spot.
4. **The ships that got out were among the outermost.** **Medium-high.**
   - Two eyewitnesses in other ships say the outermost ships weighed first. The Fougueux saw "7 ou 8 des vaisseaux les plus en dehors" make sail (annex p. 213). The Montañés says that by 8.30 most of the ships anchored further out were under sail (annex p. 373).
   - Two other eyewitnesses name the seven that got out: Philibert of the Algésiras (annex pp. 229–230) and the Héros (annex p. 165). They are the Algésiras, Achille, Argonaute, Neptune, Héros, Duguay-Trouin and Bahama.
   - That these seven were the outermost ships, and only they, is an inference. The Euryalus counted 19 under way at 8, so many more than seven weighed.
5. **Frigates.** No source puts any frigate inside the lines, toward the city. **High** for that absence.
   - The Thémis weighed "avec quelques vaisseaux et frégates qui étaient à la tête" (annex p. 273; page image read). The next sentence sets them against "les vaisseaux les plus en dedans", so "à la tête" means the outer end. **Medium-high** that she weighed with the outer group; **Medium** that she lay at the head of the anchorage.
   - The Rhin weighed on the 6.15 order and was towed out by her boats (annex pp. 207–208). **High.** **Medium** that she lay with the outer group.
   - The Hermione weighed in the morning; her report leaves the hour blank (annex p. 242). She had been named for Magon's night sortie (annex p. 101). **Medium** that she lay with the outer group.
6. **The brigs Argus and Furet and the brig Observateur were at anchor in the bay.** **High** for presence. No source gives a berth.
7. **Ships of the line in port that were not of the sailing fleet.**
   - The Terrible 74, disarmed, and the San Fulgencio 64, sent into dock in September (Gravina, annex p. 84). Their crews manned the newly armed ships. They were not with the fleet. **Medium.** Most likely at the arsenal of La Carraca, about 6 nm south-east of the anchorage, out of a close view of the outer bay and the city. **Low** for the place.
   - Two more Spanish ships of the line lay in the roads when the fleet arrived on 20 August. They were judged worthless and replaced by the Santa Ana and the Rayo (Lauriston, 2 September, annex p. 112; Desbrière p. 134). Their names and their place in October are not recorded. **Low.**
   - The arsenal held other ships laid up ("los desarmados en el arsenal", Fernández Duro VIII p. 308). Not in the view.
8. **The Spanish frigate Flora and one corvette (Mercurio or Indagadora) came from Ferrol with the fleet in August** (Desbrière p. 99; Escaño, annex p. 7). They did not sail on 19–20 October. Their place on the 19th is not recorded. **Low.**
9. **Gunboats: contested.**
   - **The Spanish version.** On the 18th the gunboat stations were withdrawn. The guns came out of the fleet's launches, the men went back to their ships, and the small craft were hoisted in (Escaño, annex p. 100; Gravina's report, annex p. 145; *Montañés*, annex p. 372; *Príncipe de Asturias*, annex p. 383; Fernández Duro VIII p. 314). **High** for the fleet's own launches.
   - **James's version.** On the evening of the 18th, "as a preparatory measure, a strong force of gun-boats drew up in line across the entrance of the harbour" (James III 385). He gives no source. The Euryalus's log of the 18th has nothing of it (NRS II 145).
   - The two can be squared only if James's gunboats were the port's own, not the fleet's launches. No other source places the port's gunboats on the 18th or 19th. **Low** that gunboats lay at the entrance on the morning of the 19th.
10. **The morning was busy.** **High.**
    - By 8 a.m. the Euryalus counted 19 of the enemy under way. All the rest but two ships of the line had their topsails at the mastheads.
    - The Argus counted 17 under sail at 9.
    - In the calm many then anchored again where they were.
    - This is far more movement than the scene's licence gives (section 6).

## 1. The combined fleet in the bay

### 1.1 How many

- **33 of the line, 5 frigates and 2 brigs** sailed (Thémis, annex p. 273; Quiot of the Achille, annex p. 266; James III 385). **High.**
- **The Observateur stayed behind by order.** On the evening of the 18th Villeneuve left her because she sailed badly. Her seamen went to the Indomptable and her gunners to the Algésiras (Villeneuve to Decrès, 18 October, annex p. 102; Desbrière p. 174). Croizé says all her seamen were taken (annex p. 158). On the morning of the 19th she was a brig at anchor with few men aboard. **High.**
- **Name.** Villeneuve's state of 16 September lists her among the "bricks" (annex p. 86). Croizé calls her a "brick" in his letter. Desbrière's heading calls her a corvette (annex p. 158). **High** for "brig".
- **The French part:** 18 of the line; the frigates Hortense, Cornélie, Thémis, Rhin and Hermione; the brigs Furet, Observateur and Argus (Villeneuve's state of 16 September, annex pp. 85–86). **High.**

### 1.2 Where the fleet lay: what the sources give in general

- **The roadstead.** From 9–10 October the fleet lay in the roadstead near the harbour mouth (Fraser p. 53; James III 385; Nelson's diary of 14 October). **High.** James says all but one ship moved, the San Fulgencio. That is wrong for the Fougueux, which stayed in the narrows (section 2).
- **Out of range of the city's guns.** Escaño writes that the fleet's position was out of range of the city's guns. So the ships were to be placed so that, moored with springs, they could defend themselves (annex p. 98). He writes of the days after Nelson arrived (28 September), before the move of 9–10 October. **High** that this held in October.
- **Villeneuve's choice, 2 October.** He wrote that placing the ships further in might have been safer. He refused, because weighing would have been even harder (annex p. 95). **High.**
- **Contested reading.** Corbett (p. 322) reads Escaño's letter as saying that, from about 2 October, fear of an attack kept the ships crowded up in the harbour. Villeneuve's letter of 2 October says the opposite. James has the fleet at anchor in the outer harbour on 1 October (III 380). **Medium** for the outer bay in early October.
- **A defensive arrangement.**
  - Gravina and Villeneuve had a defensive "ligne d'embossage" studied (Desbrière p. 137).
  - After the council of 8 October the ships were to be placed "en la forma conveniente" (Escaño, annex p. 99).
  - Churruca wrote on 11 October that, three days before, the ships at the anchorage were being gone over to arrange them so that they could not be attacked (Fernández Duro VIII pp. 318–319, printed from Salas).
  - No source read gives the shape of the lines or the order of the ships. The chart's three WNW–ESE lines are a choice, not a finding. Faden's plan of 1797 draws that year's Spanish defensive line running north–south. **Medium.**
- **Moorings.** Two anchors, laid NW and SE (*Montañés*, annex p. 372; the Héros hove up her port anchor and rode to her starboard cable, annex p. 165). **High.**
- **The fast ships went first.**
  - Within ten days of arriving, Villeneuve moved a number of his best and fastest ships from the inner port to just within the harbour mouth, as a squadron for particular service under Magon (Fraser p. 29). **Medium.**
  - On 18 October Magon was to sail that night with four French and three Spanish of the line and the Hermione (Villeneuve, annex p. 101). Escaño says only "algunos navíos" (annex p. 100). **High.**
  - Philibert says the Algésiras made sail at once on the 19th, with several ships of the division that was to sail under Magon (annex p. 229). **High.**
  - This fits outer berths for Magon's ships. But Desbrière notes that the seven that got out came from several squadrons (p. 161 n. 3). **Medium.**

### 1.3 By group, as far as any source goes

**(a) Outermost, toward the passes: the ships that weighed first.**
- The Fougueux saw "7 ou 8 des vaisseaux les plus en dehors" weigh (annex p. 213).
- The Montañés: by 8.30 most of the ships anchored further out were under sail (annex p. 373).
- Desbrière's narrative: only the most advanced ships and frigates seem to have carried out the order (p. 160).
- Philibert (annex pp. 229–230) and the Héros (annex p. 165) name the seven that got out.
- **High** that the outermost ships weighed first. **Medium-high** that the seven were the outermost.
- A weak hint on their place: at 7 the Euryalus "saw the northernmost ships under way" (NRS II 145). If those were the outer group, it lay on the north side of the anchorage. **Low.**

**(b) The main body: the other 25 of the line.**
- No source gives individual berths.
- The Montañés lay further in than the outer group: she made sail only at 8.30, after most of the outer ships (annex p. 373). **Medium.**
- **Two ships of the line without topsails.** At 8 the Euryalus saw all the ships not under way with "their topsails to the mast head", "except the Spanish Rear-Admiral and another line-of-battle ship" (NRS II 146).
  - Cisneros, a *jefe de escuadra* (rear-admiral), flew his flag in the Santísima Trinidad (James III 386), so she is the likeliest.
  - British logs used the term loosely. On the 21st two logs have a Spanish "Rear-Admiral" striking at about 4 p.m. (NRS II 185, 287).
  - The second ship is not named. It may have been the Fougueux, which made no attempt to weigh that morning. From the Euryalus's position, 4–6 nm south-west of San Sebastián, the narrows lay about 6 nm off across the low isthmus.
  - **Low** for both identifications.
- **The Neptuno's pilot.** Shortly after 7 "el navío Neptuno" asked for a pilot to weigh (Montañés diary, annex p. 373). The French Neptune was among the seven that got out. The diarist uses the French names of French ships (Algésiras, Aigle), and the Héros calls the French ship "le Neptune français". **Medium** that the Spanish Neptuno is meant.

**(c) The narrows between the Puntal and Matagorda forts: the Fougueux.**
- She lay "entre les forts du Pontal et du Port-Réal", "trop enfoncé dans la baie" for an easy weighing (annex pp. 213, 216).
- "Port-Réal" is read as the Matagorda fort, on the Puerto Real shore opposite the Puntal. Escaño names the same pair for the Argonauta in August, "entre los castillejos de Puntal y Matagorda" (annex p. 8). Fort San Luis on the Trocadero is also on the Puerto Real shore, about 0.5 nm east of Matagorda. Either way she lay in the narrows or just inside them. **Medium-high.**
- Positions, for the chart (Wikipedia as a pointer): Puntal castle about 36°30.5′ N 6°15.7′ W; Matagorda about 36°30.9′ N 6°15.0′ W; Fort San Luis about 36°30.4′ N 6°14.5′ W. The narrows between Puntal and Matagorda are about 0.7 nm wide; their midpoint is about 36°30.7′ N 6°15.3′ W.
- Why she lay so far in is not recorded. Villeneuve's state of 16 September has her "en mauvais état", needing a survey in the docks (annex p. 86). That may explain a berth near the way to the arsenal. **Low.**

**(d) The inner bay beyond the narrows.**
- No ship of the sailing fleet is placed there on the 19th.
- The Thémis writes that "les vaisseaux les plus en dedans" stayed at anchor, ready, waiting for a breeze (annex p. 273). This means the inner ships of the fleet in general and names none.
- Fraser's evening towing or warping of the ships in the harbour (pp. 84–85) gives no names or source.
- **High** that the sources name no other inner ship.

## 2. The narrows: the Fougueux, and who else

### 2.1 The Fougueux

**18 October**
- About 5 p.m. the signals were made to unmoor and hoist in the boats.
- At 9 p.m. she was unmoored and hove short on one anchor, boats in.
- With the wind from W to WSW she could not weigh from between the forts. She stayed where she was.
- (Bazin's reports, annex pp. 212–213 and 216.) **High.**

**19 October, morning**
- She stayed where she was, hove short. She made no attempt to weigh. **High.**

**19 October, evening: the warp**
- She saw 7 or 8 of the outermost ships make sail, and several frigates. The second report says seven or eight ships and some frigates went out beating (annex p. 216).
- Baudoin then ordered her warped into a berth from which she could weigh. "Il était alors 5 h. 1/2 du soir" (annex p. 213).
- She had only her own boats, hawsers and grelins. She reached a berth from which she could weigh with any wind, on one anchor, and hoisted her boats in again.
- Second report: she asked to be placed better for weighing, warped, and "vint se placer en travers de Cadix" (annex p. 216).
- **High** for the hour and the method. **Medium** for "off the city". **Low** for "between the narrows and the main group": that is an inference.

**20 October**
- She weighed at first light with the general signal (annex p. 213).
- Leaf links: annex p. 213, https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n492/mode/1up; annex p. 216, https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n495/mode/1up

### 2.2 The San Justo

- **She was armed at Cádiz after the fleet arrived.** Gravina, 16 September: since his arrival he had armed the Rayo and the San Justo (annex p. 84). Lauriston, 2 September: Gravina was replacing the two bad ships with the Santa Ana, the Rayo and the San Justo (annex p. 112). **High.**
- **Where she lay in September: contested.**
  - Desbrière's narrative: on 16 September twelve Spanish ships lay in the roads, nine from Ferrol and three armed at Cádiz. The Rayo and San Leandro went into the roads on the 28th, and the Santa Ana was ready early in October (p. 134). By elimination the San Justo was one of the three in the roads.
  - Villeneuve's own letter of 16 September has thirteen Spanish ships in the roads and three more "encore au Pontal", under repair, fitting out or waiting for crews (annex p. 87). On that count the San Justo may have been one of the three at the Pontal.
  - **Low** for her berth on 16 September.
- **By 24 September she was in the roads.** Villeneuve: all 15 were in the roads but the Santa Ana, which had come out of La Carraca; the Rayo was in the roads but not ready (annex p. 88). **Medium-high.**
- **New and badly manned.** On 8 October the council of war called the Santa Ana, the Rayo and the San Justo "à peine sortis de l'arsenal" (procès-verbal, annex p. 97; quoted by Desbrière p. 128). Villeneuve's letter of the same day has "à peine sortis du port" (annex p. 96). This says they were fresh from fitting out, not where they lay. **High.**
- **Verdict.** No source places her in the narrows on 19 October. **No evidence.**

### 2.3 The Rayo, the Santa Ana, other ships fitted out at Cádiz

**The Pontal as a fitting-out berth**
- On 16 September three Spanish ships were "encore au Pontal" for repairs, fitting out or crews (Villeneuve, annex p. 87). **High.**
- So ships coming out of La Carraca lay at the Puntal for a time before joining. **Medium** for the general rule.

**By late September they had moved down**
- Villeneuve, 24 September (annex p. 88). **High.**
- The Santa Ana was ready early in October (Desbrière p. 134). On 28 September Gravina expected her with the fleet about nine days later (Desbrière p. 136, citing his letter of that day). **Medium.**

**The Rayo on the 20th**
- She was the last out. She told the Argus she had had many anchors to weigh (annex pp. 268–269).
- The Observateur says she had much trouble getting under sail and rejoined at 10 (annex p. 158).
- This fits a heavy mooring. It does not place her. The Argus beat about under her topsails while waiting and sent a boat at 9, but where is not said. **Low** for the outer bay.

**Verdict**
- From October no source places any Spanish ship at the Puntal or further in. **Medium** (absence of evidence in the sources read).

### 2.4 James's "one ship"

- **What he says.** On 9–10 October the fleet moved to the entrance of the harbour, "with the exception of one ship, the San-Fulgencio 64 (for some unknown reason detained)" (James III 385). Gravina had put her into dock in mid-September and used her crew elsewhere (section 3), so she would not have moved.
- **The other reading.** The one ship the British saw stay behind may have been the Fougueux, which did stay in the narrows.
- **Low** for either reading. James's sentence is no evidence that the Fougueux moved.

### 2.5 Recommendation for the chart

- Keep the Fougueux alone at the outer end of the narrows until her evening warp. The chart's point, 36°31.0′ N 6°15.2′ W, lies about 0.3 nm north of the midpoint between the forts.
- Move the San Justo into the main body.
- If the chart wants to hint at "the innermost ships" (Thémis), it can do so only without names, and not in the narrows.

## 3. Men-of-war in the harbour that were not part of the sailing fleet

### 3.1 Terrible 74 and San Fulgencio 64

**Where they came from**
- The Terrible had come from the West Indies with Gravina and was damaged at Finisterre. The San Fulgencio sailed from Ferrol (Desbrière p. 99; James III 373–374). **High.**
- So the bay held 35 of the line: 18 French, 11 Spanish from Ferrol and 6 Spanish fitted out at Cádiz. **High.** It may have held more Spanish hulls besides (3.2).

**What happened to them: the sources**
- **Gravina, 16 September (annex p. 84):** "j'ai désarmé le Terrible". He was putting the San Fulgencio "dans le bassin" to survey her bottom. The crews of both went to the newly armed ships. **High.**
- **Lauriston, 2 September (annex p. 112):** Gravina "fera rentrer le Terrible qui fait eau", that is, he would send the leaking Terrible back in. **High.**
- **Desbrière's narrative (p. 134):** has the Terrible go into the dock ("rentrer au bassin"). He cites Lauriston's letter, which says only "rentrer". "Au bassin" is Desbrière's own gloss. **Low** for the Terrible in the dock.
- **Fernández Duro (VIII p. 308):** Gravina exchanged the Terrible, damaged at Finisterre, for "otro de los desarmados en el arsenal". This suggests that the Terrible joined the ships laid up in the arsenal. **Medium-low.**
- **James (III 385):** the Terrible was disarmed and her crew shared out. The San Fulgencio did not move down on 9–10 October (2.4). **High** for the Terrible.

**Where they were on 19 October**
- No source gives either ship's berth.
- The only dry docks in the bay were at the arsenal of La Carraca, at the inner end of the bay near San Fernando, about 36°29.8′ N 6°10.6′ W. Its three docks were completed in 1788 (Wikipedia, as a pointer).
- The 1796 phrase "the Ships going up to the Caraccas" shows that laid-up Spanish ships went there (Nicolas VII, addenda, pp. cv, cix).
- **Most likely both lay at La Carraca**, in or near the dock and unmanned. **Low** for the place, **medium** for "not with the fleet".

**Distances, for the view**
- La Carraca from the anchorage centre (36°33.8′ N 6°16.4′ W): about 6.1 nm, south-east.
- From the city (Torre Tavira): about 6.2 nm.
- From the narrows point (36°31.0′ N 6°15.2′ W): about 3.9 nm.
- La Carraca lies behind the narrows, among the low marshes of the Isla de León. A disarmed ship or a ship in dock would show little. **Medium.**

**What to record in the history notes**
- Neither was in the anchorage on 19 October. Neither belongs in a close view of the outer bay and the city.

**British counts (a side note)**
- British counts in late September and early October ran from 34 to 36 of the line: 34 on 1 and 8 October (James III 380); 35–36 in Nelson's letters (Nicolas VII).
- On 1 October the Euryalus saw "18 French and 16 Spanish" in the outer harbour (James III 380). One more than the 15 that sailed would fit the Terrible, the San Fulgencio or one of the two ships judged worthless being seen before 9 October. It may also be a miscount. **Low.**

### 3.2 Two Spanish ships of the line found in the roads in August

- Lauriston, 2 September: four Spanish ships were at Cádiz when the fleet arrived, two of them bad. Gravina was replacing them with the Santa Ana, the Rayo and the San Justo (annex p. 112). **High.**
- Desbrière: six new ships were to be found in the roads; only four were there, and two of them were worthless (p. 134). **High.**
- Neither names the two worthless ships or says where they went. Escaño's relation breaks off just as it begins to name the ships ready to sail inside the bay on 20 August (annex p. 8).
- **Their place on 19 October is not recorded.** Most likely laid up in the arsenal, like the others "desarmados en el arsenal" (Fernández Duro VIII p. 308). **Low.**

### 3.3 Spanish frigate and corvettes

- **What came from Ferrol.** The Spanish contingent that sailed from Ferrol in August included the frigate Flora (34) and the corvettes Mercurio and Indagadora (Desbrière p. 99, spelling "Indagora"). Desbrière sums the Spanish share as 11 of the line, 1 frigate and 1 corvette, so one of the two corvettes may not have come. Escaño gives the same share (annex p. 7). **High** for the frigate, **medium** for the corvettes.
- **Not at sea.** None sailed with the fleet on 19–20 October. The fleet at sea had only the five French frigates and the two French brigs (Thémis, annex p. 273; James III 386). **High.**
- **Not seen in the outer harbour.** On 1 October the Euryalus saw only "four frigates, and two brigs" with the fleet (James III 380). That fits the French ones. **Medium.**
- **Where they were on 19 October: not found.** A modern summary lists the Flora at Cádiz in 1808, which suggests she stayed there. **Low.**
- **For the history notes:** "Their place on 19 October is not recorded."

### 3.4 Gunboats and armed boats (*fuerzas sutiles*)

**What they were**
- After the fleet came in, the Spaniards prepared gunboats, howitzer and mortar boats, faluchos, boats and barges to defend the city and the fleet (Escaño, annex p. 98). Escaño gives no date. Corbett ties the flotilla to the news of Nelson's plans that reached Cádiz on 2 October (p. 322). It was out by 4 October (James III 380). **Medium** for the timing.
- They were organised in divisions in the same way as in 1797 (Fernández Duro VIII p. 308).
- In 1797 Mazarredo armed the ships' launches with 24-pounders while the arsenal fitted out gunboats. Of the 167 craft listed that year, 34 were ships' launches with a 24-pounder, out of about 64 that carried guns. The divisions were stationed at Rota, La Caleta, the Puerta de Sevilla and Sancti Petri (Fernández Duro VIII pp. 140, 143). The 1805 stations may have followed this pattern. **Low.**

**Before the 18th**
- About 6 October Gravina dissolved the flotilla and sent its officers and men back to their ships (Escaño, annex p. 99).
- After the council of 8 October the divisions were posted again at their former stations, manned from the fleet (Escaño, annex p. 99).
- On 4 October Spanish gunboats twice pulled out and fired on the Euryalus and Hydra, then went back to the harbour's mouth (James III 380).
- **No source gives where the 1805 stations were.**

**On the 18th: contested**
- **The Spanish version.** In Villeneuve's presence Gravina ordered the gunboat stations withdrawn and the men back to their ships. This was done that morning (Escaño, annex p. 100; Gravina's report, annex p. 145; Fernández Duro VIII p. 314). At about 5–5.30 p.m. the signals were made to take the guns out of the launches, call the crews aboard and hoist in all small craft. The *Príncipe de Asturias* records that the launches came off the stations, their guns were removed and all small craft were hoisted in (*Montañés*, annex p. 372; *Príncipe de Asturias*, annex p. 383). **High** for the fleet's armed launches: they were not on station on the morning of the 19th.
- **James's version.** That evening "a strong force of gun-boats drew up in line across the entrance of the harbour" as a preparatory measure (James III 385). No source is given. No log in NRS II mentions it. **Low.**
- If both are right, James's gunboats were the port's own craft, not the fleet's launches. No other source places the port's gunboats on the 18th or 19th.
- For the chart: no gunboats is the better-supported choice. The history notes should give James's contrary statement.

### 3.5 Hulks

- **Not found.**
- On the evening of the 20th Croizé planned to keep spare men "dans un ponton mouillé à cet effet près du brick" (annex p. 158). That is a plan, not evidence for the 19th.
- The Sirius saw "7 sail and a hulk at anchor in the mouth of Cadiz Harbour" on 24 October (NRS II 315). That is after the battle; the hulk was probably a dismasted ship.

### 3.6 Other French vessels

- The schooner Téméraire is listed with the French force leaving Ferrol in August, as having joined there, but is left out of its total of 18 of the line, 5 frigates and 3 corvettes (Desbrière p. 98). She is not in Villeneuve's state of 16 September (annex pp. 85–86). No source read puts her at Cádiz. **Low.**

### 3.7 Merchant shipping

- Not researched. The bay certainly held merchant and coasting vessels kept in by the blockade. None is a man-of-war.

## 4. Frigates and brigs in the anchorage, and that morning

### 4.1 Frigates

**Thémis.** Jugan's report (annex p. 273; page image read):
- At the 6 a.m. signal she weighed "avec quelques vaisseaux et frégates qui étaient à la tête" and made for the exit of the bay.
- She had to drop anchor because of the calm and the current, which would have carried her back in. Her report does not say where she anchored.
- Desbrière: she anchored at the bay entrance to hold against the flood (p. 160). That is his reading.
- At 4.30 p.m. she weighed again on the signal to all frigates and beat out. She reached Magon at about 6.30 (Philibert, annex p. 230).
- **Medium** that she lay at the head (outer end) of the anchorage. Between about 7 and 8.15 she was under way toward the entrance or anchored again on the way. **Medium**; the hour she anchored is not given.
- Leaf: https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n554/mode/1up

**Hermione.** Mahé's report (annex p. 242):
- She weighed in the morning in a very light ESE air. The hour is left blank.
- When it fell calm (hour blank), "the ships" dropped an anchor where they were, so as not to drift back in on the flood.
- Magon ordered her to reconnoitre. She went out with the help of her boats (annex p. 242). Philibert puts the order at 9.30 (annex p. 229). Desbrière: towed out by her boats (p. 160).
- She was named for Magon's night sortie of the 18th (annex p. 101).
- **Medium** that she lay with the outer group. **Low-medium** that she was under way during 7–8.15: she may have weighed and anchored again, or weighed later.
- Leaf: https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n521/mode/1up

**Rhin.** Her journal (annex pp. 207–208):
- The order came at 6.15. She carried it out, in a very faint easterly, and went out with the help of her boats.
- At noon she took the San Sebastián tower SW 5° S and the town of Rota NNW 5° N by compass (marginal note, annex p. 208). With 21° W variation this puts her at about 36°33.7′ N 6°18.1′ W (±0.3 nm): just outside the passes, about 2.1 nm NNE of the tower. **Medium.**
- Desbrière has her leave the bay with Magon's division from about 10, the last of them through the passes about 1 p.m. (p. 161).
- **High** that she was under way, towed, in the bay during 7–8.15. **Medium** that she lay with the outer group.

**Cornélie, Hortense.**
- Nothing on their berths.
- They were still at anchor in the morning: only one to three frigates got out (Rhin: three; Philibert, the Observateur and the Sirius: two; *Príncipe*: one).
- **Medium.** See section 6 on the evening.

**Squadrons (context only).** In the line of battle of 18 October the Thémis, the Argus and the Hermione were attached to the squadron of observation; the Rhin to Alava's squadron; the Hortense and the Furet to Villeneuve's; the Cornélie to Dumanoir's (Escaño, annex pp. 100–101). No source says the anchorage was arranged by squadrons. **Medium** for the attachments (the OCR table is broken), **Low** as a hint to berths.

**Inside the lines?**
- No source puts any frigate inside the lines, toward the city.
- The only frigate whose berth is described, the Thémis, lay at the outer end.
- On 1 October frigates and brigs lay with the fleet in the outer harbour (James III 380). **Medium.**

- At 8.30 the captain of the Observateur brought her the order to sail only with the last ship of the fleet. She stayed at anchor all the 19th and sailed at 8.30 a.m. on the 20th.

**Observateur.**

## 5. The morning hour by hour

**Weather**
- Dawn clear, with a very light air: from the NE (*Montañés*; Philibert, "variables à l'E.S.E."; Furet; the Argus in the night), E (Rhin), ESE (Hermione), SE (Observateur), S to SSE, almost calm (Héros). James has a light breeze at N by E at 7 (III 385).
- Calm by about 9 (Argus; *Montañés* at 9.30).
- From about 10 a light SSW breeze (Desbrière p. 161; Héros, annex p. 165).
- Euryalus: moderate and clear; at 10 light airs, inclining to calm; at noon calm (NRS II 146).
- **High.**

**Tide**
- The flood ran into the bay through the morning (Thémis; Hermione). Philibert says the seven got out "dans cette marée". **High.**

**Sunrise**
- For 36°32′ N on 19 October: about 6.26 a.m. apparent time, at about 102° true (ESE). Checked by a second computation. **High.**
- Robinson of the Euryalus remembered the sun rising "over the Trocadero" as the fleet inside let fall and hoisted their topsails (*Sea Drift*, p. 209). From the Euryalus the Trocadero bore about ENE, so the sun rose some 30° to its right. In the same paragraph he puts the fleet's long line before noon, which belongs to the 20th. **Low** as a record; good for the look.

**Hour by hour**
- **Night of 18–19.** Ships unmoored and hove short. The Montañés crossed her topgallant yards at nightfall, weighed her SE anchor at 2 a.m., and rode to her NW anchor with 35 fathoms of cable out from 4 (annex p. 372). **High.**
- **Daylight.** The Euryalus saw the ships in Cádiz with topgallant yards across, and eight with topsails hoisted to the mastheads (NRS II 145). **High.**
- **Before 7.** The Sirius saw the enemy with topsail yards up and signalled it (NRS II 312; Corbett p. 333 puts the signal at 6). Then she saw part of the fleet under way; the entry stands before her 7 o'clock entry. **Medium** for the hour.
- **5.45 to 6.15.** The signal to weigh "sans autre signal": 5.45 with a gun (Héros); 6 (Argus, Furet, Thémis, Philibert, *Montañés*); 6.15 (Rhin); at daybreak (Observateur); "between 5 and 6" (Desbrière p. 160). **High.**
- **6 onward.** The Algésiras made sail at once, with several ships of the division that was to sail under Magon (annex p. 229). The Rhin weighed on the 6.15 order and went out with her boats. **High.**
- **6.30.** The Héros could not cast in the light air. With two boats towing ahead she was under way by 6.30, anchor catted. She worked toward the passes with her boats through the calm forenoon, and was outside them at 1 p.m. (annex pp. 164–165). **High.**
- **7.**
  - Gravina repeated the signal.
  - The Montañés hoisted her second boat in and, hove short, hoisted her topsails (annex p. 373).
  - The Euryalus saw "the northernmost ships under way" (NRS II 145).
  - The Naiad saw several of the enemy's ships under way (NRS II 302).
  - **High.**
- **Shortly after 7.** The Neptuno, probably the Spanish one, asked for a pilot to weigh (annex p. 373). **Medium.**
- **8.**
  - The Euryalus "saw 19 of the enemy under way".
  - All the rest had their topsails to the masthead, except two ships of the line (1.3 b; NRS II 146).
  - **High** for the count as seen from 4–6 nm off. It includes frigates.
- **8.30.**
  - The Montañés made sail under three topsails in a very light easterly. By then most of the outer ships were under sail.
  - The Observateur's captain brought the Argus her order.
  - **High.**
- **9.**
  - The Argus counted 17 ships under sail, calm. Several anchored and clewed up their sails at the mastheads (annex p. 268).
  - The Hermione: when it fell calm the ships dropped an anchor where they were (hour blank).
  - **High.**
- **9.30.**
  - The Montañés, making no headway, clewed up and anchored again (annex p. 373).
  - Magon sent the Hermione to reconnoitre (annex p. 229).
  - **High.**
- **Noon.**
  - The Sirius: four of the enemy's line at anchor at the mouth of the harbour, some others under way, and two frigates outside the lighthouse (NRS II 312).
  - The Naiad: eleven of the line outside San Sebastián, from 11–12 miles off (NRS II 302), an overcount.
  - The Rhin just outside the passes (4.1).
  - The *Montañés*: only the first ships to sail, six French and one Spanish, remained under sail.
  - The Euryalus: "The enemy's ships in Cadiz Bay in a calm" (NRS II 146).

**How many got out: the versions**
- **Seven of the line, named** (Philibert, annex pp. 229–230). The Héros names the same seven as weighing with her and has them outside the passes at 1 p.m. (annex p. 165).
- **Six French and one Spanish under sail at noon** (*Montañés*, annex p. 373).
- **Six of the line and a frigate "en franquía"** outside the port (*Príncipe de Asturias*, annex p. 383).
- **Eight of the line, one of them Spanish** (Villeneuve's postscript, annex pp. 102–103).
- **Eight French of the line and two frigates** (Observateur, annex p. 158).
- **14 sail, ships and frigates** (Furet, annex p. 174).
- **Six to seven of the line and three frigates** (Rhin, annex p. 208).
- **Seven or eight of the line and several frigates** (Fougueux, annex pp. 213, 216).
- **Nine of the line** (Prigny, annex p. 138, written from memory).
- **Six "en franquía"** (Fernández Duro VIII p. 329).
- **Twelve ships** (James III 385), from the British counts, frigates included.
- **Many weighed in the morning and anchored again.** The Euryalus, Argus, Montañés, *Príncipe de Asturias* and Hermione all say so.
- **Desbrière's narrative differs:** only the most advanced seem to have tried, and the others stayed at anchor (p. 160). The eyewitnesses outweigh him on this. **High.**

## 6. Beyond the window: points that bear on the scene's licences

**1. The morning movement**
- The licence (history notes, "Assumptions and licences", 9): the seven weigh from 7 a.m. about an hour apart, and the Hermione and Rhin weigh in the early afternoon.
- The sources:
  - The signal came at about 6.
  - The Rhin weighed on the 6.15 order and the Héros was under way at 6.30, both towed by boats.
  - The Euryalus counted 19 under way at 8.
  - Ships then anchored again in the calm.
  - The Hermione was towed out after 9.30 and reconnoitred outside before Magon's division came out.
  - The seven and the Rhin got through the passes between about 10 and 1 p.m. (Desbrière p. 161; Héros; Sirius at noon; the Rhin's noon bearings).
- **High** that the licence understates the movement in the 7–8.15 window and puts the Rhin far too late. **Medium-high** for the Hermione.

**2. The evening of the 19th: contested**
- For movement:
  - At 4 p.m. the Swiftsure and all the French frigates were signalled to weigh (Furet, annex p. 174). The Thémis weighed at 4.30.
  - At 5–5.15 the Argus, herself at anchor, counted 9 of the line and 5 frigates under sail (annex p. 268; page image read; the comma makes the sentence ambiguous).
  - At nightfall Philibert saw several ships of the fleet making sail and beating out of the bay (annex p. 230).
  - The Hortense says that from the 19th several French and Spanish ships and all the frigates were beating outside the roads (annex pp. 188–189).
  - The Cornélie: at daybreak on the 20th part of the fleet had already weighed on the signal of the day before (annex p. 159).
- Against: the *Montañés* has the whole squadron at anchor at nightfall (annex p. 373), and the *Príncipe de Asturias* has the calm last all afternoon and night (annex p. 383). They may mean the Spanish ships only.
- So the Cornélie and Hortense probably went out on the evening of the 19th, not at 7 a.m. on the 20th as the licence has it. The Swiftsure and one more ship of the line may have weighed too, which would explain Prigny's "nine". **Medium.**

**3. The Fougueux's evening warp**
- From 5.30 p.m., to a berth off the city (section 2.1). **High.**

## 7. Suggested statements for the history notes

1. **The anchorage.** On the morning of 19 October all 33 of the line lay in the bay of Cádiz with the five frigates, the brigs Argus and Furet, and the brig Observateur, which stayed behind by order. **High.**
2. **The narrows.** Only the Fougueux is recorded in the narrows, between the Puntal and Matagorda forts. She stayed there, hove short, all morning, and warped out from 5.30 p.m. to a berth off the city (Desbrière 1907, annex pp. 213, 216). The Thémis's "innermost ships" are not named and are not placed in the narrows. **High.**
3. **The outer ships.** The ships that got out on the 19th were among the outermost (Fougueux; *Montañés*; named by Philibert and the Héros). The frigate Thémis weighed with the ships at the head of the anchorage (annex p. 273). **Medium-high.**
4. **The ships left in port.** The Terrible 74, disarmed, and the San Fulgencio 64, sent into dock in September, were not with the fleet. Their crews manned the new Spanish ships. They lay most likely in the arsenal of La Carraca, about 6 nm south-east of the anchorage, and are not drawn. **Medium**; **Low** for the place. Two Spanish ships judged worthless in August, and the ships laid up in the arsenal, are not named in October and are not drawn. **Low.**
5. **The Spanish frigate and corvette.** The frigate Flora and one corvette came from Ferrol with the fleet. They did not sail with it, and their place on 19 October is not recorded. **Low.**
6. **Gunboats.** The fleet's armed launches had been taken off their stations and hoisted in on the 18th (Spanish sources). James alone has a strong force of gunboats drawn up across the harbour entrance on the evening of the 18th. The chart draws none. **High** for the launches; **Low** for James's line.

## 8. Recommendations for the chart (7 to 8.15 a.m.)

- **Narrows.** The Fougueux alone, at anchor, hove short. Not the San Justo.
- **Main body.** The other 32 of the line, the San Justo among them. The seven, the Thémis and the Rhin at the outer (WNW) end toward the passes; the Hermione probably with them.
- **Frigates.** No evidence puts any frigate inside the lines toward the city, as the chart now does. The Cornélie and Hortense have no recorded berth; any place in the main body is a licence.
- **Brigs.** The Argus, the Furet and the Observateur must be drawn: present, at anchor, berths unknown (a licence).
- **Movement.** By the sources, many ships were weighing in this window: the seven and the Rhin under way or towed toward the passes from about 6–6.30, 19 sail under way at 8, the rest with topsails at the mastheads.
- **Not drawn.** The Terrible and the San Fulgencio (at La Carraca most likely, 6 nm off); the two worthless Spanish ships and the laid-up ships (place unknown); the Spanish frigate and corvette (place unknown); gunboats (contested; the better-supported version has none); hulks (none recorded).

## Open doubts

1. **Gunboats on the 19th.** James's line of gunboats across the entrance against the Spanish withdrawal of the stations. Whose gunboats, and from what source James took it, is unknown.
2. **The two worthless Spanish ships of August.** Not named; their place in October is unknown. One may explain the Euryalus's "16 Spanish" on 1 October.
3. **The date of Escaño's list:** 19 October (heading) or 16 October (index).
4. **The San Justo in September:** in the roads (Desbrière) or at the Pontal (Villeneuve's count allows it).
5. **"Port-Réal":** Matagorda or Fort San Luis. Either way the Fougueux lay in or just inside the narrows.
6. **The Fougueux's evening berth "en travers de Cadix":** the exact place is not given.
7. **The second ship without topsails at 8 a.m.:** unnamed; the Fougueux is a guess.
8. **The Hermione's hours:** blank in her report.
9. **The Spanish frigate Flora and the corvette:** which corvette came, and where they lay.
10. **The schooner Téméraire:** whether she ever came to Cádiz.
11. **The evening of the 19th:** whether any Spanish ship weighed, against the *Montañés*'s "toda la escuadra fondeada".
12. **The fleet's layout:** no source gives the lines or the order of the ships; the three WNW–ESE lines remain a choice.

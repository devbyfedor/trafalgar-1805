### 1. Gulf of Cadiz

- **Doubt 6** (`a-h4.md`; `drafts-data.json` [2] `doubts[5]`). The same text replaces the `draft-lead.md` bullet "Gulf of Cadiz":
  > **Gulf of Cadiz.** Of the English charts and maps read, two letter it. Laurie & Whittle's *New Map of the Kingdoms of Spain and Portugal* has the imprint 12 May 1794. It is attributed to Thomas Kitchin and was issued in their *New Universal Atlas* of 1804. It sets GULF OF CADIZ in italic capitals across the open water from off Cape St Mary to off Cadiz. Heather's *New Chart of the Coast of Portugal* (3 April 1800) sets it in large open roman capitals from off the mouth of the Guadiana towards Cadiz. Faden (1807), Cary (1801, 1804) and Heather's Strait plate (about 1809) leave the water unnamed. The gazetteers of 1795–1810 (Brookes, Walker, Cruttwell, the *Britannica* of 1810) use the name for the same bight, and Brookes (1807) puts Alcoutim, Faro and Tavira on it. Nelson, the logs, the *Naval Chronicle* and Malham never use it: sailors said "off Cadiz". The spelling "Gulph of Cadiz" also occurs in books of 1798–1810 (v-h4, from archive.org's full-text search). Tuckey (1815) says the name was more usually kept for the Bay of Cadiz.
- **Ruling "Gulf of Cadiz (usage)", replacing its second and third sentences:**
  > Of the English charts and maps read, two letter it. Laurie & Whittle's *Spain and Portugal* (imprint 12 May 1794; atlas of 1804) has GULF OF CADIZ in italic capitals from off Cape St Mary to off Cadiz. Heather's *Coast of Portugal* of 3 April 1800 has it in large open roman capitals from off the mouth of the Guadiana towards Cadiz. I read both maps and both imprints. Faden 1807, Cary 1801, Cary 1804 and Heather's Strait plate of c. 1809 leave the water unnamed.
- **Ruling "Gulf of Cadiz (extent)":**
  - Version 1 becomes:
    > (1) The whole bight from the Algarve to Cadiz. Laurie & Whittle's *Spain and Portugal* (1794) spreads the name from off Cape St Mary to off Cadiz. Heather (1800) sets it in the open water from off the Guadiana towards Cadiz. The gazetteers put Faro, Tavira, Ayamonte and the Guadiana mouth on the gulf.
  - Replace "as its only period chart does" with:
    > as both period maps that name the water do: each sets the name on the open bight, not on the bay.
- **Settled-table row "Gulf of Cádiz"** (and `finalNames[4]`):

### 2. The policy

This text replaces `a-h4.md` "How I chose between forms" points 1–2, "Settled conventions" 1, and `drafts-data.json` [2] `conventions[0]`:

> **Policy.**
> 1. Where the English charts and maps of 1800–1807 agree (by the date of the plate), letter their form even if it looks odd today, and record it: Barbadoes, Bourdeaux, Guadaloupe, Ivica, Algeziras, Huelba, Lizard.
> 2. Where they disagree, drop any form a modern visitor would take for a misprint, for another place or for something else: Martinico, Guadalquiver, Cape Verd, Western Islands alone, Batavia, Algier, and Start alone, which reads as an instruction. An abbreviation that would puzzle a visitor is spelled out (Isle of Wight, Scilly Isles).
> 3. Among the forms left, the naval and printed usage of 1805 decides (Nelson, the logs, the *Naval Chronicle*, Norie, Malham, the chartsellers' titles): Atlantic Ocean, English Channel, Canary Islands, Islands rather than Isles for the Azores, Marseilles, Carthagena, Rochefort, C. St. Mary, Douro, Barbary.
> 4. Faden's chart of 1807, which the scene imitates, breaks any tie that remains.
> 5. A region that no chart of the period letters in its body takes the name used in the period's prose and chart titles (West Indies).
>
> The type conventions then set each form: C., St., Pt., no accents.

`draft-lead.md`, bullet "The rule":
> **The rule.** Where the English charts of 1800–1807 agree, the chart letters their form even if it looks odd today (Barbadoes, Bourdeaux, Guadaloupe). Where they disagree, it drops any form a visitor would misread (Martinico, Guadalquiver, Cape Verd). The naval and printed usage of 1805 then decides among the rest (Atlantic Ocean, Canary Islands, Carthagena, Barbary), and Faden's chart of 1807 is the last tie-break. High for the forms, medium for the choices between equal forms.

### 3. Two new doubts

Add to `a-h4.md` doubts, `drafts-data.json` [2] `doubts`, and `draft-lead.md` "Where the period's own sources disagree":

> **West Indies.**
> - The chart letters West Indies across the Caribbean as a regional label.
> - It was the universal name in 1805: Nelson's letters (84 times in Nicolas's vols. 6–7, notes included), Malham (312) and the *Naval Chronicle* of 1805 (41).
> - It names charts and is used on them:
>   - Arrowsmith's *Chart of the West Indies* (1803).
>   - Heather's catalogue of 5 November 1806: a section for "the West Indies", and no. 60, a chart of "the whole of the West-Indies".
>   - Cary's *West India Isles* (1803).
>   - The label on Faden's outward track (1807): "Track of H.M.S. Victory to the West Indies 1805".
> - No English chart of 1794–1807 that was read letters it in the map body:
>   - The arc of islands is CARIBBEE ISLANDS on Faden (1807), Cary (1803) and Laurie & Whittle (1794), and NORTH and SOUTH CARIBBEE ISLANDS on Arrowsmith (1803).
>   - The sea where the chart's label lies is CARIBBEAN SEA on Faden, Cary and Laurie & Whittle, and on Arrowsmith per a-h4.

> **Barbary.** The chart letters Barbary. Laurie & Whittle (1794, in large spaced capitals across North Africa) and Heather (1800, and his Strait plate of about 1809) letter it too. Nelson (17 times in vols. 6–7) and Malham (58) write it. Heather also titles his harbour plates of 1809 "in Barbary" (v-h4). Faden (1807) and Cary (1804, 1805) letter the states instead: Faden FEZ, MOROCCO and ALGIER, Cary FEZ, MOROCCO, ALGIERS, TUNIS and TRIPOLI (v-h4).

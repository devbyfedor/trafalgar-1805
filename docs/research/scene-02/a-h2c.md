# Adjudication of v-h2c.md (the fact-check of h2-boulogne-cadiz.md, sections 6–10)

Notes: `docs/research/scene-02/h2-boulogne-cadiz.md`. Report: `docs/research/scene-02/v-h2c.md`. Both were read only.

## Method

- I downloaded fresh OCR text of the period books from archive.org (the hOCR search text with its page index) and split it into leaves. Every page cited below was opened as a whole page, never as a search snippet. I checked the running head of each page I cite.
- I read the page images for the three figures where the OCR was doubtful: the *Montañés* diary's "6 franceses" (annex p. 373), Escaño's "21 de Agosto" and the 14 signatures (annex p. 98), and Desbrière p. 161.
- **Links** use the archive.org leaf index, counted from 0 (`/page/nNNN`). Every link was checked against the printed page. Corbett's archive labels run one page high: p. 324 is `n360` and p. 337 is `n373`.
- **Depths.** These are modern, from the EMODnet bathymetry point service, which draws on the IHM Cádiz bay model. They show where open water is today. They are not 1805 soundings, and the main channel has since been dredged.
- **Shoals and buoys.** OpenStreetMap (Overpass).
- **Bearings** are worked as in the notes: magnetic, variation 21° W, 1 league = 3 nm.

## Evidence

### H2-30c: the count on 19 October

- **Philibert's journal** (Magon's flag-lieutenant, on board Algésiras), Desbrière annex pp. 229–230. It names Algésiras, Achille, Argonaute, Neptune, Héros, Duguay-Trouin "et le Bahama espagnol, ainsi que les frégates l'Hermione et le Rhin". These "purent sortir dans cette marée"; the rest stayed at anchor. https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n508/mode/1up and https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n509/mode/1up
- **Desbrière p. 161.** He gives the same seven plus Rhin, from Philibert and the report of the Héros. Hermione was towed out. Note 3: only Algésiras and Bahama were of Magon's division. https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n186/mode/1up
- **Desbrière p. 162.** Thémis came out at about 6 p.m. https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n187/mode/1up
- ***Montañés* diary**, annex p. 373. I read the page image. At noon only the first ships to sail were still under way, "en número de 6 franceses y uno español". https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n656/mode/1up
- **Other French reports.**
  - Rhin, annex p. 208: "six à sept vaisseaux et trois frégates". https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n487/mode/1up
  - Fougueux, annex p. 213: "7 ou 8 des vaisseaux les plus en dehors … ainsi que plusieurs frégates"; annex p. 216: "sept ou huit vaisseaux et quelques frégates". https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n492/mode/1up and https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n495/mode/1up
  - Observateur, annex p. 158: "huit vaisseaux français et deux frégates seulement". https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n431/mode/1up
  - Thémis, annex p. 273: at 4.30 p.m. "une division de 4 vaisseaux" already outside. https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n554/mode/1up
  - Prigny, annex p. 138: "Une division de neuf vaisseaux". In his covering note he says he wrote from memory. https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n407/mode/1up
- **Euryalus's log** (NRS *Logs* II p. 146). At 4 p.m. "9 of the enemy's ships out"; at 6 p.m. "12 in number" (p. 146). At daylight on the 20th, "nine … under sail off Cadiz Harbour, and 4 at anchor" (p. 147). https://archive.org/details/logsofgreatseaf02jack/page/n164/mode/1up and https://archive.org/details/logsofgreatseaf02jack/page/n165/mode/1up
- **Nicolas VII p. 130.** "twelve Ships only succeeded in quitting the Harbour", after James. https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/n165/mode/1up
- **Verdict.** Two independent primary accounts, the French journal and the Spanish diary, name or count seven. The British "12" includes frigates.

### H2-40a, H2-36c, H2-36d, H2-36e, H2-38b: the anchorage and the passes

**Where the fleet lay**

- **Escaño to MacDonnell**, annex p. 98 (page image read): "la posición de la combinada estaba fuera del tiro de la plaza". So the fleet was placed so that ships "acoderados" (moored with springs) could defend themselves. https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n367/mode/1up

- **Fraser.**
  - p. 29: within ten days of arrival Villeneuve moved his fastest ships "from the inner port, down to just within the harbour mouth".
  - p. 53: on 9 October the fleet "began to move down from the inner harbour of Cadiz to the roadstead".
  - https://archive.org/details/enemyattrafalgar00frasuoft/page/n54/mode/1up and https://archive.org/details/enemyattrafalgar00frasuoft/page/n80/mode/1up

### F6, F7, E8–E12: lights and signals

**The allied side at night**

  - By night they signalled with rockets, guns and "feux de composition".
  - "A 11 heures, nous vîmes le feu de la Tour Saint-Sébastien dans la partie du N.-E."
  - https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n408/mode/1up
- **Desbrière p. 165**: Gravina "arborant un feu à son mât d'artimon pour marquer sa position". https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n190/mode/1up

**The British side at night**

- **Sirius's log** (Nicolas VII p. 134):
  - "continued burning blue lights and rockets, as did the Phoebe, Euryalus, Naiad, and all our look-out Ships";
  - at 7.40 p.m. the enemy were "making signals with guns, rockets, &c.";
  - at 3.30 p.m. "observed the Enemy's Fleet wear".
  - https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/n169/mode/1up
- **Phoebe's log** (Nicolas VII p. 135): "At 8 Cadiz Lighthouse E. b. N. about 5 leagues … burnt blue lights and rockets occasionally, which was answered by our Fleet". https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/n170/mode/1up
- **Euryalus's log** (NRS II p. 149): at 6 p.m. she "made several lights and burnt false fires", and in the night "fired guns and burned false fires as necessary". https://archive.org/details/logsofgreatseaf02jack/page/n167/mode/1up

**Nelson's memorandum and diary**

- **Memorandum of 20 October** (Nicolas VII p. 136): the night chain, and the code of two blue lights together or three guns quick every hour. https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/n171/mode/1up
- **Diary of 20 October** (Nicolas VII pp. 136–137): "near forty Sail of Ships of War outside of Cadiz yesterday evening". https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/n172/mode/1up
- **Letter to Emma of 20 October** (p. 132): "thirty-four of the Line, and six Frigates".

**By day, 19 October**

- **Euryalus** (NRS II p. 146): "Phoebe WNW, firing three minute guns".
- **Hermione's report and Philibert**, quoted in Desbrière p. 161 and annex p. 230: the British ships made "beaucoup de signaux" and fired "par volées".
- **Rhin**, annex p. 208: the British fired guns at intervals to ships within sight of their signals.
- **Blackwood to his wife**: "Within two hours … I have let Lord N. know" (Nicolas VII p. 131 n.).

**Moon.** Desbrière p. 141 says Villeneuve was waiting for "une nuit sans lune". The scene 1 notes compute a new moon on 22 October.

### H2-39 and F8: Gibraltar and Tetuan

The town points are on land. On the modern depth model, 36.14 N 5.365 W is in 15 m of water in Gibraltar Bay, and 35.63 N 5.25 W is in 17 m in Tetuan Bay.

### H2-40b and F5: headings

- *Montañés*, annex p. 372: on 18 October she weighed "el ancla del S.-E." and hove in "por el cable del N.-O.". So the fleet was moored with two anchors. https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n655/mode/1up
- Rhin, annex p. 207, evening of 18 October: "Le temps calme, les courants allant flots". https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n486/mode/1up
- Desbrière p. 160: on the morning of the 19th the Thémis had to anchor at the bay entrance "pour résister au courant de flot". https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n185/mode/1up

### H2-40c: the chain ships

- Diary of 14 October: Defence and Agamemnon "from seven to ten leagues west of Cadiz"; Mars and Colossus five leagues east of the fleet, "whose station is from fifteen to twenty west of Cadiz" (Beatty pp. 96–97).
- These give 6.85, 7.09 and 7.41 W. The letters' 16–18 leagues give 7.06 and 7.37 W.
- https://archive.org/details/authenticnarrati00beat/page/n108/mode/1up

### H2-32i and B4: the midnight position

Desbrière p. 178 puts the British fleet about 20 miles SW of Cádiz at midnight.
- Read as true SW, that is 36.29 N 6.61 W. Then about 4 hours SW and 2–3 hours N by E end 10–15 nm from the dawn position.
- Read as magnetic SW, it is 36.22 N 6.49 W, which still leaves about 5 nm.

Either way the night tracks must be drawn loosely.

### H2-31e: the afternoon wear

- **Desbrière p. 164** and n. 1: "Entre 1 heure et 3 heures"; many reports say 3; three columns, with the 2nd squadron to the left of the 1st. https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n189/mode/1up
- **Prigny** (annex p. 138): three columns ordered about noon; at 4 p.m. "virer vent arrière tous à la fois"; Gravina's squadron of observation "à gauche de l'armée".
- **Fougueux** (annex p. 216): three columns; she took the starboard tack "le soir".
- **Sirius** (Nicolas VII p. 134): 3.30 p.m.
- **James**, via Nicolas VII p. 133, and Nicolas's note: five columns, as in the Spanish official report; *Victoires et Conquêtes* says three. https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/n168/mode/1up

### H2-36a: the arrival date

- **Escaño's relation** (annex pp. 7–8): the entry for the 19th–20th runs through dawn and sunrise on the 20th, then "Toda ella entró en el puerto en el curso de la tarde". https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n277/mode/1up
- **Desbrière p. 107**: the fleet arrived off Cádiz on "le 20 au jour" and "vint mouiller dans la soirée, en rade de Cadix". https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n132/mode/1up
- **Escaño's letter** (annex p. 98, image read): "el día 21 de Agosto".

### H2-28b, H2-28c, H2-28e: the council of war

- **The procès-verbal** (annex pp. 97–98, image read): "sur l'invitation de l'amiral Villeneuve". It lists 14 present, Hore among them, and carries 14 signatures. https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n366/mode/1up
- **Escaño's letter** (annex pp. 98–99):
  - On 6 October Gravina answered that he thought it necessary "celebrar un consejo" before weighing.
  - "El Mayor general" argued that crews at sea since 1793 would outmatch crews eight years without sailing.
  - "Magon tomó la palabra para refutar al Mayor"; Galiano took offence; Gravina called the vote.
  - The line of battle on annex p. 100 lists "Mayor Escaño" on board Príncipe de Asturias.
  - https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n368/mode/1up
- **Corbett p. 324** gives the same words to "Captain Prigny … Villeneuve's Chief of the Staff". https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n360/mode/1up

### H2-35e and H2-35b: the ships left in port, and the Trinidad

- **Gravina to Decrès** (annex p. 84): "je fait entrer dans le bassin le Saint-Fulgencio de 64 pour reconnoître ces fonds". He was manning the new ships "avec les équipages des vaisseaux le Terrible et Saint-Fulgencio". https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n353/mode/1up
- **Desbrière p. 134**: Trinidad "de 140 canons". https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n159/mode/1up

**Method.**
- I downloaded the OCR full text of every archive.org scan the notes cite, plus Correspondance vol. X and Nicolas vol. VII. I read each cited passage together with its running page head, so the page numbers below are the printed ones.
- Where one figure or date decided a verdict, I opened the page image: Corr. IX p. 406; Corr. XI pp. 121, 142 and 157; Alombert and Colin III/1 pp. 228 and 229.
- Republican dates are converted with the Correspondance's own equivalences: 15 thermidor XIII = 3 Aug 1805; 4 fructidor = 22 Aug; 10 fructidor = 28 Aug; 13 fructidor = 31 Aug; 1 vendémiaire XIV = 23 Sep; 25 vendémiaire = 17 Oct; 28 vendémiaire = 20 Oct.

**Overall.** The notes are careful and nearly every page reference holds. I found:
- **Five corrections:**
  - the date Napoleon learned of Cádiz;
  - the Rhine-crossing dates for Ney and Soult;
  - the muskets figure at Ulm (an OCR misreading);
  - the date of Napoleon's May 1805 note;
  - the "six hours → three days → 24 hours" arc, which leaves out several of Napoleon's own variants.
- **One attribution problem:** the march line is called "Soult's corps", but Ney's corps did the Elchingen–Ulm leg.
- **One small dispute:** where Napoleon was on 7 October.
- **One point the scene must change:** the Boulogne camps were huts, not tents.

## Evidence

### Section 0 (lines 18–27)

**S0-1, Nelson joins.**
- Nicolas VII, p. 53 prints Victory's log ("28th September, P.M. At 6 ... joined the Fleet off Cadiz", with 23 of the line plus 6 inshore) and the diary heading "Sunday, September 28th, 1805", followed by "Sunday, September 29th". 28 Sep 1805 was a Saturday: Monday 21 Oct minus 23 days.
- Duff, quoted in the note to p. 70: "On Saturday night we were joined by Lord Nelson".
- https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/53/mode/1up and https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/70/mode/1up

**S0-2, the chain.**
- Nelson to Duff, 4 Oct 1805 (Nicolas VII, pp. 70–71): keep "with the Mars, Defence, and Colossus, from three to four leagues between the Fleet and Cadiz". The order goes on: distant signals when flag colours cannot be made out; "fire guns by day or night" if the enemy is out or coming out; one ship to windward, "or rather to the Eastward", to extend the range of sight; Blackwood to place a frigate west of Cádiz.
- Desbrière, *Trafalgar*, p. 161: on 19 Oct Blackwood warned Nelson "par la Phœbe, reliée au vaisseau Defence".
- https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/71/mode/1up ; https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805 (p. 161)

**S0-3, postponement.**
- Corr. XI, No. 9120, to Berthier, 5 fructidor XIII. The edition prints the date as "(23 mai 1805)"; it should be 23 August (checked on the page image). The text is on pp. 121–122: tell Marmont that if the Ferrol squadron arrives "je fais l'expédition", but if it is held "à Cadix, je l'ajourne à une autre année".
- Maude, p. 120, translates Berthier's actual letter with a different condition: if they "do not reach the Channel".
- https://archive.org/details/correspondancede11napouoft/page/121/mode/1up

**S0-4, Ulm.** See H2-12. Some modern pages date the surrender to 19 October, e.g. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitulation_of_Dornbirn; the period sources put the march-out on the 20th.

**S0-5, sortie.**
- Desbrière, *Trafalgar*, pp. 160–161: on the 19th Magon got out with Algésiras, Achille, Argonaute, Neptune, Héros, Duguay-Trouin and Bahama (7 of the line) and the frigate Rhin. Note 4 on p. 161 cites Euryalus's log: about 4 p.m., "9 voiles".
- Desbrière, p. 163: "Vers 11 heures du matin" on the 20th the fleet was "hors des passes".
- James III, p. 385: "12 ships only succeeded in getting out".

**S0-6, fleet sizes.**
- James III, p. 385: 33 sail of the line, five frigates and two brigs on the 20th; Terrible disarmed, San Fulgencio 64 "for some unknown reason detained".
- Clowes V, p. 128: 29 of the line by 13 Oct, plus five away with Louis; Prince of Wales sent home on the 14th and Donegal sent to Gibraltar on the 17th, leaving 27.
- https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/385/mode/1up ; https://archive.org/details/royalnavyhistory0005clow/page/128/mode/1up

### H2-01, the camps

- **(a) and (b).** Desbrière, *Projets et tentatives*, III (archive item projetsettentat00histgoog):
  - p. 179: Berthier's draft of 23 May 1803, 50,000 men in 5 divisions "sous la dénomination de « camp de Saint-Omer »".
  - p. 184: "L'ordre d'ensemble du 14 juin 1803 va être l'origine de la Grande Armée". The order, dated Saint-Cloud, 25 prairial an XI, lists the six camps: Hollande, Gand, Saint-Omer, Compiègne, Saint-Malo, Bayonne.
  - https://archive.org/details/projetsettentat00histgoog
- **(c).** Desbrière IV (projetsettentat02histgoog):
  - p. 460: Napoleon to Berthier, "Milan, le 25 floréal an XIII (15 mai 1805)". The army is to have an advance guard (Lannes), right (Davout), centre (Soult) and left (Ney) corps, a detached corps at Brest (Augereau), a reserve under Prince Louis (the light cavalry, Nansouty's heavy cavalry, the Italian division and two dragoon divisions), and the Guard. Desbrière's own line: "L'armée a reçu sa forme définitive à la date du 15 mai."
  - pp. 460–461: the war minister to Davout, Milan, 10 prairial XIII (30 May 1805), adds the detached corps at Utrecht and gives the camp names (Bruges, Saint-Omer, Montreuil).
  - https://archive.org/details/projetsettentat02histgoog
- **(d).** Maude, p. 121, writes "Army of England" in quotation marks; the 1805 orders use "armée des côtes de l'Océan" (Desbrière IV, pp. 460–461).

  - 4th, the right wing, under Daugier at Vimereux, for Lannes's grenadiers;
  - 5th, the Gallo-Batavian flotilla under Ver-Huell at Ambleteuse, for Davout;
  - 6th, the reserve under capitaine de frégate L'Évêque at Calais, for the Italian infantry and dragoons.

### H2-04, craft types

- Desbrière III, p. 90, figures in French feet:
  - prames: 110 × 25 × 8, "gréement de corvette", 3 keels, XII 24-pdrs, 38 seamen and 120 soldiers;
  - canonnières: 76–80 × 17 × 5–6, brig rig, III 24-pdrs and a 6–8 in. howitzer, 22 seamen and 130 soldiers;
  - bateaux: 60 × 14 × 4½, lugger rig, I 24-pdr and a howitzer or field gun, 6 seamen, 2 horses and 100 soldiers;
  - péniches: 60 × 10 × 3½, lugger rig, a 6 in. howitzer and a 4-pdr or an 8 in. mortar, 5 seamen and 66 soldiers;
  - caïques: ships' launches with one 24-pdr.
- James III, pp. 216–217: prame "rigged as a ship", 12 long 24-pdrs, 38 sailors, "upwards of 100 soldiers". "Of these prames ... 20, each with stalls for 50 horses, were ordered". Péniches were "rigged chiefly as schuyts", about 400.
- Desbrière III, p. 87 (programme of 5 July 1803): boats of the prame type to carry "12 canons de 24, 60 chevaux avec leurs cavaliers".
- https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/216/mode/1up

## Points the notes missed

1. **Napoleon's other "how long" figures:**
   - "huit heures de nuit" (No. 7309, 23 Nov 1803);
   - six hours of wind, fog and night (No. 8578, 13 Apr 1805);
   - **six hours of the sea on 9 June 1805** (No. 8871, Corr. X p. 506, quoted by James III p. 327);
   - "quatre ou cinq jours" (No. 8985, 16 Jul 1805);
   - "quinze jours" in the retrospective note of 13 Sep 1805 (No. 9209).

   The jump from hours to days came in July 1805, when the plan depended on the fleet.
2. **Napoleon knew of Cádiz by 31 August.** His postscript dated 13 fructidor (Corr. XI p. 157) says "Mon escadre est entrée à Cadix" and "Tout est parti". He had already weighed the Cádiz case on 22–23 August (Nos. 9112 and 9120; Desbrière p. 110). So the popular story that he turned east in rage on hearing of Cádiz (the Daru anecdote, James III App. 28) does not fit the chronology.
3. **The camps were hutted, not tented.**
   - Orders of 29 Aug 1803 for camps "en baraques en bois ... de manière à pouvoir être occupées tout l'hiver".
   - Davout, 15 Sep 1803: huts of fir poles roofed with rushes (Desbrière III pp. 206–207).
   - The Étaples huts had tiled roofs, glazed windows and gardens (napoleon.org newsletter 410; Inrap).
   - Tents ("effets de campement ... de quoi tenter 80,000 hommes") were sent from Metz to Strasbourg for the campaign (No. 9137, p. 142).
4. **The first troops to leave the coast** were the cavalry (orders of 24–25 Aug) and Oudinot's grenadiers (26 Aug). The three corps followed on 28, 30 and 31 Aug, with the remainder on 1–2 Sep.
5. **Rhine crossing dates by corps** (A&C II pp. 33–42): Lannes and Murat 25 Sep; Davout 26–29 Sep; Soult 26–30 Sep; Ney 27 Sep.
6. **The source of the modern "27,000" and "18 generals"** is Napoleon's 7th Bulletin of 19 October. The Austrian eyewitness and the 8th Bulletin both give 16 generals.
7. **Gaps in the 17 and 19 October details.**
   - The Austrian eyewitness timed the 17 October settlement at 2 p.m. (A&C: noon).
   - Napoleon's figure the same day was 15,000 (to Talleyrand) as well as 20,000 (to Murat).
   - Some modern summaries date the surrender to 19 October.
8. **Edition misprints:** Correspondance XI prints No. 9120 as "23 mai 1805" (the notes flag only No. 9113), and No. 9209 is "date présumée".
9. **Nelson's order to Duff of 4 October** (Nicolas VII, pp. 70–71) prescribes how the chain signalled: distant signals when flag colours could not be read, and "fire guns by day or night" when the enemy came out.

## Scene impact

1. **Date line, "the army leaves Boulogne": 28 August 1805.**
   - The first divisions of the three corps left that day; the order is dated 26 August.
   - The grenadiers and cavalry left from 26 August, the last columns on 1–2 September, and Napoleon himself on 2 September.
   - If one date is shown, 28 August is the best supported. "Late August" in the caption is right.
2. **Date line, "Ulm": 20 October 1805.**
   - The garrison marched out and laid down its arms from 2 p.m. The capitulation was signed on 17 October.
   - "Lays down its arms at Ulm" is more exact than "surrenders", but "surrenders" is defensible.
   - Do not use 19 October.
3. **Boulogne camps.** Draw rows of huts along camp streets (baraques), not the brief's "rows of tiny tents".
4. **Flotilla scale.** On 20 July 1805, Boulogne alone held about 1,104 craft (578 prames and gun craft, 526 transports) out of 2,293 in seven ports. An instanced crowd of several hundred small craft fits. The silhouettes are:
   - prames: ship-rigged, three masts;
   - chaloupes: brigs;
   - bateaux and péniches: luggers.
5. **Beat 1 caption ("command of the Channel").** Fine. Napoleon's own word was the Strait ("détroit", "Pas-de-Calais"). If a quotation is used, take it from No. 7832 (2 Jul 1804) or No. 9115 (22 Aug 1805), and do not add the "Tower of London" sentence.
6. **Beat 3 caption ("gives up on England").** Acceptable. Napoleon framed it as postponing "to another year" and left 19 battalions and the flotilla as a standing threat.
7. **The dashed march line.** Label it as the army, not as one corps. Possible dates beside it:
   - Boulogne, 28 Aug;
   - the Rhine, 25–30 Sep (Kehl, Speyer/Rheinhausen, Mannheim, Karlsruhe);
   - the Danube, 7 Oct (Donauwörth);
   - Ulm, 20 Oct.

   The route Boulogne–Metz–Bitche–Speyer–Heilbronn–Nördlingen–Donauwörth is Soult's; the Elchingen–Ulm leg is Ney's. At Ulm, Napoleon watched from a rise at the foot of the Michelsberg, north of the town.
8. **Signal chain** (section 0, beat 4).
   - From 4 October, ships of the line (Mars, Defence, Colossus) stood 3–4 leagues apart between the fleet and Cádiz, with frigates inshore.
   - Signals were flags by day, and guns "by day or night" when the enemy was coming out.
   - So the chain should flash and smoke ship by ship, day and night, with flags by day. Change the beat 4 caption from "chain of frigates" to "chain of ships".
9. **Glyphs at Cádiz** (section 0).
   - 19 Oct: 7 of the line plus 1–3 frigates out with Magon (Desbrière). James's "12" and Euryalus's "9 sail" are counts of sail.
   - 20 Oct: 33 of the line, 5 frigates and 2 brigs.
   - British: 27 of the line on 21 Oct (29 at the peak on 13 Oct).

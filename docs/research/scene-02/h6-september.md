# H6. Nelson's passage, Portsmouth to the fleet off Cádiz, 13–28 September 1805

Research for the chart's leg `nelson-september` (the owner's decision 6). The leg data is `nelson-september.json` beside this file, in the format of `research/scene-02/routes-draft.json`.

## 1. What the sources are

- **Nelson's private diary** ("the Memorandum Book"), printed in full from the manuscript at the Principal Probate Registry by G. Hudson, *Nelson's Last Diary* (1917). It has an entry for every day from 13 September, with winds, places, bearings and three noon latitudes. Nicolas (VII) and Beatty print only the entries of 14, 25, 28 and 29 September, and Beatty, followed by Nicolas, abridges the 28th. This is the backbone of the leg.
- **The Victory's log**, only as the extracts Nicolas prints in footnotes (VII 36, 46, 53): 14–15 September (flag, weighing), 20 September (Stirling, the Décade) and 28 September (joining; the 8 p.m. bearing of San Sebastián light). The captain's and master's logs themselves (The National Archives, ADM 51 and ADM 52) were not available in print and were not read.
- **Nelson's letters**, Nicolas VII 35–53: their datelines place him off Portland (16th), off Plymouth (17th), off the Lizard (18th), off Cape Finisterre (23rd), off Lisbon and the Rock of Lisbon (25th) and off Cape St Vincent (26th, 27th); the letter to Collingwood of the 25th places the Décade "27 leagues S.W. from Scilly".
- **Narratives**: Beatty (1807; 3rd ed. 1825), pp. 1–3; James III 379; Desbrière 1907, pp. 146–147; Corbett 1910, pp. 292, 317, 322; Fraser 1906, p. 22; Nicolas VII, "Analysis", pp. xxxi–xxxii.
- **Modern biographies** (Sugden 2012, Knight 2005, Adkins 2004, Goodwin) could not be read here; the Royal Museums Greenwich background page was the only modern account consulted beyond Corbett and Fraser.

## 2. The dated passage

Times are ship's (apparent) time as the sources give them. Bearings are by compass unless marked true; worked with gufm1 variation for September 1805 from the NOAA calculator: 24–25° W in the Channel, 25.1° W off Scilly, 23.6° W off Finisterre, 22.3° W off Lisbon, 21.7° W off Cape St Vincent, 21.2° W off Cádiz.

- **2 September.** Blackwood, who had landed at Lymington from the Euryalus and was posting to the Admiralty with the news, called at Merton in the early morning and told Nelson that the combined fleet had put into Cádiz; Nelson was ordered to resume the Mediterranean command (Nicolas VII, Analysis, p. xxxi; Corbett 1910, p. 278). High.
- **13 September.** He drove from Merton at half past ten at night (diary, Hudson p. 15; Nicolas VII 33). High.
- **14 September.** At Portsmouth (the George Inn) at about 6 a.m. The Victory lay at St Helens; the log has the flag rehoisted at 11.30 a.m.; Nelson wrote that he hoisted it "at this anchorage about noon" (to Marsden, Nicolas VII 36). He embarked from the beach by the bathing machines, with Rose and Canning, and got on board at 2 p.m. (diary from the manuscript, Hudson pp. 15–16; Beatty 1825, p. 95). Nicolas prints the diary as "embarked ... at two; got on board", making 2 the time he left the beach (VII 35). Only the Victory and the Euryalus sailed with him; the Royal Sovereign, Defiance and Agamemnon were not ready (VII 36). High.
- **15 September (Sunday).** "At day weighed with light airs Northerly. At 6 was obliged to anchor. At 8 weighed" (diary, Hudson p. 16). The log: 8 a.m. weighed and made sail to the SSE, the Euryalus in company (Nicolas VII 36). At sunset off Christchurch; light breezes and thick fog all night (diary). His orders of that day name the rendezvous off Cape St Vincent (VII 38). High.
- **16 September.** Foggy and light, then from noon a fresh westerly. Letter to Davison dated "Off Portland, Wind W.S.W.", "beating down Channel with a foul wind" (VII 38–39). In the evening four miles off Berry Head (diary); a letter went ashore that night in a Torbay boat (VII 40). High.
- **17 September.** Fresh WSW, "dead foul wind" (letter of 9 a.m., VII 40). At 9 abreast of Plymouth; he sent the Euryalus in to call out the Ajax and Thunderer and signalled the ships in Cawsand Bay; all night standing to the westward, wind SW to SSW (diary). High.
- **18 September.** Light breezes, heavy western swell, wind south; lay to for the Ajax and Thunderer, "Lizard North"; "At noon they joined"; made all sail (diary, Hudson p. 16). To Marsden, dated off the Lizard: the two ships got under way from Cawsand Bay "yesterday, on passing Plymouth" and "this morning joined me off the Lizard" (VII 45). To Lady Hamilton the same day: they "are joining", nearly calm (VII 40). High.
- **19 September.** Fresh, then hard gales at SW; at 6 p.m. hard rain and the wind at NW (diary, Hudson pp. 16–17). Desbrière: a violent WSW gale much delayed the run out of the Channel (p. 146). High.
- **20 September.** Moderate WSW, heavy sea. At 9 a.m. saw a squadron; at 11 passed Rear-Admiral Stirling's, five of the line and a frigate (diary; the log has signals exchanged and colours shown at 11). At 1.30 p.m. hove to; Rear-Admiral Sir Richard Bickerton, going home sick in the frigate Décade, came into the fleet; 2.20 filled and made sail (log, Nicolas VII 46). The diary has 2 p.m. for speaking the Décade, Beatty "at noon" (p. 1). Place: "27 leagues S.W. from Scilly", blowing very strong at SW (Nelson to Collingwood, 25 September, VII 49); Desbrière applies the same place to both meetings (p. 146). High for the times; medium for the place.
- **21–22 September.** Fresh NNW gales on the 21st, north and north-east at night; moderate NE with a heavy NW swell on the 22nd; at 1 p.m. a convoy of seven sail under a man-of-war in the SE quarter; at 6 the Euryalus signalled a vessel reconnoitring in the east; fresh gales east to ESE all night (diary, Hudson p. 17). No position. High for the weather.
- **23 September.** Fresh gales E by S; at 6 a.m. "abreast of Cape Finisterre 17 Lgs"; at noon moderate, latitude printed "42°: 2'5 [sic.] N" (diary, Hudson pp. 17–18). Letter to Castlereagh dated off Cape Finisterre (VII 46). Desbrière: "le 23 on doublait le cap Finistère" (p. 146). High for the date; medium for the 6 a.m. place; low for the noon latitude's reading.
- **24 September.** Moderate SE; noon latitude 40°05′ N; light southerly airs at 3 p.m., northerly in the evening, light NE all night (diary, Hudson p. 18). High.
- **25 September.** Light southerly airs; "Saw the Rock of Lisbon SSE 10 Leagues"; at sunset the captain of the Constance came on board and took his letters to Lisbon (diary). To Marsden at 5 p.m., "off the Rock of Lisbon": arrived that morning, calm all day, no progress; a NW breeze springing up, he hoped to round Cape St Vincent next day (VII 50). Letters to the consul Gambier and to Sutton of the Amphion asked them to keep his approach secret (VII 48). His letter to Collingwood sent by the Euryalus is dated the 25th (VII 49, page image read). High.
- **26 September.** Light NW airs all day; the Rock of Lisbon NNE 13–14 leagues; at 4 p.m. sent the Euryalus to Collingwood with orders to put himself under Nelson's command (diary, Hudson p. 18). Nelson dated a letter to Bunbury "off Cape St. Vincent" that day (VII 51). High for the date of sending; medium for the place.
- **27 September.** At daylight Cape St Vincent SE by S by compass, 6 leagues; a sloop five or six miles east proved to be the Nautilus, homeward bound with Collingwood's dispatches; Nelson ordered her to cruise off the cape for his reinforcements, with the rendezvous between Cape St Mary's and Cádiz (diary, Hudson pp. 18–19; orders, VII 51–53). At noon abreast of Lagos Bay, fresh NW breezes. At 1 a.m. (the 28th) brought to (diary, p. 19). High.
- **28 September (Saturday).** Fresh NNW breezes; at daylight bore up and made sail; at 9 saw the bomb Aetna cruising; at noon "saw nine sail of ships of war bearing East", latitude 36°32′ N; at one, eighteen sail; nearly calm; in the evening joined the fleet under Collingwood and saw the enemy's 35 or 36 of the line in Cádiz (diary from the manuscript, Hudson p. 19). Beatty, and Nicolas after him, print "At noon saw eighteen sail" and drop the latitude (Beatty 1825, pp. 95–96; Nicolas VII 53). The log: at 6 p.m., in studding sails, joined the fleet off Cádiz, 23 of the line and six inshore; at 8 San Sebastián's lighthouse E by N, five or six leagues (VII 53 n.). High for the times; medium for the position.

Notes on the readings:

- **Reckonings and sightings.** The project reads logs' bearings by compass unless they say "true". Scilly at 81 nm and Finisterre at 51 nm were out of sight, so those two places are reckonings from the day's work, which a log states in true bearings; they are drawn as true, with the compass reading given. The Rock of Lisbon (the Sintra hills, over 500 m, visible at 40 nm), Cape St Vincent ("by compass") and San Sebastián light are sightings and are worked by compass.
- **"Abreast."** Read as the cape on the beam of a ship running south, so due east of her.
- **Unknown distances.** Christchurch, Portland, Plymouth, the Lizard and Lagos are named without a distance; the offings are estimates of 5–9 nm, enough to signal Cawsand Bay or to round a cape.
- **The 28th.** At 8 p.m. the Victory was about 16.5 nm WSW of San Sebastián light (15–18 nm for five or six leagues). From noon (36°32′ N) she had made about 17 nm to the ESE in near calm, consistent with a fleet cruising some 15 miles west of Cádiz (James III 379; Beatty p. 3).

The coast check (Natural Earth 10m land and minor islands, by script, every 0.1 nm along the straight legs and 0.2 km along the chart's own centripetal Catmull–Rom curve in Mercator space; the Eddystone, the Manacles, Wolf Rock, the Seven Stones, the Berlengas and the Farilhões by hand):

- No point, straight segment or stretch of curve touches land.
- Only Spithead (1.2 nm) and St Helens road (0.7–0.8 nm, including the leg out of the road) come within 2 nm of the coast; both are anchorages, as the project's rule allows.

## 4. Where the sources disagree

- **The sailing day.** 15 September: the Victory's log, Nelson's diary, Beatty (p. 1), James (III 379), Desbrière (p. 146) and Nicolas's chronology. Corbett has Nelson go off to the ship early in the morning of the 14th and make the signal to weigh after Rose and Canning had dined (p. 292); the Royal Museums Greenwich background page says he left Portsmouth on HMS Victory on 14 September. The 14th is the day he embarked and rehoisted his flag; the ship weighed on the 15th.
- **The hour on the 14th.** The log rehoists the flag at 11.30 a.m., Nelson's letter "about noon". The diary's "at two" is the time he got on board in the manuscript (Hudson) and in Beatty, and the time he embarked from the beach in Nicolas. Corbett's "early in the morning" contradicts both.
- **The weighing on the 15th.** The diary: weighed at daylight, anchored again at 6, weighed at 8. The log: weighed at 8 a.m. They agree; the log does not mention the first attempt.
- **Ajax and Thunderer.** Joined off the Lizard on the 18th: "this morning" (Nelson to Marsden) or at noon (diary); Desbrière off the Lizard on the 18th. James and Beatty: off Plymouth on the 18th. They had left Cawsand Bay on the 17th as the Victory passed Plymouth.
- **The Décade.** 1.30–2.20 p.m. (log), 2 p.m. (diary), noon (Beatty).
- **Where, on the 20th.** "27 leagues S.W. from Scilly" is Nelson's; read as true or by compass, the two places lie 37 nm apart.
- **The noon latitude of 23 September.** Hudson prints "42°: 2'5 [sic.]". Read as 42°25′ N (Nelson writes two-figure minutes after a colon on the 24th, "40:05", and the 28th, "36:32"), the run from Finisterre at 6 a.m. is about 28 nm in six hours; read as 42°02½′ N, about 50 nm. Both are possible in a fresh gale that moderated by noon. The leg uses 42°25′ N.
- **Sending the Euryalus ahead.** The letter she carried is dated 25 September (Nicolas VII 49, page image read). The diary has her sent at 4 p.m. on the 26th, with the Rock of Lisbon still in sight; James gives the 26th; Beatty "the preceding day" before Cape St Vincent was seen on the 27th, which is the 26th; Desbrière implies off Lisbon; Corbett has the order sent from off Cape St Vincent (p. 317).
- **Off Cape St Vincent.** Nelson heads letters "off Cape St. Vincent" on the 26th and 27th, and Desbrière keeps him "sous le cap Saint-Vincent" on both days (p. 147), but on the 26th the diary has the Rock of Lisbon NNE 13–14 leagues, some 70 nm north of the cape. The cape was first seen at daylight on the 27th (diary; Beatty).
- **The noon of the 28th.** The manuscript has nine sail at noon in 36°32′ N and eighteen at 1 p.m.; Beatty and Nicolas print "At noon saw eighteen sail".
- **The weekday.** The manuscript heads the 28th "Saturday" (Hudson p. 19), as Beatty does; Nicolas prints "Sunday" for both the 28th and the 29th, a slip.
- **The day of arrival.** The evening of 28 September: the log, the diary, Nelson's letters, Duff, Codrington, Beatty, James, Corbett, Desbrière. Fraser has Nelson arrive on 29 September (1906, p. 22), the day he took command.
- **In sight or out of sight.** Nelson's order by the Euryalus was conditional: no salute and no colours "if you are in sight of Cadiz", and no salute even out of sight of land (VII 49). The joining itself was in sight: the Victory was some 16 nm from the light at 8 p.m., Nelson saw the combined fleet in the harbour, Beatty puts the city about fifteen miles off (p. 3), and the Cádiz signal stations reported a three-decker and two 74s joining Collingwood from the westward that evening (Corbett 1910, p. 322). The main body went out of sight, 16–18 leagues west, only after Nelson took command (James III 379; his letters of 4–5 October). The leg therefore ends where the log puts her, west-south-west of Cádiz and in sight of it.

## 6. Doubts

1. **The start at Spithead.** The Victory lay at Spithead and the Motherbank from 18 August and at St Helens by 14 September; when she moved is not in the sources read. The leg starts at the Ushant–Spithead line's end, dated 14 September 6 a.m. as Nelson's arrival at Portsmouth, so the line joins up; the chart could instead start the leg at St Helens.
2. **27 leagues SW of Scilly** is drawn as a true bearing; by compass the point moves 37 nm SE. The project's precedent for an unstated bearing (Cape Sicié, `routes-draft.json`) used the mean of the two readings; here the distance (81 nm) makes it a reckoning, which logs give true.
3. **The noon latitude of 23 September**: 42°25′ N (used) or 42°02½′ N.
4. **Offings and longitudes** are estimates everywhere except the bearings; the Channel points could move a few miles either way without changing the shape.
5. **The Victory's own logs** (captain's and master's) for 15–28 September were not read beyond Nicolas's extracts; they would give noon latitudes and longitudes for every day and could replace the estimates.
6. **Modern biographies** (Sugden, Knight, Adkins, Goodwin) were not consulted; the only modern sources read are Corbett, Fraser and the Royal Museums Greenwich page.
7. **The end point** lies in sight of Cádiz, not out of sight as the task text supposed (see section 4, "In sight or out of sight"). If the scene wants the moment of joining "unseen", that is a licence and should be recorded as one.
8. Noticed in passing: Nicolas's footnote to 29 September says Nelson "completed his forty-sixth year" (VII 53 n.); born on 29 September 1758, he turned 47, as the section says.

**A. "### Nelson off Cádiz": replace the bullet "The passage." with these five bullets.**

- **Recalled.** Early on 2 September Blackwood, posting from Lymington to the Admiralty, called at Merton with the news that the combined fleet was in Cádiz, and Nelson was ordered to resume the Mediterranean command (Corbett 1910, p. 278; Nicolas VII, Analysis, p. xxxi). Of the ships he chose, only the Victory and the frigate Euryalus were ready; the Royal Sovereign, Defiance and Agamemnon were left to follow (Nicolas VII 36; Desbrière 1907, p. 146). High.
- **Portsmouth and St Helens, 13–15 September.** Nelson left Merton at 10.30 p.m. on Friday 13 September and reached Portsmouth at about 6 a.m. The Victory, at St Helens, rehoisted his flag at 11.30 a.m. (log; "about noon" in his letter). He embarked from the beach by the bathing machines and was on board at 2 p.m. (his diary from the manuscript, Hudson 1917, pp. 15–16; Nicolas VII 35–36 reads "at two" as the time he left the beach). She weighed at daylight on Sunday 15 September in light northerly airs, anchored again at 6, and weighed at 8, making sail to the SSE with the Euryalus (diary; log, Nicolas VII 36). Beatty, James, Desbrière and Nicolas's chronology give the 15th. Corbett has the signal to weigh made on the 14th, after Rose and Canning had dined on board (p. 292), and the Royal Museums Greenwich gives the 14th for leaving Portsmouth: that is the day he embarked. High. Date line: 15 September 1805.
- **Down Channel, 15–18 September.** Against light airs, fog and then a foul westerly, he was off Christchurch at sunset on the 15th, off Portland on the 16th and four miles off Berry Head that evening. At 9 a.m. on the 17th, abreast of Plymouth, he sent the Euryalus in to call the Ajax and Thunderer out of Cawsand Bay (diary, Hudson 1917, p. 16; Nicolas VII 38–40). On the 18th he lay to with the Lizard north, and the two 74s joined: in the morning by his letter to Marsden, at noon by his diary (Nicolas VII 45; Hudson p. 16). Desbrière also has the Lizard; James and Beatty say off Plymouth. High.
- **Across the Bay, 19–23 September.** A south-westerly gale on the 19th held him in the mouth of the Channel. On the 20th, "27 leagues S.W. from Scilly" by his letter of the 25th, he passed Rear-Admiral Stirling's five of the line at 11 a.m. and hove to from 1.30 to 2.20 p.m. for the frigate Décade, taking Bickerton home sick (Victory's log, Nicolas VII 46, 49; Desbrière 1907, p. 146; 2 p.m. by the diary, noon by Beatty). Northerly and then easterly gales carried him south, and at 6 a.m. on the 23rd he was "abreast of Cape Finisterre 17 Lgs" (diary, Hudson p. 17). High for the dates, medium for the places.
- **Down the coast of Portugal, 23–27 September.** The diary's noon latitudes are 42°25′ N on the 23rd (printed "42°: 2′5 [sic.]", which could also be 42°02½′) and 40°05′ N on the 24th (Hudson pp. 17–18). He made the Rock of Lisbon SSE ten leagues on the morning of the 25th and lay becalmed off it all day, asking the consul and the Amphion at Lisbon to keep his approach secret (Nicolas VII 48–51). On the 26th, with the Rock NNE 13–14 leagues, he sent the Euryalus ahead at 4 p.m. with a letter dated the 25th. Collingwood was to put himself under Nelson's command and, if in sight of Cádiz, to fire no salute and hoist no colours when Nelson arrived (diary; Nicolas VII 49; James III 379). Corbett has the Euryalus sent from off Cape St Vincent (p. 317). At daylight on the 27th Cape St Vincent bore SE by S by compass, six leagues. There he stopped the sloop Nautilus, homeward bound with Collingwood's dispatches, and left her off the cape to send his reinforcements to the rendezvous between Cape St Mary's and Cádiz; at noon he was abreast of Lagos Bay (diary, Hudson pp. 18–19; Nicolas VII 51–53). Nelson heads letters "off Cape St. Vincent" on the 26th and 27th, and Desbrière keeps him there both days (p. 147), but the cape came in sight only on the 27th. High for the dates, medium for the positions.

**B. "### Nelson off Cádiz": replace the bullet "Joining." with this one.**

- **Joining.** After lying to from 1 a.m., he bore up at daylight on Saturday 28 September with a fresh NNW breeze. At noon, in 36°32′ N, he saw nine ships of war to the east, and eighteen by 1 p.m. (the diary from the manuscript, Hudson 1917, p. 19; Beatty and Nicolas print only "At noon saw eighteen sail"). In near calm, under studding sails, the Victory joined Collingwood at about 6 p.m.; at 8 San Sebastián's light bore E by N, five or six leagues (Victory's log, Nicolas VII 53 n.), about 36°23′ N 6°36′ W by compass, 16½ nm WSW of the light. The joining was in sight of Cádiz: Nelson saw the combined fleet in the harbour, Beatty puts the city about fifteen miles off (p. 3), and the Cádiz signal stations reported a three-decker and two 74s joining from the westward that evening (Corbett 1910, p. 322). He could not communicate until morning and took command on the 29th, his forty-seventh birthday (his letters, Nicolas VII 60, 62–63; Duff, VII 70 n.; Codrington 1873, I 51). The main body went out of sight, 16–18 leagues west, only after he took command (see "The station"). Nicolas heads both 28 and 29 September "Sunday", a slip; the manuscript has "Saturday" for the 28th. Fraser has him arrive on the 29th (1906, p. 22). High; medium for the position. Date line: 28 September 1805.

- **Nelson, Portsmouth to Cádiz (table K).** Nelson's diary from the manuscript (Hudson 1917, pp. 15–19), with the Victory's log extracts and his letters (Nicolas VII 35–53). Twenty-two points run from Spithead, where the Ushant line ends, to the Victory's position at 8 p.m. on 28 September. Sixteen rest on the sources' own places: St Helens road; off Christchurch, Portland, Berry Head (four miles), Plymouth and Lagos Bay; the noon latitudes of 23, 24 and 28 September; and seven bearings: the Lizard north, Scilly NE 27 leagues, Cape Finisterre abeam at 17 leagues, the Rock of Lisbon SSE 10 and NNE 13–14 leagues, Cape St Vincent SE by S 6 leagues, and San Sebastián light E by N 5–6 leagues. The two reckonings from land out of sight, Scilly and Finisterre, are read as true; the sightings are read by compass with gufm1's 21–25° W. By compass the Scilly point would lie 37 nm to the south-east, and the noon latitude of the 23rd may be 42°02½′ rather than 42°25′. Longitudes are estimated except where a bearing gives them, and so are the offings where a place is named without a distance. Six estimated points carry the line round the Isle of Wight, Start Point, the Eddystone and Cape St Vincent. Between the Décade and Finisterre (20–23 September) no position exists, and the line runs straight. Checked like the others: no point, segment or drawn curve touches land, and all keep 2 nm or more off the coast except at Spithead and St Helens road. Closest are the Isle of Wight at 2.4 nm, the Eddystone 2.8, the coast by Dartmouth 2.9 (curve), Bolt Head 3.0, Start Point 3.1, Sagres 3.7, Cape St Vincent 6.1 and Cabo da Roca 12.2. The line ends where the Victory joined, west-south-west of Cádiz and in sight of it, not at the fleet's later station (`research/scene-02/h6-september.md`). **F. "### Open doubts": add.**

- **Nelson's September line.** Two readings remain: "27 leagues S.W. from Scilly" as a true bearing (drawn) or by compass, 37 nm apart; and the noon latitude of 23 September, 42°25′ N (drawn) or 42°02½′ N. The Victory's own logs at The National Archives, not read beyond Nicolas's extracts, would settle both and give a longitude for every noon.

# Review: facts and sources, the campaign to Cádiz (session 32)

An independent review of the draft `session-17/history-scene-02-wip.md` (branch `s23-history` at a970ab1), through one lens: facts and sources for the campaign to Cádiz. Written on 14 November 2026. The draft was read, not edited.

The sections reviewed are "Where the sources changed the brief", "Dates and conventions", "Toulon to the West Indies", "Nelson's pursuit", "Cape Finisterre, 22 and 23 July", "Ferrol, the turn south and Cádiz" and "Fleet sizes at each stage", including "The strengths lettered at the tracks' heads" and "The fleets in the close views".

**Method.** The claims were checked against the research notes (`h1-campaign.md`, `a-h1ac.md`, `a-h1b.md`, `v-h1b.md`, `v-h1c.md`, `h2-boulogne-cadiz.md`, `a-h2a.md`, `a-h2b.md`, `session-21/strengths-by-date.md`, `session-32/army-march.md`). Where the claim matters, they were also checked in the period books, using the archive.org full texts with their page indexes. The books are Desbrière 1907 with its annexes, Corbett 1910, Nicolas VI and VII, James III (1886), Beatty 1807 and 1825, Hudson 1917, Hubback 1906, Clowes V and the *Naval Chronicle* XIV and XV. The downloads stay outside the repository. About 130 concrete claims were checked: dates, hours, numbers, names, positions and pages. Most of them hold. Below are the ones that do not, most serious first. The unchecked claims are listed at the end.

## Findings

### 1. Wrong: the memorandum's "thirty-four sail"
- **Section.** "Fleet sizes at each stage", "What 'sail' counted then".
- **Claim.** "Nelson's memorandum of 9 October gives the enemy as 'thirty-four sail' (Nicolas VII, pp. 89–92)."
- **Problem.** The memorandum supposes an enemy of 46 sail of the line against a British 40. That figure is in Nelson's margin and in his deleted line, VII 90 n. "The remainder of the Enemy's Fleet, 34 Sail" on VII 91 is what is left after twelve are cut off. It is not the enemy's strength, and the words are not "thirty-four sail".
- **Fix.** Use the margin note instead: "the Enemy's Fleet is supposed to consist of 46 Sail of the Line, British Fleet of 40" (VII 90 n.). Or use Nelson to Lady Hamilton on 19 October: "forty Sail of Ships of War, which I suppose to be thirty-four of the Line, and six Frigates" (VII 132). That letter shows both uses of the word in one sentence.

### 2. Missing: Desbrière's own corrected British total, 26
- **Sections.** "Ferrol, the turn south and Cádiz", "After."; "Fleet sizes", "Not lettered: the British off Cádiz".
- **Claim.** "Desbrière's total of 32 (p. 109) counts the Minotaur, Mars and Bellerophon twice; without the double count his lists give 29…". "About 26 … (James III 379; Corbett 1910, p. 267)".
- **Problem.** On p. 143 Desbrière says that neither the Dragon nor the Goliath joined. On p. 145 he says that Calder's 18 brought the blockading squadron to 26 of the line on 30 August. The same page has 24 or 25 of the line, 7 of them three-deckers, seen from the San Sebastián tower. The draft reports only his early 32 (p. 109), as if that were his figure. Neither James III 379 nor Corbett p. 267 states 26: both give the parts (Bickerton's four and Calder's 18), and the 26 is our sum.
- **Fix.** Cite "26 (Desbrière 1907, p. 145; the sum of James III 379; Allemand's intelligence, p. 108)". Describe p. 109's 32 as his first count, which he corrects on pp. 143–145.

### 3. Missing: a fourth British position for the Finisterre action
- **Section.** "Cape Finisterre, 22 and 23 July", "Where".
- **Claim.** "There are three British noon positions: 43°30′ N 11°17′ W in Calder's dispatch …, 43°54′ N 11°38′ W in James, and 44°17′ N 11°52′ W at 11.30 a.m. in Desbrière."
- **Problem.** The Windsor Castle's minutes of the action are printed in the *Naval Chronicle* XIV, p. 168, after Calder's dispatch. They are headed with the ship's position, 44°10′ N 11°22′ W, with Cape Finisterre SE 112 miles. No hour is given. That is a fourth British position. It lies near James's, and its 112 miles agrees with the scene's 113 nm. It has not been weighed in the notes either: `a-h1ac.md` H1-26 does not have it.
- **Fix.** Add it, with "hour not stated". Check whether it shifts the box (43°20′–43°55′ N), given that the allies were about 16 miles SSW of the British.

### 4. Unsupported: "which no source shows" (the Africa before the Prince of Wales)
- **Section.** "Fleet sizes", the British fleet by date: "The peak was 28. Sources that differ".
- **Claim.** James and Clowes give a peak of 29, "which needs the Africa in before the Prince of Wales sailed, which no source shows".
- **Problem.** Beatty's narrative names the Africa among the ships that joined "between the 7th and the 13th" (1825, pp. 4–5; the same in 1807). It then has the Prince of Wales parting "On the 13th in the evening" (1825, pp. 5–6). James III 383 and Clowes V 128 put her joining between the 9th and the 13th. So three narratives imply the order the draft says no source shows. The case against them is the Victory's log, which enters the parting first under the nautical 14th (VII 121 n.). Nicolas has "joined on the 14th" (VII 94 n.), and the diary of the 13th does not name her (Hudson p. 21).
- **Fix.** "…which only the narratives' loose groupings imply (James, Clowes; Beatty 1825, pp. 4–5), against the order in the Victory's log". Add Beatty to the "Sources that differ".

### 5. Missing: Austen's log on Collingwood's strength on 30 August
- **Sections.** "Fleet sizes", Collingwood and "Not lettered"; "Ferrol…", "20 August".
- **Claim.** Collingwood has 8 from 22 August (James III 379; Desbrière p. 143). The only other version given is Codrington's "six or seven".
- **Problem.** Francis Austen's log for 30 August has Calder's ships joining "Vice-Admiral Collingwood's squadron of five sail of the line" (Hubback 1906, p. 142). It is the same page the draft cites for the Leviathan in the inshore squadron. It is a third version, and it agrees with Codrington that not all of Bickerton's four were with Collingwood that day. The same page also has two more readings. It has Calder sailing south on 17 August with "nineteen sail of the line" (18 elsewhere). And it has the Euryalus reporting the combined fleet "anchored in Cadiz on the 21st". That is very likely a log date, but it is one more source for the 21st.
- **Fix.** Add Austen's "five" beside Codrington under Collingwood, and say that this bears on "About 26". In "20 August", add Austen's log to the sources for the 21st, as a probable nautical date.

### 6. Wrong attribution: "at about 12.30 on the 10th"
- **Section.** "Fleet sizes", Villeneuve, "26 from about 2 p.m. on 10 August".
- **Claim.** "…at about 12.30 on the 10th Gourdon's five … and eight Spaniards came out of the ría of Ferrol … (Desbrière 1907, p. 97)."
- **Problem.** Desbrière p. 97 gives no hour for the sortie. He has only the wind dying "vers midi" before the last three Spaniards cleared the narrows. The 12.30 is Escaño's (annexes p. 7), and the research note says so too (`strengths-by-date.md`, row 17). Escaño's entry is headed "Días del 10 al 13". It has nine Spanish ships of the line coming out with the five French, and it does not name the day.
- **Fix.** "…came out of the ría of Ferrol about noon (Desbrière 1907, p. 97); Escaño's journal has them out at 12.30, with nine Spaniards, in an entry for the 10th to the 13th (annexes p. 7)".

### 7. Wrong: "four of Ferrol's six French 80s are drawn as 74s"
- **Section.** "Fleet sizes", "Drawn with other types, not counted wrong".
- **Problem.** The Ferrol fleet's six 80s were four French (Bucentaure, Formidable, Neptune, Indomptable) and two Spanish (Argonauta, Neptuno). Corbett p. 239 has "Six were 80-gun ships"; James III 373–374 gives the names. In `squadrons.ts` the four French 80s are drawn as `french-80` from Toulon onwards. The Spanish 80s, the San Rafael among them, are drawn as `spanish-74`. In the October close views (`fleets.ts`) the Spanish 80s and the San Leandro 64 have their own rigs (`spanish80`, `spanish64`). So "(they show only in the close views)" also seems to point at the wrong views for the Spanish ships.
- **Fix.** "…Spanish 80s (the San Rafael, the Argonauta and the Neptuno) and 64s are drawn as 74s in the squadron blocks". Drop "four of Ferrol's six French 80s". The lead should state which views each mismatch shows in.

### 8. Wrong: the Phoebe "some six leagues WSW of Toro"
- **Section.** "Nelson's pursuit", "The news".
- **Problem.** The Victory's log for 4 April has "Toro bore E. ¾ N. distance 6 leagues" (Nicolas VI 393). That puts the squadron W¾S of Toro. Desbrière p. 11 has "6 lieues O. 3/4 S. du Toro" at 10.20 a.m.
- **Fix.** "some six leagues W¾S of Toro".

### 9. Missing hour: "13 from 2 August, noon taken"
- **Section.** "Fleet sizes", Villeneuve.
- **Problem.** The draft takes noon because "no hour is recorded". But Escaño's journal (Desbrière annexes p. 7) has the Argonauta and the Terrible anchoring at Ferrol at 9 a.m. on 2 August. It says the French had been meant to follow and went to A Coruña instead. So the split was over by 9 a.m.
- **Fix.** Take 9 a.m., citing Escaño. Or keep noon and give the reason.

### 10. Confidence above the evidence: "29 from about 9 a.m. on 13 August … High"
- **Section.** "Fleet sizes", Villeneuve.
- **Problem.** The 9 a.m. is Escaño's hour for the fleet sailing from the ría of Ares (annexes p. 7). Desbrière has the last three Spaniards joining "avant midi" and the whole fleet under sail only at 2 p.m. (p. 98). Both are cited, but the single "High" also covers the hour.
- **Fix.** "High for 29 and the day; medium for the hour".

### 11. Disagreement picked silently: east "from the 19th"
- **Section.** "Cape Finisterre", "Corvo to Finisterre".
- **Claim.** "…driven back west for four days, and from the 19th ran east… (Corbett 1910, p. 195; Desbrière 1907, p. 57)".
- **Problem.** The 19th is Reille's 30 messidor (Desbrière p. 57). Corbett, cited for the same sentence, says the fleet could not head east again until the 17th (p. 195).
- **Fix.** "from the 19th by Reille (the 17th by Corbett)".

### 12. Understated: "some 450–500 nm away"
- **Section.** "Ferrol…", "20 August" (Troude's 17 August).
- **Problem.** The ruling it comes from says "at least 450–500 nm" (`a-h1b.md`, line 154), and the draft drops "at least". From the scene's own bend (43°45′ N 12°20′ W) to Cádiz is about 514 nm in a straight line, and about 565 nm by Cape St Vincent. Corbett reckons 550 miles from the latitude of Ferrol (p. 261 n.). The argument stands. The figure is low.
- **Fix.** "at least 500 nm (Corbett: 550 miles)".

### 13. Imprecise: "the Barbadoes and Netley under Harward by James"
- **Section.** "Toulon to the West Indies", "North through the islands".
- **Problem.** James III 351 has the frigate Barbadoes under Captain Joseph Nourse and the schooner Netley under Lieutenant Richard Harward. Corbett p. 166 n. says there is no authority for Harward, nor for Nourse being there.
- **Fix.** "the Barbadoes (Nourse) and the Netley (Harward) by James (III 351), which Corbett rejects (p. 166 n.)".

### 14. Disagreement picked silently: "13 for two days"
- **Section.** "Fleet sizes", Villeneuve, "14 from about 2 a.m. on 10 April".
- **Problem.** Two days is Reille's version: the América rejoined on the 12th (p. 11). Villeneuve's report has her back "le lendemain, au jour", the 11th (p. 10). Both are cited in the same bullet, but the conclusion follows only Reille.
- **Fix.** "13 for one or two days".

### 15. Disagreement not noted: the Tonnant with Bickerton or with Calder
- **Section.** "Fleet sizes", Collingwood.
- **Problem.** Desbrière p. 143 has Bickerton's four as the Queen, Tonnant, Minotaur and Bellerophon, and the draft follows it. His p. 109 n., after Leyland, has them as the Queen, Minotaur, Mars and Bellerophon, and lists the Tonnant among Calder's ships. The draft does not say that the two pages disagree. The count is not affected.
- **Fix.** Add a note: "p. 109 n. has the Mars for the Tonnant, and the Tonnant with Calder".

### 16. Inconsistent within the section: "always agree" against "counted … and not drawn"
- **Section.** "Fleet sizes", the opening paragraph and "What the chart draws for these figures".
- **Problem.** The opening says that the figure and the ships drawn at a track's head "always agree". A later paragraph says the seven British ships not at Trafalgar are counted in the label and not drawn. It explains that the October British fleet's ships of the line are not drawn one by one before the Donegal leaves. A reader meets the absolute claim first.
- **Fix.** Qualify the opening sentence: "…always agree where the ships are drawn; off Cádiz in October the British label runs ahead of the drawing (see below)".

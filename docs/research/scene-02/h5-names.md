# H5: the 1805 English forms of the names scene 2's chart adds now

Research notes, written 5 November 2026 (session 12) by the names researcher. The rule is the one settled in `docs/research/scene-02/a-h4.md` as amended by `a-v-a-h4.md`:

1. Where the English charts and maps of 1800–1807 agree, letter their form even if it looks odd today.
2. Where they disagree, drop any form a modern visitor would misread (as a misprint, another place or something else); spell out an abbreviation that would puzzle.
3. Among the forms left, the naval and printed usage of 1805 decides (Nelson, the logs, the *Naval Chronicle*, Norie, Malham, the chartsellers' titles, the *Annual Register*).
4. Faden's chart of 1807 breaks any tie that remains.

Type: ordinary characters only (a raised "St" becomes "St."), no accents, capes as "C.", spaced capitals only for oceans and seas.

Status: complete (51 names). The evidence for each is below the table, beat by beat.

## Settled forms

Sites are decimal degrees (latitude N, longitude E; west is negative): a town's site, a cape's tip, an island's or group's centre, or a label centre for waters and rivers. "Conf." is confidence that the form is the right English chart form of about 1805 under the rule.

### What differs from `a-h4.md`'s further-names table

- **Scilly Islands**, not "Scilly Isles": the engraved Iˢ does not decide, and the usage of 1805 has only "Islands" (with the one engraved spelling, LWBC's view title).
- **Lizard**: the form stands, but its ground changes. Cary's *England* (1807) engraves LIZARD POINT, so the charts disagree; the bare form wins on usage ("the Lizard") and on Faden.
- **Straits of Dover**: the form stands; it was engraved in full on four English plates of 1794–1809, not only abbreviated.
- New forms with no row there: C. Grisnez, Ambleteuse, Vimereux, Etaples, The Downs, Deal, Salvages, Spithead, St. Helens Road, Metz, Spire, Heilbronn, Nordlingen, Donawerth, Augsburg, Rhine.
- Odd-looking forms kept under the rule, for the doubts: Spire, Vimereux, Donawerth (and the settled Carthagena and Guadaloupe).

## Doubts for `history-notes.md`

1. **Vimereux.** The chart letters Vimereux, as the *Naval Chronicle* (nine times in 1805, against one "Wimereux") and both *Annual Registers* of 1805 spell the flotilla harbour north of Boulogne. No English chart of 1794–1809 that I could read letters it: the harbour was dug in 1803–04, and the older charts name only Wimille and the Pointe aux Oies.
2. **Spire.** The chart letters Spire for Speyer, as Cary (1799, twice), Wilkinson (1802) and Brookes's gazetteer (1807) do; the town was then French. The English accounts of the 1805 campaign write Spires.
3. **Heilbronn.** The chart letters Heilbronn, as three English maps of 1799–1802 do. The printed accounts of 1805 spell it three other ways: Heilbron (Brookes), Hailbron and Heilbrun (the two *Annual Registers*).
4. **Donawerth.** Donawerth: Arrowsmith 1804, Cary 1799 and 1804, the Rivington *Annual Register* and the *New Annual Register*. Donauwerth: Cary's *Swabia* (1799). Donawert: the Otridge *Annual Register* and Brookes.
5. **C. Grisnez.** C. Grisnez: Laurie & Whittle's *British Channel* (1794), Stockdale (1809), the *Naval Chronicle* of 1804–05. Cape Griznez: Malham (1795) and two accounts of 1805–06. Cape Gris ness: Norie (1805).
6. **Scilly Islands.** The engravers wrote Scilly Iˢ (Faden 1807; Laurie & Whittle 1794). Malham, Norie and the one engraved spelling (Laurie & Whittle's coastal view) give Scilly Islands; "Scilly Isles" does not occur in the print searched. a-h4 had set Scilly Isles.
7. **Lizard.** Lizard: Faden 1807 and Cary 1804. LIZARD POINT: Cary's *England* 1807. Lizard Pᵗ: Laurie & Whittle 1794. Sailors said "the Lizard" (Nelson, 18 September 1805: "off the Lizard").
8. **St. Helens Road.** Cary 1801 and 1807 name the roadstead St. Helens Road; Laurie & Whittle (1794) set "St. Hellens" in it; Nelson dated his letters of 14 September 1805 "at St. Helens"; the *Naval Chronicle* wrote "St. Helen's".
9. **Montserrat.** Montserrat: Faden 1807 and the *Victory*'s log. Monserrat: Cary 1803 and Arrowsmith 1803. Nelson wrote both in June 1805.
10. **Munich.** Munich: Cary's *Europe* 1804 and all the prose of 1805. MUNCHEN: Arrowsmith 1804, and Cary 1799 with MUNICH under it.
11. **The Downs.** The chart sets the article, as every chart that names the anchorage does (Laurie & Whittle 1794, the Ordnance Survey of Kent 1801, Cary 1807), against the chart's no-article convention for oceans and the Channel.
12. **Etaples.** Etaples on three charts of 1794–1807; the older Estaples in the *Naval Chronicle* (1805) and Malham; Cary's *England* (1807) has the slip "Etables".

## Limits

- **Wimereux** is the weakest ruling. Faden's 1805 plate of the Pas de Calais coast "with the new works … for the defence of Boulogne" is the likeliest English chart to letter the harbour, and I could not see it (only a dealer's listing, which refused my requests). Gallica and the Library of Congress answered with challenge pages, so their English charts were out of reach.
- **Not re-read by me**, resting on h4, a-h4 and a-v-a-h4: Tetuan on Heather 1800 and LW94; Lagos on LW94; Palermo, Sardinia and Plymouth on F07; Vigo and Ushant on LW94; Caribbean Sea on C03W and LW94; Fort Royal and Monserrat on A03W; Danube on C99S; MUNICH under MUNCHEN on C99G.

# Fact-check of track H2: Nelson off Cádiz, the station, the chain (H2-14 to H2-24, H2-37)

Checked file: `81d46a93-.../scratchpad/research/h2-boulogne-cadiz.md`, section 4 (lines 226–295), section 5 (lines 296–379) and section 9.3 (lines 609–635). The notes file was not edited.

## Method

- I downloaded the archive.org full texts of every period book the notes cite: Nicolas VII, Beatty (1825), James III (1886), Corbett (1910), NRS *Logs* II, Desbrière (1907) and Clowes V, plus Mahan from Gutenberg. I located each cited passage by the printed running heads.
- For the four places where the result depends on a single glyph or word, I opened the page images:
  - NRS *Logs* II, pp. 145–146 (fractions in the Euryalus bearings);
  - Nicolas VII, p. 53 ("Sunday");
  - Beatty, p. 97 ("E.S.E." against "E.N.E.").
- Independent checks came from period sources the notes do not use:
  - Francis Austen's letters from the Canopus (Hubback, *Jane Austen's Sailor Brothers*, 1906);
  - Codrington's letters from the Orion (*Memoir of Admiral Sir Edward Codrington*, vol. I, 1873);
  - the 19–20 October logs of Sirius, Naiad, Phoebe, Mars and Colossus (NRS *Logs* II);
  - the Order of Battle and Sailing (Nicolas VII, pp. 93–94);
  - the 1913 Admiralty Trafalgar Committee on log reckoning, as quoted by the Naval Marine Archive.
- Positions were re-computed by plane sailing, with San Sebastián at 36.528 N, 6.316 W.

## Evidence

### H2-14 Departure

- Merton, Portsmouth and embarkation.
  - Diary "Friday Night, 13th September. At half-past ten drove from dear dear Merton" (Nicolas VII p. 33).
  - "Saturday, September 14th … At six o'clock arrived at Portsmouth … embarked at the Bathing Machines … at two" (p. 35).
  - https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/35/mode/1up
  - Beatty p. 95 has the same entry: https://archive.org/details/authenticnarrati00beat/page/95/mode/1up
- Victory's log, p. 36. James III p. 379 says she sailed "on the morning of the 15th, accompanied by the Euryalus". https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/379/mode/1up
- Ajax and Thunderer.
  - Nelson to Marsden, "off the Lizard, 18th September": the two ships "yesterday, on passing Plymouth … got under weigh from Cawsand Bay, and this morning joined me off the Lizard" (Nicolas VII p. 45).
  - To Lady Hamilton on the 18th: "The Ajax and Thunderer are joining" (p. 41).
  - James and Beatty (p. 1) give the 18th; Desbrière p. 146 gives "dès le 18 … devant le cap Lizard".
- Euryalus sent ahead. James III p. 379 gives the 26th. Beatty pp. 2–3 says "on the preceding day", Cape St Vincent having been seen on the 27th. Corbett p. 317 says the order went "from this point", off Cape St Vincent.

### H2-15 Joining

- Victory's log and signal log (Nicolas VII p. 53 n.): "28th September, P.M. At 6, in steering-sails, joined the Fleet off Cadiz …" I viewed the page image of p. 53.
- Diary: Beatty pp. 95–96, "Saturday, Sept. 28th"; Nicolas p. 53, "Sunday, September 28th" and "Sunday, September 29th".
- Letters.
  - Lady Hamilton, 1 Oct: "late on the evening of the 28th of September, but could not communicate with them until the next morning" (p. 60).
  - Marsden, 2 Oct: "the evening of the 28th ult. … on the morning following I took the Command" (pp. 62–63).
  - Duff: "On Saturday night we were joined by Lord Nelson, with the Victory, Ajax, Thunderer, and the Euryalus" (p. 70 n.).
- Independent sources.
  - Codrington, letter of 29 Sep (Memoir I p. 51): Nelson "joined us too late yesterday evening for communication"; Codrington waited on him "this morning". https://archive.org/details/cu31924087998500/page/51/mode/1up
  - Desbrière p. 147: "le 28 septembre à 6 heures du soir", and he could communicate only "le lendemain matin". https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805
  - Corbett p. 322: the Cádiz signal stations reported a three-decker and two 74s joining that evening, and Villeneuve wrote "That makes thirty-one".
- Mahan: "At 6 P.M. of Saturday, September 28th". He calls the next day the 47th birthday. https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/16915
- The log entry is dated by the civil day, although the October entries are nautical (see H2-17b). The joining date rests on the letters, Duff and Codrington.

### H2-16 Strength

- The log says 23 + 6 (above). Nelson to Acton, 30 Sep: "I have twenty-three Sail of the Line; and six occasionally at Gibraltar, and to have an eye upon the Ships at Carthagena" (Nicolas VII p. 54).
- James III p. 379: "27 sail of the line, 22 of which cruised about 15 miles off Cadiz, while the remaining five, under Rear-admiral Louis".
- Beatty p. 3: "twenty-seven ships of the line, including the Victory, Ajax, and Thunderer … Admiral Louis, with five or six ships". https://archive.org/details/authenticnarrati00beat/page/3/mode/1up
- Mahan: "twenty-nine of the line … six ships close in with the port".
- Desbrière p. 147 says "23 vaisseaux, six autres étaient détachés vers Gibraltar, pour se reposer et en même temps observer Carthagène". That is the Acton letter, not the log.
- Ship tally.
  - The Order of Battle and Sailing (Nicolas VII pp. 93–94) lists 33 ships. That includes Superb, which never came, and excludes Belleisle and Africa, so 34 distinct ships of the line served under Nelson off Cádiz.
  - Not yet arrived on 28 Sep: Royal Sovereign, Defiance, Belleisle, Agamemnon and Africa.
  - That leaves at most 29 present, including Victory, Ajax and Thunderer, so the log's 23 + 6 cannot exclude them.
  - Desbrière p. 145: Collingwood had 26 after Calder brought 18 on 30 Aug.

### H2-17 Louis

- Order: Nelson to Marsden, 2 Oct, "send Rear-Admiral Louis with six Sail of the Line immediately to Gibraltar and Tetuan" (Nicolas VII p. 63). Corbett p. 327 also gives the 2nd.
- Departure.
  - To Ford, 2 Oct: "will leave the Fleet this day" (p. 66).
  - Log: "October 3rd. P.M. at 6, Queen, Canopus, Spencer, Zealous, Tigre, and Endymion, parted" (p. 63 n.).
  - Francis Austen, Canopus, letter of 15 Oct: he had written on the 2nd, "We sailed that evening with four other ships of the line, a frigate, and five merchant vessels under convoy" (Hubback p. 148). https://archive.org/details/janeaustenssailo00hubbuoft/page/148/mode/1up
- Log reckoning. The Admiralty circular of 11 October 1805 ordered the civil day. It "reached the Mediterranean Fleet too late for Trafalgar, so that the Trafalgar logs are in the old form" (Admiralty Trafalgar Committee, 1913, quoted by the Naval Marine Archive). Nelson's diary was kept in civil time. https://navalmarinearchive.com/research/docs/nautical_time_and_date.html
- Turning back.
  - Austen: "on the following morning fell in with the Euryalus … induce Admiral …" (pp. 148–149).
  - James III p. 380: "on the 3rd, to return".
  - Beatty p. 4: "departed the next day [2 Oct] … On the 4th he rejoined".
  - Nelson to Blackwood, 4 Oct: "I have received from Rear-Admiral Louis, your information". https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/73/mode/1up
- Ratings.
  - Clowes V: "Tigre, 74". Corbett's list: "Tigre 74". Corbett p. 326: the inshore squadron was "the Queen 98, the Canopus 80, and three seventy-fours".
  - Royal Museums Greenwich plans: "fitted as 74-gun Third Rate". https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-81018
- Tetuan and the convoy.
  - Austen: Gibraltar on the 9th; Tetuan on the evening of the 12th; "sailed again last night" (letter dated 15 Oct). Under 18 Oct: "The wind on the evening of the 15th came to the westward …". Then "Our force consists of five sail of the line and three frigates" (pp. 149–152). Under 21 Oct: he parted from the convoy on hearing that the enemy was out on the 19th (p. 153).
  - Corbett pp. 331 n. and 335: the order reached him at Tetuan on the 17th; he was 200 miles east when Nelson looked for him. https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/335/mode/1up

### H2-18 Arrivals and departures

- Defiance: "joined the Fleet this morning from Portsmouth" (Nelson to Marsden, 7 Oct, Nicolas VII p. 84). James p. 380 also gives the 7th.
- Leviathan: James p. 380 gives the 8th. Nelson to Collingwood, 8 Oct: "Bayntun seems come out loaded … not a word by Leviathan" (p. 88).
- Royal Sovereign: "joined the Fleet on the 8th inst." (p. 107). Corbett p. 328 also gives 8 Oct.
- Belleisle: "joined the Fleet this afternoon from Plymouth" (10 Oct, p. 101).
- Agamemnon, L'Aimable and Prince of Wales: Nelson to Marsden, 13 Oct, "Agamemnon and L'Aimable joined this forenoon, and … the Prince of Wales … leaves the Fleet this evening" (pp. 117 and 120). Corbett p. 330: "On the 13th … and the same evening Calder parted company". Beatty p. 5 says the same.
- Africa: "October 14th.—Prince of Wales parted. H.M. Ship Africa joined" (log, p. 121 n.). Nelson to Collingwood, 14 Oct: "Africa did not see the Rochefort Squadron".
- Donegal: the diary entry is quoted in the table above (Beatty p. 97, page image). James p. 383 and Clowes p. 128 give the 17th; Beatty's narrative (p. 6) gives the 18th. Corbett p. 332 n. quotes Nelson that she was "obliged to go into the Mole for two days". https://archive.org/details/authenticnarrati00beat/page/97/mode/1up

### H2-20 Station

- Nelson's letters.
  - To Blackwood, 4 Oct: "Cadiz, East 17 leagues … The Fleet will be from sixteen to …" (Nicolas VII p. 73).
  - To Barham, 5 Oct (p. 75).
  - Heading "16 leagues West from Cadiz", 6 Oct (p. 80).
  - "Cadiz, due East, 19 leagues", 9 Oct (p. 96).
  - "Cadiz, N. 71 E. 15 leagues" and "Cadiz, East, 13 Leagues", 10 Oct (p. 110).
  - To Ball, 11 Oct (p. 111).
  - "Noon, Cadiz, E.S.E., 16 Leagues", 19 Oct (p. 132).
- The 19 Oct diary in two transcriptions.
  - Beatty p. 97, from the autograph memorandum book: "Cadiz bearing E.S.E. by compass, distance sixteen leagues" (page image checked).
  - Nicolas p. 133, from Clarke and M'Arthur: "E.N.E. by compass".
  - Corbett p. 333 n. had only the E.N.E. text; he used the log's noon position, 36°36′ N 7°30′ W, and argued for E.S.E. https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/333/mode/1up
  - James III p. 386 says "about 16 leagues west-south-west". Nicolas p. 131 says "about fifty miles W.S.W.". Both follow the E.N.E. misprint.
- Desbrière pp. 148–149 paraphrases the 4 Oct dispositions: "sans jamais être au Nord du parallèle passant par le port". Mahan: "fifty miles west of the port"; the advanced squadron was "ten or twelve miles east of the fleet".
- Lauriston, 3 Sep: "5 vaisseaux et 2 frégates se tenaient à 3 lieues, et 17 autres à 6 lieues" (Desbrière p. 145).

### H2-21 Blackwood's squadron

- Diary, 29 Sep: "Sent Euryalus to watch the Enemy with the Hydra off Cadiz" (Nicolas p. 53; Beatty p. 96). James p. 379: the force close off the harbour was "reduced to two frigates, the Euryalus and Hydra".
- Frigate numbers.
  - "only three, instead of eight Frigates with the Fleet" (4 Oct, p. 74).
  - "I send Naiad to you, and will Phoebe and Weazle" (8 Oct, p. 87).
  - The 9 Oct note names Capel, Parker, Mundy and Prowse (p. 96 n.).
  - "Keep your five Frigates, Weazle and Pickle" (10 Oct, p. 110).
  - "five Frigates, a Brig, and a Schooner" (11 Oct, p. 111).
- The 19 Oct squadron.
  - Euryalus log, NRS II p. 146: "Made a telegraph message to the Weazle, intelligence to Gibraltar and Tetuan. At 9, ordered the Pickle to proceed … off Cape Spartel". https://archive.org/details/logsofgreatseaf02jack/page/146/mode/1up
  - Desbrière p. 161 n.: Phoebe, Naiad, Sirius, Weazle, Pickle.
  - Sirius log (NRS II p. 312): "At 9, Weasel and Pickle parted company".
  - James III p. 384 gives the captains: Blackwood, Dundas, Capel, Prowse.
- Stations by day.
  - James III p. 380 gives the 8 Oct bearings: "Rota point north half-west, San-Sebastian south half-west distant two miles and a quarter".

## Missing from the notes

1. **The night signal code of 20 October** (Nicolas VII p. 136): two blue lights together every hour if the enemy stood south or toward the Straits; three quick guns every hour if it stood west. This is the core of the night look.
2. **What the logs show was actually burnt on the nights of 19–21 October.**
   - 19 Oct (NRS II): Euryalus saw rockets and then a blue light "to the westward" at 11:45 p.m.; Naiad "burned a blue light" at 11:40 p.m.; Sirius "saw a rocket and blue light".
   - 20 Oct: Phoebe "Burnt blue lights and rockets occasionally, which was answered by our fleet"; Sirius "Burnt blue lights and rockets and fired guns all night"; Euryalus burnt "false fires"; Naiad "repeated several blue lights and sky-rockets from the Phoebe and Sirius".
   - Nicolas p. 136 (narrative): "a certain number of guns with false fires and blue lights … repeated by the look-out Ships, from the Euryalus to the Victory".
3. **Beatty's E.S.E. reading of the 19 Oct diary.** It resolves Corbett's puzzle. It also shows that James's and Nicolas's "W.S.W. of Cadiz" is a transcription artefact.
4. **Francis Austen's day-by-day account of Louis's detachment.** It gives the civil dates for the departure (evening of 2 Oct), the turn-back (morning of 3 Oct), the rejoining (morning of 5 Oct), Gibraltar and Tetuan, and the convoy.
5. **Codrington's letters of 29 September and 19 October.** They are independent eyewitness confirmation of the joining date and of the chain's composition.
6. The 11 October 1805 Admiralty circular and the 1913 Trafalgar Committee. They are the authority for reading the October logs as nautical days.
7. Ajax and Thunderer joined off the Lizard on the 18th, not off Plymouth on the 17th.
8. The distinct-ship count is 34. The simultaneous mid-October peak was probably 28; 29 is certain only at the joining.
9. Beurnonville blamed the bad weather for the fleet being out of sight. The Cádiz stations saw Nelson arrive on the evening of 28 Sep ("That makes thirty-one").
10. Corbett says one sloop went to the Victory; the Euryalus log sends Pickle to Cape Spartel instead.
11. The Pickle joined from Plymouth on 1 Oct (Nelson to Marsden, 2 Oct, p. 64). The Entreprenante joined on 4 Oct (p. 66 n.).

## What changes for the scene

1. **Date line.** Nelson joins the fleet on **28 September 1805** (evening) and takes command on the 29th. Keep this date.
2. **Glyph count at Nelson's arrival.** Show **29** British ships of the line, including Victory, Ajax and Thunderer: about 23 in the main body and 6 close in under Louis. The caption may note 27 as the figure in James and Beatty. Collingwood had 26 before Nelson came.
3. **Fleet counts through October.**
   - 2 October, evening: Louis leaves with 5 of the line, the Endymion frigate and a small convoy, leaving 23 with Nelson.
   - Reinforcements raise this to 27 by 10 October and 28 on 13 October.
   - Calder leaves on 13 October and Donegal on 17 October, so the fleet off Cádiz stands at **27** from 17 October through the sortie.
   - Avoid showing a mid-October 29.
4. **Main fleet glyphs.** Place them due west (true) of Cádiz, about 16–18 leagues (48–54 nm) out, near 36.55–36.60 N, 7.3–7.5 W at 9:30 on 19 October. Do not place them west-south-west; that comes from a misprint.
5. **The chain on the parallel.** Lay it along the parallel of Cádiz or slightly south of it (36.35–36.6 N), not on an exact line. Defence and Agamemnon were nearest Cádiz; the Euryalus log suggests they lay slightly south of the parallel.
6. **The chain by day, 19 October.** Light it in this order: Sirius → Euryalus → Naiad → Phoebe → Defence and Agamemnon (21–30 nm out) → Colossus and Mars (about 36 nm) → Victory.
   - No. 370 went up at about 7:00 and Victory had it at 9:30, about 50 nm in 2½ hours.
   - Day signals were flag hoists and Popham's telegraph, with puffs from three-minute guns. Phoebe was firing three-minute guns at 8 a.m.
7. **The chain by night, 20–21 October.** Euryalus and Sirius stay with the enemy → Phoebe and Naiad → Defence → Colossus → Mars → Victory.
   - Blue-light flares were the main night signal: two together every hour meant the enemy was standing south. Three quick guns every hour meant standing west.
   - Rockets and false fires were also used, and the advanced ships carried a stern light.
   - The light is the pale blue-white of blue lights, flaring and dying, with occasional rockets. Avoid a steady beam.
8. **Euryalus glyph** at dawn on 19 October: about 36.467 N, 6.350 W, 4 nm south-west of San Sebastián. At noon: about 36.472 N, 6.455 W.
9. **Louis's group.** Five glyphs leave toward the Strait on 2 October and are at Gibraltar and Tetuan from 9 to 17 October. On 20 October they are about 200 miles east, escorting a convoy, so they should be absent from the battle area.
10. **Period names for the lettering.** "Arrache" (Larache), "C. Spartel", "C. St. Mary's", "Tetuan", "St. Sebastian" light, "Rota", "the Porpoises" and "the Diamond" (shoals), "Canal of St. Pedro". The brig is spelled "Weazle".
11. **Beat 4 caption.** Write "a chain of ships": frigates near the port, ships of the line farther out. "A chain of frigates" is wrong for the outer links; this confirms the notes' own correction of the brief.

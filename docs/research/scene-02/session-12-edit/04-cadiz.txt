@@@ The passage -> five bullets (September passage, A; decision 6)
<<<
- **The passage.** Nelson left Merton at 10.30 p.m. on 13 September and sailed from St Helens at 8 a.m. on the 15th (Nicolas VII 33–36). Ajax and Thunderer joined off the Lizard on the morning of the 18th (Nelson to Marsden, VII 45; James and Beatty say off Plymouth). On 26 September he sent the Euryalus ahead to ask for no salute and no colours. High.
===
- **Recalled.** Early on 2 September Blackwood, posting from Lymington to the Admiralty with the news that the combined fleet was in Cádiz, called at Merton, and Nelson was ordered to resume the Mediterranean command (Corbett 1910, p. 278; Nicolas VII, Analysis, p. xxxi). Of the ships he chose at Portsmouth only the Victory and the frigate Euryalus were ready; the Royal Sovereign, Defiance and Agamemnon were left to follow, and the Ajax and Thunderer were ordered out of Plymouth to join him (Nicolas VII 36–37; Desbrière 1907, p. 146). High.
- **Portsmouth and St Helens, 13–15 September.** Nelson left Merton at 10.30 p.m. on Friday 13 September and reached Portsmouth at six the next morning (his diary, Hudson 1917, p. 15; to Marsden, Nicolas VII 36). The Victory lay at St Helens, where his flag was rehoisted at 11.30 a.m. by her log, "about noon" by his letter (Nicolas VII 36). He embarked from the beach by the bathing machines with Rose and Canning and was on board at 2 p.m., by his diary as Hudson reads the manuscript and as Beatty prints it (Hudson pp. 15–16; Beatty 1825, p. 95). Nicolas prints the "two" as the hour he left the beach (VII 35), and Corbett has him go off to the ship early in the morning (p. 292). She weighed at daylight on Sunday 15 September in light northerly airs, anchored again at 6 and weighed at 8, making sail to the SSE with the Euryalus (diary, Hudson p. 16; log, Nicolas VII 36). Beatty, James, Desbrière and Nicolas's chronology give the 15th (Beatty 1825, p. 1; James III 379; Desbrière 1907, p. 146; Nicolas VII, Analysis, p. xxxi). Corbett has the signal to weigh made on the 14th, after Rose and Canning had dined on board (p. 292), and the Royal Museums Greenwich gives the 14th for his leaving Portsmouth: that is the day he embarked. High. Date line: 15 September 1805.
- **Down Channel, 15–18 September.** Against light airs, fog and then a foul westerly he was off Christchurch at sunset on the 15th, off Portland on the 16th and four miles off Berry Head that evening. At 9 a.m. on the 17th, abreast of Plymouth, he sent the Euryalus in to call the Ajax and Thunderer out of Cawsand Bay; a letter of the same day is dated off the Eddystone (diary, Hudson 1917, p. 16; Nicolas VII 38–43). On the 18th he lay to with the Lizard north, and the two 74s joined: in the morning by his letter to Marsden, at noon by his diary (Nicolas VII 45; Hudson p. 16). Desbrière also has the Lizard (p. 146); James (III 379) and Beatty (1825, p. 1) say off Plymouth. High.
- **Across the Bay, 19–23 September.** A south-westerly gale on the 19th held him in the mouth of the Channel (diary, Hudson pp. 16–17; west-south-westerly in Desbrière 1907, p. 146). On the 20th he passed Rear-Admiral Stirling's five of the line at 11 a.m. and hove to from 1.30 to 2.20 p.m. for the frigate Décade, which was carrying Bickerton home sick (Victory's log, Nicolas VII 46). The diary has the frigate seen to windward at noon and spoken at 2; Beatty puts the meeting at noon (p. 1). Nelson placed it "27 leagues S.W. from Scilly" (to Collingwood, 25 September, Nicolas VII 49); Desbrière puts both meetings there (p. 146). Northerly and then easterly gales carried him south, and at 6 a.m. on the 23rd he was "abreast of Cape Finisterre 17 Lgs" (diary, Hudson p. 17). High for the dates, medium for the places.
- **Down the coast of Portugal, 23–27 September.** The diary's noon latitudes are 42°25′ N on the 23rd (printed "42°: 2′5 [sic.]", which could also be 42°02½′) and 40°05′ N on the 24th (Hudson 1917, p. 18). He made the Rock of Lisbon SSE ten leagues on the 25th, having arrived off it that morning, and lay becalmed there all day; he asked the consul and the Amphion at Lisbon to keep his approach secret (diary; Nicolas VII 48–50). On the 26th, with the Rock NNE 13–14 leagues, he sent the Euryalus ahead at 4 p.m. with a letter dated the 25th: Collingwood was to put himself under Nelson's command and, if in sight of Cádiz, to fire no salute and hoist no colours when Nelson joined (diary, Hudson p. 18; Nicolas VII 49). James and Beatty also give the 26th (James III 379; Beatty 1825, p. 2); Corbett has the Euryalus sent from off Cape St Vincent (p. 317). At daylight on the 27th Cape St Vincent bore SE by S by compass, six leagues. There he stopped the sloop Nautilus, homeward bound with Collingwood's dispatches, and left her off the cape to send his reinforcements to the rendezvous between Cape St Mary's and Cádiz; at noon he was abreast of Lagos Bay (diary, Hudson pp. 18–19; Nicolas VII 51–53). Nelson heads letters "off Cape St. Vincent" on the 26th and 27th, and Desbrière keeps him there both days (p. 147), but the cape came in sight only on the 27th. High for the dates, medium for the positions.

@@@ Joining (September passage, B; a-v-merged-h2 16)
<<<
- **Joining.** He joined Collingwood at about 6 p.m. on Saturday 28 September, could not communicate until morning, and took command on the 29th, his forty-seventh birthday (Victory's log, Nicolas VII 53 n.; his letters, VII 60, 62–63; Duff, VII 70 n.; Codrington 1873, I 51). Nicolas heads both 28 and 29 September "Sunday", a slip. High. Date line: 28 September 1805.
===
- **Joining.** After lying to from 1 a.m., he bore up at daylight on Saturday 28 September with a fresh NNW breeze. At noon, in 36°32′ N, he saw nine ships of war to the east, and eighteen by 1 p.m. (the diary from the manuscript, Hudson 1917, p. 19; Beatty and Nicolas print only "At noon saw eighteen sail"). In near calm, under studding sails, the Victory joined Collingwood at 6 p.m. (Victory's log, Nicolas VII 53 n.; Desbrière 1907, p. 147). At 8, the log's next entry, San Sebastián's light bore E by N, five or six leagues: about 36°23′ N 6°36′ W by compass, 16½ nm south-west by west of the light. Desbrière gives that bearing at 8 a.m., for the main body (p. 147). The joining was in sight of Cádiz: Nelson saw the combined fleet in the harbour, Beatty puts the city about fifteen miles off (p. 2), and the Cádiz signal stations reported a three-decker and two 74s joining Collingwood from the westward that evening (Corbett 1910, p. 322). He could not communicate until morning and took command on the 29th, his forty-seventh birthday (his letters, Nicolas VII 60, 62–63; Duff, VII 70 n.; Codrington 1873, I 51); his "got fairly into the Fleet yesterday", written to Ball on the 30th, means that day (Nicolas VII 54). The main body went out of sight, 16–18 leagues west, only after he took command (see "The station"). Nicolas heads both 28 and 29 September "Sunday", a slip; the manuscript and Beatty have "Saturday" for the 28th. Fraser has him arrive on the 29th (1906, p. 22), and some modern accounts give 27 September, or the 29th, the day he took command; the period record is the evening of the 28th (`research/scene-02/h2-boulogne-cadiz.md`, H2-15). High; medium for the position. Date line: 28 September 1805.

@@@ Louis on 18–21 October (a-v-merged-h2 5)
<<<
- **Louis on 18–21 October.** He left Gibraltar eastward on the 18th with the Malta convoy, five of the line and three frigates (Austen, Hubback pp. 152–153), and was some 200 miles east of the Strait at dawn on the 20th (Corbett 1910, p. 335). Medium. He is outside the Gulf frame.
===
- **Louis on 18–21 October.** He finished watering at Tetuan on the 15th; that evening a westerly wind forced him back there, and on the evening of the 17th a frigate brought the order to see the Malta convoy as far as Cartagena (Austen, Hubback 1906, p. 152; Corbett 1910, pp. 331 n., 335). On the 18th he crossed to Gibraltar and left eastward with the convoy, five of the line and three frigates (Austen, Hubback pp. 152–153); the telegraph report that reached Villeneuve that day spoke of four of the line with the convoy and two more in Gibraltar (Corbett 1910, p. 332). At dawn on the 20th he was some 200 miles east of the Strait (Corbett 1910, p. 335). On the 21st he parted from the convoy: off Cape Ténès, as the usual practice was, by Corbett (from the Queen's log), or earlier than planned, on news that the enemy was coming out, by Austen (Hubback p. 154) (`research/scene-02/a-h2b.md`, H2-17f). Medium. He is outside the Gulf frame.

@@@ The station (a-v-merged-h2 1; a-v-merged-h3 17 merged into it)
<<<
- **The station.** Nelson kept the body of the fleet "from sixteen to eighteen leagues West of Cadiz" (to Blackwood and Barham, 4–5 October; "15 lgs. to twenty" in his diary of 14 October, Hudson 1917, p. 21; 15–18 in a letter to Ball, `research/scene-02/v-draft-h3.md`, 5), never north of the port's parallel, ready to crowd sail for Cape Spartel if the enemy came out (Nicolas VII 73–75, 111, 122). His reasons: not to be caught close in by a westerly gale and swept into the Strait, and to hide his strength and tempt the enemy out (to Barham, 5 October; Corbett 1910, pp. 326–327). High.
===
- **The station.** Nelson kept the body of the fleet "from sixteen to eighteen leagues West of Cadiz" (to Blackwood, 4 October; to Barham, 5 October), fifteen to eighteen by his letter to Ball of 11 October and "15 lgs. to twenty" by his diary of 14 October (Hudson 1917, p. 21), on about the port's parallel. In fresh easterly breezes he would work up toward Cádiz, "never getting to the Northward of it", and on hearing that the enemy was coming out he would carry a press of sail south toward Cape Spartel (to Blackwood, 4 October; Nicolas VII 73, 75, 111, 122). Desbrière (p. 149) puts this as never standing north of the port's parallel. Nelson's reasons: not to be caught close in by a westerly gale and swept into the Strait, and to hide his strength and tempt the enemy out (to Barham, 5 October; Corbett 1910, pp. 326–327). High.

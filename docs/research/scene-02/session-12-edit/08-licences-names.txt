7. **Estimated points.** Positions no source fixes are estimates inside the sources' range: on the Toulon–Cádiz line, the Toulon berth, the Atlas's longitudes for 4–8 April, the points marked "est." (south of Cabo de Palos and of Cabo de Gata, through the Strait, the 4 p.m. point off Conil and the offing of Cádiz) and the anchorage of 9 April; the passages between the places named in the West Indies; the tracks between the named places on Villeneuve's way home and round Finisterre into Vigo and A Coruña; the line from Ferrol to Cádiz but for the Iris's fix of 14 August, with the bend of 15 August; Nelson's beat west in April and his passage of 15–28 September between the places his letters and diary name; the control points that keep curves off the coast (north of Capo San Vito, and at 37°58′ N 26°05′ W off São Miguel); the Finisterre smoke; the moorings of 10–19 October; the berths of Louis's ships at Gibraltar and Tetuan; the chain's single ships on 19 October; the British fleet's daybreak position on 20 October ("between Trafalgar and Cape Spartel"); every position of the night of 20–21 October; each ship's place within the two fleets at dawn on 21 October; and Boulogne's flotilla line and the left camp's site at Outreau.
8. **Signals by the book.** Some of what the chart shows comes from the rule books, not from the logs of 19–21 October. By day each hoist stays flying until the Victory's answer runs back (Popham 1803, p. 6, and the *Signal-Book* of 1796; the day book of 1799 was not read). By night, from the 1799 *Night Signals*: No. 31 made as three guns and two lights, or near an enemy a square of four lanterns; two lights at each ship's ensign staff and one at her bowsprit end while the fleet wears; one light in answer to a signal. No log records hoists held down the chain on the 19th, though on the 20th the Agamemnon, ordered to repeat signals between the Euryalus and the Admiral, logged that she kept the signal up (NRS II 148, 300); no log of the night records the wear lights. The counted pairs or threes of gun puffs at each link on the 19th follow the counted guns the Euryalus logged with her signals on the 20th (NRS II 165, 167); along the chain on the 19th the records have only guns fired by the Phoebe and by a ship seen from the fleet (NRS II 146, 312, 322). The night's leading motif, two blue lights together every hour, is the half of Nelson's code for an enemy standing south, as the French reports have the combined fleet standing for most of the night; James has it standing north-west after 7.30 p.m. (see "Which half of the code").
9. **The Mars's distant signal.** At the relay's last link the Mars shows first coloured flags the fleet cannot read, then three dark shapes at three mastheads (a flag, a ball and a pendant), on the word of one eyewitness writing years later (Cumby 1899, p. 719); no printed description of the Distant Signals of 1805 was found.
10. **The army's march.** The dashed line from Boulogne to Ulm is a composite, labelled as the army: no single corps marched the whole of it. Boulogne to Donauwörth was the road of Soult's IV corps, from the Boulogne camps; Soult then turned south, and Ney's VI corps fought at Elchingen and took Ulm (see "The dashed line"; `research/scene-02/a-h2a.md`, R6).
11. **Routes simplified.** A route keeps the positions that give its shape and drops those that would make it zigzag or double back, or that do not fit: on the crossing 8 and 11 May; on Villeneuve's way home both 19 June positions of the Curieux, James's 30 June point, the doubtful noon of 23 July and the 27 July letter's 40 leagues WSW of Finisterre; on Nelson's line 24 June, 4 and 6 July and 2, 4, 5 and 6 August; and Nelson's line in the Mediterranean starts at Pula. Short moves are left out: Nelson's detour to Tetuan and back to Tarifa (22–25 July), Villeneuve's stand towards Cape Prior after leaving Ferrol, the chase of Collingwood on 20 August, and the Victory's beating down the Channel against westerly winds on 16–18 September. The crossing has no day marks, and Villeneuve's homeward daily marks stop on 24 June (see "Routes on the chart").

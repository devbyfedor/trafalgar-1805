## Scene 2: the road to Trafalgar

Facts for scene 2, from the invasion camps at Boulogne to the fleets' positions at dawn on 21 October: Villeneuve's run to the West Indies and back with Nelson in pursuit, Calder's action off Cape Finisterre, Ferrol and Cádiz, Napoleon's turn east to Ulm, Nelson's watch on Cádiz, the sortie and the signals. Written in session 8 (31 October 2026) from the research notes in `research/scene-02/` (`h1-campaign.md`, `h2-boulogne-cadiz.md`, `h3-signals.md`, `h4-names.md`), their independent checks (`v-*.md`) and the adjudications of every contested verdict (`a-*.md`), which are the final word on the points they rule on; `research/scene-02/README.md` says what each file is. The confidence scale is the one at the top of this file.

Dates are civil, midnight to midnight; times are ship's times as logged; bearings are by compass unless marked true (the variation was about 21° W off Cádiz in 1805). Positions are given in degrees and minutes, distances in nautical miles (nm), with leagues (3 nm) where a source uses them. NRS II is *Logs of the Great Sea Fights* vol. II (Navy Records Society, 1900), printed by civil day.

### The names on the chart

The brief asks for the names used in 1805. The chart sets the English forms (the site is in English), settled in `research/scene-02/a-h4.md` after the notes (`h4-names.md`) and their check (`v-h4.md`), from the charts the adjudicator read: Faden's chart of Nelson's track (12 August 1807) first, then Heather's *Coast of Portugal* (1800), Laurie & Whittle's *Atlantic Ocean* (1794), Cary's maps of 1801–1805 and Arrowsmith's of 1802–1803; and from usage in Nelson's letters, the logs, the *Naval Chronicle*, Norie, Malham and Brookes.

- **The rule.** Letter the form the English charts of 1800–1807 engraved, Faden first; break ties by naval usage of 1805; where the charts disagree, take the form a modern visitor will not misread; where they agree on a form that looks odd today (Barbadoes, Bourdeaux, Guadaloupe, Carthagena), keep it. High for the forms, medium for the choices between equal forms.
- **Type.** Ordinary characters only: the engravers' raised letters become the printed abbreviation with a stop (Sᵗ as "St.", Pᵗ as "Pt."), as printed English of 1805 always had it ("Cape St. Vincent" in Nelson's letters). No accents (Cadiz), as on every English chart of the period. Only oceans and seas are in spaced capitals (the brief); period plates also set countries, capitals and island groups in capitals. Double names take a small "or", as the plates set it. High.
- **Changed in session 8.** "The Channel" to English Channel (Faden 1807; Heather's chart titles; Norie, Malham); "C. St Vincent" to C. St. Vincent; "Western Islands" to Azores or Western Islands (Laurie & Whittle 1794; Heather c. 1806), so that a visitor finds the Azores; "Martinico" to Martinique. The other thirty names on the chart stand. West Indies, a region rather than a water, is set in roman like a land. High; medium for Martinique.

Where the period's own sources disagree (each form with who used it):

- **Martinique or Martinico.** Martinique: Faden 1807, Cary's West India map (1803), the *Naval Chronicle* of 1805 and Norie's tables of 1805. Martinico: Nelson's own letters, Arrowsmith's West Indies chart (1803), Laurie & Whittle (1794), Malham (1795) and Brookes (1807). The chart follows Faden and the captions.
- **The Channel.** English Channel: Faden 1807, Heather's chart titles of about 1806, Norie 1805 and Malham. British Channel: Cary's *Europe* (1804). The Channel: Laurie & Whittle's Atlantic (1794), and Nelson and his officers in their letters.
- **The Azores.** Azores or Western Islands: Laurie & Whittle 1794 and a Heather chart title of about 1806. Azores or Western Isles: Faden 1807. Western Islands alone: Arrowsmith 1802, Cary 1805, Nelson and Norie.
- **The ocean.** Atlantic Ocean: Cary 1803 and Arrowsmith 1803 (The Atlantic Ocean on Heather 1800 and Cary 1804), and the usual name in print. Atlantic or Western Ocean: the ocean charts of Laurie & Whittle (1794) and Faden (1807); Western or Atlantic Ocean: Cary 1801.
- **Gulf of Cadiz.** On English charts only on Heather's *Coast of Portugal* (1800), across the open water from the Algarve to Cádiz; gazetteers of 1795–1810 (Brookes, Walker, Cruttwell, the *Britannica* of 1810) use it for the same bight. Nelson, the logs and the *Naval Chronicle* never do: sailors said "off Cadiz". Tuckey (1815) says the name was more usually kept for the Bay of Cádiz.
- **Canary Islands or Isles.** Islands: Arrowsmith 1802, Laurie & Whittle 1794, Heather's chart titles, Nelson, Malham and Brookes. Isles: Faden 1807 and Cary 1805.
- **For later scenes**, settled the same way: Gulf of Lyons (Cary 1801 and 1804, Nelson, Malham; Gulf of Lion on older charts); Rochefort (Faden, the *Naval Chronicle*, Nicolas VII; Rochfort on Cary 1804, Nicolas VI and Malham); Algeziras (Heather 1800, Cary 1801; Algesiras in Nelson's letters; Algeciras on Heather's Strait plate of about 1809); C. St. Mary (Nelson, Faden's "C. S. Mary"; C. St. Maria on Heather 1800, C. Sta. Maria on Cary 1801); Rock of Lisbon (Faden, Heather, Laurie & Whittle, Nelson; C. of Roca on Cary 1801; Cape Roxent in Malham and Tuckey); Douro (Cary 1801, Malham, Brookes; R. Duero on Cary 1804); Guadalquivir (Cary 1801; the commoner English Guadalquiver on Faden, Heather and in Malham and Brookes reads today as a misprint); Cape Verde Islands (Arrowsmith 1802; Cape Verd Isles on Faden and Cary 1805); Tangier (Faden, Heather, Nelson; Tangiers on Cary 1804). The Sahara stays unlettered, as on sea charts (Saara or Great Desert on Arrowsmith 1802, Great Desert of Zaara on Cary 1805). The full table is in `research/scene-02/a-h4.md`.

### Open doubts

The parts above give each doubt where it arises. These are the ones that change what the scene shows.

- **Dates on screen.** Villeneuve's turn for Europe: 10 June (Corbett, Desbrière) or 11 June (his letter dated at sea). A Coruña: 2 August (Desbrière, from the French records) or 1 August (James, Mahan). Rosily at Cádiz: 23 October (expected, Beurnonville) or 25 October (his first letter). The orders of 14 September reached Cádiz on 27 or 28 September.
- **Counts.** The British on Nelson's arrival: 29 under his command (Victory's log, his letter to Acton) or 27 (James, Beatty); the October peak, 28 or 29. Ships of the line out on 19 October: seven (Philibert, the Montañés, Desbrière) against counts from four to twelve, the last with frigates. Ulm: about 23,000 to 30,000 laid down their arms; 25,365 by Alombert and Colin.
- **Positions no source fixes.** The combined fleet's moorings at Cádiz (the layout rests on Escaño, the French post-battle reports, Nelson's "harbour's mouth", modern depths and Faden's plan of 1797, which shows the Spanish moorings of 1797); the turning point of 15 August; the Toulon berth, the anchorage of 9 April and the 4 p.m. point off Conil; the Defence on 19 October (on Cádiz's parallel by her orders, or about 10 nm south by the Euryalus's bearing); every position of the night of 20–21 October, which cannot be joined to the dawn positions to better than 5–15 nm.
- **The crossing.** Whether Desbrière's second table of the *Atlas*'s positions carries printed dates or the noon of the day after is genuinely disputed, so the crossing has no day marks.
- **Finisterre.** Calder's noon (43°30′ N 11°17′ W by his dispatch, 43°54′ N 11°38′ W by James, 44°17′ N 11°52′ W by Desbrière at 11.30), when the firing stopped (7.30 to 9.30 p.m.) and when the prizes struck (soon after 8 p.m. by the British, at daybreak by the French).
- **Signals.** Which ships of the line passed No. 370 on 19 October (the Defence and Colossus are inferred; the Agamemnon's part is unproven); how long the relay took (2 to 3¼ hours); the Mars's distant signal of a flag, a ball and a pendant, on one eyewitness writing years later; the wear lights and hoists held along the chain come from the 1799 rule books, not the logs of those days; all ranges are estimates.
- **The British heading at dawn.** At first light and at sunrise they steered N by E by compass, about 350° true, and bore up for the enemy only at about 6.40–6.50, to about 047° true, then 058° from 8 a.m. The lab draws them on 047°.
- **The 1805 names**: see "The names on the chart".

# Frame drops at the start of scrolling (session 15, 9 November 2026)

The owner's note 2: the frame rate sometimes falls from 60 to about 40 at the very start of scrolling, on mains. A background agent measured it this session; nothing in the site was changed yet. These are its findings, in our words, with the numbers it measured. The tools are kept in `scripts/dev/perf/` (`INDEX.txt` lists them; they write to `scripts/dev/perf/runs/`, which git ignores). The raw traces (about 1.1 GB) were not kept.

## How it was measured

- Playwright's headless Chromium on the owner's laptop (Intel Arc, ANGLE on Direct3D 11), 1280×720 at scale 1.5, on mains. Scrolling by 100 CSS px wheel steps about every 200 ms. About 40 runs, 13 of them traced: rAF intervals, long animation frames, hooks on the WebGL calls with GPU timer queries (`instrument.js`, `run.mjs`, `metrics.mjs`, `steps.mjs`, `analyze-trace.mjs`, `timeline.mjs`, `layers.mjs`).
- Both the dev server and a production export (built at 15:19, with `TerrainField.readBack` but before the glyph layout's bake).
- Some runs overlapped the glyph clash sweep (about 37 % of the GPU, 17:22–17:31) or the owner's own Chrome (about 8 %); those were used only to attribute, not for numbers (`INDEX.txt` names them).
- Headless Chromium presents through a hidden swap chain, not the real display; only the owner's `?debug` on the production preview is the verdict.

## Findings, by weight

1. **Scene 1 itself, more than the chart.** Over roughly the first 1,200 px of scroll (scene 1's p 0.08–0.64) eight wide `strip-loop` layers are re-rasterised on the GPU every frame: they carry `will-change: transform`, but the dawn SVG inside each fades by `opacity`, which repaints the whole layer. The veil, the stars, the sun's glow, the dawn section, the enemy line and the Victory's heave do the same. In that band the GPU process's main thread spends 205–232 ms of every 250 ms on tile raster. With no chart loaded at all the band runs at 54.8 fps (dev) and 50.9 fps (production); with about 8 % of other GPU load it falls to 36–38 fps, which is why the drop comes only "sometimes".
2. **The chart's load lands in the same seconds.** It starts at the first idle callback, 0.33–0.8 s after navigation, and ends 1.7–2.3 s after: 21–32 missed vsyncs over that window even without scrolling, stalls up to 100–183 ms, and 3–5 main-thread tasks over 50 ms. Step by step (the cleanest run, production with the scene 1 fix, no scroll): creating the WebGL2 context waits 15–190 ms synchronously, inside a 63–108 ms task with the three.js chunk's evaluation; `TerrainField.load` is one task of 109–130 ms (the decode, land and horizon programs compile at first use, `getProgramInfoLog` waiting 15–43 ms each, the GPU main thread blocked meanwhile; `landIndex` about 25 ms); rivers and routes (`ribbonGeometry`, small arrays per vertex) and the atlas's ship shapes one task of 55–74 ms; the glyph atlas's eight levels 13–38 ms each, run inside rAF callbacks (the `pause()` in `atlas.ts` uses `requestAnimationFrame`), so each delays its frame; lettering and troika's own SDF context 37–39 ms; soundings and the digit atlas's distance transform 34–36 ms; `readBack`: the ENCODE program's compile 41–50 ms, then `getBufferSubData` blocking 13–14 ms per tile, three times; the MSAA resize in `applyQuality` a 42–54 ms task; the warm frame `frame(0)` 41–62 ms. Synchronous shader-log waits total 103–218 ms per load.
3. **A GPU stall of about 50 ms right after the chart is ready.** troika's `BatchedText` feeds its float attribute `aTroikaTextBatchMemberIndex` from a `Uint16Array` (`node_modules/troika-three-text/src/BatchedText.js`, line 284), so ANGLE recompiles the text's vertex shader at the first real draw (`D3DCompile`, 45–52 ms on the GPU main thread), stalling every frame, scene 1's included. Uploading that data as float (a GL shim in the instrumentation) removed both the compile and the late frame. The warm frame doesn't catch it (it draws the batch with `instanceCount = Infinity`); the real first draw is the extra hidden frame `Scene02Road` draws because `drawn` starts at -1.
4. **Not causes:** garbage collection (the largest major GC 7.3 ms, minor ones up to 2.3 ms) and the `?debug` overlay.
5. Also seen: shader compiles on ANGLE's worker threads (the terrain's fragment shader 455 ms, text 125 + 81 ms, ribbons 119 ms, sky 69 ms, glyphs about 50 ms each) delay readiness by about half a second but don't block frames; a 222 ms startup task in production (GSAP and ScrollTrigger setup, 318 forced style recalculations) ends before the first second; GPU passes of the load (horizon bakes 12–32 and 6–47 ms each as one draw, the overview decode 8–21 ms).

## The scene 1 compositor hints, tried by injection

- `will-change` on every element whose opacity or transform is animated (`fix-scene1.css`): the strip band at 60.0 fps with no missed vsync (three dev runs, two production), 59.2–59.6 under the background load; each strip layer's re-rasters fell from about 300 per pass to 12–15 (`layers-nofix` against `layers-fix`).
- The look, compared on frozen frames: the strips' hint alone changes at most 0.47 % of pixels, invisibly; the full set up to 2.2 %, and promoting the warm-light layer (with the veil and the flags) visibly softens the Victory's rigging. The set without the warm-light layer (`fix-noheroplus.css`) gives 57–58 fps under load with the rigging unchanged.
- Scene 1 is approved: any hint that changes its look needs the owner's eye. The candidate is `fix-noheroplus.css` (no change to the rigging), with the warm-light layer kept unpromoted and its opacity stepped more coarsely so it re-rasters about five times less.

## The plan (session 15; done in session 16, below)

1. Scene 1's compositor hints, beside the existing hint block in `src/app/globals.css` (from `fix-noheroplus.css`); check with the `regression` project and watch GPU memory (estimated +20–40 MB).
2. Remove the GPU main-thread stalls: seed troika's batch geometry with a `Float32Array` for `aTroikaTextBatchMemberIndex` (its `cloneAndResize` keeps the array's type); compile the field passes' materials (decode, land, horizon, encode) with `renderer.compileAsync` before their first run; `renderer.debug.checkShaderErrors = false` in production; `drawn = state.p` after `warm()`; let `warm()` wait for troika's batch, then draw each layer once hidden, one or two materials a frame, uploading the big textures (glyph atlas, digits, troika's SDF) with `renderer.initTexture`, one a frame.
3. The main thread in slices of 4 ms or less: one frame-aware slicer (`scheduler.yield()` or idle callbacks with `timeRemaining()`), never rAF as a pause; the glyph atlas in a worker with OffscreenCanvas; `ribbonGeometry` without temporary arrays (or built in `data:build`); `landIndex`, the soundings' parsing and the digit distance transform in a worker or the build; troika's SDFs in its workers (`gpuAccelerateSDF = false`); `readBack` replaced by a CPU decode of the WebP tiles in a worker with the DECODE shader's integer maths (no ENCODE pass, no 16 MB of readbacks); the WebGL2 context created at its final size during hydration and handed to `WebGLRenderer`; the chunk preloaded or split.
4. GPU work at 2–3 ms a frame or less: the horizon maps precomputed in the build (their azimuths are fixed, `LightBlend.AZIMUTHS`), or baked in scissored bands; the decode in bands; one big texture upload per frame.
5. Pacing: start after hydration settles, take a few seconds, and gate the hand-off at scene 1's end (p = 1, 2,160 px).
6. Optional: split the 222 ms startup task.

To verify each step: `run.mjs` and `metrics.mjs` against the production preview, several runs right after load, on mains, nothing else on the GPU: no missed vsync in any band and no task over 50 ms after the first second; a `gpu.angle` trace with no `D3DCompile` or `MainLinkLoadEvent::wait` on the GPU main thread after first paint.

## Session 16 (10 November 2026): done, and what it showed

The plan's steps 1–5 are in, with what the measurements found on the way; the decisions and the numbers are in `docs/decisions.md`, "the frame drops at the start of scrolling (note 2)". In short:

- Scene 1's hints went in without the warm-light layer (promoted, the rigging it repaints composites darker under Victory's pitch, up to 47 levels). The warm light instead steps its opacity (24 steps) between 1/255 and 254/255, so it is drawn one way from the first paint: its first draws at p 0.47 and its last at the fade's end had compiled Skia programs mid-scroll (18–36 ms frames), and every frame of the fade repainted her layer (about 8 ms of the GPU process).
- Three causes the session-15 recordings had not separated: the digit atlas's `document.fonts.add` (a whole-page restyle that re-rasterised all of scene 1's layers at once; the atlas is now drawn in a worker), the glyph bake's end of pass (one task of up to 160 ms; now a generator run in slices) and the director's eased headings (35 ms in its constructor, 85 ms a dozen ships at a time; now sliced in `prepare`).
- The warm frame drawn into the hidden canvas presented a frame every slice and cost the GPU 4–13 ms a slice; the warm-up now draws into a 4-pixel target, the same programs.
- The instrumentation's own sync WebGL calls (GPU timer queries) add waits of their own; `run.mjs LIGHT=1` measures without them, and `PROFILE=<dir>` measures a repeat visit (warm shader caches).
- Not done: the CPU decode of the tiles in a worker (step 3's last item). The readback's waits (12–24 ms each, on `getBufferSubData` behind a GPU process blocked in Present) no longer show among the late frames; the step stays open if the owner's runs say otherwise. The startup task (step 6) is unchanged.

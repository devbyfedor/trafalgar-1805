Written 31 October 2026 (session 8) by the names adjudicator. Inputs read, not edited: `docs/research/scene-02/h4-names.md`, `docs/research/scene-02/v-h4.md`, `docs/scene-02-brief.md` (history, concept, beats), `docs/decisions.md` ("Scene 2, Stage A", type roles), `src/scenes/02-road/gazetteer.ts` (the 34 names the chart letters now).

## How I ruled

- **I read the charts myself** from IIIF crops at full resolution, enlarged with sharp where the letters were small. Every reading below marked "read" is mine; the crop links are in "Evidence".
  - Faden, *A Chart of the Atlantic or Western Ocean … Track of His Majesty's Fleet … 1805*, 12 August 1807 (Huntington). The chart the scene imitates.
  - Heather, *A New Chart of the Coast of Portugal*, imprint 3 April 1800 (BnF copy via Commons). I read the imprint myself on sheet 1.
  - Laurie & Whittle (Pownall), *The Atlantic Ocean*, 1794.
  - Cary's atlas maps: *Spain and Portugal* 1801, *West India Isles* 1803, *Europe* 1804, *Africa* 1805.
  - Two charts neither side used: **Arrowsmith, *Chart of the West Indies and Spanish Dominions in North America*, 1803** (Leventhal Map Center copy), and **Arrowsmith, *Africa*, 1802** (Rumsey). Both are by the leading English chartmaker of the decade and independent of Cary, Faden and Laurie & Whittle.
  - Heather's *Straits of Gibraltar* plate after Tofiño (c. 1809, issue of 1814), to check v-h4's readings.
- **I counted the forms myself** in Nelson's *Dispatches* vols. 6 and 7, the *Logs of the Great Sea Fights* vol. 2, the *Naval Chronicle* vol. 14, Malham's *Naval Gazetteer* (1795, 2 vols), Brookes's *General Gazetteer* (13th ed., 1807), Norie's *Epitome* (1805, with Heather's chart catalogue of late 1806 bound in) and Tuckey's *Maritime Geography* (1815). Line-break hyphens joined, case-insensitive, long s allowed for; OCR misses some hits, so the counts are orders of magnitude. I read the contexts of every count a ruling rests on.
- **How I chose between forms that are equally period-correct** (the chart's constraints, then the evidence):
  1. Set the form the English charts of 1800–1807 engraved, Faden 1807 first because the scene imitates it; break ties with naval usage of 1805 (Nelson, the logs, the *Naval Chronicle*, Norie, Malham).
  2. Where those charts disagree, prefer the form a modern visitor will not take for a misprint or for another place. Where they agree on a form that looks odd today (Barbadoes, Bourdeaux, Guadaloupe, Carthagena), keep it and record it.
  3. Type: Baskervville and Bodoni Moda with ordinary characters. Engraved superior letters become the printed abbreviation with a full stop (Sᵗ → "St.", Sᵗᵃ → "Sta.", Pᵗ → "Pt.", Mᵗ → "Mt.", Iˢ → "Is."). No accents: English charts and prose of 1805 used none.
  4. Case: only oceans and seas take spaced capitals (the brief). Every other name is set in upper and lower case, even where the period plate used capitals; the settled table says what the plate did.

## Settled table of every name

"Set" is exactly what the chart letters, in upper and lower case except oceans and seas. "Period plate" says how the engravers set it where that differs. Roles: ocean, sea, water, cape, place, land, island, river, range. Confidence is that the form is a correct English chart form of about 1805.

## Settled conventions

1. **Policy.** Letter the form the English charts of 1800–1807 engraved, Faden's chart of 1807 first. Break ties with 1805 naval usage. Where the charts disagree, take the form a modern visitor will not misread. Where they agree on a form that looks odd today, keep it and record it.
2. **Oceans.** Bodoni Moda capitals, widely spaced, solid (Cary and Arrowsmith; Faden's open letters are his habit). One line, or broken over lines along the ocean.
3. **Seas.** Baskervville italic capitals, spaced (the brief). Period plates set seas in spaced roman capitals.
4. **Waters (gulfs, bays, straits, the Channel).** Baskervville italic, upper and lower case. Period plates set the big ones in spaced capitals, roman (Faden, Heather) or italic (Cary), and small waters in italic.
5. **Capes.** "C." with a stop, italic, at every zoom ("Cape" was spelled only on large-scale coastal plates). Points as "Pt.", or spelled out where the period spelled them ("Europa Point", "Rock of Lisbon").
6. **Saints and superior letters.** Typeset with a full stop: St., Sta., Pt., Mt., Is. (Iˢ for several islands), I. for one. Never the stopless "St".
7. **No accents.** Cadiz, Malaga, Leon, Tetuan, Sicie, Rhone. English charts and prose of 1805 used none; the site's own prose may keep modern accents.
8. **Towns.** Baskervville roman, upper and lower case, beside a ring. Capitals (Lisbon, Madrid, Paris, London, Vienna) take the dotted ring and a larger cap, where the plates set capitals in capitals.
9. **Countries and regions.** Baskervville roman, upper and lower case. Period plates used spaced capitals, mostly roman, sometimes italic.
10. **Islands and groups.** Baskervville roman, upper and lower case, on the sea. Period plates set groups in spaced capitals (roman on Cary and Arrowsmith, italic on Faden and L&W), single islands in roman upper and lower case.
11. **Double names.** Set with a lower-case "or" ("Azores or Western Islands"), as the plates set a small "or" between capitals. They may break over two lines.
12. **No article.** "Atlantic Ocean", "English Channel", not "The …"; Faden, Cary 1803 and Arrowsmith 1803 letter without it.
13. **Rivers.** Italic, upper and lower case, without "R." unless needed. Period: "Tagus R." or "R. Tagus", and spaced capitals along big lower courses.
14. **Ranges.** An atlas convention (Cary, Arrowsmith); sea charts named only coastal heights. Set in upper and lower case.
15. **Engravers' slips are not copied:** "C. Trafalagar" and "Teutan" (Cary 1801), NORTH PACIFIC OCEAN off Africa (Cary 1805).

## Doubts for `history-notes.md`

1. **Martinique or Martinico.** The chart letters Martinique, as Faden's chart of Nelson's track (1807), Cary's West India map (1803), the *Naval Chronicle* of 1805 and Norie's 1805 tables do. Nelson's own letters, Arrowsmith's West Indies chart (1803), Laurie & Whittle (1794), Malham (1795) and Brookes (1807) write Martinico.
2. **The Channel.** The chart letters English Channel (Faden 1807; Heather's chart titles of about 1806; Norie 1805; Malham). Cary's *Europe* (1804) engraves BRITISH CHANNEL, Laurie & Whittle's Atlantic (1794) THE CHANNEL, and Nelson and his officers simply wrote "the Channel".
3. **The Azores.** The chart letters Azores or Western Islands, as Laurie & Whittle (1794) and a Heather chart title of about 1806 do. Faden (1807) has Azores or Western Isles; Arrowsmith (1802), Cary (1805), Nelson and Norie call them simply the Western Islands.
4. **Canary Islands or Isles.** Canary Islands: Arrowsmith 1802, Laurie & Whittle 1794, Heather's chart titles, Nelson, Malham and Brookes. Canary Isles: Faden 1807 and Cary 1805.
5. **The ocean.** ATLANTIC OCEAN: Cary 1803, Arrowsmith 1803, and with "THE" Heather 1800 and Cary 1804; also the usual name in print. The ocean charts of Laurie & Whittle (1794) and Faden (1807) and Heather's chart titles say Atlantic or Western Ocean, and Cary's *Spain* (1801) Western or Atlantic Ocean.
6. **Gulf of Cadiz.** On English charts it appears only on Heather's *Coast of Portugal* (1800), across the open water from the Algarve to Cadiz. Gazetteers of 1795–1810 (Brookes, Walker, Cruttwell, the *Britannica* of 1810) use it for the same bight, while Nelson, the logs and the *Naval Chronicle* never do: sailors said "off Cadiz". Tuckey (1815) says the name was more usually kept for the Bay of Cadiz.
7. **Guadalquivir.** The chart letters Guadalquivir, as Cary's *Spain* (1801) does. The commoner English spelling of 1795–1807 was Guadalquiver: Faden 1807, Heather 1800, Knight c. 1795, Malham and Brookes.
8. **Algeciras.** Algeziras: Heather 1800, Cary 1801, the *Naval Chronicle* 1805 and Malham. Algesiras: the usual spelling in Nelson's letters of 1805. Algeciras: Heather's Strait plate after Tofiño, c. 1809, and once in the logs.
9. **Rochefort.** Rochefort: Faden 1807, the *Naval Chronicle* of 1805 and Nicolas's vol. 7 (mostly letters of August–October 1805). Rochfort: Cary 1804, Nicolas's vol. 6 (letters of 1804–05) and Malham.
10. **Cape St Mary.** C. St. Mary: Nelson's "Cape St. Mary" and Faden's "C. S. Mary". C. St. Maria: Heather 1800. C. Sta. Maria: Cary 1801.
11. **Cabo da Roca.** Rock of Lisbon: Faden 1807, Heather 1800, Laurie & Whittle 1794, Nelson and Malham. C. of Roca: Cary 1801. Cape Roxent: Malham 1795 and Tuckey 1815.
12. **Gulf of Lyons.** Gulf of Lyons: Cary 1801 and 1804, Nelson, Malham and Brookes. Gulf of Lion: older charts (La Rochette 1780; Laurie & Whittle 1794).
13. **Douro.** Douro: Cary's *Portugal* (1801), Malham and Brookes, for the river at Oporto. Duero: Cary's *Europe* (1804), the Spanish name of the whole river.
14. **Tangier.** Tangier: Faden 1807, Heather 1800 and c. 1809, Cary 1801, Nelson and Malham. Tangiers: Cary 1804.
15. **Barbadoes.** Barbadoes: Faden 1807, Cary 1803, Arrowsmith 1803, Nelson and the *Naval Chronicle*. Barbados: Laurie & Whittle 1794, and once in Malham.
16. **Cape Verde Islands.** Cape Verde Islands: Arrowsmith 1802. Cape Verd Isles: Faden 1807 and Cary 1805. Cape Verd Islands: Laurie & Whittle 1794. Navigation books of 1800–1805 wrote Cape Verd.
17. **Sahara.** Left unlettered. Period English maps call it Saara or Great Desert (Arrowsmith 1802) or Great Desert of Zaara (Cary 1805), and Brookes (1807) Zahara.
18. **Forms kept although they look odd today.** Bourdeaux: every chart read, with Bordeaux only in rare print. Guadaloupe: Faden, Cary 1803, Arrowsmith 1803 and Nelson, with Guadeloupe on Laurie & Whittle 1794 and in a quarter of the *Naval Chronicle*'s uses. Carthagena: Cary 1804, Nelson and the *Naval Chronicle*, with Cartagena on Faden 1807.
19. **Straits of Gibraltar.** "Straits" on the charts and in naval writing. "Streights" survived in official documents of 1800–1807 (v-h4), and Faden's 1806 chart title has the singular "Strait" (h4).

## Limits

- I did not re-read h4's earlier-session sources (Cary 1799, Faden's *Spain*, Knight c. 1795, La Rochette 1780, Nantiat 1810, the L&W *British Channel* and *Caribbee* charts) where nothing was contested. Their readings stand as h4 gives them.
- The Rumsey copy of Arrowsmith's *Africa* is catalogued as 1802; dealers describe later states with additions, and I did not determine this copy's state. Its readings agree with Cary 1805, which copied it.
- Counts come from OCR. The Brookes 1807 text is poor ("West Indies" scarcely registers), so its counts are lower bounds. The chart readings carry the rulings; the counts only break ties.
- Not checked by anyone: El Puerto de Santa María (h4's "St. Maria"), Isla de León and the English forms of the French provinces. They stay at h4's medium confidence.

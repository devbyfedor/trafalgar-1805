# Fact-check: H1 notes, §3 "Nelson", §6 "Fleet sizes", claims H1-16 to H1-23

Notes checked: `81d46a93…/scratchpad/research/h1-campaign.md`, lines 121–181, 252–274 and 514–554.
Checker: independent verification, session 43fa8065. I did not edit the notes.

## How I checked

- I downloaded the full texts of every cited book I could get: Nicolas vols. VI and VII, Corbett, James vol. III (1886), Clowes vol. V, Desbrière 1907 and Mahan (Gutenberg). I added three sources the notes did not use: **Troude, *Batailles navales de la France* vol. III (1867)**, a French period account that is independent of James; the ***Naval Chronicle* vol. XIV (1805)**, which prints the journal of a lieutenant of HMS *Decade*, a frigate with Nelson's squadron; and **Nelson's own letters**, used wherever the notes lean on Corbett or Clarke & M'Arthur.
- I looked at 12 page images on archive.org to settle readings the OCR left doubtful:
  - Nicolas vi. 393, 433, 436, 451 and 467;
  - Desbrière pp. 36, 38, 39, 74 and 75, plus the "Petites Antilles" plate;
  - *Naval Chronicle* xiv. 184.
- **Dating convention: what the primary evidence shows.** The *Victory*'s log was kept by the nautical day in 1805. Nicolas states this outright twice and corrects the dates: "March 27th [i.e. 26th]" (vi. 374) and "April 1st [i.e. March 31st, p.m.]" (vi. 389). Elsewhere he prints log extracts in civil order (a.m., then noon, then p.m.), and they agree with Nelson's civil-dated letters at Barbados, Antigua and Gibraltar. So Nicolas's log extracts can be used as civil dates.
  - James and Clowes use log dates for British events: 7 May for Gibraltar, 10 May for Lagos, 12 May for Knight, 13 June for Antigua.
  - The *Decade*'s journal (*Naval Chronicle* xiv. 184–185) is also in nautical dates for June: Montserrat 12th, Antigua 13th, sailed 14th.
  - The notes' framework is therefore right. It also settles two items the notes left as "sources differ": the Gulf of Palmas (26 March) and Pula (31 March).

## Points the notes missed

1. The nautical-day evidence is printed in Nicolas himself: "March 27th [i.e. 26th]" and "April 1st [i.e. March 31st, p.m.]". It settles two "sources differ" items in favour of the earlier dates: Palmas on 26 March and Pula on 31 March.
2. **The departure from Lagos Bay** was on the morning of 11 May (*Victory*'s log 9:10 a.m.; James, Clowes, Mahan). Nelson then lay off the cape until Knight arrived at 4 p.m., and bore away at 6:50 p.m. about 7 leagues SE by S of Cape St Vincent. Corbett's evening move on the 10th is not supported by the log.
3. Nelson's own account of the American at Tobago (St Vincent, standing south) differs from the Clarke & M'Arthur version the notes adopted.
4. Maurice's false report that the "Ferrol Squadron, consisting of six Sail of French, and eight of Spaniards" had reached Fort Royal on 4 June (diary, vi. 449). On that day only Magon's two 74s arrived.
5. Desbrière p. 39 gives 4:50 p.m., not 4:30.
6. The Spaniards straggled from Cádiz: only 14 of the 18 crossed together.
7. Troude (1867) is an independent French count that agrees with every glyph number checked, but he dates the entry into Cádiz to 17 August, an outlier.
8. Nelson's West Indies ships went south with Calder and were off Cádiz from 30 August (Desbrière p. 109): continuity for the later beats.
9. On 18 April Nelson changed the rendezvous to Rosia Bay. When he anchored at Gibraltar on 19 July, *Decade* and *Amazon* were already there.
10. Page slips:
    - 3 April log on vi. 392 (not 393).
    - The Sicily quote on vi. 401 (not 396–400).
    - Brereton on vi. 446 (not 455–456).
    - "Not a single Frigate" on vi. 465 (not 466).
    - Nicolas Analysis on p. xxxiv (not xxxv).

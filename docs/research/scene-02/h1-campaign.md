# H1: the chase, March to August 1805

Research notes for scene 2 ("The road to Trafalgar"), track H1: Villeneuve's breakout from Toulon, the West Indies, Nelson's pursuit, Cape Finisterre, Vigo/Ferrol and the run to Cádiz. Claim ids H1-01 onwards are listed at the end; the body explains the evidence.

Conventions in these notes:

- Dates are civil dates (midnight to midnight) unless marked otherwise. British logs of 1805 often used the nautical day (beginning at noon of the previous civil day), so a log entry for the afternoon of "the 10th" can be the afternoon of the civil 9th. Where an author's date looks like a log date, it is flagged.
- French documents of 1805 are dated in the Republican calendar. Year XIII began on 23 September 1804, so 1 germinal XIII = 22 March 1805, 1 floréal = 21 April, 1 prairial = 21 May, 1 messidor = 20 June, 1 thermidor = 20 July. Anchors: 11 frimaire XIII = 2 December 1804 (the coronation); 29 vendémiaire XIV = 21 October 1805 (Trafalgar).
- French positions are reckoned from the meridian of Paris (2°20′14″ E of Greenwich). All longitudes in the tables below have been converted to Greenwich.
- Mid-ocean positions in the logs are dead reckoning (DR). The French DR had a 1–2° error at the end of the outward crossing (see §2).
- Quotations are short, from public-domain period texts, and only where the wording is the evidence. Everything else is paraphrase.

## Two systematic dating errors that explain most of the "sources differ"

**James's Republican calendar is one day early for 1805.** James converts French dates himself and gets every year-XIII date one day too early:

| James writes | Correct conversion | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "le 16 prairial, or 4th of June" | 16 prairial XIII = **5 June** | James iii. 351 |
| "le 21 messidor, or 9th of July" | 21 messidor XIII = **10 July** | James iii. 351 |
| "8 thermidor (26th of July)" | 8 thermidor XIII = **27 July** (Desbrière p. 58 n.: "8 thermidor an XIII (27 juillet)") | James iii. 351 |
| Martinique arrival 13 May, "proved" by the Formidable's rôle d'équipage, against the British logs' 14 May | 24 floréal = **14 May** | James iii. 343–344 |

James built his French chronology from the ships' *rôles d'équipage* (he says so, iii. 344 and 375), so the whole French side of his narrative runs one day early: Toulon 29 March (French documents: 30), Cartagena 6 April (7), Gibraltar and Cádiz 8 April (9), sailing from Cádiz 9 April (10), Martinique 13 May (14), leaving Martinique 4 June (5), Vigo departure 30 July (31), Coruña 1 August (2). Clowes (vol. V, 1900) copies James. This is the origin of the "29 March" in the brief and in many modern books.

**British dates from logs are sometimes one day late.** James also uses log (nautical) dates for some British events: Gibraltar 7 May (Nelson's own letter: 6 May), Lagos Bay 10 May (*Victory*'s log and Nelson's letter: 9 May), Knight joining 12 May (Nelson: 11 May), Tetuan departure "24th at noon" (23rd). Nelson's letters and Nicolas's log extracts are in civil dates.

## 1. Villeneuve's breakout from Toulon (30 March) to Cádiz (9–10 April)

### The sailing date: 30 March, evening

- Villeneuve's own letter to Decrès is dated "Toulon, 9 germinal an XIII" (30 March): he is using the first breath of favourable wind to sail; only two British frigates in sight, far out; wind westerly but light (Desbrière p. 6 n., leaf n25: https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n25/mode/1up).
- Desbrière, from Reille's journal: the squadron weighed **on 30 March at 4 p.m.** and steered south-west with a W or NW wind (p. 7, leaf n26).
- The *Atlas*'s log: "9 au 10 germinal … à 7 heures, le cap Sepet N.-O. 1/4 N. à une lieue, vent N.-O." (p. 9, leaf n28), i.e. about 7 p.m. on 30 March she was a league south-east of Cap Sépet.
- Corbett: "On the evening of the 30th he put to sea" (p. 55: https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/55/mode/1up). Mahan: "sailed during the night of March 30".
- Nelson to the Admiralty, 5 April: the French fleet "put to sea in the night of Saturday, March 30th", and was seen at eight on the morning of the 31st (Nicolas vi. 396: https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter06nelsiala/page/396/mode/1up). 30 March 1805 was a Saturday (21 October 1805 was a Monday).
- **31 March** appears in Nelson's letters to Otway and Ball of 6 April ("sailed from Toulon on Sunday morning, March the 31st"), which conflate the sailing with the first sighting (Nicolas vi. 398–400). Nicolas's chronology hedges: "having left Toulon on the 30th or 31st of March" (vol. VI, Analysis, p. xxxv).
- **29 March** is James iii. 339 ("on the evening of the 29th of March, to sail from Toulon road") and Clowes v. 97, explained by James's calendar error above.

**Verdict:** weighed in the afternoon of 30 March, cleared Cap Sépet by about 7 p.m., first seen by Nelson's frigates at 8 a.m. on 31 March. High confidence.

### Force

- **Ships of the line, 11:** 1st division *Bucentaure* 80 (Villeneuve), *Neptune* 80, *Pluton* 74, *Mont-Blanc* 74, *Berwick* 74, *Atlas* 74; 2nd division *Formidable* 80 (Rear-Admiral Dumanoir), *Indomptable* 80, *Swiftsure* 74, *Scipion* 74, *Intrépide* 74 (Desbrière pp. 6–7, list from Reille's journal). James iii. 339 agrees (*Pluton* had replaced the unserviceable *Annibal*).
- **Frigates, 6:** *Cornélie*, *Rhin*, *Hortense*, *Hermione* (18-pounders), *Sirène*, *Thémis* (12-pounders). **Brigs, 2:** *Furet* and one printed by Desbrière as *Pléiade* (James's list, iii. 331 with the substitutions at 339, gives *Furet* and *Naïade*; a brig *Naïade* appears in later French lists, so "Pléiade" is probably a misreading). Nelson's frigates counted "eleven Sail of the Line, seven Frigates, and two Brigs" (Nicolas vi. 396); Corbett says "eight cruisers".
- **Troops:** a division under General Lauriston with General Reille: two battalions of the 16th of the line (1,800), the battalion of the 67th (1,150) and 120 artillerymen, about 3,070 (Desbrière p. 7, read from the page image). In addition about 1,800 men of the 2nd infantry regiment were spread through the ships to complete the crews. Corbett: "over 3000 troops"; James: 3,500; a French account quoted by Nicolas (vi. 395 n.): "scarcely three thousand men". Six months' provisions per ship.

### Route to the Strait

- 31 March: the frigates *Active* and *Phoebe* saw the French at 8 a.m. (Cape Sicié N by E, 10 leagues) steering SSW under a light NE breeze; at 8 p.m. (Cape Sicié N by E true, 20 leagues) *Phoebe* left for Nelson; *Active* lost them in the night in a fresh WNW wind (Nicolas vi. 396–400). James iii. 339 and Clowes put the sighting "in the afternoon".
- Villeneuve had meant to pass east of the Balearics (he believed Nelson was off Barcelona, where Nelson had shown himself on purpose). On **1 April** a Ragusan merchant ship told him Nelson was south of Sardinia; he turned to pass between the Balearics and Spain (Desbrière pp. 7–8; James iii. 339–340; Mahan; Corbett p. 56 has the Ragusan "in the morning" and a course change in the night, slightly inconsistent with his p. 59).
- The *Atlas*'s noon positions (below) show the turn: south-west of Toulon on 1 April, then WSW to 25 nm off Barcelona on 2 April, then SSW through the channel between Ibiza and Cabo de la Nao.
- **Cartagena:** off the port in a flat calm on the morning of **7 April** (Desbrière p. 8; Corbett p. 61: "On April 7th … he was becalmed off Cartagena"). James iii. 340 and Clowes: 6 April. Salcedo's six Spanish ships of the line did not join: Villeneuve said their commander had no orders; the ambassador said they needed 36–48 hours to embark powder; Napoleon blamed the Spaniards. Villeneuve left in the evening or night of 7–8 April on a fresh easterly.
- **Cape de Gata:** a neutral later told Nelson's *Leviathan* the French had been seen "on the 7th off Cape de Gata" with a fresh easterly, steering west (James iii. 341; Mahan). The *Atlas* was at 36°05′N at noon on 8 April, south of the cape's latitude, so the fleet passed Cape de Gata in the night of 7–8 April or the morning of the 8th.
- **The Strait, 9 April:** Gibraltar Rock and Ape's Hill seen in the morning; at about 9 a.m. the squadron entered the Strait in two columns with the frigates ahead; the *Atlas* notes the forts firing several guns (alarm guns from the Rock, says James). About 4 p.m. the frigates signalled Orde's squadron (6 of the line), which drew off westward (Desbrière pp. 8–9; Corbett pp. 62–63). Sir Richard Strachan's *Renown*, off Tarifa at dawn, had seen the French coming through and warned Orde. **8 April** is the date in James, Clowes, Mahan, Nelson's intelligence (a Ragusan via the *Amazon*: "passing the Straits on April 8th", Corbett p. 93) and Adkin (per Wikipedia). The French logs and journals (Atlas, Reille, Villeneuve) are internally consistent on the 9th and fit the Cádiz sailing of 10 April that British observers at Lisbon and Hardy also reported ("since they left Cadiz on the 10th", Corbett p. 105).

### Cádiz, night of 9–10 April

- The French anchored outside the bay at nightfall, about 7:45–8 p.m. on 9 April (Atlas log; Corbett p. 68: "At eight o'clock on April 9th"). Villeneuve sent the *Hortense* in to make the recognition signals. Gravina reported six of the line and a frigate ready.
- Signal to weigh at 1 a.m.; most of the force under sail by 2 a.m. on **10 April**, heading west (Reille, in Desbrière pp. 10–11; Corbett: "After waiting less than four hours, he held away in the dark").
- **Joined at Cádiz:** the French *Aigle* 74 (which had sheltered there since early in the war), the corvette *Torche* and the brig *Argus*; the Spanish *Argonauta* 80 (Admiral Gravina; Escaño chief of staff), *San Rafael* 80, *Firme* 74, *Terrible* 74, *América* 64, *España* 64 and the frigate *Santa Magdalena* (James iii. 342; Clowes v. 99; Desbrière p. 27). *San Rafael* ran aground coming out and followed later (James: she sailed on the 10th). Spanish troops: 1,930 (Decrès's report, Desbrière p. 27) or 1,600 (James).
- The Spaniards straggled. At dawn on 10 April only *Argonauta* was with the French; *América* rejoined on the 12th; *Firme*, *Terrible*, *España* and *Magdalena* crossed separately and met Villeneuve at Martinique; *San Rafael* arrived two days after him (Reille in Desbrière pp. 10–11 and 26). The published (*Moniteur*) version claimed eight Spanish ships; Napoleon privately wrote of five and a sixth aground (James iii. 343).

## 2. The crossing and the West Indies (10 April – 11 June)

### Crossing

- Route: south along the Moroccan coast to Cape Cantin (Cap Blanc in sight), west from about 15 April in fine weather, north of the Canaries and close to the Salvages, then south-west; from about 26 April westerly winds forced a run SSW until 2 May, then west along 18–14½°N (Desbrière p. 25; Nelson later saw a captured Spanish log and chart showing the allies "went in sight of Cape Blanco, and passed close to the Salvages", diary 8 July, Nicolas vi. 468). The *Atlas* table (Desbrière p. 26, leaf n47) gives a noon position for every day; see table B.
- Villeneuve's journal: at 1 p.m. on 23 floréal (13 May) a frigate signalled land to the west, 7 leagues off; his reckoning was out by 36 leagues to the east (Desbrière p. 26 n.). The ships' DR longitudes at the end of the crossing are therefore up to about 1°50′ too far east.
- Slow sailers held the fleet back: *Formidable*, *Intrépide* and especially *Atlas* (Desbrière p. 25; James iii. 343). The frigates *Hortense* and *Hermione* went ahead and took the British sloop *Cyane* (18 guns) on 12–13 May.

### Arrival at Martinique: 14 May

- Martinique sighted 13 May; at daybreak on **14 May** the fleet entered the St Lucia Channel and worked up to the anchorage of **Fort-de-France** (Fort Royal); the whole squadron anchored during the day, cannonaded in passing by the British post on Diamond Rock. In the bay they found *Firme*, *Terrible*, *España* and *Magdalena* beating in; *San Rafael* came in on 16 May after being decoyed under Diamond Rock's guns (Desbrière pp. 25–26; Villeneuve's letter of 17 May; James iii. 343–344; Clowes v. 101).
- **13 May** is James's (and in one place Clowes's) date, from the calendar error; James himself records that the Diamond Rock's and the *Triton* West-Indiaman's logs gave the 14th. The brief's "around 14 May" stands.
- Force at Martinique: 18 of the line (12 French, 6 Spanish), 7 frigates, corvette *Torche*, brigs, the prize *Cyane* and a storeship; 5,088 effective troops (3,332 French, 1,930 Spanish, less 174 sick; Decrès's report, Desbrière p. 27).

### At Martinique, 14 May – 5 June

- Villeneuve's orders tied him to Fort-de-France to wait 40 days for Ganteaume. On 26 May he sent three frigates (*Hortense*, *Hermione*, *Thémis*) to cruise to windward; they sailed on the 28th (Desbrière pp. 28–29). A false report of 28 sail passing Gros Islet, St Lucia, on the night of 28 May, probably these frigates, later misled Nelson (Corbett p. 161).
- **Diamond Rock:** Villeneuve decided on the attack on 29 May. Force: *Pluton* and *Berwick* 74s, frigate *Sirène*, brig *Argus*, schooner *Fine*, 11 gunboats, 200–400 troops under Captain Cosmao of the *Pluton* (Desbrière p. 29: 200 men; James iii. 349: 300–400; Clowes: about 350; Corbett p. 160 quotes the French return of 260). They sailed at 5:30 p.m. on 29 May, arrived too late on the 30th, landed and opened fire on 31 May. Commander J. W. Maurice abandoned the lower works and held the summit. He surrendered at about 4:30 p.m. on **2 June** (James iii. 349; Desbrière p. 36, on the 2nd, after seamen and grenadiers climbed with ropes and took the cave holding his provisions). Corbett p. 160 gives **3 June**. Garrison: 107 (French returns, James, Corbett) or 128 (Desbrière). British loss 2 killed, 1 wounded; French loss "at least fifty" by their own count, 30 killed and 40 wounded by Maurice's estimate. Maurice was honourably acquitted at his court martial.
- **Didon** arrived from Lorient with Napoleon's orders of 29 April (attack the British islands, wait 35 days after Magon, then return to Ferrol): on **30 May** (Desbrière p. 29, from Reille), the day the attack opened, i.e. **31 May** (Corbett p. 158), or **1 June** (James iii. 350). Villeneuve answered on 1 June that his provisions would not allow 35 more days and that he would attack Antigua or Barbuda once the rock was taken (Corbett p. 159 n. argues his "Barbade" means Barbuda).
- **Magon:** Rear-Admiral Magon with the new 74s *Algésiras* and *Achille* and about 840–861 soldiers (James: 840; Desbrière p. 37: 861, itemised) left Rochefort about 1 May, reached Guadeloupe on 29 May (James) and joined Villeneuve at Fort-de-France on **4 June** (Desbrière p. 37; Corbett p. 160: "as they did so Magon appeared"). James iii. 351 has them joining at sea "on the same or following day" after the fleet sailed. Villeneuve then had **20 of the line, 14 French and 6 Spanish**.

### North to Antigua and the convoy, 5–8 June

- **5 June, 5 a.m.:** the fleet sailed from Fort-de-France northward with 796 men from the Martinique garrison (Desbrière p. 40; Corbett p. 165: "early on the morning of June 5th"). James iii. 351: 4 June (calendar error). Captain Maurice told Nelson the French expected to sail "on the evening of June 4th".
- **6 June:** passed under the lee of Dominica (a British convoy lay under the Cabrits at Prince Rupert's Bay and was left alone); at 3 p.m. off Basse-Terre, Guadeloupe, took on about 700 troops and pilots; at 9 p.m. steered north (Desbrière p. 40). British observers saw the fleet pass Prince Rupert's Head on the 6th and lie under the Saintes in the evening: 18 of the line, 6 frigates, 3 brigs and schooners (Nelson to Marsden, 11 June, Nicolas vi. 452).
- **7 June:** north again; the night under the lee of Antigua, which fired alarm guns (Desbrière p. 41).
- **8 June, the convoy:** at 10 a.m., to leeward of Barbuda, the lookouts signalled a convoy of 15 sail that had left St John's, Antigua, the evening before; after a five-hour chase 14 were taken (Desbrière p. 41, Reille). Villeneuve's report: "after having doubled Antigua", a convoy of 14 sail in the NNW under escort of one schooner; all taken by nightfall except the escort (Corbett p. 165). Nelson: "a Convoy of fourteen Sail of sugar-loaded Ships" (Nicolas vi. 456). James iii. 351: 15 merchantmen worth five million francs, escorted by the frigate *Barbadoes* and the schooner *Netley* (Lt Richard Harward); Corbett p. 166 says the escort was the *Netley* alone under Lt William Carr and calls James's names unsupported. The prizes went with the *Sirène*; on 27 June, about 180 miles NE of Barbuda, the French frigates burnt them all when the sloops *Kingfisher* and *Osprey* pretended to signal a fleet (James iii. 352–353).

### The turn for Europe: 10 or 11 June, from the waters north of Antigua and Barbuda

- From the prisoners Villeneuve learned that Nelson had reached Barbados with "twelve or fourteen" of the line. After conferring with Gravina he decided to return at once to Ferrol (his letter dated 11 June, at sea; Desbrière pp. 41–42).
- The troops taken from Martinique and Guadeloupe were put into four frigates (*Hortense*, *Hermione*, *Didon*, *Thémis*) to be landed at Guadeloupe; the fleet stood NNE for the Azores. Dates: "dès le 10 juin" the troops were embarked (Desbrière p. 43); Villeneuve "mettait à la voile le 11 juin" (Desbrière p. 42); "on June 10th they held away with all speed for the Azores" (Corbett p. 167); "on the 9th or 10th" (James iii. 352). Nelson, from local reports, believed the allies had "passed to leeward of Antigua on Saturday the 8th, standing to the Northward" (Nicolas vi. 455).
- **Why the dates differ:** there was no single departure. The fleet left Fort-de-France on 5 June, was off Antigua on 7–8 June, took the convoy on 8 June, transferred troops on 9–10 June and set course for Europe on 10–11 June, already north of Antigua. On Corbett's chart Villeneuve's 10 June position is about 19°14′N 61°44′W.

### Return route (Villeneuve): north of the Azores, by Corvo

- North-north-east to about 33–35°N (21–24 June), then ENE across the Atlantic. Observations by others: the American schooner *Sally* saw "about 22 Sail of large Ships" at 7 p.m. on Saturday 15 June, steering NNE (master) or NNW (mate), having been at 27°28′N 60°58′W that noon (report via *Amazon*, 17 June, Nicolas vi. 463). Desbrière p. 46 misdates this to the 17th. The brig *Curieux* (Captain Bettesworth), carrying Nelson's dispatches, saw the allies on **19 June**: "as high as latitude 33°12′ and in longitude 58°" (Corbett p. 181; James iii. 354), "some 900 miles NNE of Antigua" (Corbett, Mahan, Desbrière). Corbett's own chart puts the *Curieux* sighting near 30°13′N 58°06′W instead (see table D). The *Curieux* reached Plymouth on 7 July; Bettesworth was at the Admiralty late on 8 July (Corbett p. 181) or by daylight on the 9th (Mahan, Desbrière), and Barham at once ordered Stirling's Rochefort squadron to join Calder off Ferrol and both to cruise 30–40 leagues west of Finisterre.
- The four frigates rejoined on 30 June about 20 leagues NE of Corvo (James iii. 353; Clowes v. 104). Reille: on 13 messidor (2 July) "reconnu l'isle de Corvo" (Desbrière p. 57). Either way the route passed north of the central Azores.
- 3 July: took the Liverpool privateer *Mars* and recaptured her prize, the Spanish treasure galleon *Matilda* (James: 14–15 million francs; Clowes: about £600,000).
- 4–8 July: light westerlies, course E by N. 9 July (20 messidor; James and Clowes "9th", which here coincides): a north-east gale at about 20° of longitude carried away *Indomptable*'s main topmast and forced the fleet south-west. Reille's "20 degrés" is most likely west of Paris (about 17°40′W Greenwich); Corbett's chart shows ~20°W Greenwich. 12 July: closest approach, within about 300 miles of the coast due west of Oporto; then forced back west for four days (Corbett p. 195). 19 July (30 messidor): wind NW, ran east; 21–22 July: same course in fog (Reille in Desbrière p. 57).
- Villeneuve wrote of "twenty days" fighting NE and ENE winds 60 leagues from Cape Finisterre (letter of 28 July, Desbrière p. 85).

## 3. Nelson

### Where he was when Villeneuve sailed (26 March – 4 April)

- Nelson had shown himself off Barcelona (17 March) to suggest he was fixed on the Spanish coast, then went to his secret rendezvous in the **Gulf of Palmas**, south-west Sardinia: anchored 26 March (Mahan) or 27 March (Nicolas, Analysis; James iii. 339, after arriving off San Pietro on the evening of the 25th). Corbett p. 57 has him "anchoring" there as Napoleon wrote his orders of 22 March, and dates Nelson's order of battle issued there 26 March. He moved to **Pula Roads** (south Sardinia) on 30–31 March (Nicolas) or 1 April (James), and weighed again at 5:30 a.m. on 3 April ("Victory's Log", Nicolas vi. 393).
- 4 April: *Phoebe* joined at about 10 a.m. a few leagues west of the island of Toro (Victory's log at noon: Toro E ¾ N, 6 leagues); *Active* at 2–3 p.m. (Nicolas vi. 393–396; Desbrière p. 11).
- Force: eleven of the line (Nelson's statement for the minister at Constantinople: "Eleven Sail of the Line, four Frigates, two Corvettes", Nicolas vi. 398 n.). The eleven were *Victory*, *Royal Sovereign* (Rear-Admiral Bickerton), *Canopus* (Rear-Admiral Louis), *Superb*, *Spencer*, *Swiftsure*, *Belleisle*, *Conqueror*, *Tigre*, *Leviathan*, *Donegal* (derived: the ten later taken to the West Indies plus *Royal Sovereign*; medium).

### Search of the central Mediterranean (4–18 April)

- Thinking the French bound for Sicily, Naples or Egypt, he took a central position between Sardinia and Galita (La Galite, off Tunisia), spread his frigates across the channel, and waited 48 hours (4–6 April) (Corbett pp. 59–60; Nicolas vi. 396–400: "I shall neither go to the eastward of Sicily, or to the westward of Sardinia, until I know something positive").
- Then to the Palermo/Ustica area: Corbett p. 60 says he reached Ustica on the 7th; James and Clowes have him bearing up for Palermo on the 7th and, "on the 9th, being then off the western end of Sicily", standing west; Mahan has him off Palermo on the 9th–10th; Desbrière p. 13 "le 10 il était devant Palerme". On 10 April, 7 a.m., news via Hallowell that Craig's expedition had sailed made him suppose the French had gone west (Nicolas, letter to Ball).
- **Contrary winds:** beating west against westerlies took nine days to get back south of Sardinia (Mahan). 18 April, off Toro, the *Amazon* reported that a Ragusan had seen the Toulon squadron passing the Straits on the 8th (Corbett p. 93). 19 April, still only 10 leagues west of Toro, he wrote of the wind as "dead foul! dead foul!" (Corbett p. 95; James iii. 341). He left five frigates and a bomb under Capel to guard Sardinia, Sicily and Egypt.
- 30 April: first sight of Gibraltar Rock (James iii. 341; Clowes v. 98); 1 May: Ceuta in sight (Corbett p. 97).

### Tetuan, Gibraltar and Lagos (4–11 May)

- **4 May, 10:30 a.m.:** anchored in Mazari Bay, Tetuan, to water (Victory's log, Nicolas vi. 419). **5 May:** sailed on a change of wind, recalling *Superb* from Tetuan before her cattle were embarked.
- **6 May:** "Yesterday, at 2 p.m., we anchored in Gibraltar Bay … at 6 o'clock the whole Fleet was under sail, steering through the Gut" (Nelson to Marsden, 7 May, Nicolas vi. 428). James and Clowes: 7 May (log date). Here Nelson probably received Rear-Admiral Donald Campbell's (Portuguese navy) intelligence pointing to the West Indies (Corbett p. 104; James iii. 345).
- **9 May:** made Cape St Vincent in the morning (Corbett p. 105); at 9 p.m. anchored in Lagos Bay to clear Orde's storeships and complete to five months' provisions ("May 9th. p.m. At 9, anchored in Lagos Bay", Victory's log, Nicolas vi. 429). James: 10th.
- **10 May:** at Lagos; "My lot is cast, and I am going to the West Indies" (to Ball). The *Martin* sloop went ahead to Barbados. In the evening the fleet moved back off St Vincent (Corbett p. 105).
- **11 May:** off Cape St Vincent; Rear-Admiral Knight (*Queen* 98, *Dragon* 74) arrived with Craig's troop convoy; Nelson gave him the *Royal Sovereign* to see it safely to Gibraltar; "I this day steer for the West Indies" (to Sidmouth, Nicolas vi. 436). That evening, some 20 miles south of the cape, the squadrons parted and Nelson bore away (Corbett p. 108; Desbrière p. 37: "le 11 mai au soir").

### The squadron

- **Ten of the line:** *Victory* 100, *Canopus* 80 (Rear-Admiral Louis), *Superb* 74, *Spencer* 74, *Donegal* 74 (rated 80 by some), *Tigre* 80, *Leviathan* 74, *Belleisle* 74, *Conqueror* 74, *Swiftsure* 74. **Three frigates:** *Decade*, *Amphion*, *Amazon* (Nicolas vi. 432 n.; Corbett p. 108). James iii. 346 prints only nine names (no *Donegal*) and notes that another writer substituted *Excellent*; Nelson's Barbados order list and Corbett's order of battle (p. 163) include *Donegal*, so James's list is incomplete.
- **At Barbados, +2:** *Northumberland* 74 (Rear-Admiral Cochrane's flag; rated 80 by Corbett) and *Spartiate* 74 = **12**. Cochrane's other four ships had been kept at Jamaica by Rear-Admiral Dacres (James iii. 346; Corbett pp. 156–157, 161).
- **Return: 11** (*Northumberland* stayed on station, *Spartiate* came). The frigates were detached on the way home (*Decade* and *Martin* 19 June, *Amphion* 30 June, *Amazon* 13 July); on 30 June Nelson wrote "I have not a single Frigate with me" (Nicolas vi. 466).

### Outward passage and Barbados (11 May – 4 June)

- Positions: 14 May, "38 leagues from Madeira" (Nicolas vi. 436); James: "On the 15th of May the British fleet made Madeira". Corbett's chart (after Faden) gives daily positions: table F. On 29 May the *Amazon* went ahead to Barbados.
- 3 June, 8:20 a.m.: *Amphion* spoke two English merchantmen, the first certain news that the French were in the West Indies. **4 June:** 6 a.m. Barbados in sight; off Carlisle Bay at noon ("I arrived off here at noon this day"); 4:50 p.m. anchored in Carlisle Bay; Cochrane in *Northumberland* there, *Spartiate* joining (Nicolas vi. 445; Desbrière p. 39: 4:30 p.m.). Run from Cape St Vincent: 3,227 miles (Nelson's diary, Nicolas vi. 471); he had gained about ten days on Villeneuve (Corbett p. 160). **The brief's 4 June is right; no source found gives another date.**

### Tobago, Trinidad, Grenada, Antigua (5–13 June)

- Brigadier-General Brereton at St Lucia reported 28 enemy sail passing Gros Islet on the night of 28 May, bound for Barbados or Trinidad. General Sir William Myers embarked about 2,000 troops (Nicolas vi. 455–456; Corbett p. 161).
- **5 June, 9:30 a.m.:** weighed, steering south. **6 June:** noon off Tobago; in the evening, off Sandy Point, a private schooner made by chance the signal Cochrane had fixed for "enemy at Trinidad" (a merchant's clerk sent out to identify the fleet), and an American reported the French off Grenada heading for the Bocas (Nicolas vi. 447–448; Corbett pp. 162–164).
- **7 June:** through the Dragon's Mouth into the Gulf of Paria, expecting "a second Aboukir"; a British post burning its blockhouse added to the illusion; no enemy; anchored at 5:30 p.m. near the west entrance of the Bocas (Victory's log). **8 June:** weighed at 7 a.m.; at daylight, coming out, Maurice's report that Diamond Rock had fallen and the enemy was still at Martinique on the 4th (Nicolas vi. 448, 451).
- **9 June:** noon in St George's Bay, Grenada; General Maitland: all safe; at 1 p.m. the *Jason* brought General Prevost's news that the enemy had passed Prince Rupert's Head, Dominica, on the 6th, steering north (Nicolas vi. 452). Napoleon, the same day at Milan, thought Nelson was still in Europe (James iii. 347).
- **11 June:** off Guadeloupe (noon, Basse-Terre S 21° E 8 leagues) and under Montserrat (6:50 p.m., north end E ½ N 6 miles) (Victory's log, Nicolas vi. 451).
- **12 June:** 6 a.m. Antigua SE by E 7–8 leagues; 7 p.m. anchored in St John's Road; the *Curieux* sent home with dispatches. **13 June:** troops landed in the morning; 11:50 a.m. weighed for Europe; about an hour later the *Netley* came in with Carr's report of 32 enemy sail (Nicolas vi. 452–455; Corbett pp. 168–169). Clowes's "Early on June 13th, Nelson reached Antigua" is a log-date slip.

### Return passage (13 June – 19 July): south of the Azores, north of São Miguel

- Nelson first thought the French were bound for Cádiz or Toulon and steered for the Strait; he kept south of Villeneuve, who went north of the Azores. Log fixes (Nicolas vi. 467–471):
  - 29 June noon: Santa Maria (Azores) N 85° E, 210 leagues.
  - 1 July noon: Santa Maria S 89° E, 175 leagues.
  - 8 July, 11:30 a.m.: São Miguel ESE (compass) 17½ leagues; "standing to the Eastward, to go to the Northward of St. Michael's"; only 33 miles in 24 hours.
  - 13 July forenoon (signal log, when *Amazon* left): Cape St Vincent S 89° E, about 183 leagues. This does not fit Faden's track, which puts the 13 July noon tick about 135 leagues from the cape; the 183 leagues fits 11–12 July. Unresolved.
  - 17 July: Cape St Vincent in sight; "Our whole run from Barbuda, day by day, was 3459 miles" (diary).
  - 18 July noon: Cape Spartel S 75° E, 12 leagues.
- **19 July:** anchored at Gibraltar (Nicolas; James; Desbrière p. 47). **20 July:** Nelson went ashore "for the first time since June 16, 1803" (diary). Mahan once says he anchored on the 20th. Collingwood off Cádiz had four of the line (*Dreadnought*, *Mars*, *Colossus*, *Achille*), Bickerton off Cartagena four (Desbrière p. 48).

### North to Ushant and Spithead (22 July – 19 August)

- **22 July:** 9:30 a.m. weighed from Gibraltar; 7:30 p.m. anchored in Mazari Bay, Tetuan, for cattle and water (Mahan; James iii. 354: 8 p.m.). **23 July:** noon, weighed for Ceuta (James: 24th, log date). Night 24–25 July in the Gut in fog. **25 July, about 3:30 a.m.,** off Tarifa: the sloop *Termagant* brought a Lisbon paper reporting the *Curieux*'s sighting; Nelson decided to go north at once (Corbett p. 210; James iii. 354).
- Diary (Desbrière pp. 74–75, transcribed from BL Add. MS 34968, "très difficile à déchiffrer"): 27 July at daylight off Cape St Vincent, wind north; 29 July 143 miles from Cape St Vincent; contrary northerlies; **3 Aug lat 39°1′, long 16° W** (the page image reads 16°; the OCR reads 10°; James: 39°N 16°W); **7 Aug lat 41°8′, long 18°14′ W**; 12 Aug spoke the *Niobe*; 13 Aug noon spoke a ship from Cowes, "latitude 49°7, longitude 25° Ouest" as printed (the longitude is impossible two days from Ushant; Faden's track gives about 11°43′W); 15 Aug "18 lieues d'Ouessant", joined the Channel fleet.
- **15 August:** at noon the Channel fleet in sight (24 of the line); 3 p.m. Ushant SE by E 8–9 leagues; 6 p.m. saluted Cornwallis's *Ville de Paris* (Victory's log, Nicolas vii. 4–5). Nelson left nine ships with Cornwallis (*Belleisle* went to Plymouth) and was ordered on with *Victory* and *Superb* to Spithead.
- **17 August, 10:15 p.m.:** anchored at Dunnose; **18 August, 8:30 a.m.:** anchored at Spithead, then the Motherbank (Nicolas vii. 8). **19 August:** struck his flag and went to Merton (Nicolas vii, chronology).

## 4. Cape Finisterre, 22–24 July 1805 (Calder's action)

### Forces

- **Calder:** reinforced on 15 July by Stirling's five from Rochefort, he had **15 of the line**: *Prince of Wales* 98 (Vice-Admiral Sir Robert Calder), *Glory* 98 (Rear-Admiral Stirling), *Barfleur* 98, *Windsor Castle* 98, *Malta* 80, *Thunderer* 74, *Hero* 74, *Repulse* 74, *Defiance* 74, *Ajax* 74, *Warrior* 74, *Dragon* 74, *Triumph* 74, *Agamemnon* 64, *Raisonnable* 64; frigates *Egyptienne* and *Sirius*, lugger *Nile*, cutter *Frisk* (James iii. 356–357; Desbrière p. 61; Corbett p. 197). Ordered to cruise 30–40 leagues west of Finisterre; by 19 July about 100 miles W by N of the cape.
- **Villeneuve:** **20 of the line** (Spanish *Argonauta* 80, *Terrible* 74, *América* 64, *España* 64, *San Rafael* 80, *Firme* 74; French *Pluton*, *Mont-Blanc*, *Atlas*, *Berwick*, *Neptune* 80, *Bucentaure* 80, *Formidable* 80, *Intrépide*, *Scipion*, *Swiftsure*, *Indomptable* 80, *Aigle*, *Achille*, *Algésiras*), **7 frigates, 2 brigs**, and the recaptured galleon *Matilda* (James iii. 357–358; Desbrière pp. 60–61).

### Fog, position and the action of 22 July

- Morning of 22 July: thick fog. The allies, in three columns with the Spaniards on the right, steered ESE for Ferrol with a light WNW breeze; it cleared a little about noon (Reille). James: at about 11 a.m. *Indomptable* signalled 10 sail NNE. The British look-out *Defiance* signalled a strange fleet SSW about noon, first 24 then 27 sail (Corbett p. 196).
- **Positions:**
  - British, noon: **43°54′N 11°38′W (Greenwich)**, Ferrol ESE 49 leagues, Cape Finisterre SE 39 leagues (James iii. 358, apparently from the *Prince of Wales*'s log). Desbrière p. 61: Calder at 11:30 a.m. at **44°17′N 11°52′W**, heading WSW in two columns.
  - Allies: 43°34′N and 16°13′W of Paris (= 13°53′W Greenwich) on the morning of the 22nd (James iii. 357); 43°34′N "and, it seems, 13° or 14° of longitude, about 130 miles west of Ferrol" (Desbrière p. 57). Corbett: the flagships' noon positions were about 16 miles apart. The French DR longitude was evidently 1–2° too far west.
  - **Best estimate for the action:** about 43°30′–44°N, 11°30′–12°30′W, some 110–130 nautical miles WNW to NW of Cape Finisterre. Medium-low.
- The action began about 5:15–5:20 p.m. when *Hero* tacked and *Argonauta* opened fire; the lines fought on the starboard tack heading WSW in fog and smoke so thick that "no ship could see much beyond her own length" (James iii. 361). The first lieutenant of *Egyptienne* called it the thickest fog he remembered (Desbrière p. 62 n.). Firing died away about 8:30–9:30 p.m.
- **Prizes:** the Spanish ***San Rafael* 80** (Captain Francisco Montes) and ***Firme* 74** (Captain Rafael de Villavicencio), both dismasted after drifting to leeward into the British line, struck soon after 8 p.m. (James iii. 361). *San Rafael*: built Havana 1771, 2,130 tons, mounting 88 guns with 800 men and 104 soldiers; *Firme*: built Cádiz 1754, 1,805 tons (James iii. 370–371). They reached Plymouth on 30 or 31 July (Desbrière p. 64 n.: 30th; James: 31st) and served only as prison ships.
- **Casualties:**
  - British: **39 killed, 159 wounded** (James iii. 362, from Calder's return) or **41 killed, 158 wounded** (Corbett p. 202; Desbrière p. 64). *Windsor Castle* (foretopmast lost) and *Malta* suffered most.
  - Allies: Corbett's table (p. 202) gives 155 killed and 341 wounded in all, including *San Rafael* 53 killed / 114 wounded and *Firme* 41 / 97; Spanish 111 / 246, French 44 / 95. Desbrière p. 64: 82 dead and 179 wounded excluding the two prizes, with a footnote giving 180 killed and 40 wounded on *Firme* and 20 killed and 80 wounded on *San Rafael* (as printed; the *Firme* figure looks garbled). James iii. 362: the prizes' loss was "stated" at about 600 but he reduced the allies' total to 476 killed and wounded.

### The indecisive second day (23–24 July)

- Dawn 23 July: fleets about 17 miles apart, both scattered; Calder's van to windward, his crippled *Windsor Castle* and the prizes to leeward. He closed his fleet on the cripples and steered NE (James iii. 363–364).
- Villeneuve moved to the frigate *Hortense* and passed word that he meant to bring on a decisive action. Around noon to 3 p.m. the allies bore down in line abreast, but at about 3:15–4 p.m. hauled to the wind 3–4 leagues off (James iii. 364–365, quoting *Victoires et conquêtes*; Escaño in Desbrière p. 65). Villeneuve's journal gives his noon position as 43°41′N, 12°48′ (Paris, = 10°28′W Greenwich); the Spanish reports 43°34′, 9°13′ (meridian not stated).
- 24 July: the wind went round to north and NNE, giving Calder the weather gauge; he did not attack and continued with his prizes; by evening the fleets had lost sight of each other (James iii. 365); Corbett p. 205: no longer in sight on the morning of the 25th; Mahan: "on the 26th lost sight".

### Consequences for Calder

- His dispatch to Cornwallis was published with the cautious closing paragraph (about guarding against the Ferrol squadron and possibly falling back on Ushant) suppressed; the French official accounts claimed victory; public opinion expected a renewed battle (James iii. 368–369; Corbett p. 206).
- Nelson, sympathetic but critical ("He appears to have had the ships at Ferrol more in his head than the squadron in sight", to Gambier, 2 October, quoted by Corbett p. 207), let Calder go home in his own flagship: "October 14th. — Prince of Wales parted" (Victory's log, Nicolas vii. 121), so he missed Trafalgar.
- Court martial at his own request on board *Prince of Wales* in Portsmouth harbour, **23–26 December 1805**: not cowardice or disaffection, but **"severely reprimanded"** for not having done his utmost to renew the engagement on 23–24 July and take or destroy every enemy ship (James iii. 369; Corbett p. 206).

## 5. Vigo, Coruña and Ferrol, the turn south and Cádiz (25 July – 21 August)

### Vigo (28–31 July)

- 25 July noon: the *Didon* sighted Cape Finisterre ESE. Fearing dismastings in the rising NE weather, Villeneuve and Gravina first chose Cádiz (course SW by S), then at 5 p.m. Vigo (Desbrière p. 67). 26 July: the wind went south then south-west, and he tried for Ferrol again, but made no ground; his letter of 27 July was written "à 40 lieues à l'O.-S.-O. du cap Finistère" (Desbrière p. 85 n.). With sickness rising (*Achille* had 200 sick and five days' water) he turned for Vigo: 6 p.m. the Bayona (Cíes) islands in sight, and **on the evening of 28 July** the whole fleet anchored in Vigo Bay at nightfall (Desbrière pp. 68–69; Villeneuve's letters of 28 July "en rade de Vigo"; Mahan: "on the 28th, put into Vigo Bay"). James iii. 372: evening of the 26th.
- **Left at Vigo:** the French *Atlas* (with about 800 sick) and the Spanish *España* and *América*, as unfit to sail with the fleet (Desbrière p. 86; James iii. 373, who says they were simply slow sailers).
- **31 July:** sailed with **15 of the line** (13 French, 2 Spanish) despite a NW gale, gaining only 5 leagues that day (Desbrière p. 86; Corbett p. 212). James: 30 July (calendar error). With 7 frigates and 2 brigs (James).

### Coruña and Ferrol (1–13 August)

- **1 August:** the wind backed SW; the fleet ran north close along the coast and doubled Cape Finisterre in the evening. The same wind blew Calder, who had resumed the blockade of Ferrol on 29–31 July, well to the north-east of the port (Desbrière p. 86; James iii. 371; Corbett p. 218).
- **2 August:** Villeneuve entered the bay. As Gravina, leading, was already in the channel to Ferrol, a boat brought Napoleon's order of 16 July forbidding the fleet to enter Ferrol; the 13 French ships anchored at **Coruña**, the 2 Spanish went on into **Ferrol** (Desbrière p. 86). James iii. 373: entered Coruña on the evening of 1 August; Mahan: "reached Ferrol on the first of August"; Corbett p. 218 has him running into Coruña on 1 August and "on August 2nd … making the junction at Coruna". James's 1 August is consistent with his one-day calendar shift (14 thermidor = 2 August). **The brief's "around 1 August" is fair; the French documents say 2 August.**
- 3–7 August: revictualled to 45 days at reduced rations; no stores at Coruña, everything 8 miles up at Ferrol; Admiral Gourdon gravely ill (Desbrière p. 96).
- **Combined fleet assembled: 29 of the line, 6 frigates, 4 corvettes** (Desbrière pp. 98–99; Corbett p. 239):
  - French 18: *Bucentaure*, *Formidable*, *Neptune*, *Indomptable*, *Pluton*, *Scipion*, *Swiftsure*, *Mont-Blanc*, *Intrépide*, *Berwick*, *Achille*, *Algésiras*, *Aigle*, plus Gourdon's five from Ferrol: *Fougueux*, *Redoutable*, *Argonaute*, *Duguay-Trouin*, *Héros*. Frigates *Rhin*, *Hermione*, *Cornélie*, *Hortense*, *Thémis*; brigs *Furet*, *Argus*; *Observateur* and the schooner *Téméraire* joined at Ferrol. (*Atlas* at Vigo; *Sirène*, *Revanche*, *Guerrière* stayed at Ferrol; *Didon*, sent to find Allemand, was taken by the *Phoenix* on 10 August.)
  - Spanish 11: *Argonauta* and *Terrible* (the survivors of Gravina's original six) plus from Ferrol *Príncipe de Asturias* 112, *Neptuno* 80, *San Juan Nepomuceno*, *San Francisco de Asís*, *Monarca*, *San Agustín*, *San Ildefonso*, *San Fulgencio* (74; James: 64) and *Montañés*; frigate *Flora*, corvettes *Mercurio* and *Indagadora*. *San Julián* was left at Ferrol. Only one three-decker; six 80s; the rest 74s.
- **Sailing:** 8 August the Coruña ships came out and anchored; 9th gained a mile; **10 August** 5 French and 8 Spanish ships came out of Ferrol but the wind fell before the last three Spaniards cleared the narrows, and all anchored in the Bay of Ares (Villeneuve that day: "Je pars. Je me dirigerai suivant les circonstances sur Brest ou sur Cadix"); 11–12 August east wind and calm; **13 August** before noon the last three joined and by 2 p.m. the whole fleet was under sail (Desbrière pp. 97–98). Corbett p. 239: all together outside on the 13th; Escaño's journal under 13 August: "We sailed for Cadiz". Villeneuve's letter of 22 August: "étant parti de devant le Ferrol, le 25 thermidor (13 août)" (Desbrière p. 100). James iii. 373–374: weighed on the 9th, anchored at "Zerez" (Ares) on the 10th, out to sea on the 11th.

### The turn south (nightfall, 15 August)

- 13 August afternoon: very fresh NE wind; ran west until about 6 p.m.; then, a British warship being seen, stood NNE towards Cape Prior; 2 a.m. on the 14th resumed NW (Reille, Desbrière p. 100). The frigate *Iris* (Captain Edward Brace) watched the fleet: course W by N ½ N, position 44°17′N 9°26′W (Brace's report enclosed in Blackwood's dispatch of 16 August, Corbett p. 259 n.); she dropped it about 2 p.m. on the 14th.
- 14 August evening: the *Dragon*, with *Phoenix* towing the captured *Didon*, saw the allies (26 sail) at sunset; a Danish merchantman she had boarded, told that the *Dragon* belonged to a fleet of 25 of the line, passed this on to a French frigate (Corbett p. 262; James iii. 374).
- 15 August: still WNW against a NE gale (*San Francisco de Asís* lost her main topmast). Reille's noon position: about "40 lieues Ouest-nord-ouest du Cap Finisterre" (Corbett p. 263 n.); Corbett deduces a mean course due west (true) from Ferrol. At nightfall Villeneuve turned for Cádiz: he decided "le 27 (15 août), à l'entrée de la nuit" (letter of 22 August, Desbrière pp. 102–103 and p. 112 n.). Corbett p. 265 n. calls the 80 leagues impossible from the noon position. Desbrière once misprints the date as "le 27 (13 août)" and calls the 16th "cette journée décisive"; the letter itself says 15 August. James iii. 375 implies the evening of the 14th.
- **Estimated turning point:** about 43°40′–44°20′N, 11°45′–12°30′W (from Reille's noon position at 40 leagues WNW of Finisterre plus an afternoon's run west). Desbrière's "Situation au 15 août" staff map puts the fleet further north, near 45°N (plate, leaf n126: https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n126/mode/1up); Corbett rejects it. Low confidence.

### South to Cádiz (16–20 August)

- Ran south before the NE wind, out of sight of Galicia and Portugal, chasing every sail; Escaño lumps 15–18 August together (Corbett p. 264).
- Off Cape St Vincent: 17 August (Desbrière p. 106 text) or **18 August** (James iii. 375; Desbrière's map label "Cap St. Vincent 18 Août"); took and burnt three merchantmen of a Gibraltar–Lisbon convoy under the brig *Halcyon*, which escaped. On the 18th Blackwood in *Euryalus*, off the cape, heard from a Dane of a large fleet to the westward and shadowed it (Corbett p. 267).
- **20 August:** at 10 a.m., Cádiz NE about 9 leagues, steering SE with the wind WSW, the allies sighted Collingwood's three ships right ahead (James iii. 375). Desbrière p. 106: at daybreak on the 20th, still some leagues from Cádiz. Magon's light squadron chased Collingwood off towards the Strait, and the fleet **anchored in Cádiz road that evening** (Desbrière p. 107; James: bore up for the harbour at 3 p.m.; Corbett p. 271: "on the 20th the whole fleet ignominiously re-entered Cadiz"; Collingwood to his wife, 21 August: "what a squeeze we had like to have got yesterday"). Villeneuve's report is dated "en rade de Cadix, le 4 fructidor (22 août)".
- **21 August** is the date in the *Précis des événements militaires* (vol. xii, p. 71: "entra à Cadix le 21 août, le jour même qu'il était attendu à Brest"), quoted and rejected by James iii. 375 on the evidence of the ships' rôles d'équipage. **The brief's "around 20 August" is right.**

### Collingwood's squadron off Cádiz

- 20 August: *Dreadnought* 98 (Vice-Admiral Collingwood, Captain Rotheram), *Colossus* 74 (Morris), *Achille* 74 (King), with the frigate *Endymion* and the bomb *Thunder*. *Mars* 74 (Duff), his fourth ship, rejoined from Tangier about midnight. He drew off about 21 miles towards the Strait, meaning to fall back on Bickerton if pursued, and before daylight on the **21st** resumed his station off the port (James iii. 375; Desbrière p. 107 and n.; Corbett pp. 270–271). Collingwood counted "36 sail of men-of-war" in the combined fleet and expected them to sail again "reinforced by eight from that port".
- Reinforcements: Bickerton's ships from Cartagena (Desbrière names *Queen*, *Minotaur*, *Bellerophon* and others, arriving from 22 August; Corbett: "By the 28th the two squadrons were together"). **30 August:** Calder arrived with 18 of the line (Corbett p. 271) or 21 including *Dragon* and *Goliath* (Desbrière p. 109), bringing the British force off Cádiz to about 26 (from Corbett) or 32 (Desbrière).
- Combined fleet at Cádiz: the 29 that arrived plus the Spanish ships already in port; James iii. 375: 35 of the line in all, including six Spaniards previously there.

## 7. Waypoints for drawing the routes (Mercator)

Latitudes N; longitudes E or W of Greenwich. "Est." means my estimate (reason given). DR positions are French or British dead reckoning at noon. Positions read off Corbett's chart (after Faden for Nelson, after Desbrière for Villeneuve) were measured on the full-resolution scan against the printed 10° graticule, which is Mercator to within a few pixels; I checked them against independent log fixes (29 June, 1 July, 8 July, 3 August, 7 August, 14 May, 19 June), which agree within about 0.3°. Expect ±0.3° on those rows.

### A. Villeneuve, Toulon to Cádiz (the leg Stage A draws)

The *Atlas* noon latitudes are firm; her longitudes are recorded only for 1–3 and 10 April, so the longitudes for 4–8 April are estimates that keep the line at sea in the channels described by the sources. ## Open questions

1. The dating convention of Desbrière's second *Atlas* table (Cádiz–Martinique): printed dates or the following day? Checking the manuscript log (Archives de la Marine BB4 231) or Desbrière's *Projets et tentatives* vol. V would settle it.
2. Where exactly the *Curieux* saw the allies on 19 June: 33°12′N 58°W (Corbett's text, James) or about 30°N (Corbett's own chart, from Desbrière's chart).
3. The 13 July signal-log fix for Nelson (Cape St Vincent S 89° E, 183 leagues) does not fit Faden's track for that day.
4. The allied casualties at Finisterre, especially on the two prizes: Corbett's table, James's amended total and Desbrière's footnote all differ.
5. The position where Villeneuve turned south on 15 August: Reille's 40 leagues versus Villeneuve's 80 leagues WNW of Finisterre, and Desbrière's staff map further north still.
6. The exact longitudes of the Toulon–Cádiz leg on 4–8 April (only latitudes are in the *Atlas*'s log); a Spanish or French account of the passage of Cape de Gata would pin 8 April.
7. Whether Corbett's daily ticks on Faden's chart are noon positions in civil or log time; the four log fixes checked fit civil dates.

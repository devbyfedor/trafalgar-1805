# A-H3: adjudication of the fact-checks of the H3 signal notes

Adjudicates `docs/research/scene-02/v-h3a.md` (summary, §§1–2, H3-01 to H3-14) and `v-h3b.md` (§§3–7, H3-15 to H3-32), which check `h3-signals.md`. All three were read, not edited. Session 7, 29 October 2026.

**Method.**
- I took every verdict that is not a plain "confirmed", the "unverifiable" verdicts that bear on the screen, and the points in both reports' "missed" and "scene" sections that would change what the scene shows.
- For each one I opened the primary or period page myself:
  - NRS *Logs of the Great Sea Fights* II: the Euryalus signal tables, viewed as page images (pp. 160, 161, 165, 167), and the log text;
  - Hudson's 1917 transcription of Nelson's diary (page images, pp. 23–24);
  - Beatty 1807; Nicolas VII; Corbett 1910; James III (page image, p. 389);
  - Cumby's letter in *The Nineteenth Century* (Nov 1899);
  - the 1799 *Night Signals*; Popham 1803; Perrin 1922;
  - Fraser 1906; Desbrière 1907 (annexes);
  - Willaumez 1831 and Smyth 1867 (for terms); Mahan 1897.
- Modern sources (FOTW, Nicolls) are paraphrased only.

- Times are ship's time as logged. NRS dates are civil dates (both checkers agree, and the contents bear it out).
- Compass bearings are converted to true with about 20° W variation (Corbett 333 n. 1, from the Montañés log).
- Scan links use archive.org indexes (`/page/nNNN`), checked against archive.org's page map. Pages marked "image" I viewed.

**Bottom line.**
- **v-h3b is right on almost everything it corrected.** The 19 October chain ran about **due west (true)** of Cádiz and was **48–58 nm** long. No. 370 went Sirius → Euryalus (7.00) → Phoebe (7.20). The **last link** was a colour-blind "distant signal" (flag, ball and pendant at different mastheads) that the Victory answered directly. The relay took about **2½ hours on the logged clocks**, not "within two hours". The Naiad's 31 sail was at **5 p.m.**
- **v-h3a is right** on these points:
  - the "three minute guns" attribution;
  - the lantern figures (they serve the fleet's manoeuvres, not the look-outs' reports);
  - "amorces" (brief powder flashes, separate from blue lights);
  - the visibility arithmetic;
  - the wear lights (three per ship by the 1799 book).
- **v-h3a is wrong** in two places: it says No. 370 rests on Corbett alone (the Euryalus signal log has it), and it guesses that No. 370 was a manuscript addition (the evidence points to the printed book).
- **Still open:** the Agamemnon's part in the relay of the 19th.

## Evidence

### R1–R3. Direction and length of the 19 October chain (SUM-1c, H3-18c, H3-18d, H3-18e)

**Notes.** §3.3 and the summary give "about 50 nm ... west to west-south-west" and "about a degree of longitude west". They call E.N.E. "the outlier" but still recommend "west to west-south-west".

**Checkers.**
- v-h3a calls it disputed: 48–58 nm, nearly due west (true).
- v-h3b corrects it: 16–19 leagues; the original diary reads ESE, so the chain ran due west.

**Sources opened.**
- **Hudson 1917, p. 23 (image).** The original diary for 19 October, transcribed from the manuscript at the Probate Registry (introduction, p. 9), reads "Cadiz bearing ESE by compass distance 16 leagues". https://archive.org/details/nelsonslastdiary00nelsuoft/page/n28/mode/1up
- **Beatty 1807, appendix p. 97**, printed from the same "memorandum book": "Cadiz bearing E.S.E. by compass, distance sixteen leagues". https://archive.org/details/b22036611_0001/page/n108/mode/1up
- **Nicolas VII 132.** Nelson to Lady Hamilton, headed "Noon, Cadiz, E.S.E., 16 Leagues". https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/n167/mode/1up
- **Nicolas VII 133.** The same diary entry "[From Clarke and M'Arthur, vol. ii. p. 441]" reads "E.N.E. by compass". https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/n168/mode/1up
- **Nicolas VII 131**, narrative: "about fifty miles W.S.W. of Cadiz". https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/n166/mode/1up
- **James III 389 (image)**: the fleet "lying to about 16 leagues west-south-west from Cadiz". https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n406/mode/1up
- **Browne (Victory), NRS II 194**: "(Cadiz bearing east, 16 leagues), received intelligence at 10 a.m.". https://archive.org/details/logsofgreatseaf02jack/page/n211/mode/1up
- **Corbett 333 n. 1.** He read E.N.E. in Nicolas and doubted it: the Victory's noon position, 36°36′ N 7°30′ W, "makes Cadiz bear about 19 leagues E.S.E. magnetic and about E. true". He takes the variation of 20° W from the Montañés log. https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n368/mode/1up
- **Mahan II**: the main body "fifty miles west of Cadiz", with "two groups of ships-of-the-line extended between these extremes". https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/16915

**Arithmetic (mine).** Cádiz is at about 36°32′ N 6°18′ W.
- The noon position is Δλ = 72′ × cos 36.6° ≈ 58 nm west and 4′ north: about 58 nm on a bearing of about 274° true from Cádiz.
- From the Victory, Cádiz then bears about 94° true, which is about 114° magnetic: E.S.E. by compass.
- Nelson's 16 leagues at E.S.E. magnetic puts the Victory about 48 nm at about 272° true, near 36°34′ N 7°18′ W.
- E.N.E. magnetic would be about NE true, which Corbett already showed cannot fit a chase S.E. towards the Straits.

**Ruling.** The original manuscript (Hudson), the earliest printing (Beatty 1807), the letter written that noon and the log position all agree on about due east (true) for Cádiz. The E.N.E. reading is a copying slip in Clarke and M'Arthur, and the W.S.W. versions are its mirror image. Checker right. On length, both checkers' 48–58 nm is the honest range.

**Scene impact.**
- Draw the chain about due west from Cádiz along about 36°32′–36°36′ N.
- Put the fleet at about 7°18′–7°30′ W, 48–58 nm out (16–19 leagues), not WSW.
- Inshore end, from the logs (compass bearings converted at 20° W):
  - **Sirius**, at first light: Cádiz NE by E, 4 miles, so about 4 nm SW (true) of Cádiz (NRS II 312).
  - **Euryalus**, at 5.30: about 4 nm SSW of San Sebastián (NRS II 145).
  - **Euryalus**, at noon: 7–8 nm WSW of San Sebastián, latitude observed 36°28′ N (NRS II 146).
  - **Phoebe**, at 8 a.m.: WNW by compass from the Euryalus, about due west true (NRS II 146).
  - **Phoebe and Naiad**, at noon: NW by W by compass from the Sirius, about W by N true (NRS II 312).
  - **Defence**, at 8 a.m.: "west" by compass from the Euryalus's masthead, about WSW true (NRS II 146).

### R4. "As near as possible in the latitude" (H3-15)

- Nelson to Blackwood, 4 October, "Cadiz, East 17 leagues". The Mars, Colossus and Defence were to be "four leagues East from the Fleet, and one of them advanced to the East towards Cadiz, and as near as possible in the latitude". The fleet was to be "from sixteen to eighteen leagues West of Cadiz" (Nicolas VII 73). https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/n108/mode/1up
- **Ruling:** the notes quote the letter but leave out the phrase. Checker right.
- **Scene impact:** confirms the east–west line of R1.

**Twilight check (mine).** At 36.5° N on 19 October (declination about −10°), local apparent time gives:
- sunrise about 6.26;
- civil twilight from about 6.00;
- nautical twilight from about 5.30.

So "At 4" in the Sirius log cannot be the hour of the sighting; it is the watch heading. The 6.04 telegraph at first light is consistent.

**Rulings.**
- **R5.** No. 370 went Sirius → Euryalus → Phoebe, and the Naiad came in only from 8.10. v-h3b right. The links between the Phoebe and the Mars are not in any printed log; the Defence and the Colossus are inferred from the station orders and Beatty 6.
- **R8.** The 6.04 and 7.00 times are logged. v-h3b right; v-h3a's "rests on Corbett alone" is wrong.
- **R9.** 7.20, not 7.10. v-h3b right.
- **R23.** "Three minute guns" is the Euryalus's log describing the Phoebe. v-h3a right; the notes' §1.4 already had it right.

**Scene impact.**
- First pulse:
  - 6.04: telegraph hoists at the Sirius;
  - 7.00: No. 370, Sirius to Euryalus;

## Notes for whoever writes the Scene 2 section of `history-notes.md`

1. **Nelson's diary for 20 October has two sources.**
   - The original manuscript lacks the leaf with the first half of the entry. That half is known only from the detached autograph leaf (J. Wild) printed by Nicolas.
   - The rest ("At 5 telegraph'd ..." to "at 4 a.m. wore and stood to the NE") survives in the manuscript (Hudson pp. 23–24). It agrees with Beatty 1807, not with Nicolas's facsimile text.
2. **Times are ship's times.**
   - The Euryalus ran about 45 minutes ahead of the Victory on the 20th (Corbett).
   - The twilight check suggests that on the 19th the Euryalus was near local time and the Victory slow.
   - So "9.30" is the Victory's clock. Cross-ship intervals carry ± 45 minutes.
3. **Glyph positions on the morning of the 19th** come from the logs' compass bearings converted at about 20° W variation (see R1). They are good to a few miles at best.

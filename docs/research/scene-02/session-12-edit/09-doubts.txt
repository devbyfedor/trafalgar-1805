@@@ Doubts, the British off Cádiz: the Prince of Wales, the Donegal, the Royal Sovereign (a-v-merged-h2 6); then Nelson's September passage (September passage, F; a-v-merged-lead, decision 6)
<<<

===
On 18 October coast signals told Villeneuve that four of the line had left Gibraltar with a convoy (Desbrière p. 141); Louis had five (Austen). The Prince of Wales parted on the evening of 13 October (Beatty, pp. 5–6; Corbett p. 330; Desbrière p. 148 n.) or on the 14th (James III 383; Clowes V 128); the Victory's log enters it under "October 14th", a nautical day that began at noon on the 13th. The Donegal left for Gibraltar on 17 October (Nelson's diary, Beatty, p. 97; James III 383; Clowes V 128) or on the 18th (Beatty's narrative, p. 6, probably the log's nautical date). The Royal Sovereign joined on 8 October (Nelson, Nicolas VII 88 n., 107; Corbett p. 328); James (III 383) and Clowes (V 128) put her among the ships that joined between the 9th and the 13th (`research/scene-02/a-h2b.md`, H2-18, H2-18h).
- **Nelson's September passage.** "27 leagues S.W. from Scilly" is drawn by compass; read as true it lies 35 nm WNW, and the mean of the two, as drawn for Cape Sicié, lies between them. The noon latitude of 23 September is drawn as 42°25′ N; the manuscript's "2′5" could be 42°02½′, which the six-hour run from Finisterre in a fresh beam gale would favour. Desbrière gives the San Sebastián bearing of the 28th at 8 a.m. (p. 147), the log at 8 p.m. The Euryalus was sent ahead at 4 p.m. on the 26th by the diary, James and Beatty, with a letter dated the 25th (Nicolas VII 49), or from off Cape St Vincent by Corbett (p. 317). Duff names the Euryalus among the ships that joined with Nelson (Nicolas VII 70 n.), although she had been sent ahead on the 26th. The Victory's own logs, read only in Nicolas's extracts, would settle these and give a latitude and longitude for every noon. When the Victory moved from the Motherbank to St Helens before 14 September is not recorded in the sources read; the line starts at Spithead so as to join the Ushant line. Low.

The distance between the fleets at daylight runs from 6 to 12 nm in the sources: 10–12 from the Victory by her logs (9–10 at 8 a.m.), James and Browne, about 12 by Beatty, four leagues by the Neptune and the Britannia, 9 by the Belleisle and an officer quoted by Fraser, about 8 by Wheeler (1995), 6–7 by Collingwood, about 2½ leagues by Villeneuve, about three by the Algésiras and "dos leguas" by the Montañés, while Gravina's squadron, to windward and so nearer, had the British 4–5 miles off; the scene uses 9–12 nm from the Victory, as scene 1 does.

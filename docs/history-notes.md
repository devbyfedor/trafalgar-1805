# History notes

Facts the scene uses, with their sources, confidence and doubts. This file is the summary; the full research notes, with measurements, tables, short quotations from period sources and every URL, are in `docs/research/` (what may and may not go there: `docs/research/README.md`):

- `research/victory-hull.md`: Victory's hull as of October 1805 (dimensions, ports, paint, details, what not to copy from the present ship).
- `research/victory-rig.md`: masts, spars, sails, rigging and flags, with an elevation coordinate table.
- `research/dawn-setting.md`: positions, sun, moon, weather, both fleets, and a fact-check of 54 claims against independent sources (its last section).

Confidence: **high** means several independent primary sources agree, a museum artefact, or a computation confirmed by a second ephemeris; **medium** means one good source or a consistent inference; **low** means an estimate, a measurement from a photograph, or conflicting sources.

Times are local apparent time (ship's time). Bearings in 1805 logs are magnetic; the variation off Cádiz was about 21° W, so true = magnetic − 21°.

## The setting

- **Place and layout.** The British fleet was about 20 nm west of Cape Trafalgar (the Cape "bore E by S about seven leagues", Collingwood). The Combined Fleet lay between it and the Spanish coast, 9–12 nm east of Victory, strung out north–south over about 5 nm, heading south on the starboard tack. Layout high; distances medium.
- **Sunrise.** Upper limb at 06:27 local apparent time (06:36 UT), azimuth 102.5° true; the whole disc clear by about 06:30. Civil dawn 06:01, nautical dawn 05:31; the logs' "daylight" falls at 05:30–06:10. The sun climbs at about 53° to the horizon, up and to the right. High (computed; matches USNO and JPL Horizons DE441).
- **Moon.** A waning crescent about 2 % lit, rising at 05:19 in the east and about 12° up at sunrise; new moon on 22 October. The night had been moonless. No account mentions seeing it. High (computed). The scene shows it faintly at p = 0 and lets it fade.
- **Stars.** The scene's sparse stars fade in the first third of the scroll, before civil dawn would have washed them out. Mercury (magnitude −1.1) was about 5° up in the east at civil dawn, deep in the glow and haze; nobody records it, and the scene leaves it out.
- **Sky and cloud.** The night's squalls had passed. Gravina's flagship logged squall cloud in the north-east and south-east quadrants at dawn, so cloud banks on the eastern horizon are supported; several British logs say "cloudy". The scene has low, broken strands near the sunrise. High.
- **Wind.** Very light (Beaufort 1–2), westerly, dying toward calm by noon. Victory's log at 08:00: "light breezes and cloudy". High for "light westerly"; the exact direction is disputed (NW, WNW, W and WSW in different logs, all magnetic).
- **Swell.** "A great swell from the westward" (Euryalus), "the sea smooth with a great ground swell setting from the westward" (James), "la houle de l'Ouest très forte" (French logs). Few or no whitecaps. High. Period and height were not recorded; the scene uses a long, slow swell.
- **Visibility.** At least 10–12 nm: the enemy's 33 ships were counted at that range. Their hulls were partly below the horizon (3–12 m hidden from an eye 5–10 m up), so sails and upper works show and waterlines do not. Each rig subtends about 0.3 of the sun's diameter. High (computed).

## The fleets

- **British.** 27 ships of the line. At dawn they were in loose cruising order, not yet two tidy columns ("sans ordre", Dumanoir and Scipion), and the columns formed over about two hours. Once formed, the first two ships astern of Victory in the weather column were the three-deckers Téméraire (98) and Neptune (98). Collingwood's lee column was to the south, out of a north-facing frame. High. The scene shows Téméraire, Neptune and a 74 astern, faint in the haze.
- **Combined Fleet.** 33 ships of the line (18 French, 15 Spanish), plus 5 frigates and 2 brigs; easy sail at dawn (mostly topsails), close-hauled on the starboard tack, heading south, a ragged line of about 5 nm. The crescent shape came only after Villeneuve's order to wear (about 08:00–08:30), finished around 10:00, so the dawn line is a ragged straight line, not a crescent. High. Seen from the west, ships heading south show their bows to the right.
- **The enemy's paint** (one eyewitness, Midshipman Badcock): some black and yellow like the British, some with a single red or yellow band, some all black; the Santísima Trinidad had four red bands with white between. Irrelevant at silhouette scale; against the sunrise they read as soft mid-tone silhouettes.

## HMS Victory as of October 1805

After the 1800–1803 great repair. The most valuable source is the contemporary 1:48 model made during that repair (Royal Museums Greenwich SLR0513), photographed broadside-on; several heights and positions are measured from those photographs and from RMG plans (ZAZ0120–0122), scaled from the 186 ft gun deck.

### Hull
- Gun deck 186 ft (56.7 m); figurehead to taffrail about 226 ft 6 in (69.0 m); extreme breadth 51 ft 10 in. High.
- Armament at Trafalgar: 104 guns. Guns per side 15 lower, 14 middle, 15 upper, 6 quarterdeck, 2 forecastle (one a carronade), so some ports carried no gun. High.
- Ports (see the table above). Port spacing about 11.5 ft; the middle-deck ports are staggered between the others. The eight central upper-deck ports (3rd–10th from forward) and the forward quarterdeck openings had no lids, so they show as dark openings even with the ports closed. Medium.
- Heights above the waterline (measured, about ±1 ft): lower port sills about 4 ft 6 in; bands at about 1.5–2.7, 3.7–5.0 and 5.9–7.0 m; channels about 7 m (above the upper-deck ports, moved there in 1800–03); waist rail about 8.8 m, quarterdeck 10.1, forecastle 10.3–10.9, poop at the taffrail about 12.5. Medium-low.
- Sheer: gentle, lowest a little abaft amidships, rising about 1.5 m at the stern and 1.7 m at the bow. Medium-low.
- Paint: black (near-neutral) hull and lids, pale ochre bands: the Nelson chequer, correct for Trafalgar (when she left the repair in 1803 the lids were still ochre). Port linings and inner lid faces red. Medium-high. The whole of the British lower masts, hoops included, were painted yellow; the enemy's mast hoops were black, and Nelson had Belleisle and Polyphemus paint theirs to avoid confusion (Nicolas VII 131). High.
- Copper: only a thin strip shows at the waterline. Low (inferred; no documented figure).
- Bow: an open beakhead with a bulkhead at upper-deck level, three curved headrails, catheads, and a figurehead of the crowned royal arms held by two cupids (blue sash to starboard). The round bow of pre-1922 photographs is the 1814 bow and wrong for 1805. Medium-high.
- Stern: closed in 1803, three tiers of windows; the quarter gallery has three tiers from the middle deck to the quarterdeck; no stern davits (removed in 1800–03). Medium-high.

### Rig and sails
- Spars from David Steel's 1794 Royal Navy table for a 100-gun ship of 2,164 tons (Victory: 2,142). It matches her surviving 1803 fore topsail (80 ft foot, 54 ft head, 54 ft deep) within a foot or two. Main mast 117 ft, topmast 70, topgallant 35; main yard 102 ft 4 in; bowsprit 74 ft at "nearly thirty-six degrees" (drawn at 34°), jib-boom 53 ft. Medium (Steel as a close proxy; McKay's Victory-specific tables were not available).
- Mast positions by the Royal Navy rule (fore 1/9, main 5/9, mizzen 17/20 of the gun deck from the stem); rake about 0.5°, 2° and 3.5°. Low-medium.
- Sails set: fore and main course, topsail, topgallant, royal; mizzen topsail and topgallant; the spanker on gaff and boom (by 1805 a gaff and boom had replaced the old mizzen yard; logs of 1805 say "spanker"); fore topmast staysail, jib, flying jib. Medium-high.
- Topsails had four reef bands (Victory's log, 24 October: "out fourth reef of main topsail") and straight feet; courses one reef band plus a middle band, feet cut slightly hollow. High (main topsail), medium (others).
- The wind was 2–3 points abaft the beam, so the yards were braced about 30–40° from square, turning the sails' forward, sunlit faces toward a viewer on the starboard side. In strict broadside they show about 50–64 % of their width. The ship is drawn with yards braced about 30° and sails at 64 % width, as period ship portraitists (Pocock, Turner) did. Medium.
- Rigging: shrouds about 10 fore, 11 main, 7 mizzen a side (from the restored ship and a kit manual, not an 1805 source); topmast shrouds about 5/5/4. Low-medium.

### Flags
- Vice-admiral's flag of the White (white, plain red St George's cross) at the fore topgallant masthead: confirmed by the 1808 Regulations, an RMG catalogue note and Pocock's 1805 drawing. High for the position.
- White Ensign with the post-1801 Union canton, at the peak of the spanker gaff when flown under sail (Perrin 1922). High for design and position. **Not flown at dawn** (owner's decision, see below); the spec keeps it, with its position and size, for the approach scene.
- No commission pennant while an admiral's flag flies. Medium-high.
- Ensigns were about twice as long as deep (a surviving 1794 ensign is 20 × 40 ft). The flags are drawn about 1:2 and foreshortened, since they stream toward the viewer as well as to the right.
- The Union Jacks on the stays and "England expects" (about 11:45) belong to late morning, not dawn, and are not shown.

## Assumptions and licences

1. **Ports closed at dawn.** An assumption, as the brief says. The lidless ports still show as openings.
2. **No White Ensign at dawn** (owner's decision, session 3). The Euryalus log and James have colours hoisted at about 12:15, as the action began; no source has ensigns flying at dawn. The brief asked for one; the owner removed it. It will be hoisted in the approach scene, before battle, as a deliberate moment.
3. **The vice-admiral's flag at dawn.** Kept (owner's decision, session 3): as he understands it, a flag officer's flag flew for as long as he was aboard in command. The sources do not clearly say otherwise. For: Basil Hall (via Perrin) has the commission pendant never hauled down, day or night, and in a flagship the admiral's flag takes its place; the 1808 Regulations keep even a fallen commander-in-chief's flag flying until the battle ends. Against, at most: the Euryalus log and James have the admirals "hoisting their respective flags" at about 12:16, which can also be read as battle flags added to the ones already flying. No source says Nelson's flag was down at dawn.
4. **No studding sails in scene 1** (owner's decision, session 3). The log has them set on bearing up, about 20–30 minutes after sunrise, and later accounts have them on both sides; at the moment of sunrise Victory was most likely still steering N by E under reefed topsails and foresail. They belong to the approach scene, where the fleet sets every scrap of canvas in the light wind (`docs/backlog.md`).
5. **The British in a column.** At dawn they were in loose order; the scene shows the weather column already forming astern of Victory, which is how it looked within the next two hours.
6. **Scale of the distant ships.** At true angular size the enemy rigs would be a few pixels tall. They are drawn larger, still about a third of the sun's diameter as the research advises, so they read as ships.
7. **The sun in frame.** Artistic licence, accepted by the owner: the real sun rose behind a north-facing viewer's right shoulder (see 8), but the scene shows it rising on the right of the frame.
8. **Viewpoint.** A side view looking north with the sun and the enemy on the right is a stylised panorama: the real enemy line ran north–south across 90° of the eastern horizon, and the sun rose at 102.5° true, behind a north-facing viewer's right shoulder.

## Open doubts

- Lower-deck ports: 16 a side (a Trafalgar midshipman, the 2012 official site, the present ship) against 15 on the contemporary model. Settled by the owner for the scene: 15, as on the model, matching her 15 lower-deck guns a side.
- The main truck height (59.5–61 m by reconstruction against the quoted 62.5 m) depends on the assumed heel depth and Steel's long-pole rule. The owner accepted the 60.2 m reconstruction from Steel's 1794 tables.
- Gaff, spanker boom, flying jib-boom and dolphin striker lengths are rule-based estimates.
- Quarterdeck (7 or 8) and forecastle (2 or 3) openings; the poop bulwark (open rail on the model, solid in one reconstruction).
- Red inside the ports: the museum's specifications use red, but a summary of Goodwin's 2013 paint article says the 1805 accounts list no red. Deferred by the owner until a scene shows the ports open (`docs/backlog.md`); in scene 1 the colour shows only through the lidless upper-deck, quarterdeck and forecastle openings.
- The exact wind direction at dawn; the distances to the enemy (6–12 nm across sources) and to the coast.
- Whether the Spanish coast was visible from deck at first light (certainly seen later in the morning).
- Not consulted first-hand: McKay's *Anatomy of the Ship* drawings and spar tables, McGowan (2003), Goodwin's *Pocket Manual* (2015) and his 2013 *Mariner's Mirror* article.

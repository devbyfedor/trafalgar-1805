# V-H5: adversarial check of `h5-names.md` (the 1805 English forms of the names scene 2's chart adds now)

Written 6 November 2026 by the independent checker. Material checked: `docs/research/scene-02/h5-names.md` (its settled table first, then the evidence name by name), under the rule of `a-h4.md` as amended by `a-v-a-h4.md`. Nothing in `D:/dev/trafalgar-1805` was edited.

Status: in progress (written name by name as the check goes).

## How I checked

- **Charts.** For every form a ruling turns on, I opened the cited crop myself (the earlier sessions' downloads, or a new IIIF crop where none existed) and read the letters. Where I could, I looked for further English charts and maps of 1795-1810 that the research did not use, to try to refute the form.
- **Texts.** I recounted the forms in the archive.org OCR texts already downloaded (line-break hyphens joined, long s normalised, case ignored; orders of magnitude only) and read the contexts the rulings rest on.
- **Sites.** I tested every site against Natural Earth 10 m (`data/raw/natural-earth/`: land, minor islands, rivers): a town on its shore or inland where it is, a cape at its tip, an island's centre on the island, a water name on the water, a river label near its river line.
- **The rule.** (1) Where the English charts of 1800-1807 agree, letter their form. (2) Where they disagree, drop any form a visitor would misread. (3) Then the naval and printed usage of 1805 decides. (4) Faden 1807 breaks a tie. Type: ordinary characters, "St." with a stop, "C." for capes, no accents, spaced capitals only for oceans and seas.

### C. Grisnez: confirmed, but on different grounds, and at medium confidence

- Form **C. Grisnez**, cape, 50.870 N, 1.584 E. On Natural Earth's coast (0.05 km); NE's westernmost vertex there is 50.869 N, 1.581 E, 0.3 km away. Good.
- What h5 gets wrong:
  - It calls C. Grisnez "the one clear chart form" and rates the form high on LWBC and ST09. Neither plate is of 1800-1807 (1794 and 1809).
  - ST09 is not clear: at native resolution (crop `8800,1860,240,90/full/0/default.jpg`, enlarged) the fourth letter has the same hooked descender as the final z. I read **C. Griznez** there (doubtful), not Grisnez.
  - The one English chart of 1800-1807 I found with the cape, KN06 (12 January 1806), engraves **Gris Nez**, two words, without "C." (as it sets "Blanc Nez").
- So the English charts of 1794-1809 disagree: C. Grisnez (LWBC 1794, read), Gris Nez (KN06 1806, read), probably C. Griznez (ST09 1809).
- Policy 2 drops nothing decisive ("Griznez" and Norie's "Gris ness" read as slips today; "Grisnez" and "Gris Nez" do not).
- Policy 3, usage of 1804-05, which I recounted with OCR variants and read in context:
  - *Naval Chronicle* 12 (1804): "Cape Grisnez" 1, plus "Grisncz" 1 (an OCR slip for Grisnez, in a dispatch dated off Boulogne, 23 October 1804), and one "Grisney".
  - *Naval Chronicle* 13 (1805): "Cape Grisnez" 3, plus 2 that OCR garbles ("Grisnci", "Grisiu-x": the Archer's report of 25 April 1805), one "Griznes", and one "Griz-Nez" (a table of bearings: "The Signal House on Cape Griz-Nez").
  - Griznez: Malham (1795) 1, *Naval Chronicle* 15 (1806) 1, *New Annual Register* (1805) 1. Norie: "Cape Gris ness" 1.
  - So "Grisnez" is clearly the commonest printed form of 1804-05. Faden 1807 does not letter the cape.
- Verdict: the form stands under policy 3. Change the table's ground ("LWBC, ST09") to "usage of 1804-05; LWBC 1794", and lower the confidence to medium. Add **C. Gris Nez** (KN06, 1806) to the alternatives and to doubt 5: it is the one in-range chart form, equally period, and the closest to today's Cap Gris-Nez. Read strictly (only plates dated 1800-1807 count at step 1, and a single plate counts as agreement), the rule would give C. Gris Nez; I do not recommend that reading, because the plates on both sides of 1806 and the printed usage of 1804-05 all close the name up.

### St. Helens Road: corrected to **St. Helens**

- The spelling holds: every chart of 1800-1807 that names the roadstead writes "Helens", single l, no apostrophe (C01H 1801 Sᵗ HELENS ROAD in spaced italic capitals, read; KN06 1806 "Sᵗ Helens", read; C07E 1807 "Sᵗ Helens Rᵈ", read). LWBC's and C01H's village "Hellens" is rightly dropped (policy 2).
- The generic does not hold:
  - h5 says the charts of 1801-1807 "name the anchorage St. Helens Road" (policy 1). KN06, the one sea chart of those years, sets plain **Sᵗ Helens** in italic in the water off Bembridge, without "Road". So the charts disagree on the generic.
  - Policy 2 removes only Cary 1807's "Rᵈ" (a puzzling abbreviation). Both "St. Helens" and "St. Helens Road" survive it (though "St. Helens Road" also looks like a street name today).
  - Policy 3, the usage of 1804-05, decides, and it has no "Road" at all. My recount: "St. Helens Road(s)" 0 in every text; "St. Helen's Road(s)" only in Malham (1795, 4+1). The roadstead is plain "St. Helen's" in the *Naval Chronicle* (NC11 26, NC12 19, NC13 6, with the apostrophe; "at St. Helen's" NC11 12, NC12 4, NC13 2) and "St. Helens" in Nelson (Nel7 24; "Victory, at St. Helens", 14 September 1805, and "got on board the Victory at St. Helens").
- Verdict: letter **St. Helens**, role water, in italic on the roadstead, at h5's site (50.712 N, 1.065 W: on the water, 1.0 km off NE's shore off St Helens and Bembridge; good). This is the form of the one sea chart of 1806 and of Nelson's own dateline, which the September beat quotes. St. Helens Road (Cary 1801, 1807) stays a period alternative if the lead wants the word "Road" to say it is an anchorage. Confidence: high for the spelling, medium for dropping "Road".

### Scilly Islands: refuted; set **Scilly Isles**, as a-h4 had it

  - C06C (Cary, *A New Map of Cornwall*, 1 November 1806): the inset is titled **SCILLY ISLES.** in spaced roman capitals, and the engraved note under it begins "The Scilly Isles lying too far west of the Lands End to be inserted in their proper place ..." (read).
  - F07 (1807) "Scilly Iˢ" is consistent with either.
  - No English chart or map of 1800-1807 that anyone has read engraves "Scilly Islands". LWBC's view title is of 1794.
- So the charts of 1800-1807 agree on Scilly Isles, and policy 1 applies: the usage of Malham (1795: "Scilly Islands" 8+10, "Islands of Scilly" 2+2) and Norie (1805: "Scilly Islands" 2, in his tables) is never reached. My recount confirms h5's "Scilly Isles 0 in print", but print does not decide here. Nor does the match with the chart's "Canary Islands" and "Azores or Western Islands": those were decided by usage because their charts disagreed.
- The form will not mislead a visitor (Scilly Isles is still current English beside the official Isles of Scilly).
- Site: 49.930 N, 6.310 W lies on the water 0.3 km off St Mary's; the area-weighted centre of NE's islands is 49.941 N, 6.319 W, also on the water among them. For a group label, acceptable.
- Confidence: high. Doubt 6 should be rewritten: Scilly Isles on Knight's *British Channel* (1806) and Cary's *Cornwall* (1806), Scilly Iˢ on Faden (1807) and Laurie & Whittle (1794); Scilly Islands in Malham (1795), Norie (1805) and Laurie & Whittle's coastal view (1794); sailors mostly said "Scilly".

## 1. Summary

- The provisional set is sound. Thirty of the thirty-four names are right as they stand.
- Four want a change or a decision:
  - **"Western Islands"**: a modern reader takes it for the Hebrides. Use Faden's "Azores or Western Isles" (H4-26).
  - **"C. St Vincent"**: the modern stopless "St" is anachronistic. Use "C. St. Vincent" or the engraved "C. Sᵗ Vincent" (H4-08).
  - **"The Channel"**: correct, but Faden's chart of Nelson's track letters "ENGLISH CHANNEL" (H4-04).
  - **"Martinico"**: Nelson's own spelling, but Faden 1807 and the *Naval Chronicle* use "Martinique". Both are period-correct (H4-28).
- **"Gulf of Cadiz" is period-correct.** Heather and Williams' chart of 3 April 1800 letters "GULF OF CADIZ" in the open water between Ayamonte and Cádiz (H4-05). In prose of 1805 nobody uses it: 0 hits in Nelson, the logs, the *Naval Chronicle*, Norie, Moore and Brookes. Sailors said "off Cadiz", and "Cadiz Bay" for the bay itself.
- **No accents.** English charts and prose of 1805 write "Cadiz", never "Cádiz".
- **Forms to flag** for a modern reader:
  - "Western Islands" (the Azores);
  - "Batavia" (Holland; today Jakarta);
  - "the Groyne" (Corunna);
  - "Leeward/Windward Islands", whose English meaning differed from today's;
  - "Streights" (looks like a typo; out of fashion by 1800).
- **Spellings that look wrong but are right:** "Guadalquiver", "Bourdeaux", "Algeziras", "Guadaloupe", "Porto Rico", "Cape Verd", "Barbadoes", "Carthagena". Each is given with its alternatives below.

## 2. The names on the chart now (first group)

**H4-01 — Atlantic Ocean.**

- [F07] letters the ocean in huge, widely spaced, open roman capitals on three lines, "ATLANTIC" / small "OR" / "WESTERN OCEAN". The title reads "A Chart of the ATLANTIC OR WESTERN OCEAN".
- [LW94] has the same three-line form.
- [H00], a coastal chart, letters "THE / ATLANTIC / OCEAN" in large spaced roman capitals.
- obs: L&W/Jefferys *British Channel* has "WESTERN or ATLANTIC OCEAN"; Cary 1801 *Portugal* has "WESTERN OR ATLANTIC OCEAN".
- Heather's own catalogue (bound into Norie05, dated 5 November 1806; obs) lists "The Atlantic or Western Ocean".
- Prose: "Western Ocean" NC14 4, Norie05 2; "Atlantic Ocean" NC14 1, Norie05 6, Moore 3/7.

Verdict: "ATLANTIC OCEAN" is period-correct (it is Heather 1800's form). The ocean-chart form of 1794–1807 is "ATLANTIC or WESTERN OCEAN", with a small "or". For the wide Atlantic view, Faden's form is the most faithful and still clear. Role: ocean. Confidence **high**.

**H4-02 — Mediterranean Sea.**

- [F07] "MEDITERRANEAN SEA", spaced roman capitals spread across the whole basin, with "SEA" far east past Sicily.
- [H00] "THE MEDITERRANEAN SEA", spaced roman capitals.
- [LW94] "MEDITERR(ANEAN) SEA".
- Prose says "the Mediterranean" (Nel6 137, Nel7 104); "Mediterranean Sea" appears occasionally (Norie05 5).

Keep "MEDITERRANEAN SEA". Role: sea. Confidence **high**.

**H4-03 — Bay of Biscay.**

- [F07] "BAY OF" / "BISCAY", in large spaced roman capitals on two lines.
- [LW94] "BAY OF BISCAY", spaced.
- Prose: "Bay of Biscay" in Norie05 (5) and NC.

Keep. Period charts set it in spaced capitals like a sea, so the "sea" style suits it better than italic lower case. Role: water (or sea). Confidence **high**.

**H4-04 — The Channel.**

- [F07] "ENGLISH CHANNEL", spaced roman capitals sloping along the axis from the Lizard towards Beachy Head.
- [LW94] "THE CHANNEL", roman capitals curved off the Lizard.
- obs: the title of the L&W/Jefferys chart is "THE BRITISH CHANNEL"; Cary 1806 *France* has "ENGLISH CHANNEL"; Cary 1807 *England* has "THE ENGLISH CHANNEL"; Heather's 1806 catalogue lists "The English Channel on three Sheets".
- Prose: "the Channel" Nel6 23, Nel7 10, NC15 26; "English Channel" NC14 1, NC15 2, Norie05 5; "British Channel" Nel6 1, NC14 1, Norie05 2.

All three forms are period-correct. Best for the chart: **"English Channel"**, which matches Faden 1807 and Cary. "The Channel" is the sailors' word and L&W's lettering, and is an acceptable alternative. Role: water. Confidence **high**.

**H4-05 — Gulf of Cadiz.**

- [H00] letters **"GULF OF CADIZ"** in large spaced roman capitals. The name runs on a slant through the soundings, from off Ayamonte towards Cádiz. I read it on Commons view 3 and checked the 1800 imprint on view 1.
- [F07] and [LW94] leave this water unnamed.
- Prose: 0 hits for "Gulf of Cadiz" or "Gulph of Cadiz" in Nel6, Nel7, Logs, NC14, NC15, Norie05, Moore and Brookes. Tofiño's *Derrotero* (1789) never uses "golfo" in the OCR text.
- The bay proper is "CADIZ BAY" ([H00] inset, in spaced roman capitals; Nel7 "Cadiz Bay" 5, Logs 3, against "Bay of Cadiz" Nel7 2).

Verdict: keep "Gulf of Cadiz". It is an English chart name of 1800, though rare. Confidence: **high** that it was lettered on an English chart of 1800; **medium** that it was common, because one chart carries it and prose never does. Role: water.

**H4-06 — Straits of Gibraltar.**

- [H00] "STRAITS OF GIBRALTAR", spaced roman capitals along the strait's axis.
- [LW94] "the Straits".
- obs: Heather 1809/1814 lettering "STRAITS OF GIBRALTAR" and cartouche "THE STRAITS of GIBRALTAR"; La Rochette/Faden 1780 "STREIGHTS OF GIBRALTAR"; Cary 1805 *Africa* "STR. of GIBRALTAR".
- Faden's 1806 chart title (t4 [B]) is "Chart of the Strait of Gibraltar".
- Nelson: "Straits" Nel6 79, Nel7 23, and "the Gut" 13/3; "Streights" only twice in Nel7, in other men's letters ("the Streights' entrance").

Keep "Straits of Gibraltar". "Streights" belongs to the 1780s and reads like a typo now. Role: water. Confidence **high**.

**H4-07 — C. Trafalgar.** [F07] "C. Trafalgar", italic, written perpendicular to the coast. [H00] "C. Trafalgar", italic, beside "Trafalgar Bay". Prose: "Cape Trafalgar" (Logs 45, Nel7 49). Keep. Role: water (cape). Confidence **high**.

**H4-08 — C. St Vincent.**

- [F07] "C. Sᵗ Vincent", upright roman with a raised t. [LW94] has the same.
- [H00] "CAPE Sᵗ VINCENT" in roman capitals.
- Printed prose writes "Cape St. Vincent" with a full stop (Nel6 30, Nel7 35).
- The stopless "St" is a twentieth-century British convention.

Set **"C. Sᵗ Vincent"** (raised t) if the font has a superior t, otherwise **"C. St. Vincent"**. The same applies to every "St" below. Role: water (cape); Faden sets the big landfall capes upright (see H4-81). Confidence **high**.

**H4-09 — C. Finisterre.** [F07] "C. Finisterre", roman. [LW94] "C. Finisterre". Norie05 8, NC 5/6, Nel7 6, all "Cape Finisterre". Keep. Confidence **high**.

**H4-10 — Cadiz.**

- [F07] "Cadiz", roman.
- [H00] "CADIZ", bold capitals in the inset and on the chart.
- [LW94] "Cadiz".
- Prose: "Cadiz" everywhere (Nel7 311). There are no accents in any English source. The old "Cales" has 0 hits.
- obs: La Rochette 1780 has the French "CADIX".

Keep **"Cadiz" without the accent**. The site's own prose may keep "Cádiz". Role: place. Confidence **high**.

**H4-11 — Gibraltar.** [F07], [LW94] and [H00] "Gibraltar" (in [H00]'s inset, "THE ROCK OF GIBRALTAR" and "Town"). Keep. Confidence **high**.

**H4-12 — Tangier.**

- [F07] "Tangier", italic, perpendicular to the coast.
- [H00] "TANGIER" and "Old Tangier".
- [LW94] "Tanger"; obs: La Rochette "Tinja or Tanger".
- Prose: "Tangier" Nel6 6, Nel7 3; "Tangiers" 0.

Keep "Tangier". Confidence **high**.

**H4-13 — Ceuta.** [F07], [LW94] and [H00] "Ceuta". Keep. Confidence **high**.

**H4-14 — Seville.** [H00] "Seville". [LW94] has the Spanish "Sevilla", as does [FadSP] in capitals. Brookes "Seville" 14/28. Faden 1807 does not show the city. Keep "Seville". Confidence **high**.

**H4-18 — Toulon.** [F07] "Toulon", roman. Keep. Confidence **high**.

**H4-19 — Brest.** [F07] and [LW94] "Brest". Keep. Confidence **high**.

**H4-23 — Spain, Portugal, France, England.**

- [F07]: "SPAIN", "FRANCE", "PORTUGAL" (set vertically down the country), "ENGLAND" (vertically down the island), "WALES", "SCOTLAND" and "IRELAND", all in spaced roman capitals.
- [LW94] "SPAIN", "PORTUGAL", "FRANCE", "ENGLAND".

**H4-24 — Barbary.**

- [LW94] "BARBARY" in very large spaced capitals across North Africa, with "MAROCCO" and "ALGARB" smaller.
- [H00] "BARBARY", spaced italic capitals opposite Tarifa.
- obs: La Rochette 1780 has "BARBARY" and "KINGDOM OF FEZ"; Heather 1809 has "BARBARY".
- [F07] instead letters the countries: "FEZ" and "MOROCCO" (spaced capitals along the Atlantic coast) and "ALGIER".
- Prose: "Barbary" Nel6 11, Nel7 6.

Keep "Barbary" for the coast and region. Confidence **high**.

**H4-25 — Madeira.** [F07] has "MADEIRA ISLES" (spaced italic capitals on an arc) over "Madeira", "Funchal", "Porto Santo", "Deserters" and "Salvages". Keep "Madeira" for the island. Confidence **high**.

**H4-26 — Western Islands (the Azores).**

- [F07] "AZORES OR WESTERN ISLES", spaced italic capitals on an arc over the group.
- [LW94] "AZORES or WESTERN ISLANDS".
- obs: L&W's 1794 Azores chart is titled "Açores (Hawks Islands), called also Flemish and Western Islands"; Heather's 1806 catalogue lists "The Azores, or Western Islands".
- Prose: "Azores" Nel6 3, Nel7 1; "Western Islands" Nel6 2, Nel7 2, Norie05 3.

**Flag:** "Western Islands" alone now means the Hebrides. Set Faden's **"Azores or Western Isles"**: it is period-exact and cannot mislead. Role: island group (spaced italic capitals). Confidence **high**.

**H4-27 — Canary Islands.**

- [F07] "CANARY ISLES", spaced italic capitals.
- [LW94] "CANARY ISLANDS".
- obs: Cary 1805 *Africa* "CANARY ISLES".
- Prose: "Canary Islands" Nel6 1, Nel7 1.

Both are right. "Canary Isles" matches Faden. Confidence **high**.

**H4-28 — West Indies.**

- [F07] letters the Lesser Antilles "CARIBBEE ISLANDS", spaced capitals on an arc east of the chain.
- "West Indies" was the universal word in 1805 prose (Nelson, NC). Atlas titles use it, such as Cary's *West India Isles* 1803 (obs).
- The t4 notes read it in Faden's track legend ("… to the West Indies 1805"); I did not re-read that legend.

Keep "West Indies" as a regional name in spaced capitals. It is a land or region label rather than a water. Confidence: **high** for the name; **medium** that a chart of this scale would set it as a big label.

**H4-29 — Martinico.**

- [F07] "Martinique".
- NC14/15: "Martinique" 11/15, "Martinico" 2/2.
- Nelson's own letters say **"Martinico"**. Examples in Nel6: "they will only arrive seventeen days before us at Martinico"; "the Enemy had not moved from Martinico". Nicolas's notes and the French plans in Nel6 say "Martinique".
- obs: L&W 1794 *Caribbee* has "MARTINICO French". Brookes has "Martinico" 2/5.

Both forms are period-correct. For a chart in Faden's style, **"Martinique"** is the better-attested lettering. "Martinico" is Nelson's word and a fine choice if the scene wants his voice. Owner's call. Confidence: **high** that both were current; **medium** on which is better.

**H4-30 — Barbadoes.** [F07] "Barbadoes". Prose: Nel6 22, Nel7 13, NC14 20, NC15 18; "Barbados" appears once (NC15 1). obs: L&W 1794 "BARBADOS English". Keep **"Barbadoes"**. Confidence **high**.

## 3. Further names

### Oceans and seas

**H4-31 — Atlantic or Western Ocean.**

- On ocean charts: "ATLANTIC OR WESTERN OCEAN" ([F07], [LW94]; Heather's 1806 catalogue lists "The Atlantic or Western Ocean" and "A Mercator's Chart of the Atlantic or Western Ocean").
- On coast charts: "THE ATLANTIC OCEAN" ([H00]).
- Sailors said "the Western Ocean" (NC14).

See H4-01. Confidence **high**.

**H4-32 — North Sea.**

- [F07] "THE / NORTH SEA" in spaced roman capitals.
- [LW94] "NORTH SEA" with "GERMAN" (sea) interleaved (obs: "NORTH SEA or GERMAN SEA").
- obs: L&W/Jefferys *British Channel* "NORTH SEA or GERMAN OCEAN"; Cary 1807 "THE NORTH SEA".
- Prose: "North Sea" Norie05 12, NC15 8; "German Ocean" Brookes 41, Moore 8.

Use **"North Sea"**. "German Ocean" is authentic but would puzzle a reader. Confidence **high**.

**H4-33 — Caribbean Sea.** [F07] "CARIBBEAN SEA" (spaced roman capitals) and [LW94] "CARIBBEAN SEA". "Caribbee" is used for the islands (H4-73), not the sea; I found no "Caribbee Sea". Confidence **high**.

**H4-34 — Bay of Cadiz.**

- [H00] inset: "CADIZ BAY" (spaced roman capitals), "CADIZ ROAD" (spaced italic capitals, the outer anchorage), and "Bay" beside Cádiz on the main sheet.
- Prose: "Cadiz Bay" Nel7 5, Logs 3; "Bay of Cadiz" Nel7 2.

Use **"Cadiz Bay"**; use "Cadiz Road" if the anchorage needs a name. Confidence **high**.

**H4-35 — Strait of Dover.** [LW94] "Str. of Dover"; obs: Cary 1806 "Straits of Dover". Confidence **high**.

**H4-36 — Gulf of Lion.** obs: La Rochette 1780 and L&W 1794 "GULF OF LION"; Cary 1806 "GULF OF LYONS". Not lettered on F07. Confidence **medium**.

## 4. How English charts of the period lettered names (read on F07, H00 and LW94)

**H4-94 — Oceans.**

- Huge, very widely spaced roman capitals.
- On [F07] and [LW94] they are open, hatched or outline letters.
- The name is broken over lines ("ATLANTIC" / small "OR" / "WESTERN OCEAN").
- It spans a third of the chart.
- Confidence **high**; t4 measured the sizes.

**H4-95 — Seas, the Channel, big bays and straits.** Spaced roman capitals, smaller than the ocean:

- [F07] "MEDITERRANEAN SEA", "THE NORTH SEA", "CARIBBEAN SEA", "ENGLISH CHANNEL", "BAY OF / BISCAY";
- [H00] "GULF OF CADIZ", "STRAITS OF GIBRALTAR", "THE MEDITERRANEAN SEA".

They follow the axis of the water, sloping (the Channel, the Strait) or gently curved ([LW94] "THE CHANNEL"). Italic capitals are used for some named waters on [H00]: "CADIZ ROAD", "GIBRALTAR BAY", "ALGEZIRAS BAY".

So the brief's "waters in italic lower case" is a modern simplification. The big waters were capitals; only small waters (bays, roads) were italic. A faithful choice: Bodoni or Baskervville capitals for seas, the Channel, the Gulf and the Strait, and italic lower case for bays and capes. Confidence **high**.

**H4-96 — Island groups.**

- Spaced italic capitals on an arc over the group ([F07] "AZORES OR WESTERN ISLES", "MADEIRA ISLES", "CANARY ISLES", "CAPE VERD ISLES").
- The big Lesser Antilles arc is in spaced capitals ("CARIBBEE ISLANDS").
- Single islands are in roman lower case ("Madeira", "Antigua", "Barbadoes"); small ones and towns on islands in italic ("Funchal", "Formentera").

Confidence **high**.

**H4-97 — Countries and provinces.**

- Spaced roman capitals, often running along the length of the country or the coast: [F07] "PORTUGAL" and "ENGLAND" set vertically; "MOROCCO", "BATAVIA" and "AZANAGA" along the coast; [H00] "ALGARVE" vertical and "ANDALUSIA" curved.
- Continents ("EUROPE", "AFRICA") are larger still.
- Italic capitals occur ([H00] "BARBARY").

Confidence **high**.

**H4-98 — Towns.**

- **Capitals** in small capitals over a building pictogram ([F07] "MADRID", "PARIS", "LONDON").
- **Large towns** in capitals ("GENOA", "NAPLES", "ROME", "LISBON" spaced along the Tagus).
- **Ports and towns** in roman lower case ("Cadiz", "Gibraltar", "Ceuta", "Ferrol", "Brest", "Toulon", "Boulogne").
- **Minor places** in italic ("Corunna", "Tangier", "Huelva", "Estepona", "Malaga").
- On crowded coasts, names are set perpendicular to the coast, running inland like comb teeth (all of southern Iberia on [F07]).

Confidence **high**.

**H4-99 — Capes and rivers.**

- Capes always abbreviate "C." with a full stop ("Cape" is spelled out only on [H00]'s "Cape Spartel", "CAPE Sᵗ VINCENT", and on some big headlands).
- **Major landfall capes** are upright roman on [F07]: "C. Sᵗ Vincent", "C. Finisterre", "C. Palos", "C. de Gata", "C. Spartivento".
- **Lesser capes** are italic: "C. Trafalgar", "C. Spartel", "C. S. Mary", "C. de Ferro".
- **Points:** "Pᵗ".
- **Saints:** "Sᵗ" (raised t; in print "St.") and "Sᵗᵃ" for Santa.
- **Islands abbreviated:** "I." for one island, "Iˢ" for several ("Scilly Iˢ", "Iˢ of Hyeres").
- **Rivers** in italic ("Tagus R.", "R. Mersey", "Morocco R.", "River Nun"), or in spaced capitals along a big lower course ([H00] "GUADALQUIVER RIVER").

Confidence **high**.

**H4-100 — Notes and sizes.**

- Notes are in small italic ("Vigia doubtful", "Rock doubtful", "Var. 22°50' W. 1801", "Steenground doubtful").
- The size hierarchy is steep between ocean, country and sea, and nearly flat among places. t4-chart-style.md §7 has the measured ratios, about 6.5 : 2.8 : 1.4 : 1.2 : 1 (ocean : country : capital : port : minor).
- Seas sit between the ocean and the countries. On [F07] "MEDITERRANEAN SEA" and "ENGLISH CHANNEL" are smaller than "SPAIN" and "FRANCE" but larger than the port names. That is my visual judgment, not measured.

Confidence **medium-high**.

## 5. Doubts and limits

- **Gulf of Cadiz rests on one chart**, H00 (1800). I did not look at Heather's later editions or at Norie's charts after 1805, which may also letter it. Prose never uses it.
- **Earlier-session readings (marked obs) were not re-checked**, including the L&W *Caribbee*, Cary's 1801–1807 atlas maps, Heather 1809/1814, La Rochette 1780 and Nantiat 1810. Several are only supporting evidence. Where a name rests on obs alone, the confidence is medium.
- **The counts come from OCR.** Long-s and broken words reduce hits. The Jefferys 1795 and Stephenson 1795 pilot texts OCR so badly that their counts are useless, and I left them out.
- **Nicolas (1845–46)** keeps Nelson's spellings in the letters. His notes use the forms of the 1840s; I checked contexts for the forms that matter.
- **No British Library, Gallica (direct), LOC or Hathi images were used.** BnF charts were read via Wikimedia Commons copies.
- **Edition years.** The FadSP item and the edition years of the two Brookes gazetteers were not verified.

# Review of the Scene 2 history draft: rules, duplication, readability (session 32)

An independent review of `docs/research/scene-02/session-17/history-scene-02-wip.md` at commit a970ab1 (branch `s23-history`, main merged at 1a1378d), before it is merged into `docs/history-notes.md`. The lens is the repository's rules (public repository, paraphrase, links), duplication (inside the section, against "Scene 2: Boulogne from the sea (beat 1)" and against scene 1's sections) and readability (order, conventions, stale markers, things described as drawn that are not drawn). Facts were not re-checked against the sources. Line numbers are the draft's at a970ab1 unless "history-notes" is named.

## What was checked and found clean

- **Quotations.** Every quotation read is a short phrase from a period source in the public domain: Nelson's letters and memorandum, the logs, Napoleon's *Correspondance*, Desbrière, Alombert and Colin, Escaño and the French reports. Examples: "from sixteen to eighteen leagues West of Cadiz", "entre les Porques et le Diamant, par les 7 brasses d'eau", "burn two blue lights together, every hour". No wording from a modern book was found. Berjaud (2009), Wheeler (1995), Werrett (2015), Lancaster (2012) and the Winfield registers are used for facts or figures only, in the draft's own words.
- **Code and decision references.** Every code name the draft cites exists in main's tree: `stagesOf`, `strengthOf`, `lineStrength`, `NOT_AT_TRAFALGAR`, `DAWN_EASE`, `CHAIN`, `LINES`, `UNDER_SAIL`, `CAMP_PLANS`, and `scripts/dev/fleet-gates.mjs`. Every `decisions.md` heading it names exists: "No glyph stands on the shore", "Kept off the land", "Smooth from near to far", "Narrowed, ship by ship", the two flotilla decisions, "The minor names give way…" and the rest. The Weazle's fading and the chain ships' stations are in the code as described.
- **The not-yet-drawn items** (the Finisterre smoke, the lanterns, the relay, the night signals) are marked as not drawn (lines 16, 25, 74 and 299, and licence 11).
- **Licence cross-references.** Every "Assumptions and licences", N pointer lands on the right item.

## Findings, most serious first

1. **The section is three times the size of the whole file, against the file's own contract.** The draft is 308 KB. `history-notes.md` is 105 KB now, and its line 3 says it is the summary, with "the full research notes, with measurements, tables… in `docs/research/`". By section: Open doubts 44 KB, names 29 KB, key sources 26 KB, positions 25 KB, licences 22 KB, strengths 21 KB and routes 20 KB. Much of this is research material: the routes' closest approaches to a tenth of a mile, the Natural Earth gaps, the dropped points, the 22-point army table, the coordinate tables, the names' full evidence and the per-ship km shifts of licence 16 (already in `decisions.md`, line 596). **Fix (the lead decides):** keep this draft, finished, as the research note, for example `docs/research/scene-02/history-scene-02.md`. Merge a summary section that keeps the narrative, the strengths, the licences in short form and short doubts, and points to the note for the tables and the evidence. At the least, move the route QA, the position tables and the names' evidence lists out.

2. **The strengths list repeats the campaign narrative, event by event.** Each step under "The strengths lettered at the tracks' heads" restates a narrative bullet with the same sources and the same "versions that differ". 168 = 39–40 (the eleven named twice; James's 29 March slip appears at 12, 31, 39 and 168). 169 = 13 and 45 (the 14, the Argonauta at dawn, the America on the 12th, James's 17). 170–171 = 45 and 47. 172 = 48, nearly word for word ("James has the junction at sea, on the day the fleet sailed or the next"). 173 = 76. 174–175 = 17, 31 and 84. 176–177 = 31 and 85. 178 = 92. 181–185 = 55, 58, 60, 62 and 64. 189–190 = 125–126 (the 11.30 rehoisting and "about noon"; the Lizard and Plymouth). 193 = 43. 195 = 71. 197 = 91. 199 = 92 and 136 (Lauriston's count from the San Sebastián tower is word for word). **Fix:** in the strengths list keep the figure, the date and hour taken and why (the rule's own choices), and point to the narrative bullet for the event and its variants. Or strip the strength facts from the narrative instead, but do one of the two.

3. **"Open doubts" restates the body (44 KB), contrary to its own heading.** Its first line says "the sections above give the argument where it arises", then it repeats the argument. 541 = 39–44. 543 = 47–50. 544 = 54–63. 546 = 74–78. 547 = 83–85. 548 = 88–90. 549 = 125–129. 552 = 111–120, plus the army research details (Berjaud's "Livry", "Courcelles", Surques, the "1er vendémiaire" misprint), which are all in `session-32/army-march.md`. 553 = 143–148. 557–558 = 150–152. 561 = 269–287. 564 = 367–370. **Fix:** give one line per open question, with its confidence and a pointer to the bullet that carries the versions. Drop the questions the body settles at High, such as James's conversion slips. Leave research-level detail in the research notes. "Boulogne from the sea" (history-notes 309–321) shows the right length.

4. **Louis's detachment is told twice in full.** Line 131, in "Nelson off Cádiz", and lines 203, 213 and 215, in "Fleet sizes", give the same ships, the nautical "October 3rd, P.M. at 6", Desbrière's literal 3 October, the Leviathan's "six Sail", the Euryalus's news, the 4th or 5th (Beatty or Austen) and Gibraltar on the 9th. Line 550 repeats parts of it a third time. **Fix:** keep one account, probably in "Fleet sizes", since the label hangs on it, and reduce line 131 to a pointer.

5. **The sortie and the Fougueux appear in four to seven places.** The sortie's hours are at 150 (the record), 245 (the table's Drawn column), 373 and licence 9 (453). Licence 9 restates the record of line 150 almost word for word before it gives the drawn hours. The Fougueux in the narrows is at 23, 148, 150, 359, 376, 449 and 554. **Fix:** licence 9 should give only the drawn hours and where they depart from the record, pointing to "19 October" and "20 October". Elsewhere, a pointer to "Outer and inner ships".

6. **Scene 1's table contradicts this section on the courses after bearing up.** History-notes line 22 (scene 1, "Where the sources changed the brief") says "ENE then E by N (magnetic), so about NE then ENE true". The draft (370; licence 1 at 445; 367; 564) gives 047° and 058° true, NE and NE by E, as `research/dawn-setting.md` F-27 corrected it. E by N less 21° is about 058°, which is NE by E, not ENE. Merged, the file would give both. **Fix:** correct the scene 1 row's words to "about NE, then NE by E true (047° and 058°)". This changes a note, not the approved scene. Then drop the explanation in line 370. The bearing-up sequence is also told four times (367, 370, 445 and 564); keep it once.

11. **The owner's "same chain by night" is recorded without being reconciled with the history.** Line 299 records the answer of 14 November 2026: the night signals of 20–21 October are built "as the same chain by night". The section itself says the night chain "was a different, short chain between the closing fleets" (table row 25), "under 10 nm long, not 50" (291) and "a short chain… running SW from the enemy's western flank" (313). By the memorandum its ships of the line were the Defence, Colossus and Mars. The Agamemnon is not named, though on the 20th she was ordered to repeat signals by day (455). **Fix:** say in "How the chart shows the signals" how the answer maps onto the history. The links were the same kind of ships, three of the four ships of the line among them. The geometry was not the same: a short chain between the fleets, not the 48–58 nm line due west of Cádiz. The night of 19–20 October (289), when rockets and blue lights went along the long chain, is the nearest to "the same chain by night". This is a point for the lead, and perhaps for the owner, before the relay session.

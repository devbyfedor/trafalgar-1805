# V-H4: independent check of the 1805 English names

A fact-check of `h4-names.md`, written 29 October 2026. For each name I tried to find the form on an English chart, atlas map or pilot book of about 1795–1810 that H4 does not cite. I also recounted the forms in Nelson's letters, the *Naval Chronicle* and the logs, and checked the lettering conventions H4 states.

**Verdicts.** *Confirmed*: a second, independent period source has the same form. *Corrected*: a better 1805 form is given, with sources. *Disputed*: period sources disagree; each form is given with who used it. *Unverifiable*: I found no second source.

**Confidence.** High: several independent period sources agree, or I read the form on a period chart myself. Medium: one good source, or a consistent inference. Low: an estimate, or conflicting sources.

| Convention (H4) | Verdict | What the other charts show |
|---|---|---|
| **Oceans:** huge, widely spaced roman capitals, often open or hatched, broken over lines (ATLANTIC / or / WESTERN OCEAN), spanning about a third of the chart | confirmed in the core; the details are Faden/L&W habits | C01, C03, C04 and C05 all use spaced roman capitals. | **Seas, Channel, gulfs, straits:** spaced roman capitals following the axis; italic only for small waters | disputed | Seas, the Channel and big bays are spaced roman capitals on Cary: MEDITERRANEAN SEA, BAY OF BISCAY, BRITISH CHANNEL, NORTH SEA, IRISH SEA. ## 4. Evidence and notes

### 4.1 Gulf of Cadiz

- **Charts.** H4's chart evidence ([H00], Heather 1800, lettering GULF OF CADIZ) stands; I did not re-read it. On the three other English maps of this coast that I read, C01, C04 and HT09, the water between Cape St Mary and Cadiz is unnamed. So on charts the name rests on H00.
- **Gazetteers and geographies of 1795–1810 use it often.** That contradicts H4's "0 hits in … Brookes".
  - **Brookes, 1807 edition** ([Br07](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.91219), the very edition H4 cites): Alcoutim is on the Guadiana a few miles from its entrance "into the gulf of Cadiz", and Faro is "seated on the gulf of Cadiz". A third port, at the mouth of the Gilão and 100 miles NW of Cadiz (by that description Tavira; the headword is lost in the OCR), is placed "in the gulf of Cadiz".
  - **Earlier Brookes editions**, with the same entries, in 1795, 1797, 1800 and 1801:
    - [1795](https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_the-general-gazetteer-o_brookes-r-richard_1795)
    - [1797](https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_the-general-gazetteer-o_brookes-r_1797)
    - [1800](https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_the-general-gazetteer-o_brookes-r-richard_1800)
    - [1801](https://archive.org/details/dli.ministry.02268)
  - **Walker's *Universal Gazetteer***, 1795 ([link](https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_the-universal-gazetteer_walker-john_1795)) and 1801 ([link](https://archive.org/details/universalgazette00walk_0)).
  - **Cruttwell's *New Universal Gazetteer***, 1800 ([link](https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_the-new-universal-gazett_cruttwell-clement_1800)).
  - ***Encyclopaedia Britannica***, 1810: "the bay or gulf of Cadiz", for the Guadiana's mouth ([link](https://archive.org/details/encyclopaediabri10edin)).
  - **Counts.** FTS for 1798–1810 finds 28 items with "gulf of Cadiz" and 12 with "gulph of Cadiz", against 306 with "bay of Cadiz" and 106 with "Cadiz bay".
- **Naval prose does not use it.** My recount gives 0 in Nel6, Nel7, NC14, Logs and Mal95. That agrees with H4.
- **Extent.** The two versions differ:
  - **Version 1, the whole bight.** The gazetteers place the Algarve towns of Faro and (by its description) Tavira, Ayamonte (Cruttwell) and the Guadiana mouth on the gulf, so they mean the whole bight from the Algarve to Cadiz. H00 places the name in that open water, per H4.
  - **Version 2, mostly the bay.** Tuckey, a naval officer writing in 1815, describes the curve from Cape St Mary to Cape Trafalgar. He says it "is sometimes called the Gulf of Cadiz", but that the name is "more generally confined to the Bay of Cadiz" ([Tuck15](https://archive.org/details/maritimegeograp09tuckgoog), vol. 2, coasts of Spain).
  - The chart follows version 1, which is defensible for 1800–1810. Record version 2 beside it.

### 4.2 The Channel

- **Two forms on two different charts.** C04 (Cary 1804) letters **BRITISH CHANNEL** in large spaced roman capitals along the Channel's axis, from off Start Point towards Beachy Head. H4 reads "ENGLISH CHANNEL" on Faden 1807 and "THE CHANNEL" on L&W 1794.
- **Malham's formal name is "English Channel".** Mal95 has "English Channel" 68+53 against "British Channel" 2+5.
- **Sailors write "the Channel".** With a capital C: Nel6 23, Nel7 10, NC14 11, Mal95 11+3.
- **Counts.** FTS 1798–1810 finds 769 items with "English Channel" and 997 with "British Channel".
- **Conclusion.** "The Channel" is authentic, but as chart lettering I found it only on L&W 1794. The two formal forms each appear on a period chart.

### 4.3 C. St Vincent

- **Engraved form.** C01 and C05 engrave "C. Sᵗ Vincent", upright roman with a raised t.
- **Printed form.** In type the stop is constant: Mal95 6+14, Nel6 30, Nel7 35 and NC14 2 "Cape St. Vincent", with 0 stopless.
- **Correction.** "C. St Vincent" should become "C. St. Vincent", or "C. Sᵗ Vincent" with a superior t. The same applies to every other "St".

### 4.4 Straits, not Streights

- **Charts.** C01 and HT09 have STRAITS OF GIBRALTAR; C04 and C05 abbreviate it "STR. of".
- **Prose.** Mal95 has "Straits" 284+278 (all uses) against "Streights" 2+1.
- **Streights lingered in official texts.** FTS 1798–1810 still finds "Streights of Gibraltar" in 232 items. They include the *London Gazette* of August 1800 and the customs schedules of 1801–1807.
- **Conclusion.** "Out of fashion by 1800" is true for charts and naval writing, not for official documents. "Straits" remains the right choice.

### 4.5 Martinico against Martinique

- **Martinico.** Nelson's own sentences in Nel6 use it, for example "arrive seventeen days before us at Martinico". It is also Malham's form (1795: Martinico 20+19, Martinique 5+1).
- **Martinique.** Cary's 1803 West India map engraves "MARTINIQUE" with Fort Royal and Diamond Pᵗ (read myself). H4 reads the same on Faden 1807, and NC14 prefers it (12 against 2).
- **Where "Martinique" appears in Nel6.** In Nicolas's notes, the French plans, a journal extract ("Heard that the French Fleet had arrived at Martinique on the 4th") and letters by others.
- **Conclusion.** The engraved charts of 1803–1807 say Martinique; Nelson and the older pilot usage say Martinico. Both are period-correct. The choice is the owner's.

### 4.6 Western Islands

- **On a chart.** C05 (Cary 1805) labels the Azores **WESTERN Iˢ**, with no "Azores". So the name on the chart now has its own engraved precedent.
- **Nelson's usage means the Azores.** His uses in Nel6 and Nel7 include "off the Western Islands", "to the Southward of the Western Islands" and "West of Cape St. Vincent, towards the Western Islands".
- **The ambiguity H4 flags already existed in 1805.** Johnson's *Journey to the Western Islands of Scotland* was reprinted in 1798, 1800 and 1810 (FTS).
- **Conclusion.** In mid-Atlantic on the chart the label is unambiguous. H4's "Azores or Western Isles" is an equally period-correct option, not a correction.

### 4.7 West Indies as a label

- **A region, not a water.** "West Indies" was the universal name: Mal95 122+192, Nel6 62, Nel7 22, NC14 41. It names the islands as a region.
- **How the charts treat it.** C03 is titled *A New Map of the West India Isles*. In the map body it letters only CARIBBEE ISLANDS, LEEWARD ISLANDS, WINDWARD ISLANDS, CARIBBEAN SEA and ATLANTIC OCEAN. Arrowsmith's 1803 chart also uses the name in its title (RMG record).
- **Conclusion.** The form is right. Set it as a land or island-group label in spaced roman capitals, not in the italic used for waters. "CARIBBEE ISLANDS" is the engraved alternative if the label sits on the arc.

### 4.8 Other things noticed on the charts

- **Engravers' slips on C01:** "C. Trafalagar" and "Teutan" (for Tetuan). Do not copy them.
- **C01 also has** "C. of Roca", "C. of Espichel", "Sᵗ Ubes or Setuval" and "RIVER TAJO or TAGUS".
- **C04's spellings for the Iberian part:** "Tangiers", "C. Espartel", "Rochfort", "R. Duero", "Marseilles", "Bourdeaux", "Carthagena", "Strasburg", "Valetta", "IVICA".
- **HT09** has "Algeciras", "Cape Trafalgar", "CAPE SPARTEL", "TANGIER", "Old Tangier", "CEUTA", "TARIFA", "Conil", "Cape Plata" and "Europa Pᵗ". Its STRAITS OF GIBRALTAR is in large open (shaded outline) roman capitals.

## 5. Limits

- **I did not re-read H4's own charts** (Faden 1807, Heather 1800, L&W 1794). The task was to find other sources, so where my table says "H4 reads…", that is H4's reading.
- **All four Cary maps are from one engraver.** They are independent of Faden, Heather and L&W, but not of each other. Where Cary is the only second source, a wider check (Arrowsmith, L&W or Steel charts) would strengthen it.
- **The FTS counts are noisy.** Item years are sometimes wrong (a "1802" *David Copperfield* appears), and non-English items inflate shared forms such as Martinique, Barbados and Rochefort. Several FTS queries for further names failed: Gulf of Lyons, Gulf of Lion, Rock of Lisbon, Tangiers, Algeziras, Algesiras, Leeward Islands and Windward Islands.
- **The date of HT09.** I could not read an imprint on the Rumsey scan. I give c. 1809 from the pilot's other plates, and the 1814 issue from the BL record.
- **Not checked:** Isla de León, El Puerto de Santa María, Diamond Rock (C03 shows only "Diamond Pᵗ"), Cape Verd Isles, the French provinces, Swabia and Galicia.

## Method

- Fresh copies of the OCR text of nine period books were downloaded from archive.org (Desbrière 1907, NRS *Logs* II, Nicolas VII, James III, Corbett, Clowes V, Fraser, *Correspondance* XI, Beatty 1825) and read page by page. Mahan's *Life of Nelson* II came from Project Gutenberg. Place coordinates were re-queried from the Wikipedia geodata API and OpenStreetMap (Nominatim).
- Independent of the notes, I used the French and Spanish ship reports and letters printed in Desbrière's annex that the notes did not use: the *Montañés*'s diary, the journals or reports of the *Rhin*, *Fougueux*, *Thémis* and *Observateur*, Prigny's report, Gravina's letter of 16 September, the procès-verbal of the council of 8 October, and Escaño's letter to MacDonnell.
- **Page numbers.** Archive.org's page labels are off by one (Desbrière, James 378–383) or by four (*Correspondance* XI) in places. Below, "p." is always the **printed** page. Links use the archive.org leaf index (`/page/nNNN`, counted from 0).
- **Bearings.** These are recomputed with variation 21° W and 1 league = 3 nm from San Sebastián castle (36.5282 N, 6.3160 W, Wikipedia es), as in the notes.

## A. Corrections, with evidence

### A1. Council of war of 8 October: 14 signatories, and the argument was Escaño's

- **The procès-verbal** is printed in Desbrière's annex on pp. 97–98. It lists those present: Gravina; Alava; Escaño and Cisneros; brigadiers Mac-Donell, **Hore** and Galiano; Dumanoir and Magon; Cosmao, Maistral and Lavillegris; and Prigny. It is signed by Prigny, Galiano, **Hore**, Lavillegris, Maistral, Cosmao, Macdonell, Cisneros, Magon, Escaño, Dumanoir le Pelley, Alava, Gravina and Villeneuve: 14 names. The narrative on p. 138 drops Hore. https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n366/mode/1up (annex pp. 97–98, leaves 367–368)
- **Escaño's letter to MacDonnell** (annex pp. 98–100, Spanish) gives the argument about crews at sea since 1793 against crews eight years in port to "el Mayor general", that is Escaño, Gravina's chief of staff, writing of himself. "Magon tomó la palabra para refutar al Mayor." Desbrière (p. 140) translates it "Le major général (Escaño) posa la question". Corbett (p. 324) attributes the same words to "Captain Prigny, … Villeneuve's Chief of the Staff", which is a misreading.

  - https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n165/mode/1up
  - https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n359/mode/1up
- **Desbrière doubts the violence.** On p. 140 he judges a council "marqué par des dissensions violentes" to be "fort peu vraisemblable". He also notes that Churruca, who appears in the Apodaca story, did not sit on the council.

### A2. San Fulgencio: the reason she stayed is known

Gravina wrote to Decrès from Príncipe de Asturias on 16 September 1805 (annex p. 84). He says he had commissioned Rayo and San Justo, disarmed Terrible, put San Fulgencio 64 into dock to survey her bottom, and was manning the newly commissioned ships with the crews of Terrible and San Fulgencio. James's "for some unknown reason detained" (p. 385, copied by Clowes p. 129) can be replaced. https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n353/mode/1up

### A3. Nelson's diary, 19 October: Beatty prints "E.S.E."

Beatty, *Authentic Narrative* (3rd ed., 1825), p. 97, prints: "Cadiz bearing E.S.E. by compass, distance sixteen leagues". Nicolas (VII p. 133), who took the diary from Clarke and M'Arthur, prints "E.N.E.". Corbett's suspected slip (p. 333 n.) is therefore a transcription variant. Nelson's diary, his noon letter ("Cadiz, E.S.E., 16 Leagues") and Victory's noon log position agree.

### A4. Villeneuve's night course: "S.-O. 1/4 S." means SW by S

Desbrière (p. 164 n. 3) writes "S.-O. 1/4 S.". In French compass usage, "quart" is one point (11.25°): "sud-ouest quart sud" is SW by S, 213.75° magnetic, about 193° true, i.e. S by W. The same notation appears elsewhere, for example "à 10 lieues S.-1/4 S.-O. de Cadix" (p. 174 n. 1), and the *Montañés* diary's "rumbo del S.-O.-1/4 S." (annex p. 373). In English, "SW¼S" means a quarter point (222°, about 201° true, nearer SSW). The notes' result ("about S by W true") is right only if the course is written **SW by S**.

**Best supported: 7 of the line (6 French + Bahama) and 2–3 frigates.** The figures 9 and 12 count frigates, or come from memory.

### B2. When the fleet was out on 20 October

The eyewitnesses agree on about **8 a.m.**, with Rayo last at about 10:
- *Observateur* (annex p. 158);
- *Montañés* (p. 373);
- *Fougueux* (p. 216);
- Euryalus counted 34 out by 8:50–9:10 (NRS II pp. 147–148).

Desbrière's "vers 11 heures" (p. 163) and Corbett's "nearly noon" (p. 337) are later reconstructions. Both may describe the fleet as formed and under way, not just clear of the passes.

### B3. The wear on the afternoon of 20 October

Prigny gives 4 p.m. (annex p. 138), Sirius 3:30 p.m. (Nicolas VII p. 134), and Corbett about 4 (p. 338). Desbrière says "between 1 and 3", adding that many reports say 3 (p. 164 and n. 1). James has them tacking about 5 p.m. (p. 388). Euryalus at 4:40 p.m. still has the enemy on the larboard tack, but she was away with the Admiral at the time (NRS II p. 148; Corbett p. 339).

### B4. Midnight 20–21 October against dawn

Desbrière's estimate of the British fleet at midnight, about 20 miles SW of Cádiz (p. 178), plus about 7 hours SW under easy sail and 2 hours N by E, does not reach the dawn position anchored on Collingwood's "Cape Trafalgar E by S, seven leagues". The gap is 10–15 nm. Draw the 20–21 October tracks loosely and anchor them on the dawn positions.

## E. Points the notes missed

1. The *Montañés* diary (Spanish primary source) independently confirms 6 French + 1 Spanish on 19 October. The French ship reports range from 4 to 9.
2. Eyewitnesses have the whole fleet out of the passes by about 8 a.m. on 20 October, with Rayo last at about 10.
3. The procès-verbal has 14 signatories (Hore), and was formally convened "sur l'invitation de l'amiral Villeneuve".
4. Escaño, not Prigny, made the "untrained crews" argument (Corbett's error).
5. Gravina's letter of 16 September explains why San Fulgencio and Terrible stayed in port.
6. Beatty's printing of the 19 October diary has "E.S.E."
7. French "1/4" in compass notation means "by" (one point).
8. **Lights on the allied side at night.**
   - Prigny: the fleet lit its poop lanterns and the top light, and fired rockets at intervals to mark its position. The British frigates kept signalling by rockets, guns and blue lights or false fires (annex pp. 138–139).
   - Gravina led the line carrying a light at the mizzen (Desbrière p. 165).
   - Sirius saw the enemy "making signals with guns, rockets, &c." (Nicolas p. 134).
9. **The San Sebastián lighthouse was lit every night** by Villeneuve's order (Fraser p. 29). Euryalus and Phoebe took bearings on it on 19 and 20 October.
10. **The day signals were heard as well as seen.**
    - The French saw the British frigates making many signals and firing guns in volleys (Hermione's report, annex; Contamine, annex: "elles tirèrent des bordées, qui furent répétées par d'autres frégates intermédiaires").
    - Phoebe was "firing three minute guns" at 8 a.m. on 19 October (NRS II p. 146).
    - The news covered 16 leagues to Nelson in about two hours (Blackwood: "Within two hours"; Desbrière p. 177 "En deux heures").
11. **Nelson's memorandum of 20 October also fixed the night chain**: Blackwood with two frigates in sight of the enemy, two frigates between him and Defence, Colossus between Defence and Mars, and Mars in touch with Victory (Nicolas VII p. 136; Desbrière p. 178 n. 2). The notes quote only the "signals by night" part.
12. On the morning of the 20th Nelson was told that "near forty sail" had been seen outside Cádiz the evening before (diary, Nicolas p. 136; Corbett p. 337). This was an overcount, and it helps explain the dash to the Strait.
13. Fougueux lay in the inner bay until 19 October; not the whole fleet was at the harbour mouth from 10 October.
14. **Date variants.**
    - Escaño's letter has the fleet entering Cádiz bay on 21 August.
    - James and Clowes date the Mediterranean orders "September 17", and James dates the "pusillanimité" letter 17 September (the *Correspondance* has 14 and 15 September).
    - Corbett says the troops' embarkation was complete by 2 October.
    - Fraser (p. 36) has Decrès's draft recall order dated 17 September, to be carried by Rosily.
15. Desbrière gives Santísima Trinidad 140 guns (p. 134).

## F. What this changes for the scene

1. **19 October glyphs.** Show 7 ships of the line (6 French + 1 Spanish) and 2–3 frigates leaving Cádiz, not 12 of the line. Caption: "seven ships of the line got out on the 19th".
2. **20 October.** The rest were out by mid-morning (8–11 a.m.), so the date line can say 20 October. Avoid "at noon".
3. **Cádiz anchorage.**
   - Move the cluster off the Las Puercas reef: the bulk in the outer roads NE of the reef, around 36.555–36.560 N, 6.285–6.290 W, strung NW–SE, with a few ships near the Puntal–Matagorda narrows (around 36.512 N, 6.256 W) until 19 October.
   - Keep the cluster clear of the city's north wall (36.538 N).
   - Check against the chart's own coastline. The position of El Diamante is unverified.
4. **Glyph count at Cádiz.** If glyph counts encode fleet size, show 33 combined-fleet ships of the line (18 French + 15 Spanish), not 8–12, with 5 frigates and 2 brigs as smaller glyphs if frigates are shown at all. Terrible and San Fulgencio were laid up or docked: omit them, or show them unrigged.
5. **Headings.** All log bearings and courses are magnetic. Rotate glyph headings by about −21° to true (N by E magnetic ≈ 350° true; ENE magnetic ≈ NE true; Villeneuve's night course SW by S ≈ S by W true).
6. **The look at night.** Both fleets showed lights.
   - British frigates: blue lights and false fires, rockets, signal guns.
   - British fleet: answered with blue lights.
   - Allied fleet: stern and top lanterns, rockets, guns; Gravina's light at the head of the line.
   - The San Sebastián lighthouse was lit, under a moonless sky.
7. **The look by day.** Flag signals plus signal guns (puffs of smoke) passed ship to ship. The message reached Nelson, about 48 nm away, in about 2 to 2½ hours (7:00 to 9:30). On the night of 20–21 October, light the chain in this order: Euryalus (+ Sirius), Phoebe and Naiad, Defence, Colossus, Mars, Victory.
8. **Gibraltar and Tetuan glyphs.** The place coordinates are on land. Put Louis's ships in Gibraltar Bay (around 36.13 N, 5.37 W) and in Tetuan Bay off Martil (around 35.63 N, 5.25 W).
9. **Dawn positions.** These stay as in scene 1 (combined fleet centre 36.15 N 6.23 W; British 36.13 N 6.47 W).
10. **Captions about the council of war.** Give the "stay in port" argument to Escaño, Gravina's chief of staff, not to Prigny.

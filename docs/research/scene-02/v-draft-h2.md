# V-draft-h2: adversarial check of the Boulogne–Cádiz draft for the "Scene 2" section

Draft checked: `docs/research/scene-02/draft-h2.md` (85 lines) with its structured data, element [1] of `docs/research/scene-02/drafts-data.json` (12 fleet rows, 16 dates, 35 positions, no routes, 16 doubts, 19 key sources, 2 owner questions). Measured against `a-h2a.md`, `a-h2b.md`, `a-h2c.md` (the final word), then `v-h2a.md`, `v-h2b.md`, `v-h2c.md` and `h2-boulogne-cadiz.md`; scene 1's "The setting" and "The fleets" in `docs/history-notes.md` with `docs/research/dawn-setting.md`; the brief's `<history>`, `<concept>` and `<beats>`; and the lab's `src/scenes/02-road/fleets.ts` as of commit `aa7a358`. No file in the repository was changed.

## Verdict

The draft is sound in substance and follows the adjudications closely: every date on the date line (3 August, 28 August, 28 September, 19, 20 and 21 October), every glyph count (29 at Nelson's arrival, 23 after Louis left, a peak of 28, 27 from 17 October; 33 at anchor, 7 out on the 19th) and every contested figure I tested (Ulm's 25,365 and 13,600 muskets, the 14 signatories, Escaño not Prigny, SW by S, E.S.E. not E.N.E., 8–8.30 a.m. on the 20th) matches the rulings, and the pages I reopened (James III 217, 227, 232, 325–326, 332; Maude 90, 98, 123; NRS II 312–313; Nicolas VII 134) say what the draft says they say. All 35 structured positions are where the text puts them; every sea position is in Natural Earth's water and every camp on its land. The anchorage is well argued: modern depths put the main group in 6–10 m, the outermost ships in 9–10 m (deeper than the group, not on El Diamante, whose shallow core today lies at about 36°33.9′–34.6′ N, 6°17.2′–18.1′ W), and Faden's plan of 1797, which I opened, is what the draft says it is. The faults are elsewhere. One chart point matters to the owner first: the lab's narrows ship stands on today's Matagorda shore, while the draft's point (supported by the sources) is in 7 m but only 0.14 nm off the chart's coast, because Natural Earth has no Matagorda shore at all. The text still says the lab draws the British on 068°, which it no longer does. The lab's dawn layout stretches the combined line to 16 nm with its van under 5 nm off the coast, a licence the notes do not record. The Colossus departs from H2-40c without saying so. The draft never says the army turned east against Austria, though the brief asks it. And it leaves out three things the notes and checks hold: the British movements of 20–21 October, the combined fleet's nightfall position and the dispute over whether it wore in the night. Smaller slips follow (the Algésiras's latitude, the Sirius's hour, dropped versions, a few confidence levels, key-source gaps). With the fixes below it can be merged.

**1. The narrows (chart-mismatch, high).** The lab's narrows ship (`fleets.ts`, the last `french-74-anchored`, 36.5178 N 6.2488 W) is not at the draft's point (36°30.9′ N 6°15.3′ W) but 0.34 nm ENE of it and 0.20 nm north of Matagorda fort, on today's shoreline: EMODnet gives 0 to −0.3 m there. It shows as water on the chart only because Natural Earth leaves out the Matagorda side of the narrows altogether (Matagorda fort itself is "sea" in Natural Earth; its land starts on the Puntal side). This is the owner's first complaint, ships at anchor standing on the shore. The sources support the draft: Escaño has the Argonauta "entre los castillejos de Puntal y Matagorda", `a-h2c.md` H2-36e sets the glyph between the forts, and the draft's point is in 7 m today. But the draft's point lies only 0.14 nm off the chart's Puntal shore, and the draft does not say that the chart has no narrows. The point in the real channel that stands farthest from the chart's coast is about 36°31.0′ N 6°15.2′ W: 8 m today, 0.25 nm off the chart's coast, 0.2 nm from Matagorda fort and 0.6 nm from Puntal. Fix, the whole bullet (it also carries issue 11):

**5. Why east (omission, medium).** The brief's `<history>` has the army march east "against Austria (the Third Coalition)". The draft never says against whom or why; "Austria" appears only in "the Austrian eyewitness". Its own notes quote Napoleon on it (H2-08), and Maude gives the coalition's dates. Fix, a bullet after "The decision, 22–26 August":

> - **Why east.** Austria had joined Russia in a new coalition against France: Russia first approached her in October 1803, and the courier with Austria's adherence left Vienna on 7 July 1805 (Maude 1912, pp. 76, 90). On 23 August Napoleon wrote that he would raise his camps, be in Germany with 200,000 men by 1 vendémiaire (23 September) and march on Vienna; on the 25th, that Austria's preparations had stopped his embarkation (Corr. XI Nos. 9117, 9130). Mack's columns were ordered across the Inn into Bavaria on 8 September (Maude 1912, p. 98). High for Napoleon's letters; medium for the coalition's dates, which rest on Maude alone (the treaties, and Britain's part, were not researched).

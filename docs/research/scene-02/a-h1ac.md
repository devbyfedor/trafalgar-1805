# Adjudication a-h1ac: fact-checks v-h1a and v-h1c of h1-campaign.md

Scope: every verdict in `v-h1a.md` and `v-h1c.md` that is not a plain "confirmed", the "unverifiable" points that bear on the scene, and the points in both reports' "missed" and "scene impact" sections that would change what the scene shows. Points that change the screen come first.

Method: I downloaded the archive.org page-indexed OCR (hOCR search text plus page index) for Desbrière (1907), Corbett (1910), James vol. III (1886), Nicolas vol. VI and the *Naval Chronicle* vols XIV and XV. I split each into pages, read every cited page in full, and checked the page images wherever a number decided the ruling: Calder's position and casualty return, Desbrière's 11:30 a.m. position, both *Atlas* log tables, Corbett's chart, and the leaf headers used to fix the links. Mahan is the Project Gutenberg text (#16915). Downloads are in the session scratchpad (`dl/ah1ac-*`).

Links: every archive.org link below is a BookReader leaf link (`/page/nNNN`), checked against the printed page number on the page image. **Correction to v-h1a's links:** v-h1a's James links are leaf numbers three higher than the viewer index (its "iii. 351, n369" opens p. 354; p. 351 is at n366). Its Corbett links are one too high (its "p. 68, n94" opens p. 69; p. 68 is at n93). v-h1c's James and Corbett links are right. The Desbrière links in both reports are right.

## Evidence

### H1-26, S4-pos-British, S4-pos-allies: where Finisterre was fought

- **Calder's dispatch.** *Prince of Wales*, 23 July 1805, printed from the London Gazette Extraordinary of 31 July. On the page image it opens: "Yesterday, at noon, lat. 43° 30′ N., long. 11° 17′ W." (*Naval Chronicle* xiv. 163, [n184](https://archive.org/details/navalchronicleco14londiala/page/n184/mode/1up)). The same letter describes the enemy as 20 of the line, three large ships *en flûte*, five frigates and three brigs. It also says the action "lasted upwards of four hours".
- **James iii. 358** ([n373](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n373/mode/1up)): at noon, 43°54′ N, 11°38′ W from Greenwich, with Ferrol ESE 49 leagues and Cape Finisterre SE 39 leagues. My rhumb-line check puts James's point 120 nm from the cape on 301° and 150 nm from Ferrol. That fits his own bearings and distances, so it is a separate figure, not a misprint of Calder's.
- **James iii. 357** ([n372](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n372/mode/1up)): the combined fleet in the morning at 43°34′ N, 16°13′ W from Paris.
- **Desbrière pp. 60–61** ([n81](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n81/mode/1up), [n82](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n82/mode/1up)). The page image reads "44°,17 Nord et 11°,52 Ouest de Greewich" for Calder at 11.30 a.m., heading WSW in two columns. The same page has his line formed on the starboard tack and going "à peu près vers le Sud", which carried the fight south of the noon positions.
- **Desbrière p. 57** ([n78](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n78/mode/1up)): the allies at noon at 43°34′ and "semble-t-il, à 13° ou 14° de longitude, à environ 130 milles à l'Ouest du Ferrol". 130 nm west of Ferrol at 43.5° N is 11°13′ W of Greenwich, which is 13°33′ W of Paris. So the "13° or 14°" is a Paris figure, and the French reckoning in James (13°53′ W of Greenwich) ran about 2° too far west.
- **Corbett p. 196** ([n227](https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n227/mode/1up)): *Defiance* saw 24, then 27, sail to the SSW shortly before noon. The flagships' noon positions were about sixteen miles apart.
- **Corbett p. 195** ([n226](https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n226/mode/1up)): on 19 July Calder was "about a hundred miles west by north of Finisterre". On the morning of the 22nd he was "some three hundred miles west by north of" Ferrol. That second figure would put him near 14° W, which disagrees with every British fix. I treat it as a slip.
- **Rhumb-line distances from Cape Finisterre** (42°53′ N 9°16′ W), computed: Calder's fix 95 nm on 293°; James's 120 nm on 301°; Desbrière's 141 nm on 307°; Corbett's chart point (43°28′ N 12°20′ W) 139 nm on 285°; the French reckoning 206 nm.
- **Ruling.** The notes' box (43.5–44° N, 11.5–12.5° W, 110–130 nm WNW–NW) leaned on the chart and the French reckoning. The checker's box covers all three British fixes and the shift south during the afternoon. A single point for the smoke, 43°35′ N 11°40′ W, is 113 nm from the cape on 292°, which is WNW.

### H1-27a, H1-27b: losses

- **The Gazette return** (*Naval Chronicle* xiv. 163–164, [n184](https://archive.org/details/navalchronicleco14londiala/page/n184/mode/1up), [n185](https://archive.org/details/navalchronicleco14londiala/page/n185/mode/1up)). The page images read "Syrius — W. Prowse, 2 killed, 3 wounded" and "Total — 41 killed, 158 wounded". The ship rows add up to that total.
- **James iii. 362** ([n377](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n377/mode/1up)):
  - He gives the British "loss in gross" as 39 killed and 159 wounded, and remarks only that Calder's official return left out the officers' names. He does not say his figures come from the return.
  - His per-ship remarks differ from the return: he counts four *Dragon* men wounded by an accidental explosion, while the return has *Dragon* "none". So the total is his own.
  - He also says the ~600 claimed for the prizes' loss was "reduced" on his own comparison, giving 476 killed and wounded for the allies.
- **Corbett p. 202** ([n233](https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n233/mode/1up)): a table with 41 killed and 158 wounded for the British, and 155 killed and 341 wounded for the allies.
- **Desbrière p. 64** ([n85](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n85/mode/1up)): 41 killed and 158 wounded for the British; 82 dead and 179 wounded for the allies, not counting the prizes. Its footnote reads "180 tués et 40 blessés sur le Firme, 20 tués et 80 blessés sur le San-Raphaël".

### S4-end, S4-prizes: the end of the fight

- **Calder's defence** at his court martial (*Naval Chronicle* xv. 164, [n183](https://archive.org/details/navalchronicleco15londiala/page/n183/mode/1up)): "The firing did not cease till nine o'clock". He adds that it is completely dark at that hour in that latitude.
- **James iii. 361–362** ([n376](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n376/mode/1up), [n377](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n377/mode/1up)): *Firme* struck soon after 8 p.m. and *San Rafael* "did not hold out many minutes longer". The signal to discontinue was made at 8.25, but the general firing did not cease until 9.30.
- **Desbrière p. 63** ([n84](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n84/mode/1up)): the blind fight went on "Jusqu'à 7 h. 1/2" and then "L'obscurité fit cesser le feu".
- **Desbrière p. 64** ([n85](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n85/mode/1up)): *Firme* and *San Rafael* fought part of the night and struck "au jour".
- **Ratings.** The Gazette's margin note reads "St. Rafael, 84 guns. La Firme, 74 guns." (*Naval Chronicle* xiv. 163).
- **Captains.** Desbrière p. 60 n. prints "M. Montés" and "M. Villa Vicentio". A web search found no confirmation of the forenames Francisco and Rafael.

### S5-Vigo: Vigo

- **Desbrière p. 68** ([n89](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n89/mode/1up)):
  - On the 26th the wind went south, then south-west, and the fleet stood NE. It tacked through the night and at 4 a.m. (the 27th) was heading NE again, making little ground.
  - Villeneuve then decided on Vigo, and the ships bore away south.
  - "A 6 heures du soir on avait connaissance des îles Bayona et, le 28 au soir, toute l'armée … entrait dans la baie", anchoring at nightfall (p. 69, [n90](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n90/mode/1up)).
  - Read as written, the 6 p.m. sighting falls on the 27th.
- **Desbrière p. 85** ([n106](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n106/mode/1up)): the *Furet* was sent on 27 July with a letter to Decrès dated 27 July "à 40 lieues à l'O.-S.-O. du cap Finistère". The letters of the 28th are dated from the road of Vigo. A ship 40 leagues WSW of the cape on the 27th was about 110 nm from the Cíes, which makes a sighting at 6 p.m. that day doubtful. So the Cíes day stays open; the anchoring on the evening of 28 July is secure.
- **James iii. 372** ([n387](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n387/mode/1up)): "on the evening of the 26th, he came to an anchor", two days early.
- **Mahan**, *Life of Nelson* II: "on the 28th, put into Vigo Bay".

### H1-30: A Coruña

- **Desbrière p. 86** ([n107](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n107/mode/1up)):
  - The fleet sailed on the 31st and gained only 5 leagues against a NW gale.
  - On 1 August a shift of wind to the SW let it make for Cape Finisterre, which it doubled in the evening; "le 2, Villeneuve entre dans la baie".
  - Napoleon's letter of 16 July then stopped it entering Ferrol. The 13 French anchored at A Coruña and the 2 Spanish went into Ferrol.
- **Desbrière p. 96** ([n119](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n119/mode/1up)): victualling ran from 3 to 7 August, and Villeneuve wrote on 3 August.
- **James iii. 373** ([n388](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n388/mode/1up)): sailed 30 July with a fair WSW wind, and entered Corunna on the evening of 1 August, the same day the British were driven from their station that morning.
- **Corbett p. 218** ([n249](https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n249/mode/1up)): under 1 August, the SW wind drove Calder to leeward and Villeneuve "was able to run into Coruna without firing a shot".
- **Corbett p. 219** ([n250](https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n250/mode/1up)): "On August 2nd as the Combined Squadron were making the junction at Coruna".
- **Mahan**: "reached Ferrol on the first of August".
- **Ruling.** The notes put the French date right. But James's 1 August is not explained by his calendar slip alone: he dates the British side from British sources and merges the two. The 2 August entry rests on Desbrière's narrative from the French records; I found no letter dated at A Coruña on the 2nd, hence medium confidence.

### H1-34, S5-reinf, S5-35: Collingwood and the fleets at Cádiz

- **James iii. 375** ([n390](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n390/mode/1up)):
  - At 10 a.m. on the 20th, with Cádiz NE about nine leagues, the allies saw three British ships of the line right ahead, and at 3 p.m. bore up for the harbour.
  - At midnight *Mars* joined from Tangier Bay, and Collingwood "with his four sail of the line" resumed his station before daylight on the 21st.
  - James puts 35 French and Spanish ships of the line in port, including six Spanish ships already there.
- **Corbett pp. 270–271** ([n303](https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n303/mode/1up), [n304](https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n304/mode/1up)):
  - Collingwood to his wife, 21 August: "down came the Combined Fleet of 36 sail of men-of-war. We were only three poor things with a frigate and a bomb".
  - Gravina led the light squadron's push, the fleet re-entered Cádiz on the 20th, and Collingwood resumed his station next day.
  - "By the 28th the two squadrons were together off Cadiz, and two days later Calder appeared."
- **Corbett p. 267** ([n300](https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n300/mode/1up)): Calder crossing the Bay "with his eighteen of the line".
- **Desbrière p. 107** ([n132](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n132/mode/1up)):
  - The allies met "les 4 vaisseaux de Collingwood". His footnote counts *Mars*, "just" back from Tangier, with *Dreadnought*, *Colossus* and *Achille*, plus *Endymion* and *Thunder*, citing Collingwood's letter of 20 August from off Cape Trafalgar.
  - Magon led the light squadron's chase, Collingwood drew off to 21 miles, and he was back off the bay "le soir même".
- **Desbrière pp. 108–109** ([n133](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n133/mode/1up)–[n134](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n134/mode/1up)): Bickerton's ships from Cartagena joined from 22 August. Desbrière names *Queen*, *Minotaur*, *Mars* and *Bellerophon*, although *Mars* was already with Collingwood (James). Collingwood had 11 when, on 30 August, Calder arrived with 21 "autres vaisseaux", named in the footnote (including *Dragon* and *Goliath*): 32 in all.
- **Ruling on H1-34.** Collingwood's own words written the next day outweigh Desbrière's count. *Mars* was not with him when the allies appeared.

### H1-05: the Strait and the origin of "8 April"

- **Desbrière p. 9** ([n28](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n28/mode/1up)): on the morning of 9 April the Rock and Ape's Hill were in sight. At 9 a.m. the fleet entered the Strait in two columns, at about 4 p.m. its frigates signalled 11 warships including 6 of the line, and at nightfall it anchored off Cádiz.
- **Desbrière p. 17 n.** ([n38](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n38/mode/1up)), in French translation:
  - Orde to Gardner, 10 April: "Hier, étant à l'ancre", he had nearly had to fight the 20 or 21 sail of the Toulon fleet.
  - Orde to Nelson, 10 April (BM Add. MS 34919): a superior force from the east appeared "l'après-midi du 9 courant". He names the ships he is taking north: *Renown*, *Defence*, *Polyphemus*, *Agamemnon*, *Ruby*.
- **Desbrière p. 18** ([n39](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n39/mode/1up)): Gardner, 22 April, learned from the *Melampus* that 11 French ships and 7 frigates "ont passé le 9 à Gibraltar".
- **Desbrière p. 21** ([n42](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n42/mode/1up)): Admiralty order of 25 April, "L'ennemi ayant passé le détroit le 9 courant".
- **Corbett p. 111** ([n136](https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n136/mode/1up)): the *Beagle*, under Cape Spartel on 9 April, saw strange vessels passing the Straits.
- **Corbett p. 93** ([n118](https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n118/mode/1up)): Parker of the *Amazon* reported a Ragusan who had seen the Toulon squadron passing the Straits "on April 8th".
- **Nicolas vi. 408** ([n447](https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter06nelsiala/page/n447/mode/1up)): Nelson to Marsden, off Toro, 18 April, has just received "the account of the Enemy having passed the Straits the 8th of April".
- **Nicolas vi. xxxiv** ([n37](https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter06nelsiala/page/n37/mode/1up)): the chronology under 18 April reads "passed Gibraltar on the 8th".
- **Mahan** (Gutenberg #16915): Gibraltar on the 8th, anchored off Cádiz on the 9th.
- **Ruling.** The date to show is 9 April, which both sides already agree on. Only the notes' attribution changes: the 8th is second-hand intelligence that Nelson relayed, plus later historians.

### H1-05b: Orde

About midday, while clearing store-ships, his squadron saw *Renown* (Strachan) running down under a press of sail with the signal for a superior enemy. Strachan had seen Villeneuve coming out of the Straits at daybreak off Tarifa.
- **Corbett p. 63** ([n88](https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n88/mode/1up)): Orde weighed and drew off towards Lagos.
- **Desbrière p. 17 n.** ([n38](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n38/mode/1up)): Orde told Gardner he meant to join him "avec ses cinq vaisseaux". His letter to Nelson lists *Renown* first among the ships going with him; his flagship *Glory* makes up the six.

### GLYPH-crossing: who crossed together

- **Desbrière p. 27** ([n48](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n48/mode/1up)): Decrès's return for the combined force, totalling 29 sail.

### H1-15: five frigates at Corvo

### H1-07 / H1-36a: the dating of table B

- **Table B** (Desbrière p. 26 n., [n47](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n47/mode/1up), image): an opening line "10 avril (20 germinal)" with the Cádiz stop and no position, then numbered rows from "11 — 21", 35°27′, 10°30′ (Paris).
- **Table A** (p. 9, [n28](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n28/mode/1up), image):
  - It opens with "9 au 10 germinal" (the departure from Toulon), then rows 10 to 19. Its row 19 (36°9′, 9°35′30″) can only be noon on 10 April, so table A's labels are start dates.
  - Table B's opening line describes the same noon-to-noon day as table A's row 19, but labels it with that day's end date (20 germinal).
  - Read with end-date labels, table B follows table A with no gap and no overlap. Read with start-date labels, the noon of 11 April is missing.
- **The westward turn** (Desbrière p. 25, [n46](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n46/mode/1up)): "à partir du 15 avril, faire route à l'Ouest" fits the printed 15→16 April rows. The same paragraph's "Le 26 étant par 25° de longitude" fits neither reading, so this text cannot settle it.
- **The landfall arithmetic** (v-h1a): it does fit a one-day shift exactly. Its premise, that the *Atlas* kept the flagship's reckoning, is the open point. The 11 May jump (5°35′ in a day, against 2°–2½° on the days either side) shows her reckoning changed that day. If she then took the flagship's longitude by signal, the shift reading gains weight; if she corrected her own, it loses it.

### H1-35e: table A labels and the 4 p.m. point

- Desbrière p. 9 ([n28](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n28/mode/1up)) gives times (9 a.m. entering the Strait, about 4 p.m. frigates' signal, nightfall anchoring) but no place for 4 p.m.
- Along the track Europa Point → Tarifa → Cape Trafalgar → the Cádiz offing (about 65 nm), the 9 a.m. to 7.45 p.m. passage averages about 6 knots.
- Seven hours from the Strait entrance at that speed puts the fleet about 5 nm past Cape Trafalgar, off Conil. A point a few miles offshore there, about 36°14′ N 6°10′–6°12′ W, keeps the line at sea.
- Barcelona (41°23′ N 2°11′ E) to row 6 (41°04′ N 2°33′ E) bears about S 41° E, which is SE.

## What changes on screen

1. **Dates.** Toulon 30 March. Strait 9 April. Cádiz 9–10 April. Martinique 14 May. Leaving Martinique 5 June. Convoy 8 June. Turn for Europe 10 June, with 11 June recorded. Finisterre 22 July. Vigo 28 July. A Coruña 2 August, with James's 1 August recorded; "1–2 August" also works. Cádiz 20 August.

   - Allied crossing group: 14 (17 at Martinique on 14 May, 18 from 16 May, 20 after Magon on 4 June).
   - Orde on 9 April: 6 (5 plus *Renown*).
   - Frigates rejoining off Corvo: 5.
   - Collingwood when the allies arrive: 3 ships of the line, plus a frigate and a bomb; 4 from that night.
   - British off Cádiz at the end of August: 26 or 32, disputed.
3. **Position of the Finisterre smoke.** About 43°35′ N 11°40′ W, 113 nm WNW of Cape Finisterre, not 12°20′ W.
4. **Captions.** If losses are shown: British 41 killed, 158 wounded; allied "about 500" (range 476–580).

   - Draw the 9 April 4 p.m. point just past Cape Trafalgar, off Conil, and closer inshore than in the notes.

## How I checked

- **Charts.** For every form a ruling turns on, I opened the cited crop myself (the earlier sessions' downloads, or a new IIIF crop where none existed) and read the letters. Where I could, I looked for further English charts and maps of 1795-1810 that the research did not use, to try to refute the form.
- **Texts.** I recounted the forms in the archive.org OCR texts already downloaded (line-break hyphens joined, long s normalised, case ignored; orders of magnitude only) and read the contexts the rulings rest on.
- **Sites.** I tested every site against Natural Earth 10 m (`data/raw/natural-earth/`: land, minor islands, rivers): a town on its shore or inland where it is, a cape at its tip, an island's centre on the island, a water name on the water, a river label near its river line.
- **The rule.** (1) Where the English charts of 1800-1807 agree, letter their form. (2) Where they disagree, drop any form a visitor would misread. (3) Then the naval and printed usage of 1805 decides. (4) Faden 1807 breaks a tie. Type: ordinary characters, "St." with a stop, "C." for capes, no accents, spaced capitals only for oceans and seas.

### C. Grisnez: confirmed, but on different grounds, and at medium confidence

- Form **C. Grisnez**, cape, 50.870 N, 1.584 E. On Natural Earth's coast (0.05 km); NE's westernmost vertex there is 50.869 N, 1.581 E, 0.3 km away. Good.
- What h5 gets wrong:
  - It calls C. Grisnez "the one clear chart form" and rates the form high on LWBC and ST09. Neither plate is of 1800-1807 (1794 and 1809).
  - ST09 is not clear: at native resolution (crop `8800,1860,240,90/full/0/default.jpg`, enlarged) the fourth letter has the same hooked descender as the final z. I read **C. Griznez** there (doubtful), not Grisnez.

- So the English charts of 1794-1809 disagree: C. Grisnez (LWBC 1794, read), Gris Nez (KN06 1806, read), probably C. Griznez (ST09 1809).
- Policy 2 drops nothing decisive ("Griznez" and Norie's "Gris ness" read as slips today; "Grisnez" and "Gris Nez" do not).
- Policy 3, usage of 1804-05, which I recounted with OCR variants and read in context:
  - *Naval Chronicle* 12 (1804): "Cape Grisnez" 1, plus "Grisncz" 1 (an OCR slip for Grisnez, in a dispatch dated off Boulogne, 23 October 1804), and one "Grisney".
  - *Naval Chronicle* 13 (1805): "Cape Grisnez" 3, plus 2 that OCR garbles ("Grisnci", "Grisiu-x": the Archer's report of 25 April 1805), one "Griznes", and one "Griz-Nez" (a table of bearings: "The Signal House on Cape Griz-Nez").

  - So "Grisnez" is clearly the commonest printed form of 1804-05. Faden 1807 does not letter the cape.
- Verdict: the form stands under policy 3. Change the table's ground ("LWBC, ST09") to "usage of 1804-05; LWBC 1794", and lower the confidence to medium. Add **C. Gris Nez** (KN06, 1806) to the alternatives and to doubt 5: it is the one in-range chart form, equally period, and the closest to today's Cap Gris-Nez. Read strictly (only plates dated 1800-1807 count at step 1, and a single plate counts as agreement), the rule would give C. Gris Nez; I do not recommend that reading, because the plates on both sides of 1806 and the printed usage of 1804-05 all close the name up.

# A-V-merged-h2: rulings on the check of the merged "Scene 2" section, Boulogne to the dawn positions

Object: `docs/research/scene-02/history-scene-02-merged.md`, the part on Boulogne, Ulm, Nelson off Cádiz, the sortie and the positions on the chart. Check ruled on: `scratchpad/history/v-merged-h2.md` (20 issues). 1 November 2026. Nothing in the repository was changed.

## Verdict

The check is right on 16 of its 20 issues and partly right on 4. None fails outright. What I found on the pages:

- **"The station" is conditional in Nelson's letter.** To Blackwood, 4 October: in fresh easterly breezes he would work up for Cádiz, "never getting to the Northward of it" (Nicolas VII 73). Desbrière (p. 149) turns this into never being north of the parallel through the port. The section gave Desbrière's flat version as Nelson's rule.
- **James dates the "pusillanimité" letter 17 September** (III 378), and puts the new orders in the same letter. Clowes (V 128) dates only the orders 17 September, so the check's "Clowes follows him" needs splitting.
- **James's "22nd" is Berthier's letter to Marmont.** James quotes it from Dumas (III 332). It has a different condition from No. 9120: if the fleets could not reach the Channel. That version belongs in the body and the doubts, not in the brief table.
- **Louis's convoy.** Austen's letter of 21 October (Hubback p. 154) has them part from the convoy early, on news of the sortie. Corbett (p. 335, from the Queen's log) has the usual parting off Cape Ténès. Both fall on the 21st.
- **Beatty's pages.** "Wore twice" is p. 10 and the 2 a.m. wear p. 11 in both editions. The Donegal's 18th is p. 6 in both.
- **Corbett p. 340.** The Africa holding on north is in Corbett's own text, so "(Corbett 1910, p. 340)" in the Fleet-sizes night row is right as it stands.
- **A dropped version the check missed.** The Prince of Wales sailed on the evening of the 13th (Corbett, Beatty, Desbrière) or on the 14th (James, Clowes). I added it to the text for issue 6.

**The owner's decisions.**
- **Decision 1** (true spacing) overrides the check's texts for issue 14, which still describe the six-per-fleet lab layout. It also overrides `a-v-draft-h2.md` A3, which recorded the spread-out layout as a licence and kept it as drawn.
- **Decision 2** makes A2's 047° a deliberate licence.
- **Decision 3** fixes the beat 4 caption.

The texts for these places are in "The owner's decisions in this part" below.

## Final texts and evidence, issue by issue

### 1. The station (holds)

**Apply.** This replaces the bullet "The station":

> - **The station.** Nelson kept the body of the fleet "from sixteen to eighteen leagues West of Cadiz" (to Blackwood, 4 October; to Barham, 5 October), fifteen to eighteen by his letter to Ball of 11 October and "15 lgs. to twenty" by his diary of 14 October (Hudson 1917, p. 21), on about the port's parallel. In fresh easterly breezes he would work up toward Cádiz, "never getting to the Northward of it", and on hearing that the enemy was coming out he would carry a press of sail south toward Cape Spartel (to Blackwood, 4 October; Nicolas VII 73, 75, 111, 122). Desbrière (p. 149) puts this as never standing north of the port's parallel. Nelson's reasons: not to be caught close in by a westerly gale and swept into the Strait, and to hide his strength and tempt the enemy out (to Barham, 5 October; Corbett 1910, pp. 326–327). High.

In Open doubts, "The British off Cádiz, August to October": replace "or 15–18 (a letter to Ball)" with "or 15–18 (to Ball, 11 October, Nicolas VII 111)".

**Why.**
- **The letter.** Nelson to Blackwood, 4 October (headed "Cadiz, East 17 leagues"). The fleet would be 16–18 leagues west of Cádiz. In fresh breezes from the east he would work up for Cádiz, "never getting to the Northward of it". On hearing the enemy was standing out, he would crowd sail south toward Cape Spartel and Larache.
  - The clause about the north hangs on the easterly-breeze sentence.
  - Nelson's "it" is Cádiz, not a parallel.
- **Desbrière's reading.** Desbrière (p. 149) has the main body at 16–18 leagues, closing in east winds, "mais sans jamais être au Nord du parallèle passant par le port".
- **The other letters.**
  - To Barham, 5 October (p. 75): the same 16–18 leagues, with the westerly-gale reason.
  - To Ball, 11 October (p. 111): "the body of the Fleet from fifteen to eighteen leagues West of Cadiz".
  - The diary, 14 October (p. 122): "from fifteen to twenty".
- **The section's own positions.** The flat rule clashes with them: the Victory at 36°34′–35′ N at 9.30 and 36°36′ N at noon on 19 October, against Cádiz at 36°32′ N.
- **Small changes to the check's text.**
  - Desbrière's version is named as his reading.
  - "His reasons" becomes "Nelson's reasons", so that it cannot be read as Desbrière's.
  - The Blackwood page and the Barham page are cited apart.

### 5. Louis on 18–21 October (holds)

**Apply.** This replaces the bullet "Louis on 18–21 October":

> - **Louis on 18–21 October.** He finished watering at Tetuan on the 15th; that evening a westerly wind forced him back there, and on the evening of the 17th a frigate brought the order to see the Malta convoy as far as Cartagena (Austen, Hubback 1906, p. 152; Corbett 1910, pp. 331 n., 335).

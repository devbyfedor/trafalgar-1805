# Fact-check v-h1c: H1 notes, §4 Cape Finisterre, §5 Vigo–Ferrol–Cádiz, claims H1-24 to H1-36, §7 waypoints

Checked file: `C:/Users/User/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/d--dev-trafalgar-1805/81d46a93-68b9-4541-8365-f913ef045b3d/scratchpad/research/h1-campaign.md` (lines 182–251, 275–513, 514–554). The notes were not edited.

Method. I downloaded the archive.org OCR for Desbrière (1907), Corbett (1910), James vol. III (1886 printing), Nicolas vols VI and VII, and the *Naval Chronicle* vols XIII–XV, and split each into pages. I then looked at the page images wherever a number mattered: the *Atlas* log tables, Calder's position, the casualty return, the diary positions and Corbett's chart. Independent checks came from Calder's own dispatch and casualty return (London Gazette Extraordinary, reprinted in the *Naval Chronicle*), the minutes and sentence of his court martial (*Naval Chronicle* XV), and letters that US consuls at Cádiz and Lisbon sent to James Madison in April 1805. I recomputed every Paris→Greenwich and Republican→Gregorian conversion in scope (Paris = 2°20′14″ E; 1 germinal XIII = 22 Mar 1805, 1 messidor = 20 Jun, 1 thermidor = 20 Jul, 1 fructidor = 19 Aug).

Page links use archive.org leaf numbers (`/page/nNN`). James's scan has its own page labels, so all James references below give the printed page numbers.

## Evidence

### §4 Cape Finisterre

**H1-24, Calder's force.** James iii. 356–357 lists the 15 ships with their guns, both frigates, *Nile* and *Frisk*, and Stirling's five joining on 15 July ([n371](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n371/mode/1up), [n372](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n372/mode/1up)). Calder's dispatch, *Prince of Wales*, 23 July 1805 (London Gazette Extraordinary, Admiralty Office, 31 July 1805, reprinted in *Naval Chronicle* xiv. 163), gives the same force in his own words ([n184](https://archive.org/details/navalchronicleco14londiala/page/n184/mode/1up)). Desbrière p. 60 ([n81](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n81/mode/1up)) has "15 vaisseaux, 2 frégates, 1 cutter et 1 lougre". Corbett p. 195 gives the cruising orders and "By July 19th … about a hundred miles west by north of Finisterre" ([n226](https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n226/mode/1up)).

**H1-25, Allied force.** James iii. 357: 20 SoL, seven frigates, two brigs and the *Matilda*. The 20 named ships are in the orders of battle in Desbrière p. 60 n. and Corbett p. 197 n. ([n228](https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n228/mode/1up)). Calder's dispatch (*Naval Chronicle* xiv. 163) reports what the British believed they saw: 20 SoL, three large ships *en flûte* of about 50 guns, five frigates and three brigs.

**Morning and first contact.** Reille, in Desbrière p. 57 ([n78](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n78/mode/1up)), has three close columns, the Spaniards on the right, and clearing about noon. James iii. 357–358 has the light WNW breeze, the course ESE, and *Indomptable*'s "10 sail in the NNE" at about 11 a.m. ([n373](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n373/mode/1up)). Corbett p. 196 has *Defiance* reporting 24, then 27 sail SSW shortly before noon, and the flagships about sixteen miles apart at noon ([n227](https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n227/mode/1up)).

**Positions, H1-26.**
- Calder's dispatch: "Yesterday, at noon, lat. 43° 30′ N., long. 11° 17′ W." I read this on the page image of *Naval Chronicle* xiv. 163 ([n184](https://archive.org/details/navalchronicleco14londiala/page/n184/mode/1up)). The notes do not mention it.
- James iii. 358: noon 43°54′N 11°38′W, with Ferrol ESE 49 leagues and Finisterre SE 39 leagues. The distances fit his own coordinates (147 and 117 nm), not Calder's (133 and 95 nm). The two are independent figures.
- Desbrière pp. 60–61, from the page image: 11:30 a.m. "44°,17 Nord et 11°,52 Ouest de Greewich, le cap à l'O.-S.-O." ([n82](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n82/mode/1up)).
- Desbrière p. 57 for the allies: 43°34′, "semble-t-il, à 13° ou 14° de longitude, à environ 130 milles à l'Ouest du Ferrol". 130 nm W of Ferrol is ≈11°13′W Greenwich, so the "13–14°" must be from Paris (10°40′–11°40′ Gr). The allied DR in James (13°53′W Gr) therefore ran about 2° too far west.
- Corbett p. 195 also says Calder was "some three hundred miles west by north of" Ferrol that morning (checked on the image, [n226](https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n226/mode/1up)). That conflicts with every British fix and should be treated as an error.
- Conclusion: the British noon positions lie between 43°30′N 11°17′W and 44°17′N 11°52′W, and the allies were about 16 miles SW–SSW of them. The fighting was some miles further SW. A fair box is ~43°20′–43°55′N, 11°15′–12°00′W.

**Start of the action, fog, end of firing.**
- James iii. 359–361: about 5:15 p.m. the Spaniards opened fire as *Hero* went about; *Hero* answered at 5:20; "no ship could see much beyond her own length"; signal to discontinue at 8:25; firing continued until 9:30 ([n374](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n374/mode/1up)–[n377](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n377/mode/1up)).
- Desbrière p. 62 has *Argonaute* opening fire at 5 p.m., and in the note the *Egyptienne* lieutenant on "la brume la plus épaisse" ([n83](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n83/mode/1up)).
- Desbrière p. 63: the blind fight went on until about 7:30, when darkness stopped the firing ([n84](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n84/mode/1up)). The notes omit this variant.

**Prizes.**
- James iii. 361: *Firme* struck soon after 8 p.m. and *San Rafael* a few minutes later.
- Desbrière pp. 63–64: the two ships drifted into the British line, fought part of the night and struck at daybreak. This is the French account and differs on the time.
- Calder's note in the Gazette: "St. Rafael, 84 guns. La Firme, 74 guns."
- James iii. 370–371 gives build, tonnage, guns and crew, the prison-ship use, and "On the 31st of July … the two prizes anchored in Plymouth Sound" ([n385](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n385/mode/1up), [n386](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n386/mode/1up)). Desbrière p. 64 n. has them arriving "le 30" ([n85](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n85/mode/1up)).
- Captains: Corbett p. 197 n. has "Montes" and "Villa Vicentio", and so does Desbrière p. 60 n. The National Archives prize paper HCA 32/1026/1861 lists "El Firme (master Villavicecio)" (found by web search). I could not confirm the forenames Francisco and Rafael.

**Casualties, H1-27.**
- The Gazette return, page images of *Naval Chronicle* xiv. 163–164 ([n184](https://archive.org/details/navalchronicleco14londiala/page/n184/mode/1up), [n185](https://archive.org/details/navalchronicleco14londiala/page/n185/mode/1up)), runs: Hero 1/4, Ajax 2/16, Triumph 5/6, Barfleur 3/7, Agamemnon 0/3, Windsor Castle 10/35, Defiance 1/7, Prince of Wales 3/20, Repulse 0/4, Raisonable 1/1, Dragon none, Glory 1/1, Warrior none, Thunderer 7/11, Malta 5/40, Sirius 2/3. "Total—41 killed, 158 wounded." The rows add up to that total.
- Desbrière p. 64 and Corbett's table p. 202 agree on 41/158 ([n233](https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n233/mode/1up)).
- James iii. 362 gives 39/159 without saying it comes from the return ([n377](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n377/mode/1up)).
- The allied figures (Corbett 155/341; Desbrière 82/179 plus the footnote "180 tués et 40 blessés sur le Firme, 20 tués et 80 blessés sur le San-Raphaël"; James 476 after reducing the "about 600" said for the prizes) are all printed as the notes give them.

**Second day, 24 July, the dispatch and the court martial (H1-28).**
- James iii. 363–365 has the fleets 17 miles apart at dawn, Calder concentrating and hauling up NE, Villeneuve going aboard *Hortense*, the allies bearing up at noon and hauling their wind at 4 p.m. about three leagues off, and the wind going N–NNE on the 24th with the fleets out of sight by 6 p.m. ([n378](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n378/mode/1up)–[n380](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n380/mode/1up)).
- Desbrière pp. 65–66 has Escaño's times (1:30 general chase, 3 p.m. line abreast heading ENE, hauled up a quarter of an hour later). It also prints, as cited, Villeneuve's noon "43°,41 et 12°,48" and the Spanish "43°,34 … et 9°,13", which I read on the image ([n86](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n86/mode/1up)).
- Corbett p. 205: no longer in sight on the morning of the 25th ([n236](https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n236/mode/1up)).
- James iii. 368–369 has the suppressed paragraph and the court martial in *Prince of Wales*, Portsmouth harbour, 23–26 December, "severely reprimanded" ([n383](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n383/mode/1up), [n384](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n384/mode/1up)).
- An independent primary source is the trial report in *Naval Chronicle* xv. 79: "First day, Portsmouth, December 23, 1805", signal hoisted on *Prince of Wales* ([n94](https://archive.org/details/navalchronicleco15londiala/page/n94/mode/1up)). The sentence at xv. 174–175 reads: assembled "on the 23d day of December, 1805 … until the 26th"; "not … actuated either by cowardice or disaffection, but … from error in judgment"; "severely reprimanded" ([n193](https://archive.org/details/navalchronicleco15londiala/page/n193/mode/1up), [n194](https://archive.org/details/navalchronicleco15londiala/page/n194/mode/1up)).
- Corbett p. 207 has Nelson to Gambier, 2 Oct ([n238](https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n238/mode/1up)). Nicolas vii. 121 n. has "October 14th.—Prince of Wales parted. H.M. Ship Africa joined" ([n160](https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nels/page/n160/mode/1up)).

### §5 Vigo, Coruña, Ferrol, the turn south and Cádiz

**25–28 July.**
- Desbrière p. 67: noon 25th, *Didon* sighted Finisterre to the ESE; the course for Cádiz "au S.-O. 1/4 S."; at 5 p.m. all ships made for Vigo ([n88](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n88/mode/1up)).
- Desbrière p. 68: on the 26th the wind went S then SW and the fleet headed NE for Ferrol. The decision for Vigo followed. "A 6 heures du soir on avait connaissance des îles Bayona et, le 28 au soir, toute l'armée … entrait dans la baie", anchoring at nightfall (pp. 68–69, [n89](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n89/mode/1up)).
- Desbrière p. 85 has the *Furet* sent on 27 July, the letter "datée du 27 juillet, à 40 lieues à l'O.-S.-O. du cap Finistère", the letters dated 28 July "en rade de Vigo", and *Achille*'s 200 sick and five days' water ([n106](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n106/mode/1up)).
- James iii. 372 has "on the evening of the 26th" ([n387](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n387/mode/1up)).
- The Cíes sighting at 6 p.m. is ambiguous. If the 27 July letter really was written 40 leagues WSW of Finisterre, a sighting on the 28th is more plausible. Desbrière's wording reads as the 27th.

**Vigo to Coruña (H1-29, H1-30).**
- Desbrière p. 86 ([n107](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n107/mode/1up)): 800 sick put into *Atlas*, left with *España* and *América*; "dès le 31 … réduite à 15 vaisseaux … malgré une tempête de Nord-Ouest … 5 lieues"; 1 August SW wind, Finisterre doubled that evening; "le 2, Villeneuve entre dans la baie"; the order of 16 July; 13 French at Coruña, 2 Spanish at Ferrol.
- James iii. 373 has 30 July, 13 French and 2 Spanish, 7 frigates, 2 brigs, "slow sailers", and Coruña on the evening of 1 August ([n388](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n388/mode/1up)).
- Corbett p. 212 has "on the last day of July"; pp. 218–219 have "On August 2nd as the Combined Squadron were making the junction at Coruna" ([n243](https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n243/mode/1up), [n250](https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n250/mode/1up)).

**The combined fleet at Ferrol (H1-31).**
- Desbrière pp. 96–99 ([n119](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n119/mode/1up)–[n122](https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n122/mode/1up)) gives: 45 days' victuals 3–7 Aug; the 8th out and anchored; the 9th one mile gained; the 10th, 5 French and 8 Spanish out of Ferrol, then the Bay of Ares; the 11th–12th wind E and calm; the 13th the last three joined before noon and all were under sail by 2 p.m. Totals: "29 vaisseaux, 6 frégates, 4 corvettes"; French 18 and Spanish 11 by name; *Príncipe de Asturias* 112; *San Julián* left behind.
- Corbett p. 239: twenty-nine of the line, only one three-decker (*Príncipe de Asturias* "120"), six 80s, six frigates and four corvettes ([n270](https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n270/mode/1up)).
- James iii. 373–374: weighed on the 9th, "Zerez" on the 10th, out on the 11th; *San Fulgencio* rated 64 ([n389](https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n389/mode/1up)).

## Points the notes missed

1. Calder's own dispatch and casualty return (London Gazette Extraordinary, 31 Jul 1805; *Naval Chronicle* xiv. 163–164). They give noon 43°30′N 11°17′W, his view of the enemy (20 SoL plus 3 large ships *en flûte*, 5 frigates and 3 brigs) and the official 41 killed, 158 wounded.
2. Desbrière's two variants on the action: the firing ended about 7:30 p.m., and the Spanish prizes struck at daybreak on the 23rd in the French account.
3. Desbrière's "130 milles à l'Ouest du Ferrol" makes sense only with a Paris longitude, and it supports Calder's 11°17′W. Corbett p. 195's "three hundred miles" is an outlier.
4. Two independent non-naval contemporaries confirm the 9 April arrival off Cádiz and the 10 April sailing: Terry from Cádiz, and Jarvis from Lisbon in the Madison papers.
5. Desbrière prints "7 h. 45 du matin" for the Cádiz anchoring. Table A rightly reads it as evening but should flag the slip.
6. The link to Corbett's chart should be n180, not n181.
7. Collingwood variants. Desbrière: 4 ships including *Mars* when the allies appeared, back on station the same evening. Corbett: Gravina, not Magon, led the light squadron's chase.
8. Rating variants: *San Rafael* 84 guns in the Gazette; *Príncipe de Asturias* 112 (Desbrière) or 120 (Corbett).
9. The Cíes sighting may be 27 July 6 p.m., not 28 July. James's 26 July for Vigo is two days early, not one.
10. The 30/31 July Plymouth arrival of the prizes is probably a civil/nautical-day difference.
11. Period English names that the sources in this scope use: "Carthagena", "Cape de Gata", "Corunna", "Cape Ortugal" (James) or "Ortegal", "Cape Prior", "Bayona Islands" for the Cíes, "Zerez" (James) for Ares, "Cape St. Vincent", "Cadiz".

## Scene impact

- **Finisterre smoke and glyphs.** Place them around **43°35′–43°50′N, 11°20′–11°50′W**, about 100–120 nm WNW of Cape Finisterre, not at 12°20′W as on Corbett's chart. Calder's noon fix is 43°30′N 11°17′W, the allies were about 16 nm SW of the British, and the fighting ran WSW from there.
- **Date line for this stretch.** Toulon 30 Mar (evening). Off Cartagena 7 Apr. Strait 9 Apr. Off Cádiz evening 9 Apr, sailing early 10 Apr; this is now confirmed independently, so do not show 8 April. Finisterre 22 Jul. Vigo 28 Jul. Coruña/Ferrol 2 Aug; "1–2 August" is acceptable, as James and British accounts say 1 Aug. Sailed from Ferrol 13 Aug. Turned south 15 Aug at nightfall. Cádiz 20 Aug.
- **Glyph counts** are confirmed: Calder 15; allies at Finisterre 20 (+7 frigates); 18 after the action; 15 from Vigo; 29 from Ferrol; Collingwood 3, becoming 4 with *Mars* at midnight. In Cádiz, use 29 arrived plus 6 in port = 35 (James), with 36 as Collingwood's count of all men-of-war.
- **Caption numbers.** British loss at Finisterre: 41 killed, 158 wounded (Calder's return), not 39/159. Allied loss: give a range or "about 500" (Corbett 496, James 476).
- **The Toulon–Cádiz line in table A is sound** and can be drawn as given. Small refinements: row 5 is in the open sea south of the Gulf of Lion; row 6 is SE of Barcelona; the 4 p.m. point is better off Cape Trafalgar than off Conil.
- **Atlantic legs.** The Table B values are exact. Whether intermediate ticks fall a day early or late does not change the drawn line.

# V-H3a: independent check of the H3 signal notes (summary, §1 Day signals, §2 Night signals; H3-01 to H3-14)

Notes checked: `C:/Users/User/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/d--dev-trafalgar-1805/81d46a93-68b9-4541-8365-f913ef045b3d/scratchpad/research/h3-signals.md`, lines 1–272 (not edited).

Method:
- I pulled the OCR of every scanned book cited in scope, split it leaf by leaf, and checked each cited page against its printed page header.
- I viewed the two lantern-figure plates (1799 *Night Signals* pp. 25–26) as images.
- For each statement I looked for a second, independent source: the 1796 *Signal-Book*, Falconer 1780, Smyth 1867, the 1778 *Britannica*, Desbrière's French reports, Fraser, Beatty, NRS vols. XXXV and XVIII, FOTW, Nicolls, RMG and the 1858 *New Zealand Gazette*.

**Bottom line.**
- Sections 1 and 2 are accurate. Every primary citation I opened says what the notes claim, and all the page links are right, including the stated scan offsets (+13 for the night book, +17 for NRS II).
- One misattribution must be fixed: the summary's "three minute guns" line comes from the **Euryalus's** log, not the Phoebe's.
- One summary figure is disputed: the length and direction of the 19 October chain.
- The rest are refinements: a few nuances, and points the notes missed.

**Dating.** All dates from the NRS *Logs* are already **civil** dates. Jackson says so in vol. I: he "altered" the noon-to-noon log dates "to those of the civil day". So the notes' 19 and 20 October times need no shift. The original ADM 51/52 logs keep the nautical day, because the Admiralty's civil-day order of 11 October 1805 reached the fleet too late. No Republican-calendar dates occur in this scope.

### SUM-1c: length and direction of the 19 October chain
- Nelson to Lady Hamilton, 19 Oct, noon: "Cadiz, E.S.E., 16 Leagues". Blackwood to his wife: "our Fleet was at sixteen leagues off". Nicolas narrative: "about fifty miles W.S.W. of Cadiz". Diary: "Cadiz bearing E.N.E. by compass, distant sixteen leagues". Nicolas VII 131–133: https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/n166/mode/1up and https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/n168/mode/1up
- Corbett 333 n. 1. The Victory's noon position, 36°36′N 7°30′W, makes Cádiz bear "about 19 leagues E.S.E. magnetic and about E. true". He suspects "E.N.E." is a slip for "E.S.E.". https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/n368/mode/1up
- My arithmetic: Cádiz is about 36°32′N, 6°18′W. That gives Δλ = 72′ × cos 36.6° ≈ 58 nm, so the Victory at noon lay about 58 nm on a bearing of about 274° true from Cádiz. She had been chasing south-east since 9.30, so at 9.30 she was probably at least as far out.

### H3-08 and H3-09: the 1799 night numeral code and directions
- Explanation, pp. 3–5:
  - The means of making each number: two lights at equal heights = 1; one over the other = 2; three lights = 3; four lights = 4; false fires or rockets = 5; guns = tens.
  - Guns first, then lights, then false fires.
  - All guns on the same side.
  - Lights "at the least, twelve feet distant from each other".
  - Any number of false fires = 5.
  - If the book is compromised, the signal numbers are changed by written memorandum.
  - https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_night-signals-and-instru_1799/page/n16/mode/1up ; https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_night-signals-and-instru_1799/page/n18/mode/1up
- Directions, pp. 7–8:
  - Dir. I: four minutes between repeats, six if one fails; guns at "five seconds ... saluting time"; guns within one minute count as one signal.
  - Dir. II–III: one-light answers; the Admiral's light hoisted and lowered once for each sail counted.
  - Dir. IV: the quarters of the compass.
  - https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_night-signals-and-instru_1799/page/n20/mode/1up ; https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_night-signals-and-instru_1799/page/n21/mode/1up
- Sailing Instructions by Night, pp. 28–30:
  - art. V: the chasing ship's stern light.
  - art. VII: avoid guns and lights in clear weather.
  - art. VIII: "The repeating frigates are not to repeat signals with guns, in the night".
  - art. IX: fog signals.
  - https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_night-signals-and-instru_1799/page/n41/mode/1up ; https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_night-signals-and-instru_1799/page/n43/mode/1up

## Points the notes missed (within this scope)

1. **Execution flash and wearing lights.** Under Dir. XIV, after a tack, wear, haul-wind or alter-course signal has been repeated, the Admiral marks the moment of execution with false fires or rockets, or one gun. Under signal 31 and Dir. X, every ship then shows two lights at the ensign staff and one at the bowsprit end until the fleet is on the new tack. So the "dark" British fleet briefly shows three lights per ship at its wears (about 8–8.40 p.m. on the 20th and 4 a.m. on the 21st).
2. **Who used the lantern figures.** They serve only the Admiral's manoeuvring signals (8, 20, 22, 30, 31, 34, 40, 42, 47, and the same with false fires). The look-outs' reports (17, 21–29, 33, 35–37) still carry guns in the standard code. Otherwise they used the Admiral's private signals (watching instructions art. IV), which on 20–21 Oct were Nelson's blue lights and guns.
3. **Report signals 33 and 35–37** (enemy going large on either quarter, before the wind, separating) complete the set the frigates could use besides 28/29 (tack).
4. **No. 370 was probably a manuscript addition.** Perrin puts the printed 1799 series at about 340 signals, and 412 and 413 are attested manuscript additions. This is an inference (low–medium) that bears on the notes' open question 3.
5. **Copies for the frigates.** In September 1805 the Admiralty decided that every ship, cruisers included, should have Popham's code (Corbett 298–301). This explains how Blackwood's frigates could relay telegraph messages on 19 October.
6. **Falconer's warning applies to rockets too.** Rockets near an enemy also give away the squadron's track. This is the period rationale for the British keeping dark while the look-outs signalled.
7. **Kent: rarely more than two or three hoists were up at once.** This supports the planned 2–4-flag clusters.
8. **Perrin's "about fifteen months before Trafalgar" for the Redbridge is wrong.** She was captured by 8 Aug 1803. The notes avoided the error but do not flag it; anyone reading Perrin directly should be warned.
9. **The 1799 numeral flags are known in full** from the URI Madras copy (FOTW): 1 red; 2 white with a blue cross; 3 blue with a white pale; 4 yellow with black borders; 5 quartered red/white; 6 white over blue diagonally; 7 blue with a yellow saltire; 8 yellow and blue per pale; 9 blue/white/red; 0 blue pierced white; substitute white. The 1804 Trafalgar values are these designs re-assigned (Perrin's Plate XIII, last column, still not transcribed).

## Scene impact

1. **Caption and notes wording.** The "three minute guns" line must be attributed to the Euryalus's log (describing the Phoebe), not the Phoebe's.
2. **Geometry of the 19 October chain.** Draw it as a 48–58 nm line **nearly due west (true)** of Cádiz, not WSW. The 16-league figure (48 nm) is what Nelson and Blackwood wrote; the Victory's noon position gives about 58 nm, almost exactly on Cádiz's latitude.
3. **Day look.**
   - Small clusters of two or three numeral flags (bold red/white/blue/yellow), held until the next link repeats.
   - Telegraph messages start under a diagonal red-and-white flag.
   - White gun puffs: every 3 minutes per Nelson's orders, "minute guns" in the rain on the 20th, every 10 minutes ordered for the Agamemnon.
   - Nelson's answer: one flag plus the S.E. compass flag, and two puffs.
4. **Night look.**
   - **Blue lights.** Light-blue or bluish-white flares that burn from half a minute to several minutes (Smyth), burned in pairs every hour while the enemy stands south. A 30–60 s burn is at the short end.
   - **Three quick gun flashes** every hour for "standing west".
   - **Gold rockets** from the mast-head.
   - **Brief priming flashes** ("amorces") as well, distinct from the blue lights.
   - **Flash before report.** At about 2 nm the report follows the flash by about 10 s.
   - **No coloured lanterns.** No red or green anywhere (sidelights date from 1848/1858).
5. **Lanterns.** Plain, warm, at least 12 ft apart, uncovered all at once and held. Watching ships show them below their poop lights, spaced like the upper-deck ports. Lantern figures belong to the Admiral's manoeuvres, so do not use them as the frigates' main reporting signal.
6. **The fleet's wears** (about 8 p.m. on the 20th and 4 a.m. on the 21st): the Victory's guns and blue lights, an execution flash, then three lights per ship until the fleet is round.
7. **Dates on the date line.** All the in-scope dates are civil, so no shift is needed: 19 Oct about 7.00 (No. 370) to 9.30 (Victory); 20 Oct memorandum; night of 20–21 Oct.
8. **Range cues** if the scene fades signals with distance: lanterns readable to about 1–3 nm; blue lights to about 5–14 nm (not 9–16); flags read to about 4–11 nm depending on haze (estimate).

# Fact-check of H1 notes, part A: the introduction, "Sources used", "Two systematic dating errors", §1 Toulon to Cádiz, §2 the crossing and the West Indies, and claims H1-01 to H1-15

Notes checked: `C:/Users/User/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/d--dev-trafalgar-1805/81d46a93-68b9-4541-8365-f913ef045b3d/scratchpad/research/h1-campaign.md`, lines 1–120 and 514–554.

Method: I read every cited page that bears on a date the scene shows. I used the archive.org OCR and checked two page images (Desbrière's *Atlas* log tables). Then I went looking for evidence that could refute each claim, in sources the notes did not use: Orde's own letters of 10 April 1805, the Admiralty order of 25 April, Gardner's letter of 22 April, the *Beagle*'s report, Troude (1867), Mahan's dates, Clowes's dates, the *Sally*'s own log, and a fresh Republican-calendar computation.

Overall: the notes hold up well. Every date the scene will show is right: Toulon 30 March, the Strait 9 April, Cádiz 9–10 April, Martinique 14 May, leaving Martinique 5 June, the convoy 8 June and the turn for Europe 10–11 June. The central claim about James's calendar conversions is also right. The main corrections are these:

1. The notes say "British sources 8 April" for the Strait. That is wrong for the British primary documents, which all say the 9th. The 8th comes only from Nelson's second-hand intelligence and from later historians.
2. James's one-day shift applies only to dates he took from French Republican-dated documents. It does not cover "the whole French side" of his account.
3. There are a few small citation and number fixes: Orde had 5 of the line, not 6; the brig was *Naïade*; James says *five* frigates rejoined near Corvo; and some page numbers and links need fixing.

## Points the notes missed

1. The decisive British primary documents for 9 April: Orde's two letters of 10 April, the Admiralty order of 25 April, Gardner's letter of 22 April with the *Melampus* report, and the *Beagle*. The notes present 8 April as "British sources"; it is really Nelson's second-hand intelligence plus later historians.
2. Mahan gives mixed dates: Gibraltar on the 8th but Cádiz and Orde on the 9th, 4 June for leaving Martinique, and 29 May for *Didon*.
3. In the *Atlas* table A, the Strait passage is printed against "18 germinal", which is 8 April. Only the start-day reading of the rows gives 9 April. If anyone converts the printed labels naively, the line will be a day out.
4. Desbrière prints the Cádiz anchoring as "7 h. 45 du matin"; it must be p.m.
5. Decrès's return on Desbrière p. 27 settles the brig as *Naïade*.
6. Orde had 5 of the line; *Renown* made 6.
7. James's "five frigates" rejoined near Corvo, not four.
8. British observers counted 18 SoL on 6 June, but the French returns give 20.
9. The *Sally*'s own log shows the nautical-day convention at work.
10. Citation fixes: Nicolas Analysis p. xxxiv; James's list at iii. 334; Desbrière's Magon passage on p. 36; Nicolas vol. VI page labels on archive.org are one page off.

## What this changes for the scene

1. **Date line and route labels.**
   - Toulon: **30 March 1805**, evening. Drop "29 March"; it is James's conversion slip, copied by Clowes.
   - The Strait: **9 April**.
   - Cádiz: **9 April** (anchored outside the bay at nightfall), sailing **10 April** (about 2 a.m.). A single label could read "Cádiz, 9–10 April".
   - Martinique: **14 May**.
   - Leaving Fort-de-France / Fort Royal: **5 June**.
   - The convoy off Barbuda: **8 June**.
   - The turn for Europe: **10–11 June**. If one date is needed, use 11 June (Villeneuve's own letter, "at sea, 11 June"; Desbrière) or 10 June (Corbett), and record the other in history-notes.
2. **Glyph counts.**
   - Toulon: 11 SoL (plus 6 frigates and 2 brigs: *Furet*, *Naïade*).
   - After Cádiz: nominally 18 SoL (12 French including *Aigle*, and 6 Spanish). Only 17 weighed that night. At dawn on 10 April only 13 were together; 14 sailed in company from 12 April. *Firme*, *Terrible*, *España*, *Magdalena* and *San Rafael* crossed separately.
   - At Martinique: 17 on 14 May and 18 on 16 May.
   - After Magon (4 June): **20** (14 French, 6 Spanish).
   - If the crossing is drawn as one fleet, 18 is a simplification. The accurate picture is 14 together with stragglers apart.
3. **Cádiz glyph position.** The French anchored *outside* the bay, and the Spaniards came out to them. Put the glyphs in the offing, around 36°33′N 6°22′W (an estimate), not in the inner harbour. They leave westward in the dark.
4. **The Strait.** The fleet went through on the morning of 9 April in two columns, frigates ahead. If Orde's squadron is shown, it is 5 SoL (6 with *Renown*) drawing off west toward Lagos.
5. **Plotting the *Atlas* tables.** Do not plot the printed row dates. In table A, row "N germinal" is the noon of N+1. Table B very probably works the same way. The shape of the line is unaffected, but daily date ticks would be a day early.
6. **The convoy.** Taken to leeward (west) of Barbuda after a five-hour chase from 10 a.m.: 14 of 15 sail. The escort schooner *Netley* escaped.
7. **Period spellings in James and Nelson** (for the lettering track to weigh): Carthagena, Cape de Gata, Cape Sicie, Cape Sepet, Cape Spartel, Fort Royal (the British name; French Fort-de-France), Barbadoes, Guadaloupe, Barbuda, Diamond Rock, Western Islands (the Azores), Corvo.

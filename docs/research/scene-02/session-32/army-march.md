# The army's march, Boulogne to Ulm: the line's road and dates (session 32)

Research for the dashed "army" line that the chart draws by dates from Boulogne (28 August) to Ulm (20 October 1805). The line follows the head of one column: Soult's IV corps, the corps from the Boulogne camps, as far as Donauwörth, and then Ney's VI corps by Günzburg and Elchingen to Ulm. Until now the notes had the corps' road only at Metz, Bitche, Speyer, Heilbronn, Nördlingen and Donauwörth. The stages between Boulogne and Metz had never been traced (`research/scene-02/h2-boulogne-cadiz.md`, H2-11: "not traced for the centre corps"). This note traces the whole road and dates it.

Sources are paraphrased and linked at the end. Coordinates are present-day town centres (WGS84, from OpenStreetMap's Nominatim), rounded to three decimals.

## Verdict on the draft's bullet "The dashed line"

The bullet is in `session-17/history-scene-02-wip.md`, "The turn east and Ulm". Its substance stands: the line is a composite labelled as the army. Boulogne to Donauwörth was Soult's road, Soult then turned south, and Ney's corps fought at Elchingen. Five things need changing.

1. **Boulogne to Metz.** The centre road did not run straight across Picardy and Champagne. It went north-east by Saint-Omer, Béthune, Douai and Cambrai, east by Landrecies, Avesnes and Hirson to Mézières, then up the Meuse by Sedan and Stenay to Verdun, and on to Metz. The lab's straight Boulogne–Metz chord cuts across that bow. It misses the road by about 15 km at Douai, about 20 km at Avesnes (north of the chord) and about 23 km at Verdun (south of it).
2. **The Rhine.** Soult crossed at Rheinhausen, on the right bank a little upstream of Speyer, not at Speyer itself. The crossing began at noon on 26 September, when the advance guard went over by ferry. It ended with the 4th division on the 30th. No troops crossed there on the 25th: that day's crossing was Lannes and Murat at Kehl, on another road. The line's head should reach the Rhine on 26 September. "25–30 September" is the whole army's span and fits only as a label for the army.
3. **Rhine to Danube.** From Rheinhausen the head went by Bruchsal and Eppingen to Heilbronn. The order of 27 September said Wiesloch and Sinsheim, but Soult took Bruchsal to keep clear of Davout. From Hall the head (Vandamme's 2nd division) went by Rosenberg and Ellwangen to Nördlingen. The 1st Bulletin's Gaildorf–Abtsgmünd–Aalen was the plan, and the 1st division's road. Vandamme's advance guard took Donauwörth in the night of 6–7 October, and the Danube was crossed on the 7th. The bullet's "7 October" stands.
4. **Ney.** Ney's corps did not come by Donauwörth. It reached the Danube from Heidenheim and Giengen, and Malher's division fought for the bridge at Günzburg on 9 October. So the composite changes corps between Donauwörth and Günzburg. "Took Elchingen and Ulm" is loose. Ney won Elchingen on 14 October and stormed the heights above Ulm on the 15th, with part of Suchet's division. Ulm capitulated on the 17th, a brigade of Ney's corps took over a gate on the 18th, and the garrison laid down its arms on the 20th.
5. **A page reference.** The draft's "To the Rhine" bullet cites Alombert and Colin I 236, 414 for Soult's road through Metz and Bitche. The IV corps' itinerary is on p. 415. Page 414 has Nansouty's and Davout's itineraries.

## The road and its dates

The head is the leading division of the corps. From Boulogne to the Rhine that was Saint-Hilaire's 1st division, which left first. The advance guard that crossed the Rhine (10th light infantry, 26th chasseurs) was the 1st division's. From Heilbronn the lead passed to Vandamme's 2nd division. "Derived" means the date was worked out as explained in the next section, not read from the 1st division's own report.

### For the chart: a thinner set

The full table is the road stage by stage. On a chart of western Europe, about 22 points keep the line on the road. Every left-out stage lies within about 8 km of a straight segment between its neighbours. These are:

Boulogne (28 August), Saint-Omer (29 August), Douai (2 September), Cambrai (3 September), Avesnes (6 September), Hirson (7 September), Mézières (9 September, held to the 11th), Sedan (11 September), Verdun (14 September), Metz (16 September, held to the 18th), Bitche (21 September), Wissembourg (23 September), Rheinhausen (26 September, noon; held to the 30th if the line shows the whole corps crossing), Bruchsal (27 September), Heilbronn (29 September, held to 2 October), Hall (3 October), Ellwangen (5 October), Nördlingen (6 October), Donauwörth (7 October), Günzburg (9 October), Elchingen (14 October), Ulm (20 October, 2 p.m.).

- Germersheim (24 September) lies on the Wissembourg–Rheinhausen segment, within 1 km of it. Keep it only to pace the head (24 September there, 26 September at the river).
- Öhringen lies about 8 km off the Heilbronn–Hall segment and Stenay about 5 km off Sedan–Verdun. Either can go in for a closer fit.
- Dillingen lies on the Donauwörth–Günzburg segment.

The suggested date labels are four:
- "28 August" at Boulogne.
- "26–30 September" at the Rhine by Speyer. The notes' "25–30 September" can stay only as the whole army's span.
- "7 October" at Donauwörth.
- "20 October" at Ulm.

A fifth label, "18 September" at Metz, would show the pace across France if the line looks too long without one.

## How the dates between Boulogne and Metz were found

No report of the 1st division gives its stages across France. Two dates are fixed by period documents:

- **Mézières.** Saint-Hilaire's division arrived on 22 fructidor (9 September) and left on 24 fructidor (11 September). This is in the war ministry's digest of march reports, from Saint-Hilaire's letter and the report of the general commanding the 2nd military division (A&C II 74–77).
- **Metz.** By the second order of movement, the 1st division was to leave Metz on the 1st complementary day (18 September) for Germersheim (A&C I 415).

Departure from Boulogne on 28 August is also fixed (Corr. XI No. 9137; Berthier, A&C I 373).

The full nightly itinerary of another division of the same corps on the same road survives in a modern chronicle. J. M. Berjaud's *Sur les pas de la Grande Armée, 1805* gives day by day where the 40th of the line slept; the 40th was in Suchet's 4th division of Soult's corps (PDF pp. 35–42). It left the Boulogne camp at 5 a.m. on 2 September and slept at:

- Surques (2 September), Saint-Omer (3), Aire (4), Béthune (5), Lens (6), Douai (7) and Cambrai (8);
- Landrecies (10), Avesnes (11), Hirson (12), Maubert-Fontaine (13) and Charleville (14);
- Sedan (16), Stenay (17), "Livry" (18), Verdun (19) and Metz (21);
- Courcelles (23) and Saint-Avold (24).

The gaps on the 9th, 15th, 20th and 22nd are halts.

That itinerary agrees with every period date found for the 4th division:

- Suchet reported his division at Cambrai on 21 fructidor (8 September), with a review there on the 22nd (9 September) (A&C II 74–75);
- he wrote from Avesnes on 24 fructidor (11 September) (A&C II 72);
- by the second order the 4th division was to leave Metz on 1 vendémiaire (23 September), sleeping at Courcelles that night and at Saint-Avold on the 24th (A&C I 416);
- it was due at Wissembourg on 28 September (A&C I 416, 421).

The 1st division left Boulogne five days before the 40th. Shifted five days earlier, the 40th's itinerary puts the 1st division at Mézières on 9 September, halted on the 10th, gone on the 11th, and leaving Metz on the 18th. Those are exactly the two fixed dates. The 3rd division (Legrand) left three days after the 1st and two before the 40th, and it fits the same pattern:

- it was at Landrecies on 21 fructidor (8 September), two days ahead of the 40th (A&C II 74–75);
- Legrand wrote from Mézières on 26 fructidor (13 September), during a halt, two days ahead of the 40th's halt at Charleville (A&C II 73).

The same road was used a few days earlier by Klein's 1st dragoon division, which supports the places:

- it left Saint-Omer on 8 fructidor (26 August) by Aire, Béthune, Lens and Douai to Cambrai (A&C I 255, 284, 291);
- Klein then wrote from Hirson on 16 fructidor (3 September), from Stenay on the 21st (8 September) and from Mars-la-Tour on the 24th (11 September) (A&C II 70–71).

Alombert and Colin's own summary has the same road. Bread at Cambrai and Landrecies was bad, and the road from Cambrai by Landrecies, Avesnes and Hirson to Mézières was among the worst (A&C II 5). The 4th division of the 4th corps complained of its reception in the Ardennes, "notably at Hirson" (A&C II 4, n. 3). The meat contractor failed at Verdun as the centre and right columns passed (A&C II 5). The artillery convoy on the centre road was to collect its companies at Saint-Omer and its matériel at Metz (A&C I 236, 373).

So the derived dates for the 1st division are those of the table. They may be a day out where Berthier changed the halts between divisions "to avoid crowding" (his letter to Soult of 28 August, A&C I 365). They cannot be further out, because the dates at Mézières and Metz hold them.

## Metz to the Rhine

On 30 August Napoleon changed the destinations. Soult's four divisions were to stand in a square Germersheim–Lauterbourg–Bergzabern–Wissembourg instead of going to Strasbourg (A&C I 220–221). From Metz the 4th corps therefore took the Saarbrücken road to Saint-Avold, then turned south-east across the Vosges by Sarreguemines, Bitche and Lembach to Wissembourg. The prefects were told to mend the Bitche–Wissembourg road for it (A&C I 221).

The 1st division's itinerary, as printed on A&C I 415, leaves Metz on 18 September. It sleeps at Courcelles, Saint-Avold, Sarreguemines, Bitche, Lembach and Wissembourg, and reaches Germersheim on 2 vendémiaire (24 September). The first line of the table is printed "1er vendémiaire". That is a misprint for the 1st complementary day, since the column runs 1–5 and then 1–2 vendémiaire. The summary of destinations has the 1st division at Germersheim on 24 September (A&C I 421).

On 26 September Soult wrote to Murat from Speyer that the pontoon train from Strasbourg had reached Rheinhausen in the night. He chose that point, a little upstream of Speyer, because the river was narrower there and he had too few boats lower down. He would send the 10th light infantry and the 26th chasseurs, the 1st division's advance guard, over by ferry and boats at noon. The 1st and 2nd divisions would cross the next morning (A&C II 507–508). By Alombert and Colin's account the bridge was laid in the night of 26–27 September. The 1st division crossed at 9 a.m. on the 27th, the 2nd at noon, the 3rd the next day and the 4th on the 30th (A&C II 42). Napoleon's 1st Bulletin has Soult over at Speyer on the 4th (26 September).

## The Rhine to the Danube

- 27 September: the advance guard beyond Heidelsheim, with the divisions in echelon from Bruchsal back to the Rhine (A&C II 43).
- 28 September: the advance guard and three divisions between Eppingen and Bruchsal (A&C II 44). Soult had written that he would go by Bruchsal and Eppingen to Heilbronn, to stay clear of Davout's posts (A&C II 508).
- 29 September: the first two divisions at Heilbronn; three on the 30th; the whole corps on 1 October (A&C II 44).
- 2 October: the 2nd division behind Öhringen, the light cavalry beyond it; the 1st division at Finsterrot (A&C II 638).
- 3 October: the 2nd division beyond Hall on the Ellwangen road; the 1st on the Gaildorf road (A&C II 48–49, 685).
- 4 October: Vandamme's advance guard at Rosenberg; the 1st division at Gaildorf (A&C II 685, 812).
- 5 October: Vandamme's advance guard entered Ellwangen at 6 a.m., an hour after 300 Austrian uhlans had left, and went on towards Zöbingen. The 1st division was at Abtsgmünd and the 3rd at Ellwangen (A&C II 57–58, 812–814).
- 6 October: the 2nd and 3rd divisions marched on Nördlingen. The 2nd went through to Möttingen, and its advance guard occupied Harburg and Ebermergen. In the night that advance guard took Donauwörth, which the Austrians had just left after cutting the bridge (Soult's report of 7 October, A&C II 867–868). Maude puts the seizure at about 8 p.m. on the 6th (pp. 199–200). Ney was held at Giengen (A&C II 61).
- 7 October: the Danube was crossed at Donauwörth. Soult then went south to Augsburg.

The 1st Bulletin's list for Soult (Heilbronn, Öhringen, Hall, Gaildorf, Abtsgmünd, Aalen, Nördlingen; Corr. XI No. 9348) is the plan, and the 1st division's road as far as Abtsgmünd. On 6 October the 1st division went from Abtsgmünd to Bopfingen (A&C II 868). Thiébault, a brigadier in the 1st division, remembered the road as Speyer, Heilbronn, Hall, Ellwangen, Nördlingen and Donauwörth (Memoirs, Butler's translation, II 128). His dates in that passage are confused, so only the road is used.

## Donauwörth to Ulm

From Donauwörth Soult went south (Augsburg, Landsberg, Memmingen), so the line changes corps here. Ney's VI corps had come down from Heidenheim and Giengen on the left bank. On 9 October Malher's division seized the Günzburg bridge in the evening, after a hard fight (Maude 176–177, 210–211). On 14 October at 8 a.m. Ney, with Loison's division leading, forced the Elchingen bridge and drove the Austrians back on Ulm; the fighting lasted ten hours (Maude 231–233). On the 15th, at about 3 p.m., Ney and part of Suchet's division took the Michelsberg and Geisberg above the town (Maude 240–241). The capitulation followed on the 17th and the march-out on 20 October from 2 p.m. (see the draft's "Ulm").

For the chart, the line can run up the Danube's left bank from Donauwörth by Dillingen to Günzburg, then by Leipheim to Elchingen and Ulm. That keeps it on the river the army closed along. It is honest as a composite: no one corps marched from Donauwörth to Günzburg.

## Doubts

- **The dates between Boulogne and Metz are derived.** They come from the 40th's itinerary in a modern chronicle, shifted five days. They are not read from the 1st division's own reports. They match every period date found, but a halt may fall a day differently.
- **Berjaud is a secondary source** (2009, posted by the SEHRI in 2010). It gives no source for each entry. Its 40th of the line itinerary is used because five period dates confirm it. Its "Livry" between Stenay and Verdun is no place on that road; Liny-devant-Dun or Dun-sur-Meuse is meant.
- **Metz to Germersheim is the order's itinerary.** It was carried out to the day by the 4th division (Berjaud, against A&C I 416). The 1st division's advance guard was at the Rhine by noon on the 26th, which fits the 1st division reaching Germersheim on the 24th.
- **"Courcelles"** is taken as Courcelles-Chaussy, on the Metz–Saint-Avold highway, not Courcelles-sur-Nied south-east of Metz.
- **Surques as the first night** is the 4th division's stop. The 1st division's first night is not recorded.
- **The camp the line starts from.** In 1804 Saint-Hilaire's division held the right camp, north of the harbour (`docs/history-notes.md`, "Where they stood"). Where the divisions were hutted in the summer of 1805 was not found, so the line starts at the town.
- **The hour at Boulogne.** "Morning" is all the sources give ("partent demain matin": Berthier, A&C I 373). The 4th division left at 5 a.m. (Berjaud). The lab's 6 a.m. is a convention.
- **Donauwörth.** The town was taken "during the night" by Soult's report and at about 8 p.m. on the 6th by Maude. The crossing was on the 7th.
- **Pace.** The planned 24 days to the Rhine (No. 9137) became 29 for the 1st division (28 August to 26 September), because of the change of destinations and the halts. Measured stage to stage in straight lines, Boulogne to Rheinhausen is about 590 km (about 365 miles); the roads were longer. Maude's round figure is "400 miles from the coast to the Rhine" (p. 123).

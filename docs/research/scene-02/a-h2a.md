**Method.**
- I downloaded fresh archive.org OCR texts into my own folder and read each passage with its running page head.
- I opened the page image wherever one figure, date or quotation decided a ruling:
  - Nicolas VII pp. 71 and 136;
  - Jackson, *Logs of the Great Sea Fights* II p. 146;

  - Alombert and Colin III/1 pp. 228 and 229;
  - Schuermans, *Itinéraire* p. 180;

- Leaf links (`/page/nNNN`) were checked against the printed page number on the image. archive.org's `page_numbers.json` is wrong for some of these scans: in Corr. XI it is 4 leaves off. So I give leaf links and not page-label links. Where a leaf was inferred from a checked offset in the same scan, that is said.
- Republican dates are converted with the Correspondance's own equivalents: 10 fructidor XIII = 28 Aug 1805; 13 fructidor = 31 Aug; 25 floréal XIII = 15 May 1805; 3–5 vendémiaire XIV = 25–27 Sep 1805; 15 vendémiaire = 7 Oct.

**Overall.**
- Most of the checker's corrections stand.
- Two rulings go to the notes:
  - Napoleon was at Donauwörth on 7 October;
  - the army left Boulogne on 28 August.
- The checker is partly wrong in four places:
  - the Euryalus log gives both "9" and "12" on 19 October;
  - the guard ships stood about four leagues east of the fleet, not "3–4 leagues apart";
  - the Tower of London sentence is genuine Napoleon, from another letter;
  - the Tour d'Ordre camp site is in period sources, not only on Wikipedia.

## Evidence

### R1. The signal chain (S0-2; missed point 9; scene impact 8)

**Notes.** The links were mostly ships of the line (Mars, Colossus and Defence from 4 October, Agamemnon from about 14 October), with Phoebe and Naiad at the inshore end. The caption's "chain of frigates" is wrong.

**Checker.** Confirms this, and adds that Nelson's order of 4 October prescribes guns "by day or night". Its scene-impact item 8 reads the order as the ships standing "3–4 leagues apart between the fleet and Cádiz".

**Primary sources.**
- **Nelson to Captain Duff, 4 October 1805** (Nicolas VII pp. 70–71, from Clarke and M'Arthur; page image of p. 71 viewed):
  - keep with Mars, Defence and Colossus "from three to four leagues between the Fleet and Cadiz", to pass on the inshore frigates' information quickly;
  - use distant signals when the weather keeps flag colours from being made out;
  - if the enemy is out or coming out, "fire guns by day or night, in order to draw my attention";
  - in thick weather, close within signal of Victory;
  - one ship to windward, "or rather to the Eastward" of the other two, to extend the range of sight;
  - Blackwood to put a frigate west of Cádiz.
  - Links: https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/n105/mode/1up (p. 70) and https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/n106/mode/1up (p. 71).
- **Nelson to Blackwood, the same day** (Victory, "Cadiz, East 17 leagues"; Nicolas VII p. 73). This settles the geometry: Mars, Colossus and Defence "will be stationed four leagues East from the Fleet", one of them advanced east toward Cádiz, and the fleet 16–18 leagues west of Cádiz.
  - Read this way, "three to four leagues" in the Duff order is the squadron's distance east of the fleet, not the spacing between ships.
  - https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/n108/mode/1up
- **Nelson's diary, 14 October** (Nicolas VII p. 122): Defence and Agamemnon 7–10 leagues west of Cádiz; Mars and Colossus 5 leagues east of the fleet, whose station was 15–20 leagues west of Cádiz; "by this chain" he hoped for constant communication with the frigates. https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/n157/mode/1up
- **Nelson's memorandum, 20 October 1805** (Nicolas VII p. 136; page image viewed):
  - Blackwood with two frigates to keep the enemy in sight through the night, two more frigates between him and Defence, Colossus between Defence and Mars, and Mars to communicate with Victory;
  - signals by night: two blue lights together every hour if the enemy stands south or toward the Strait; three quick guns every hour if it stands west.
  - https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/n171/mode/1up
- **Euryalus's log, 19 October** (Jackson, *Logs of the Great Sea Fights* II p. 146; page image viewed):
  - at 7.20 Phoebe was sent to repeat signals between the frigates and the fleet;
  - at 8 "Phoebe WNW, firing three minute guns", with Defence in sight from the masthead;
  - Naiad was ordered to repeat between Euryalus and Phoebe;
  - that night rockets and blue lights were seen from the ships to the westward (p. 147).
  - https://archive.org/details/logsofgreatseaf02jack/page/n163/mode/1up
- **Desbrière, *Trafalgar*, p. 161**: Blackwood warned Nelson through the Phoebe, linked to the Defence. https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n186/mode/1up

**Ruling: both-partly.**
- The notes are right about who made up the chain.
- The checker is right that the order adds distant signals and guns by day or night, which the notes left out.
- The checker's "3–4 leagues apart" misreads the order: the squadron stood about four leagues east of the fleet, with one ship pushed further on.
- The detailed signal look belongs to the H3 adjudication. The facts here fix only the chain's make-up and the gun and blue-light signals Nelson ordered.

**Scene.**
- Beat 4 caption: "chain of ships", not "chain of frigates".
- Chain glyphs: 3–4 ships of the line plus 2–4 frigates, strung from about 4–5 leagues east of the fleet toward the inshore frigates.
- When the signal runs:
  - by day: flag hoists, with a puff of gun smoke at each ship;
  - on the night of 20–21 October: paired blue-light flares and gun flashes, repeated hourly.

### R2. The sortie of 19 October (S0-5; scene impact 9)

**Notes.** 7 of the line plus frigates (Desbrière), "nine" (Prigny) or "12 ships" (James, Nicolas, Euryalus's log).

**Checker.** Desbrière's note cites Euryalus for "9 voiles". It treats James's 12 and Euryalus's 9 as counts of sail.

**Primary and period sources.**
- **Euryalus's log** (Jackson II pp. 145–147; page image of p. 146 viewed):
  - 8 a.m.: 19 of the enemy under way, in the harbour;
  - 4 p.m.: "9 of the enemy's ships out";
  - 6 p.m.: the enemy's ships ENE about 3 miles, "12 in number", all twelve then standing north on the larboard tack;
  - at daylight on the 20th: nine under sail off the harbour and 4 at anchor.

  So the log gives both numbers, at different hours. The notes are right that it gives 12; the checker is right that Desbrière cites it for 9.
- **Desbrière, *Trafalgar*, pp. 161–162**, from the French reports and journals:
  - about 10 a.m. Magon got out with Algésiras, Achille, Argonaute, Neptune, Héros, Duguay-Trouin and Bahama (7 of the line) and the frigate Rhin; the last were through the passes only about 1 p.m.;
  - Hermione was out by about 4 p.m.;
  - Thémis came out about 6 p.m.;
  - note 4 cites the Euryalus log for "9 voiles" at about 4 p.m.
  - https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n186/mode/1up and https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n187/mode/1up
- **James III p. 385**: owing to the light wind "12 ships only succeeded in getting out". He does not say "of the line"; his figure matches the 6 p.m. log count. https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/n402/mode/1up
- **Nicolas VII p. 130**: "twelve Ships only succeeded in quitting the Harbour", Nicolas's own narrative following James, so not an independent source. https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/n165/mode/1up
- **Nelson's diary, 20 October** (Nicolas VII p. 137): the frigates signalled 9 sail outside the harbour, which matches Euryalus's daylight count of the 20th. https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nelsuoft/page/n172/mode/1up

**Ruling: both-partly.** The best-supported number of ships of the line out on the 19th is seven, named by the French reports. The British 9 and 12 are counts of all sail at 4 p.m. and 6 p.m.

**Scene.**
- 19 October: 7 ship-of-the-line glyphs outside Cádiz (plus up to 3 frigate glyphs if frigates are drawn), not 12 of the line.
- 20 October: 33 of the line, 5 frigates and 2 brigs (S0-6, uncontested).

### R3. Huts, not tents (H2-02d; missed point 3; scene impact 3)

**Notes.** They mention the 19 Étaples huts but do not flag the brief's "rows of tiny tents".

**Checker.** The camps were hutted.

**Sources.**
- **Desbrière III p. 206** (minister's orders of 29 Aug 1803): Davout's and Soult's camps (two at Ostend, one at Dunkirk, two at Boulogne) were to be ready by 24 September and built of wooden huts so they could be lived in all winter. https://archive.org/details/projetsettentat00histgoog/page/n233/mode/1up
- **Desbrière III p. 207** (Davout to the First Consul, 28 fructidor XI = 15 Sep 1803): his huts were "perches de sapin" roofed with rushes, on the dunes either side of Ostend.
- **Desbrière III pp. 432–433**: the monthly report for prairial XII (21 May–19 June 1804) for the camp of Saint-Omer. This was Soult's corps, with divisions at Boulogne and the ports beside it; the same report has one division sending 511 workers a day to the port of Boulogne and another sending 1,000 to Ambleteuse.
  - The 3rd division had 748 huts standing (778 planned).
  - The 4th division had 518, and a note refers to the first "rang" of huts of the 17th regiment, so huts stood in rows by regiment.
  - https://archive.org/details/projetsettentat00histgoog/page/n459/mode/1up and https://archive.org/details/projetsettentat00histgoog/page/n460/mode/1up
- **Fondation Napoléon newsletter 410** (28 Oct 2005): Inrap excavated 19 huts at Étaples, with roof tiles and glass and lead from glazed windows, and buttons of the 6e léger. https://www.napoleon.org/wp-content/archives/newsletters/410.html
- **Salavert, Hello and Lemaire**, "Firewood of the Napoleonic Wars", *Antiquity* 90 (2016), pp. 1334–1347: the Étaples camp was occupied 1803–1805 (abstract read; the full text is paywalled). https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/firewood-of-the-napoleonic-wars-the-first-application-of-archaeological-charcoal-analysis-to-a-military-camp-in-the-north-of-france-18031805/647D032216BD70892C35B9E88C34874B
- The Inrap pages returned 403 to every fetch, so the "October 1803 to August 1805" dates and the hut sizes rest on the search summary only.
- **Napoleon to Berthier, 26 Aug 1805** (No. 9137, Corr. XI p. 142): camp equipment to go from Metz to Strasbourg so that by 1 vendémiaire there would be "de quoi tenter 80,000 hommes". Tents were campaign kit. https://archive.org/details/correspondancede11napouoft/page/n151/mode/1up

**Ruling: checker-right.**

**Scene.** Beat 1 should show rows of small huts along camp streets (two camps by Boulogne, and others at Étaples, Wimereux and Ambleteuse), not tents.

### R4. The army leaves Boulogne (H2-09a and H2-09c notes; missed point 4; scene impact 1)

**Sources.**
- **No. 9137, 26 Aug 1805** (Corr. XI pp. 141–144): first divisions of Davout (left-hand road), Soult (centre) and Ney (right-hand) on the 10th; second on the 12th; third on the 13th; 24 days' march to the Rhine. https://archive.org/details/correspondancede11napouoft/page/n151/mode/1up
- **Berthier's movement order** (A&C I p. 337):
  - 10 fructidor (28 Aug): first divisions;
  - "Le 11 (29 août), il ne partira personne";
  - 12 (30 Aug): second divisions;
  - 13 (31 Aug): third divisions;
  - 14 (1 Sep): the rest.
  - https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-de-1805-en-allemagne-vol.-1/page/n348/mode/1up
- **A&C I pp. 216–218**:
  - Berthier wrote on the 25th that Oudinot's division would leave the next day;
  - the Grande Armée was named on 26 August and announced in the order of the day of 29 August;
  - movement orders went out on the 27th and itineraries on the 28th.
  - https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-de-1805-en-allemagne-vol.-1/page/n229/mode/1up
- **No. 9155 to Duroc, 28 Aug** (p. 157): the grenadiers and the first divisions "sont partis", and the second divisions leave "demain". This conflicts with Berthier's order (nobody on the 29th); the formal order is the better guide. https://archive.org/details/correspondancede11napouoft/page/n166/mode/1up
- **No. 9172 to Cambacérès, 1 Sep** (p. 170): not a man left at Boulogne beyond those needed to defend it.
- **No. 9177** (2 Sep): Napoleon leaves for Paris within the hour.

**Ruling: notes-right.** The 28 August date stands. The checker's additions (the grenadiers on the 26th, the cavalry orders of 24–25 August) are correct and compatible with it.

**Scene.** Date line: **28 August 1805**. The caption's "At the end of August" is right.

### R5. Ulm (S0-4; scene impact 2; H2-12e note)

**Sources.**
- **A&C III/1 p. 225**: "la capitulation fut signée le 17 à midi", with a delay to the 25th inclusive. In the footnote, the Austrian *Relation de la prise d'Ulm* has Berthier come "A 2 heures" to settle the terms. https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-de-1805-en-allemagne-vol.-3-p.-1/page/n236/mode/1up
- **A&C III/1 p. 228** (page image viewed):
  - on the 20th at 2 p.m. the garrison left the town and laid down its arms before the Emperor, part of the Guard, the II and VI corps and the dragoons;
  - the eyewitness note puts Marmont's troops through the town at 11 a.m., Napoleon on a rise at the foot of the mont Saint-Michel, the march-out by the Frauenthor, the arms grounded near the porte Neuve, and 23,000 men.
  - https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-de-1805-en-allemagne-vol.-3-p.-1/page/n239/mode/1up
- **7th Bulletin**, Elchingen, 19 Oct (No. 9398, Corr. XI pp. 339–340): the march-out was announced for "demain 28, à trois heures après midi". https://archive.org/details/correspondancede11napouoft/page/n348/mode/1up
- **8th Bulletin**, 20 Oct (No. 9404, p. 342): Napoleon was on the height of Ulm from 2 p.m. until 7 p.m. while the army filed past. https://archive.org/details/correspondancede11napouoft/page/n351/mode/1up

**Ruling: notes-right.** The checker's notes (the 2 p.m. time on the 17th, the 3 p.m. announcement, some modern summaries giving 19 October) are accurate refinements.

**Scene.** Date line and cut-away: **20 October 1805**. "Surrenders" is acceptable and "lays down its arms" more exact. Do not use 19 October.

### R6. The dashed march line (H2-11i; scene impact 7)

**Sources.**
- **Maude p. 205**: on the 8th Soult's divisions were marching on Landsberg (Thiébault's memoir).
- **Maude p. 231**: Soult before Memmingen on the 13th. **Maude p. 240**: on the 14th Soult went on to cut the Biberach road, leaving Vandamme's division at Memmingen.
- **No. 9385** (17 Oct): Soult occupies Biberach.
- **Maude p. 250**: Soult, Baraguey d'Hilliers and the Guard sent "for Augsburg on the morning of the 18th". The checker wrote "toward Landsberg on the 18th", a slip. https://archive.org/details/ulmcampaign180500mauduoft/page/n281/mode/1up
- **A&C III/1 p. 228**: the troops present at the surrender were part of the Guard, the II and VI corps and the dragoons.
- **No. 9348** (1st Bulletin, Corr. XI p. 295): the four corps' roads from the Rhine:
  - Soult: Heilbronn, Öhringen, Hall, Gaildorf, Abtsgmünd, Aalen and Nördlingen;
  - Ney: Stuttgart, Esslingen, Göppingen, Weissenstein, Heidenheim and Neresheim;

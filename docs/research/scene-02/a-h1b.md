## Scene impact, in one place

1. **Glyphs on the Cádiz–Martinique leg:** 14 ships of the line in the main body (12 French, 2 Spanish). If stragglers are drawn, add 4 Spanish sailing apart. The count is 18 at Martinique, complete on 16 May.
2. **Nelson's Lagos and bore-away waypoints:**
   - Lagos Bay is dated 9–11 May.
   - Move the bore-away point from ~36°40′N 9°00′W to ~36°47′N 8°41′W, dated 11 May, 6.50 p.m.
3. **Palmas and Pula waypoints:**
   - Palmas 26–30 March.
   - Pula 31 March – 3 April.
   - The date label "26/27 Mar" becomes "26 Mar".
4. **Palermo waypoint:** label it "off Palermo, 9–10 Apr". Drop "Ustica 7 Apr". The present point (~38°25′N 13°00′E) can stay; ~38°15′N 13°15′E would sit closer to "off Palermo".
5. **Homeward line through the Azores:**
   - Table I's points already pass north of São Miguel.
   - Make sure the drawn spline clears São Miguel's north-west tip (Mosteiros, about 37°53′N 25°49′W). A straight segment from 8 July (37°42′N 27°10′W) to 9 July (37°58′N 25°30′W) passes only 1–2 nm north of it, and a thick ink line would touch the island. Adding a point at about 37°58′N 26°05′W keeps the line 4–5 nm clear.
   - Describe the route as "through the Azores, north of São Miguel", not "south of the Azores".
6. **Cádiz arrival date:** "20 Aug" stands. History-notes records 21 August (*Précis*) and 17 August (Troude) as rejected variants.
7. **Other counts:** British glyphs off Cádiz between 30 August and Nelson's arrival, if ever drawn: about 26. Channel fleet off Ushant on 15 August, if drawn: about 36–40, with 24 seen from the *Victory* at noon.
8. **Nothing changes** for Barbados on 4 June, Nelson's 10 → 12 → 11 of the line, Calder's 15, or the allies' 20 → 18 → 15 → 29.

## Evidence

### S6-CADIZ-APR: only 14 crossed together

- **Reille's journal**, printed by Desbrière p. 11 (leaf n30: https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n30/mode/1up):
  - At daybreak on 10 April all the French ships were together, "mais il n'y avait parmi nous que le vaisseau amiral espagnol". Ships a few leagues astern seemed to be the other five.
  - The next day only *América* was seen, and she could not rejoin until 12 April.
  - Reille blames the Spanish ships, which had anchored at the bay's entrance and veered cable, so they were not ready as quickly as the French.
- **Desbrière p. 26** (text from the full-text file: https://archive.org/download/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/La_campagne_maritime_de_1805_djvu.txt): off Fort-de-France on arrival day they found *Firme*, *Terrible*, *España* and the frigate *Maddalena* beating in; *San Rafael* arrived on the 16th.
- **Troude iii. 342** (https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_rhIR5D5quFYC, leaf n348 per the checker), an independent French account: *San Rafael* "s'échoua en appareillant"; three other Spaniards "restèrent de l'arrière" and were not seen again until Martinique.
- **The notes contradict themselves.** Their §1 already describes the straggling correctly, but the §6 table ("17 sailed together") and H1-06 ("5 at once") do not. The table row is what needs fixing.

### S3-HOMEROUTE / H1-22: through the Azores, north of São Miguel

- **Nicolas vi. 468** (leaf n507: https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter06nelsiala/page/n507/mode/1up):
  - The log of 8 July at 11.30 a.m. has São Miguel E.S.E. "per compass", 17½ leagues.
  - The diary has "standing to the Eastward, to go to the Northward of St. Michael's". It also says the Spanish log taken from a La Guaira bark showed the allies had gone in sight of Cape Blanco and close to the Salvages.
- **The fix lies between the island groups.** With roughly 22° W variation the bearing is almost due east true, which puts the *Victory* near 37°50′N 26°57′W at 11.30 a.m. on 8 July. That is about 50 nm south of Terceira and 50 nm west of São Miguel, in the channel between the central group and São Miguel.
- **The other fixes:**
  - 29 June: Santa Maria N 85° E, 210 leagues (vi. 468 n.).
  - 1 July: Santa Maria S 89° E, 175 leagues (vi. 467).
  - Both put the approach at the latitude of Santa Maria and São Miguel, well south of Faial, Pico, Flores and Corvo.
- **Mahan**, *Life of Nelson* vol. II ch. XX (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/16915): the French sighted the islands "on the 30th of June to the northward of the group, while the British passed through it on the 8th of July".

### S3-LAGOS-DEP and S3-11MAY: 11 May, from Lagos Bay to the bore-away

- **The *Victory*'s log, Nicolas vi. 433** (leaf n472, image checked: https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter06nelsiala/page/n472/mode/1up):
  - "May 11th. A.M. At 9·10, weighed under the staystails and jib: in launch and all boats. At 10·30, set the topsails …"
  - At 4 p.m. *Queen* (Knight) joined; *Dragon* and the transports joined; "Martin parted"; *Queen*, *Royal Sovereign*, *Dragon* and the transports parted.
  - "At 6·50, bore up and made sail. Cape St. Vincent N.W. by N., distance 7 leagues."
  - The extract comes from Clarke & M'Arthur ii. 407. It is printed in civil order and must be: the bore-up cannot precede the weighing.
- **At anchor on 9 and 10 May:**
  - Lagos log, 9 May: "May 9th. P.M. At 9, anchored in Lagos Bay" (vi. 429, leaf n468).
  - Letters of 10 May are headed "in Lagos Bay, 10th May" (vi. 432) and "May 10th, 1805, off Lagos" (vi. 431).
  - To Campbell on 10 May: "to-morrow I start for the West Indies" (vi. 431).
- **Later authors who agree:**
  - James iii. 345–346 (https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/346/mode/1up): "at 9 a.m. on the 11th, weighed and sailed out of the bay". His Lagos "10th" and Knight "12th" are log dates.
  - Clowes v. 102 (https://archive.org/details/royalnavyhistory05clowuoft/page/102/mode/1up) says the same.
  - Mahan ch. XX: "In the morning of the 11th the fleet …"
- **Corbett:**
  - p. 105 (https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/105/mode/1up): "In the evening he moved back to St. Vincent". It rests on Hardy's 10 p.m. postscript, "We are now off St. Vincent". Lagos Bay lies only about 17 nm east of the cape, so the phrase is loose.
  - p. 107: "Next morning … Knight met him", but the log has 4 p.m.
  - p. 108: "some twenty miles to the southward of St. Vincent".
- **Desbrière p. 19** (leaf n40) has two errors:
  - "à sept heures du soir il leva l'ancre" conflates weighing with bearing up.
  - "A la nuit il était déjà à sept lieues au Nord-Ouest du cap Saint Vincent" inverts the log's bearing: the cape bore NW by N from the ship, so the ship lay SE of the cape.
- **Computing the bore-away position.** Cape St Vincent is at 37°01′N 8°59.8′W; the cape lay NW by N of the ship, 21 nm off.
  - Read as a true bearing: 36°43′N 8°45′W.
  - Read as a compass bearing with 20–24° W variation: 36°49′–36°50′N 8°37′–8°38′W.
  - Taken together: about 36°47′N 8°41′W, ±5 nm.

### S3-PALMAS, S3-PULA, H1-16: the nautical day in Nicolas's own brackets

- **Palmas, Nicolas vi. 374** (leaf n413, image checked: https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter06nelsiala/page/n413/mode/1up):
  - Log: "March 27th [i. e. 26th]. Off Toro, P.M. … At 4·40, shortened sail and anchored in the Gulf of Palma."
  - Letter dated "Victory, in Palma Bay, Sardinia, 26th March, 1805": the fleet arrived "this evening".
- **Pula, Nicolas vi. 390** (leaf n429, image checked: https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter06nelsiala/page/n429/mode/1up), in the headnote to the letter to Vice-Admiral Bligh:
  - "March 31st [i. e. 30th]. P.M. At 6·15, weighed and made sail to the Southward …"
  - "April 1st [i. e. March 31st, P.M.] Squally. At 5·30, anchored in Pula Roads."
  - Leaf n428 is p. 389 (the letter to Dr Sewell), so the checker's "vi. 389" is a slip.
- **Nicolas vi. 392–393** (leaves n431–n432): "April 3rd. A.M. At 5·30, weighed and made sail". On 4 April, *Phoebe* rejoined at 10 a.m.; "Prepare for Battle" at 10.23; Toro E ¾ N, 6 leagues, at noon.
- **Nicolas vol. VI, Analysis, p. xxxiv** (full text: https://archive.org/download/dispatchesletter06nelsiala/dispatchesletter06nelsiala_djvu.txt): "27th Anchored in the Gulf of Palma … …". The p. xxxv header begins later.
- **James iii. 339–340** (https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/339/mode/1up): "on the 27th, to anchor in the gulf of Palma"; "on the 1st of April, removed from Palma to Pulla bay". Both are log dates.
- Villeneuve weighed at 4 p.m. on 30 March (Desbrière p. 7, as cited in the notes; not re-checked here), so the two fleets weighed within about two hours of each other.

### S3-PALERMO: no Ustica on the 7th

- **Nicolas vi. 399–400** (leaves n438–n439), to Ball on 6 April: "I shall take a position off Ustica, ready to communicate with the Vessels which will join me". This is an intention.
- **Nicolas vi. 401**, the order to Mundy of 6 April: join "off Palermo, which is my next Rendezvous".
- **Nicolas vi. 401** (leaf n440: https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter06nelsiala/page/n440/mode/1up), to Elliot, "South End of Sardinia, April 7th": "I am this moment bearing up to go off Palermo". It contains the "neither go to the Eastward of Sicily …" sentence the notes cite. The same date and place head the letter to Sotheron (vi. 402).
- **Nicolas vi. 402** (leaf n441), to Ball, dated "Victory, April 10th, 1805, off Palermo": "I shall now stand towards Madalena and Cape Corse". A 7 a.m. postscript says Hallowell has just arrived from Palermo and the French may be bound west.
- **Nicolas vi. 408** (leaf n447), to Marsden, 18 April: after waiting 48 hours "I proceeded off Sicily … On Tuesday the 9th, I made sail from the West end of Sicily for the Westward". 9 April 1805 was a Tuesday.
- **Corbett pp. 60–61** (https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/60/mode/1up): "This position was at the island of Ustica … His new station he reached on the 7th", and "On April 7th, as Nelson was taking up his position at Ustica". Ustica lies about 200 nm from the south end of Sardinia, where Nelson wrote on the 7th that he was only then bearing up.
- **Other accounts:**
  - Desbrière p. 13 (leaf n32): quotes the Ustica intention, then "Effectivement le 10 il était devant Palerme".
  - Mahan: "On the 9th and 10th he was off Palermo".
  - James iii. 340: bore up for Palermo on the 7th; "on the 9th, being then off the western end of Sicily, stood to the westward".
- **How the two dates fit.** They are reconcilable if the 10 April letter was begun before dawn, or dated by the log day, with the postscript added on the morning of the 10th. Either way he was in the Palermo area on 9–10 April.

### S6-CADIZ-AUG: 20 August; Troude's 17 August

- **James iii. 375** (https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/375/mode/1up):
  - At 10 a.m. on the 20th, with Cádiz NE about nine leagues, the allies sighted Collingwood's three; at 3 p.m. Villeneuve bore up for the harbour.
  - The *Précis*' "21 aout" is quoted and rejected on the evidence of the rôles d'équipage.
  - James counts 35 of the line in port, including six Spaniards already there.
- **Corbett pp. 270–271** (https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/270/mode/1up):
  - Collingwood to his wife, 21 August: "what a squeeze we had like to have got yesterday … down came the Combined Fleet of 36 sail of men-of-war". That count includes frigates.
  - Corbett: "on the 20th the whole fleet ignominiously re-entered Cadiz".
  - Collingwood to Gambier on the 20th expects them to sail again "reinforced by eight from that port". 29 + 8 would be 37, a forecast rather than a count.
- **Troude iii. 360**: "Le 17, les escadres alliées jetèrent l'ancre à Cadix." **Troude iii. 361**: "les escadres entrèrent à Cadix dans la journée du 17 août".
  - On p. 361 Troude also quotes Villeneuve's letter: he turned for Cádiz "le 15 à l'entrée de la nuit", 80 leagues WNW of Finisterre.
  - That makes the 17th physically impossible: at least 450–500 nm in under two days for 29 ships with slow sailers.

### S6-BRIT-CADIZ: 26 against 32

- **James iii. 379** (https://archive.org/details/navalhistoryofgr03jameuoft/page/379/mode/1up): Bickerton's four joined on 22 August; on the 30th Calder joined "with 18 line-of-battle ships". With Collingwood's four, that makes 26.
- **Desbrière p. 108** (leaf n133):
  - Cornwallis heard on 7 September that Knight had joined Collingwood with *Minotaur*, *Mars*, *Bellerophon* and *Illustrious*.
  - "déjà le 22 août Bickerton … en avait amené quatre autres, Queen, Minotaur, Mars et Bellerophon". Three names appear in both fours.
- **Desbrière p. 109** (leaf n134, image checked: https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n134/mode/1up):
  - "Collingwood avait déjà 11 vaisseaux …", then Calder with "21 autres vaisseaux" including *Dragon* and *Goliath*: "32 vaisseaux".
  - The footnote lists Calder's 21: *Prince of Wales*, *Britannia*, *Canopus*, *Spencer*, *Spartiate*, *Donegal*, *Tonnant*, *Tigre*, *Swiftsure*, *Conqueror*, *Leviathan*, *Prince*, *Temeraire*, *Neptune*, *Revenge*, *Zealous*, *Defence*, *Orion*, *Polyphemus*, *Dragon*, *Goliath*.
  - The duplicated names inflate the 11, so 32 is too high by his own lists.
- **Desbrière p. 108**: from the captured brig *Phoebus* (11 September), Allemand learned "que 26 vaisseaux étaient devant cette place et cinq autres devant Carthagène".

### S6-CORNWALLIS

- **Nicolas vii. 4** (https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nels/page/4/mode/1up), signal log of 15 August: "saw the Channel Fleet E. b. S.: twenty-four Sail of the Line, one Sloop, one Brig, one Cutter, and a Schooner". At 3 p.m. Ushant SE by E 8–9 leagues; at 6 p.m. Nelson saluted *Ville de Paris*.
- **Desbrière p. 88**: "ses 11 vaisseaux rallièrent l'armée de Cornwallis qui se trouva forte de 40 vaisseaux".
- **Desbrière p. 103**: "Ce même jour, 13 août, Cornwallis renforcé de Calder et de Nelson s'était trouvé fort de 40 vaisseaux". Nelson joined on the 15th.
- **Corbett p. 240** (https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/240/mode/1up): after sending Nelson, the *Victory* and others in, "In the end he had some thirty-six of the line with his flag, or on their way to join it, besides the Dragon and Goliath".

### S3-RETURN: the frigates

- **Nicolas vi. 465** (leaf n504):
  - Order to Sutton of 30 June to go to Tangier Bay "when I shall make the signal for that purpose": "I have not a single Frigate with me".
  - On 30 June Nelson also reports a survey on a lieutenant of the *Amazon* (vi. 465–466), so she was still in company then.
  - His letter to Matra of the same date ("I have sent the Amphion") was written in advance, to travel with her.
- **Nicolas vi. 481**: by 21 July Nelson tells Bickerton he may "join me in the Amphion".
- **Departure dates on record:** *Decade* and *Martin*, noon on 19 June (vi. 462 n.); *Amazon*, 13 July (vi. 471 n.). *Amphion*'s parting is not printed.

### S3-MARTIN

- **Nicolas vi. 432–433** (leaves n471–n472): to Marsden, 10 May, "I forgot to mention that the Martin Sloop goes off this day for Barbadoes".
- **Log of 11 May, afternoon** (vi. 433): "Martin parted".

### S3-GIB-6MAY: Donald Campbell

- **Nicolas vi. 431 n.** (leaf n470: https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter06nelsiala/page/n470/mode/1up):
  - Campbell's own complaint (Clarke & M'Arthur ii. 489) gives as a ground of dismissal "going on board your Lordship when you passed the Gut, and giving you information where the combined Fleets had gone".
  - The 10 May letter on the same page is headed "To Rear-Admiral George Campbell".
- **Corbett p. 104**: "At Gibraltar he had got into contact with Admiral Campbell", citing Hargood's life.
- **Corbett p. 105 n. 1**: Corbett thinks Nelson confused the two Campbells. Mahan, though, reads the letter as written to his "late junior in the Mediterranean".
- **For Lagos:** Mahan ch. XX ("At Lagos, Rear-Admiral Campbell … visited the 'Victory'") and Desbrière p. 19.
- **For the Strait:** James iii. 345 and Clowes v. 102 ("while in the Strait").

### S3-AMERICAN and S3-TOBAGO

- **Nicolas vi. 451** (leaf n490: https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter06nelsiala/page/n490/mode/1up), Nelson to Marsden, 11 June:
  - Off Tobago at noon on the 6th he learned that an American ship "had arrived there the day before". The American said he had been boarded by a French ship "to windward of St. Vincent; three days before", the fleet standing south.
  - In the evening, off Sandy Point, "a Schooner to leeward made the signal established by Admiral Cochrane".
- **Nicolas vi. 447–448** (leaves n486–n487):
  - Signal log: "At 6·10, a Schooner made the signal for the Enemy being at Trinidada"; at 9 a.m. on 7 June "observed Fort Abercrombie on fire".
  - The Clarke & M'Arthur note has the signal "agreed on by Colonel Shipley", and an American brig spoken by the *Curieux*, boarded "off Grenada, standing towards the Bocaz".
- **Corbett p. 162**: Bettesworth (*Curieux*) brought the report; its content is Nelson's (boarded off St Vincent, fleet going south).

### S3-BARB+2

- **Corbett p. 157** (https://archive.org/details/campaignoftrafal00corb/page/157/mode/1up): Cochrane handed "to Dacres five of his six vessels", keeping only *Northumberland*.
- **Corbett p. 161**: Laforey's *Spartiate* had been cruising to windward on the look-out.
- **James iii. 346**: Cochrane "with only the Northumberland and Spartiate 74s, his remaining four ships having been detained by Rear-admiral Dacres at Jamaica".
- **Clowes v. 104**: the same.

### S3-BARBADOS

- **Desbrière p. 39** (leaf n60, image checked: https://archive.org/details/la-campagne-maritime-de-1805/page/n60/mode/1up): "les vaisseaux mouillèrent à 4 h. 50 du soir dans la baie de Carlisle". The OCR misreads "50" as "30".

### S3-TETUAN-JUL

- **Nicolas vi. 495 and n.** (leaf n534): log "25th July. A.M. … hove to, and spoke H.M. Ship Termagant". The hour is garbled in the OCR; Mahan and the checker read 3.30, and I did not view the image. The Clarke & M'Arthur note is described in the table above.
- **Nicolas vi. 497**: Nelson to Collingwood, "Termagant came to me at four o'clock this morning, off Tariffa".
- **Letters dated 23 July** are headed "Tetuan Bay" (vi. 486–492).

### MISSED-4, MISSED-8, MISSED-9

- **Diary of 8 June, Nicolas vi. 449** (leaf n488): Maurice's letters said the French commodore told him "the Ferrol Squadron, consisting of six Sail of French, and eight of Spaniards, had arrived in Fort Royal, June the 4th". Nelson's next note doubts it.
- **Desbrière p. 109 n.**: Calder's 21, as listed above.
- **Nicolas vi. 473**: "19th July. A.M. At 7·30, anchored in Rosia Bay, Gibraltar … Found riding here H. M. Ships Decade and Amazon".
